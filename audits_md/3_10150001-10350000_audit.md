### PMID 42570138 — CLAIM_MISMATCH (ATP2B2 addendum)

Cited by the researcher as a tangential finding that Atp2b2 was "incidentally listed" among
cardiac calcium-signaling transcripts in a rat cardiotoxicity study of a lung-cancer drug's
off-target cardiac effects. Citation-verifier's verdict and justification, verbatim:

> PMID 42570138, DOI 10.1007/s12012-026-10169-w (Yan et al. 2026, Cardiovasc Toxicol) —
> **CLAIM_MISMATCH** — Article exists and is genuine (publication date 2026-08-08 confirmed in
> PubMed) — the unusually high PMID is a real, recently-issued record, not fabricated. However,
> the abstract does NOT characterize Atp2b2 as merely "incidental." Instead: "Notably, Atp2b2 was
> upregulated in mobocertinib group but downregulated in salidroside combination group...
> salidroside mitigates mobocertinib-induced electrophysiological toxicity by modulating
> Atp2b2/Agtr1b-related signaling networks." Atp2b2 is a key mechanistic finding in this paper, not
> an incidental listing as the researcher's claim implied.

Excluded from the evidence table. Note: the citation-verifier specifically checked whether the
unusually high PMID (42570138) indicated a fabricated/hallucinated record — it confirmed the
record is genuine and simply reflects a real, very recently issued PMID, and the mismatch is about
the *characterization* of the finding (incidental vs. key mechanistic), not the record's
existence. Independent of the mismatch, this paper is cardiac-electrophysiology biology in a
lung-cancer-drug cardiotoxicity model, not lung biology, so it would not have supported any
COPD-relevant claim for ATP2B2 even if accurately characterized.

The other 13 ATP2B2 addendum citations were all VERIFIED — see
`evidence_tables/_status/3_10150001-10350000/verified_citations.md` for the full addendum

## Genes marked CONTESTED / RULED OUT by red-team-skeptic

### TATDN2 — CONTESTED

Verbatim reasoning from red-team-skeptic (original review; unchanged by the ATP2B2 addendum):

> The credible-set PIP mass is real and substantial (lead variant PIP=0.87, plus most of the
> remaining 17-variant set), but that is a statement about variant-to-nearest-gene annotation, not
> about TATDN2 gene function. The researcher found literally zero COPD/lung-function/airway
> literature for TATDN2, and its established biology (TatD nuclease, R-loop resolution at stalled
> forks, BRCA1-deficient cancer cell survival, primordial-germ-cell genome maintenance in fish)
> offers no plausible COPD mechanism — the researcher says this itself and I agree it's a fair,
> non-overreaching conclusion. The candidate-gene note (rs394558/rs942379, small Kuwaiti familial
> T1D linkage study) is appropriately caveated as weak and, importantly, is about different SNPs
> than the credible set — it shouldn't be given interpretive weight either for TATDN2 or for the
> eQTL-to-IRAK2 hypothesis. The GHRL antisense-overlap architecture note is a legitimate
> alternative-explanation candidate (regulatory variants upstream of/within TATDN2 could plausibly
> act on the embedded GHRL promoter rather than on TATDN2 itself, and ghrelin/cachexia biology has
> some COPD relevance) — this deserves a one-line mention to synthesis lead as an underexplored
> alternative, even though it's correctly not being prioritized given zero credible-set overlap
> and one excluded (CLAIM_MISMATCH) citation.
>
> This is a textbook nearest-gene/LD-block case: the statistical fine-mapping signal sits on
> TATDN2-annotated variants, but two independent gene-level prioritization methods (MAGMA, PoPS)
> point away from TATDN2 toward the neighboring gene, and there is no ABC/Hi-C or other
> regulatory-linking evidence bridging the TATDN2-annotated regulatory variants to any target
> gene's promoter. That is a genuine, not manufactured, disconnect.
>
> **Verdict: CONTESTED.** The PIP mass is real, but MAGMA and PoPS — independent
> statistical/computational methods — implicate a different gene (IRAK2) at the same locus, and no
> biology supports TATDN2 as the functional candidate. Neither side of this locus is resolved.

### IRAK2 — CONTESTED

Verbatim reasoning from red-team-skeptic (original review; unchanged by the ATP2B2 addendum):

> The mechanistic case for IRAK2 in airway inflammation generally (Myddosome/TLR/IL-1R signaling,
> NF-kB/MAPK activation) is well established and not in dispute. But scrutinizing what's actually
> COPD-specific here: Baines 2017 (sputum expression, mixed asthma/COPD cohort, correlative
> biomarker not genetic) and Banaganapalli 2022 (bioinformatic DEG-network "hub gene" in COPD
> blood/lung) are the only two pieces of evidence that even mention COPD directly, and both are
> transcriptomic/correlative — expression differences in existing inflamed tissue can as easily be
> a consequence of disease/inflammation state as a cause, and "hub gene in a co-expression network"
> is not evidence of genetic causality. Everything else cited — Wang et al. 2026 (pediatric
> house-dust-mite asthma mouse model + 16HBE cells), Wei et al. 2020 (pediatric asthma peripheral
> blood), Li et al. 2018 (asthma-model Myddosome disruptors), Ritter et al. 2005
> (poly(I:C)-stimulated epithelial cells, proposed viral-exacerbation mechanism) — is asthma or
> generic-inflammation biology being extrapolated to COPD. The researcher flags this itself, which
> I credit, but the overall gene-level narrative ("IRAK2 has substantially stronger mechanistic
> support for airway-relevant inflammatory biology") risks reading as COPD-specific support when
> the primary literature is disproportionately asthma/pediatric. Per the stated priors (COPD
> inflammatory heterogeneity is real, but asthma and COPD are frequently distinct
> Th2/eosinophilic vs. neutrophilic/Th1/Tc17 programs), this extrapolation should be treated as
> suggestive of airway-immune plausibility in general, not as COPD-specific mechanistic
> validation.
>
> On statistical genetics: IRAK2 is MAGMA- and PoPS-prioritized, but is not the nearest gene, is
> not overlapped by the lead variant, and receives only ~2% of cumulative PIP mass via low-PIP
> intronic variants. This matters for interpreting what "MAGMA/PoPS-prioritized" means here. MAGMA
> is a gene-based test that aggregates SNP-level signal within a window around a gene; if that
> window overlaps the same TATDN2-dominant association signal (plausible given the genes'
> proximity), MAGMA's "hit" for IRAK2 may not be statistically independent of the TATDN2 signal at
> all — it could be the same SNPs counted under a different gene label. PoPS is explicitly a
> similarity-to-known-genes method built on expression/pathway/protein-interaction features; IRAK2
> is a canonical, richly annotated innate-immune gene, which is exactly the profile PoPS is prone
> to favor (a well-characterized "biologically compelling" gene can score well due to
> annotation-richness rather than a genuine causal signal at this specific locus — this is a
> recognized limitation of PoPS, sometimes described as it prioritizing generically GWAS-similar
> genes over the true local causal gene). So "MAGMA/PoPS-prioritized" should not be read by
> synthesis lead as independent statistical-genetic confirmation equivalent to fine-mapping or
> colocalization — it is a computational prediction with a known bias toward well-annotated genes,
> and here it is not corroborated by any fine-mapping, colocalization, TWAS, or MR study, which the
> researcher explicitly confirms is absent (Open Targets: literature-mining only, no
> genetic_association datatype, 0 hits for FEV1/FVC).
>
> The broader immune evidence (IRAK2 germline mutations/n=2 case report, small Tunisian SLE
> candidate-gene study n=142/149, subthreshold sepsis GWAS signal, rodent LPS/sepsis mechanistic
> papers) is generic innate-immune biology, none lung-specific, and none reaching genome-wide
> significance for anything — this is appropriately characterized by the researcher as background
> mechanism, not COPD evidence, and I agree it shouldn't be upgraded.
>
> **Verdict: CONTESTED.** Real innate-immune mechanistic plausibility exists, but essentially none
> of the "COPD-relevant" evidence is COPD-specific genetic evidence — it's disease-extrapolated
> (mostly asthma/pediatric) functional data plus correlative transcriptomics, while the actual
> fine-mapping PIP mass at this locus sits almost entirely on TATDN2-annotated variants, not IRAK2.
> The gene-level statistical prioritization (MAGMA/PoPS) is a plausible but non-independent,
> annotation-biased signal that should not be conflated with genetic evidence for causality at this
> specific locus.

### ATP2B2 — RULED OUT (new, ATP2B2 addendum)

**Positional background:** ATP2B2 was surfaced as a candidate gene at this locus only after the
study team corrected a genome-build error in their MAGMA/PoPS gene-location merge file. The
orchestrator independently confirmed via Open Targets that ATP2B2 spans chr3:10,324,023-10,708,007
(GRCh38, strand -1) and overlaps the locus analysis window (3:10,150,001-10,350,000) only
marginally — about 26 kb at its extreme 3' end — and does not overlap the actual credible-set
variant span (3:10,196,440-10,281,424) at all; the nearest credible-set variant (rs4684676,
PIP=0.0011) is ~42.6 kb from ATP2B2's nearest edge. ATP2B2 was not flagged by MAGMA, PoPS,
nearest-gene, or ABC prioritization in the locus input file.

Verbatim reasoning from red-team-skeptic on this addendum:

> **1. Is the window overlap a legitimate basis for candidacy?** No. A 26 kb overlap confined to
> the extreme 3' UTR/terminal-exon end of a >380 kb gene, with zero credible-set variant overlap
> and the nearest credible-set variant sitting ~42.6 kb away, is not evidence of a regulatory or
> functional relationship between this locus's causal signal and ATP2B2 — it's evidence that the
> analysis-window boundary (chosen presumably as a fixed flanking distance around the lead
> variant) happens to clip the tail of a neighboring gene. This is exactly the kind of artifact the
> window-boundary process should be expected to produce at any locus where a large gene sits just
> outside the credible set. Compounding this, ATP2B2 was independently flagged by none of MAGMA,
> PoPS, nearest-gene, or ABC — the four prioritization methods already run on this locus all
> declined to nominate it. The "corrected" merge file surfacing it looks like exactly the artifact
> it's being suspected of being: a bug fix that changed which genes' coordinate ranges get flagged
> as "overlapping the window," not new evidence that this gene is mechanistically at play. I'd
> treat this as a bookkeeping candidate, not a biological one, and recommend the orchestrator
> sanity-check whether the window/merge logic should be filtering on credible-set span rather than
> the wider analysis window before this happens again at other loci.
>
> **2. Is oe=0.123 (extreme LoF-intolerance) interpretively useful here?** Largely a red herring
> for locus-prioritization purposes, for two independent reasons. First, constraint metrics like
> LOEUF/oe reflect genome-wide selective pressure against LoF variants — a proxy for
> essentiality/haploinsufficiency sensitivity in general, not tissue- or trait-specific relevance.
> A gene can be exquisitely LoF-intolerant (as PMCA2 plausibly is, given its role in hair-cell and
> Purkinje-cell calcium homeostasis where haploinsufficiency causes hearing loss and ataxia
> phenotypes) while having no connection to COPD whatsoever. Second, and more decisively,
> constraint doesn't diagnose direction of association with a GWAS signal at all — it's a prior on
> "this gene could matter for some phenotype if perturbed," not evidence this gene is the one
> perturbed here. Given ATP2B2 has zero Open Targets COPD/FEV1-FVC association score (identical
> null to TATDN2) and its actual top disease associations are hearing loss, neurodevelopmental
> phenotypes, and epilepsy, the constraint score is consistent with "important gene for other
> tissues," not "candidate lung gene." I'd flag constraint being cited at all here as a mild
> overclaiming risk if it were used to argue for biological plausibility — the researcher didn't do
> this, appropriately treating it as background, but future synthesis should not let a high
> constraint score substitute for a mechanism.
>
> **3. Does the PMCA1/PMCA4-not-PMCA2 paralog finding strengthen or weaken the case?** This is a
> genuinely useful negative finding, not merely an absence-of-evidence artifact, and it weakens the
> case for ATP2B2 further. The researcher didn't just fail to find PMCA2-airway papers — they found
> a body of literature that specifically profiled PMCA-family isoform expression in airway smooth
> muscle by PCR/Western blot (PMID 30280184, 24912144, 38763427) and explicitly reported
> PMCA1/PMCA4 as the expressed isoforms, with PMCA2 conspicuously not detected. That's
> isoform-resolved negative data, not an unsearched gap — it's closer to "we looked and it's not
> there" than "we didn't look." Similarly for T-cell calcium signaling, the STIM1/CD147 axis papers
> name PMCA4 specifically. This pattern (real family-level relevance, consistently attributed to
> different paralogs, never PMCA2) is a stronger and more specific form of negative evidence than a
> simple absence of ATP2B2 papers would be, because it shows the field has looked at this exact
> question (which PMCA isoform matters in airway/immune calcium handling) and answered it in a way
> that excludes PMCA2. I would still flag residual uncertainty: PMCA2 is far better studied in
> inner-ear/cerebellar contexts than in airway tissue generally (understudied ≠ absent), so this
> shouldn't be treated as a definitive mechanistic exclusion — but it is legitimate, specific,
> differentiating evidence, not a null-result artifact of incomplete search.
>
> **4. Does ATP2B2 change the TATDN2-vs-IRAK2 contest?** No, and it does not become a three-way
> tie. ATP2B2 is categorically weaker than either existing candidate on every axis that matters for
> this pipeline: no credible-set variant overlap at all (vs. TATDN2's 99% PIP mass and IRAK2's 2%
> PIP mass via multiple intronic variants), no MAGMA/PoPS/ABC support (vs. IRAK2's MAGMA+PoPS
> support), no COPD-adjacent transcriptomic/expression literature of any kind (vs. IRAK2's
> sputum-exacerbation and hub-gene findings, however correlative), and no plausible cell-type/
> pathway bridge to lung biology (vs. TATDN2's admittedly absent but at least genomically-proximal
> claim, and IRAK2's well-established innate-immune signaling role that at minimum is
> mechanistically pluripotent). If anything, ATP2B2's inclusion strengthens the existing contest
> only by contrast/elimination: it is a useful negative control demonstrating what a purely
> positional, non-mechanistic, non-statistically-supported candidate looks like at this locus,
> which sharpens the reviewer's sense that TATDN2's case (real PIP mass, zero function) and IRAK2's
> case (real function, thin PIP mass) are each incomplete in different ways rather than both being
> as weak as ATP2B2. It does not resolve the TATDN2/IRAK2 tension in either direction and should
> not be given comparable weight in synthesis.
>
> **5. Verdict for ATP2B2: RULED OUT (as a candidate at this locus).** Reasoning: the candidacy
> itself rests on a window-boundary artifact (26 kb of 3'-UTR-end overlap, zero credible-set
> variant overlap, ~42.6 kb from the nearest credible-set variant) that was not corroborated by any
> of the four independent prioritization methods already applied to this locus (MAGMA, PoPS,
> nearest-gene, ABC). Open Targets shows zero COPD and zero FEV1/FVC association, matching TATDN2's
> null but without even TATDN2's fine-mapping PIP mass to offset it. The extreme LoF-constraint is
> biologically real but nonspecific and does not argue for lung relevance — it argues for hearing/
> neurodevelopmental relevance, consistent with Open Targets' own top disease associations for this
> gene. The one substantive biological angle actively investigated (PMCA-family paralog
> compensation in airway/immune calcium signaling) returned a specific, isoform-resolved negative
> result: airway and T-cell literature consistently implicates PMCA1/PMCA4, never PMCA2. Combined
> with a complete absence of GWAS/fine-mapping/colocalization/TWAS/MR/smoking-trait evidence, this
> gene should not be carried forward into the locus's candidate-gene table on equal footing with
> TATDN2 or IRAK2; it should be reported to the study team primarily as a flag that the merge-file
> "fix" may be over-including genes based on window-boundary overlap rather than credible-set
> overlap, which is worth checking at other loci in the re-run.

**Orchestrator's note on process implication:** the red-team-skeptic's recommendation in point 5
above — that the study team audit whether the corrected merge file is over-including genes based
on wide analysis-window overlap rather than credible-set-span overlap at *other* reprocessed loci
— is passed through here verbatim as a process flag for the study team, separate from this locus's
own gene-prioritization conclusion (which is unaffected).

## Known limitations of this locus's review (both subagents flagged this, in both review rounds)

Both the researcher and the red-team-skeptic exhausted their session's WebSearch budget partway
through and were unable to confirm or rule out overlap between this locus and known
smoking-initiation/cessation/cigarettes-per-day GWAS signals (e.g., GSCAN consortium loci), and a
direct GWAS Catalog REST API query for the lead variant (rs2543459) returned no record (404). This
is an open, unresolved gap that should be revisited with full WebSearch/GWAS Catalog access before
this locus's gene assignment is treated as final — see the "Prioritized gene" section of the main
evidence table for what specific follow-up analyses would help resolve both this gap and the
TATDN2-vs-IRAK2 contest. This same WebSearch-budget exhaustion recurred in the ATP2B2 addendum
round: the red-team-skeptic's two independent contradiction-search queries for ATP2B2 both failed
for the same reason, so its ATP2B2 critique is likewise a desk review of the researcher's pasted
findings rather than an independently re-searched second pass — disclosed here for full
traceability, as with the original round.

The red-team-skeptic additionally reported in the original review round that all of its own
WebSearch calls failed (budget already exhausted at 200/200 before its first call) and its two
WebFetch attempts also failed (404 / cookie-wall), meaning its critique of TATDN2/IRAK2/GHRL/VHL/
SEC13 (and the brief original ATP2B2 stub, since superseded) is a desk review of the researcher's
pasted findings and domain knowledge, not an independently re-searched second pass. This is
disclosed here for full traceability.
