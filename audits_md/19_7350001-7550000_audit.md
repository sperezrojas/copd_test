## Gene-level verdicts (CONTESTED / WEAKENED) — full reasoning preserved verbatim

### ARHGEF18 — Verdict: WEAKENED

> **LD/nearest-gene bias:** Genuinely low here — all 55 credible-set variants are intronic to ARHGEF18 and it is the sole gene with any Open Targets L2G prediction. The positional/fine-mapping case for ARHGEF18-over-neighbors is about as clean as this pipeline sees. But note the L2G score itself is only ~0.09–0.12 — "sole candidate" and "confidently implicated" are different claims, and the researcher's phrase "very strongly supported as the physically/regulatorily implicated gene" blurs them. The *positional* assignment is very strong; the *regulatory/functional* assignment is weak (L2G well below the ~0.5 confidence threshold, and the only expression-colocalization evidence (fibroblast eQTL, h4 0.64–0.77) is with HDL/WBC/neutrophil/bicarbonate/waist-circumference — non-COPD traits — not with COPD or lung function). Calling ARHGEF18 "regulatorily implicated" in COPD specifically is not supported by anything in the record; it is regulatorily implicated in a lipid/hematological trait cluster.
>
> **Smoking-mediation:** No smoking-behavior GWAS association was found at this locus at all (absence of evidence, not evidence of absence — this remains a genuine unknown rather than a resolved "not smoking-mediated" conclusion). Should not be treated as ruling smoking mediation in or out.
>
> **Pulmonary-function prior:** This is the central weakness. Per Open Targets, every genome-wide-significant trait indexed at this locus is lipid/hematological/metabolic (HDL, WBC count, neutrophil count, bicarbonate, waist circumference) — no spirometry or lung-function trait appears at all, and the fibroblast eQTL colocalizes with those same non-lung traits. Under this pipeline's own prior #2, concordance with FEV1/FEV1/FVC would be the strongest supporting evidence for a pulmonary mechanism; its total absence here, combined with a locus that is otherwise a textbook pleiotropic lipid/hematological signal, makes a competing hypothesis at least as plausible as the structural-lung-biology one: this may be a neutrophil-count/hematological pleiotropy locus that reaches nominal/GW significance in the COPD GWAS via neutrophil-count-mediated confounding (COPD patients have altered circulating neutrophil counts), rather than a locus whose COPD association reflects airway epithelial or RhoA-junction pathobiology. The researcher flagged this tension honestly ("does not cleanly fit either... prior") but the summary judgment's headline ("very strongly supported," "mechanistically coherent") doesn't carry that caveat forward with equal weight — this is where the overclaiming lives.
>
> **Structural-biology narrative — stretched across tissues:** Of the ~12 mechanistic papers cited, only two are in bronchial/airway epithelium (PMID 28512143, 23648482) and one is in lung cancer tissue (23512329, disease-irrelevant to COPD). The rest are retina (medaka fish and human Mendelian eye disease), placental trophoblast, breast cancer cell line, generic (non-airway) ciliated cells, and a cardiomyocyte knockout with an explicitly null phenotype. This is a real, well-replicated generic RhoA/tight-junction pathway — but "substantial, mechanistically coherent" overstates convergence on a lung-specific mechanism. A more accurate characterization: ARHGEF18 is a pleiotropically-expressed epithelial-junction regulator studied incidentally in bronchial cell lines among many other tissues; no paper ties its expression or genetic variation to a COPD-relevant perturbation (e.g., cigarette-smoke injury response, mucociliary dysfunction, small-airway remodeling). This is the classic pattern of matching a well-known gene function to a disease post hoc rather than the biology being discovered in a disease-relevant context.
>
> **Immunology:** Weak and off-target relative to COPD's usual neutrophilic/Th1/Tc17 programs — the eosinophil paper (29601110) is type-2/eosinophilic biology, the TLR2 monocyte paper is purely associative, the sepsis biomarker paper is disease-nonspecific, and the NADPH-oxidase paper was never tested in any leukocyte. None of this supports a COPD-specific inflammatory mechanism; it's a scattershot of "ARHGEF18 shows up somewhere in immune cells" findings.
>
> **Verdict: WEAKENED.** The gene-assignment (positional) inference holds up well, but the inference that ARHGEF18 acts on COPD risk via lung-structural/epithelial biology overreaches: the locus's only trait associations are non-pulmonary (lipid/hematological), the eQTL colocalizes with those same non-pulmonary traits rather than COPD or spirometry, and the cited mechanistic biology is drawn overwhelmingly from non-lung tissue contexts. A pleiotropic hematological/neutrophil-count-mediated explanation for the COPD signal is at least as well supported by the assembled evidence as a lung-structural one, and this alternative should be surfaced to synthesis-lead as a live possibility, not a footnote.

### PEX11G — Verdict: HOLDS (i.e., the researcher's negative conclusion about PEX11G holds up)

> Convergent negative evidence across every axis: no fine-mapping overlap (0/55 credible-set variants), no Open Targets L2G score at all, gnomAD-tolerant (LOEUF ~1.55, arguing against strong constraint/dosage sensitivity), and literally zero COPD/lung/smoking/immune-relevant literature (only peroxisome biology in liver/fibroblast/adipose, a Drosophila lipid paper, a colorectal-cancer bioinformatics paper, and one incidental unrelated case report). The researcher did not overclaim here — it reported absence accurately and did not try to rescue PEX11G's candidacy.
>
> One thoroughness flag for synthesis-lead: "PEX11G" is an unusual/relatively recently-assigned HGNC symbol for this paralog family (PEX11A/B/G), and gene-symbol history changes are a common source of false-negative literature searches. I could not verify (search budget exhausted) whether the researcher checked older aliases for this locus before concluding "no literature found." This should be spot-checked before synthesis treats the absence as fully confirmed, though even if some alias-based literature exists, the complete absence of L2G/fine-mapping support makes it unlikely to change the bottom line.
>
> **Verdict: HOLDS** — the inference that PEX11G is not well supported as the causal gene at this locus (despite PoPS prioritization) is sound and appropriately non-overreaching. Flag the alias-search thoroughness point above as a minor open item, not a reason to doubt the conclusion.
