## Contested gene: TCF7L2

**Verdict from red-team-skeptic: CONTESTED.**

Full verbatim critique (see also `evidence_tables/_status/10_112900001-113150000/critique.md`):

> **LD/nearest-gene bias:** Not a concern here — confirmed single protein-coding gene in the interval via Ensembl REST, all 12 credible-set variants intronic to TCF7L2, and VTI1A is too distant with no credible-set variant nearby. This is a clean single-gene locus, unusual in this pipeline's favor. No critique needed on this axis.
>
> **Smoking-mediation:** Independent search corroborates the researcher's negative finding — no rs7903146/TCF7L2-COPD, -lung-function, or -smoking-behavior GWAS association was found via my own cross-check either. The TCF7L2/rs7903146 literature is overwhelmingly and specifically about T2D (and T1D pathogenesis, tangentially), not smoking neurobiology or pulmonary biology. So the "smoking-mediated" alternative from the priors is not supported — but neither is a direct lung mechanism. This locus doesn't fit cleanly into either of the two priors (pulmonary biology vs. smoking mediation); it points to a third possibility the researcher already flagged: metabolic/comorbidity confounding in the COPD GWAS itself.
>
> **The "shared-risk-factor confounding" interpretation — is it adequately evidence-based?**
> No. This is the weakest link in the researcher's synthesis and should be flagged as such. None of citations 9-12 (the T2D-COPD MR/epidemiology papers) examine this locus, rs7903146, or TCF7L2 at all — they are genome-wide/phenotype-wide MR studies of T2D-COPD comorbidity in general. Citing them to explain *why this specific GWAS hit appeared* is an inferential leap: it borrows plausibility from a different, aggregate-level literature and applies it to a single-variant question the cited papers never address. The researcher is transparent about this ("candidate hypothesis," "flags... for synthesis-lead"), which is the correct posture, but as stated in the findings it reads more confidently than the underlying support justifies. This should be labeled explicitly as *the researcher's own hypothesis, not literature-supported* — it is a reasonable statistical-genetics inference (T2D is the single most robust and highly powered signal at this locus in human genetics, dwarfing anything COPD-specific in Open Targets' own scores: genetic_association 0.773 for T2D vs. 0.217 for COPD, with zero colocalization to the COPD credible set), but it is an inference from absence-of-alternative-evidence plus prior knowledge of T2D-locus strength, not a positive citation-backed finding.
>
> **Should this be treated as a pure statistical/pleiotropy artifact rather than requiring gene-level biological interpretation?**
> This is a defensible and arguably the most parsimonious reading of the total evidence, and I'd encourage the synthesis-lead to weight it heavily: (a) zero colocalization between the COPD credible set and any GTEx lung or BLUEPRINT neutrophil eQTL at this locus (different lead SNPs), (b) zero literature connecting this specific variant set to COPD/FEV1/FEV1-FVC/smoking, (c) this is literally the most replicated, most extensively functionally characterized T2D variant in human genetics with a well-established mechanism (pancreatic beta-cell Wnt signaling, GLP-1/incretin axis) that has nothing to do with lung biology, and (d) COPD GWAS of this design (large biobank-based case-control, unadjusted for T2D/BMI/metabolic syndrome) are well known to be vulnerable to case-misclassification and comorbidity-driven collider bias, especially when COPD cases are ascertained via ICD codes/self-report in older, multimorbid populations where T2D and COPD co-occur at high rates independent of shared causal biology. Given the effect is genome-wide significant only in COPD (not lung function) and Open Targets literature score for COPD is a mere 0.070, the balance of evidence favors "pleiotropy/ascertainment artifact of a T2D locus" over "TCF7L2 has genuine COPD-relevant lung or immune biology." I'd flag this for synthesis-lead as the primary candidate explanation, with appropriate hedging since it, too, is not directly tested by any cited paper.
>
> **The tangential lung literature (asthma remission methylation, AT2-aging bioinformatics, hypoxic-PH splicing, alveolar macrophage preprint) — real weight or cherry-picked pattern-matching?**
> Mostly the latter, and this is the second major issue with the researcher's presentation. Each of these should be weighted separately and skeptically:
>
> - **Qi et al. (asthma remission, CpG methylation)**: different disease (asthma, not COPD), a distal epigenetic association (CpG "annotated to" TCF7L2, not a coding/regulatory variant at this credible set), and "complete remission" is a specific and unusual phenotype. This is weak circumstantial pattern-matching — a methylation mark near the gene, not evidence the GWAS credible-set variants do anything.
> - **Chen et al. 2026 (AT2 aging)**: explicitly computational/bioinformatic, not experimentally validated for TCF7L2 specifically (per the researcher's own caveat), and about aging-related decline, not COPD pathogenesis or the credible-set variants. This should carry very little weight.
> - **Yuan et al. 2026 (hypoxic PH, tRNA-fragment-driven alternative splicing)**: mechanistically interesting but explicitly a splicing-isoform effect unrelated to common noncoding variants; disease context is pulmonary hypertension in rodent models, not COPD (even granting PH as a COPD complication, this is several inferential steps removed and the connection to *this locus's actual variants* is null).
> - **Arish et al. 2025 (alveolar macrophage stemness, preprint)**: the most mechanistically COPD-adjacent finding (macrophage self-renewal, post-viral inflammatory outcomes, beta-catenin antagonism) but it is an unreviewed bioRxiv preprint in mice, with no connection to the human noncoding credible-set variants, and the gene-identity confusion in the source nomenclature (TCF4 vs TCF7L2) — even though correctly resolved by the citation-verifier — is itself a flag for how easily this literature gets mixed up under generic search terms.
>
> None of these four papers connect to the actual credible-set variants, to COPD directly (except tangentially via PH), or to each other via a coherent mechanistic thread — they span T2D-adjacent methylation, aging bioinformatics, PH splicing, and viral-infection macrophage biology in mice. Presenting them together risks giving an impression of "converging tangential support" that they do not actually provide; each is a one-off, low-power, mostly indirect finding in a different disease/tissue/species context. The researcher does contextualize each appropriately in the findings (I don't think this rises to overclaiming in the write-up itself — the caveats are present), but synthesis-lead should be warned not to let volume-of-tangential-citations create an appearance of consensus.
>
> **Overclaiming check (claim vs. verified abstract text):** Comparing the one-line researcher claims against the verified citation descriptions, I don't find claims strengthened beyond the source — "showed among the most dramatic H3K9ac changes" for Bompada, "was associated with...replicated" for Qi, "identifies TCF7L2...as a transcription factor regulating a gene module" (correctly hedged as computational) for Chen, "did NOT impair" for Zemke, "drives alternative splicing" for Yuan (mechanism paper, appropriately stated), and "governs alveolar macrophage self-renewal" for Arish (correctly flagged as preprint) all track their verified descriptions reasonably faithfully. No overreach detected at the individual-citation level.
>
> **Contradicting evidence:** Zemke et al. (beta-catenin KO, no impairment of airway epithelial repair) is itself a useful contradicting/null data point already surfaced by the researcher — it argues against a straightforward "Wnt/beta-catenin pathway is essential to airway epithelial regeneration" mechanism that might otherwise be invoked to link TCF7L2 (a Wnt pathway transcription factor) to COPD pathophysiology. My own independent search for rs7903146/TCF7L2 + COPD/lung-function found nothing new, consistent with (not contradicting) the researcher's negative finding — no new UNVERIFIED contradicting citation to flag.
>
> **Thoroughness:** The researcher's search was adequately thorough given a single-gene, well-characterized locus — negative literature search plus targeted follow-up on tangential lung/immune connections plus T2D-comorbidity MR literature is a reasonable search strategy. I don't believe additional gene-level search is needed before synthesis (there is only one gene, and it has been searched from multiple angles: COPD-direct, smoking, lung-function, tangential lung/immune biology, and T2D-comorbidity). No thoroughness flag.
>
> **Verdict: CONTESTED**, though not on the basis of a specific paper arguing against TCF7L2 in COPD (none exists — the field is largely silent on this specific question). Rather, this is CONTESTED in the sense that the null/negative evidence (no colocalization, no lung-function association, no smoking-GWAS overlap, minimal Open Targets literature score, and the field-defining strength of the T2D signal at the identical variant) constitutes active evidence *against* a genuine COPD-specific mechanism for TCF7L2, sufficient to outweigh the tangential and largely disease-mismatched lung/immune literature offered in support. Synthesis-lead should treat this locus's primary hypothesis as "likely T2D-pleiotropy/comorbidity artifact in an unadjusted COPD GWAS," treat the tangential lung papers as weak/exploratory rather than corroborating, and explicitly flag the "shared-risk-factor confounding" explanation as the researcher's own reasonable-but-untested hypothesis rather than literature-established fact.

No new citations were introduced by this critique requiring additional citation-verifier passes.
