## Gene-level CONTESTED / WEAKENED verdicts (verbatim reasoning from red-team-skeptic)

### PTPN22 — CONTESTED

> The regional autoimmune genetics (R620W) are solid but establish PTPN22 causality
> for RA/T1D/celiac only — not COPD, and Lobo-Alves 2019 shows the same region is
> explicitly *non*-associated with another autoimmune disease (pemphigus foliaceus),
> undercutting any "PTPN22 = generic autoimmune driver, therefore relevant to COPD's
> immune component" inference. More importantly, Cao et al. 2024 is the *only*
> COPD-specific, gene-and-disease-matched test in the whole dossier, and its own
> Mendelian randomization found **no causal effect** of PTPN22 on COPD — the
> correlational "characteristic gene"/immune-infiltration finding from the same paper
> is observational and confounded by disease state, almost certainly including
> smoking status. As written, the finding is listed as supporting evidence "BUT"
> qualified, when it is more accurately net evidence *against* a causal
> PTPN22-COPD relationship from the most direct available test. Additionally, the
> immune mechanism cited (Chemin 2018: CD4+ EOMES+ cytotoxic subset in RA synovium;
> Zhang 2022: Kupffer cell M2 polarization in mouse liver injury) is doubly
> analogical — wrong tissue (synovium, liver) and, per prior #3, arguably the wrong
> cell type (COPD's PTPN22-relevant literature would need to implicate CD8+ T cells,
> alveolar macrophages, or airway epithelium/neutrophils specifically; a CD4+ synovial
> subset and a hepatic Kupffer-cell circuit are not that). Mishra 2025's pleiotropic
> comorbidity-factor finding is honestly the most defensible line of evidence, but it
> is explicitly non-COPD-specific.

### PHTF1 — WEAKENED

> The researcher's own "partially replicated" framing is generous. Ban et al. 2016 is
> a genuinely discriminating test — in a population where PTPN22 R620W is
> monomorphic, PHTF1's own tag SNP showed no effect on autoimmune thyroid disease,
> with the BCL2L15/GPR103 region significant instead. That's evidence pointing *away*
> from PHTF1 as an independently causal gene at this locus, not merely "partial"
> support. Zhu 2016 (gene-based RA signal) is the only finding cutting the other way.
> The headlined "highest smoking-initiation OT score of the three" is doing a lot of
> rhetorical work in the ranking but is unconfirmed by any primary smoking GWAS and is
> subject to the same LD/nearest-gene ambiguity flagged at the locus level — it should
> not be elevated above the (also weak) autoimmune genetics as the basis for ranking
> PHTF1 second. The leukemia and breast-cancer-isoform citations are correctly treated
> by the researcher as background gene-biology only, not disease-relevant.

### RSBN1 — HOLDS (as evidence of non-relevance)

> No overreach. Every citation is explicitly non-lung, non-immune (spermatogenesis,
> chromatin/TAD biology), and the researcher says so plainly rather than stretching an
> analogy. Consistent with OT L2G data (near-zero score). This is the one gene in the
> writeup where the claim exactly matches the evidence with no inferential leap
> attempted.

### AP4B1, DCLRE1B, BCL2L15 — HOLDS (as unlikely candidates)

> No overreach detected. The DCLRE1B/pulmonary-fibrosis point is handled well —
> researcher correctly distinguishes restrictive telomere-related pulmonary fibrosis
> from obstructive COPD rather than conflating them, and correctly notes the finding
> is disease-class-level, not DCLRE1B-specific. AP4B1 (SPG47 neurodevelopmental) and
> BCL2L15 (GI apoptosis) are honestly presented as unrelated to lung/immune biology.

## Locus-level critique (verbatim)

> The Open Targets background data is the single most important piece of evidence
> here, and it argues strongly against this locus being lung-biology-mediated: 356
> credible sets at the lead SNP, essentially all autoimmune/blood-cell, **zero** COPD
> or spirometric, with L2G overwhelmingly favoring PTPN22, not RSBN1. Per prior #2,
> absence of spirometric concordance is a real gap, not a neutral non-finding — and
> here it's a *complete* absence across 356 independent GWAS signals at the same
> variant, which is much stronger negative evidence than a typical single-trait null.
> The researcher correctly surfaced this rather than downplaying it. Applying prior
> #1, the smoking-initiation evidence is also weak: PHTF1's OT score (0.44) is not
> corroborated by any primary smoking-GWAS publication, and given that this is the
> same LD block where nearest-gene/L2G assignment is already contested between PTPN22
> and PHTF1, the smoking-initiation score itself is vulnerable to the identical
> LD-attribution ambiguity the researcher rightly worries about for COPD attribution.
> It should not be treated as semi-independent corroboration.

## Overall skeptic assessment (verbatim)

> The ranking (PTPN22 > PHTF1 > RSBN1 > AP4B1/DCLRE1B/BCL2L15) is defensible only as a
> ranking of *general regional autoimmune-genetics plausibility*, and the researcher's
> own hedged language ("inferential/analogical," "not established," "pending direct
> verification") already signals this. But presenting it as a ranked list of COPD
> candidates risks synthesis-lead over-weighting PTPN22 as a "leading" gene. Given (1)
> zero spirometric or COPD credible-set concordance across 356 signals at the lead
> variant, (2) the only direct COPD test (Cao et al.'s own MR) being null, and (3) no
> confirmed smoking-GWAS signal for any gene here, I think the more defensible framing
> for synthesis is closer to the alternative you proposed: **this is predominantly an
> autoimmune/blood-cell locus (most likely driven by PTPN22 R620W for those traits),
> with no established direct COPD mechanism for any gene, and at most a weak,
> unconfirmed, pleiotropy/smoking-adjacent link.** The rank-ordering should be
> presented as "least implausible among non-established candidates," not as evidence
> any of them is a real COPD gene.
