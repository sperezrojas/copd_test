## Critique: Locus 12:31,600,001–31,750,000 (AMN1/DENND5B/ETFBKMT)

Note on process: I attempted four targeted searches (AMN1 + lung/COPD, ETFBKMT/METTL20 + lung/oxidative stress, DENND5B + lung eQTL, and the two top credible-set rsIDs + lung function) specifically to hunt for contradicting evidence or alternative gene assignments at this locus, but the session's web search budget was exhausted before any of these returned results. I could not independently confirm the researcher's negative findings ("no lung-function GWAS/eQTL for X") via a fresh search. This is a genuine thoroughness gap — flagging it explicitly per instruction 5, since absence-of-evidence claims for all three genes rest entirely on the researcher's own search completeness, which I cannot corroborate. Synthesis lead should treat the "no pulmonary literature found" claims as provisional unless the researcher documents its search strategy (terms, databases) for these specific negative results.

### AMN1 — WEAKENED

The case for AMN1 rests entirely on combined PIP (~0.78) across two intronic, non-coding, non-eQTL-annotated variants, with zero human functional or genetic literature. This is being treated as the "leading" gene at the locus, but that inference conflates statistical fine-mapping with causal-gene identification. Two structural points the researcher's synthesis underweights:

1. **The credible set is a single LD block spanning two VEP-annotated genes (AMN1 and ETFBKMT), not independent evidence for AMN1 over ETFBKMT.** rs144301505 and rs76930395 sit in AMN1 introns; rs75048934 and rs2086777 sit in ETFBKMT introns/3'UTR — all four are in the same statistical credible set. Summing PIP "by gene" based on which gene body a SNP happens to fall in is a positional-annotation artifact, not evidence that the causal regulatory element functionally belongs to AMN1 rather than ETFBKMT (or a shared/bidirectional regulatory region between them, given their apparent physical proximity). The 0.78-vs-0.18 gap looks like meaningful separation but may just reflect where intron/exon boundaries happen to fall relative to each SNP's genomic position, not which gene's expression is actually perturbed.
2. **No eQTL, no chromatin/regulatory annotation, no colocalization was cited for any variant.** Absent that, "combined PIP favors AMN1" should be reported as *positional fine-mapping favors the AMN1 gene body*, not as genetic evidence AMN1 is the effector gene. The yeast-only functional literature (mitotic exit network) adds essentially no mammalian mechanistic plausibility and should not be allowed to shore up the positional case — it's included for completeness, not as corroboration, and the researcher correctly hedges this, but the overall cross-gene framing ("fine-mapping favoring AMN1") risks reading as more decisive than it is.

Verdict: WEAKENED — the inference that AMN1 is the best-supported gene overreaches what intronic-variant PIP alone can establish, especially given the shared credible set with ETFBKMT and complete absence of eQTL/regulatory/human functional data.

### DENND5B — WEAKENED (bordering CONTESTED on the structural point)

Two real, verified human/mammalian phenotypes exist (BMI via common variant, Gordon et al. 2019 PMID 30837651; severe neurodevelopmental syndrome via de novo variants, Scala et al. 2024 PMID 38387458), but neither is lung-relevant, and the architecture of the neurodevelopmental finding (rare de novo coding/structural variants causing protein-misfolding disease) is not informative about a common noncoding COPD-associated variant's mechanism — these are different allelic classes acting through plausibly different mechanisms, and citing the neurodevelopmental paper as supporting evidence for DENND5B's "dosage sensitivity" is reasonable in isolation but shouldn't be read as increasing plausibility for a COPD role specifically.

More importantly: the gene body sits ~9kb outside the locus boundary, and no credible-set variant sits within DENND5B itself — only the antisense lncRNA DENND5B-AS1 overlaps. This means the PoPS-prioritized gene and the physical credible-set region are not the same interval. PoPS is a similarity-based (not strictly local) method so distal prioritization isn't inherently invalid, but when the only physical overlap is with a distinct transcript (DENND5B-AS1, an antisense lncRNA with its own potential independent regulatory function, not characterized here at all), calling this "PoPS supports DENND5B" conflates two different causal-gene hypotheses: (a) DENND5B itself is the effector via some long-range regulatory contact, vs. (b) DENND5B-AS1 is the proximal functional element and DENND5B is guilt-by-association. The researcher's findings don't distinguish these, and neither does PoPS by construction. This is worth explicit downgrading, not just noting.

Verdict: WEAKENED — real biology, but non-pulmonary phenotypes plus a structural mismatch between the statistical prioritization method and physical locus boundaries. I stop short of CONTESTED because no source directly argues against DENND5B or for a competing causal mechanism at this locus — it's an evidentiary gap/mismatch, not a contradiction.

### ETFBKMT — HOLDS (as reported, i.e., correctly flagged as speculative)

The mitochondrial ETFB-methyltransferase biology (Małecki et al. 2014, PMID 25416781) is solid biochemistry, but the "oxidative-metabolic stress in smoking-exposed lung" link is pure extrapolation with zero direct citations connecting ETFBKMT to lung, inflammation, or smoking biology. The researcher explicitly labels this "plausible but wholly untested," which is the correct level of hedging — I'd flag this as an area where the synthesis lead should ensure the hedge survives into any downstream ranking/prose (i.e., this should not be described anywhere as "supporting a lung mechanism" without the "untested" qualifier attached every time). Also worth noting: ETFBKMT is not LoF-constrained (pLI-like 0.0001) and its strongest OT disease link is T2D, not COPD — mildly disfavoring a haploinsufficiency-type disease mechanism, though this doesn't rule out a regulatory/expression-level GWAS mechanism.

Verdict: HOLDS — as long as "plausible but wholly untested" is preserved verbatim or equivalent in any downstream synthesis and not silently upgraded.

### Smoking-mediation assessment

Prior 1 asks whether this locus is more plausibly smoking-mediated (neurobiological) vs. direct pulmonary biology. Here, none of the three genes shows a meaningfully elevated smoking-initiation signal in Open Targets (AMN1 not reported at all; DENND5B 0.0514; ETFBKMT 0.0709 — both near baseline/weak). Taken at face value, this argues against a smoking-behavior-mediated mechanism for this locus, which is worth stating explicitly rather than leaving implicit — it's evidence, not absence of evidence, for prior 1's alternative hypothesis (direct pulmonary or other biology).

However, this needs to be read alongside prior 2: there is a **complete absence of any spirometric (FEV1, FEV1/FVC) association evidence** for AMN1, DENND5B, or ETFBKMT in what's reported. Per the priors, concordance with lung-function traits is the strongest evidence for a direct pulmonary mechanism, and its total absence here is a real negative, not a neutral gap. So the correct synthesis is: this locus's COPD association is *not* well-explained as smoking-mediated (weak/null smoking-initiation signals) *but is also not corroborated by direct pulmonary-function genetics* (no FEV1/FEV1FVC hits found for any candidate gene). That leaves the locus without strong support for either of the two main mechanistic hypotheses the priors ask us to weigh — this ambiguity should be stated plainly in the synthesis rather than allowing the "not smoking" inference to be read as positive evidence for a pulmonary mechanism by elimination.

### Cross-gene / overall

- The researcher's honest statement that "fine-mapping (AMN1) and PoPS (DENND5B) disagree and independent literature does not adjudicate" is appropriately cautious and should be preserved as the headline framing — I'd only add that fine-mapping itself doesn't cleanly favor AMN1 over ETFBKMT either, given the shared credible set (see AMN1 critique above), so it's really a three-way ambiguity, not a two-way one.
- No contradicting/null-finding papers were identified in the verified citation set for any of the three genes, and I was unable to independently search for such papers due to budget exhaustion — flagging this explicitly as an unresolved thoroughness gap rather than asserting confidence that no such papers exist.
- Overall, this locus's candidate-gene case is weak across the board: no gene here has both statistical (fine-mapping/PoPS) and biological (lung-relevant functional/genetic) support simultaneously. That should be reflected as a low-confidence locus in any final ranking, not resolved toward AMN1 by default of having the highest PIP.

**Summary verdicts:** AMN1 — WEAKENED. DENND5B — WEAKENED (structural mismatch between credible set and gene body flagged for synthesis-lead attention). ETFBKMT — HOLDS (contingent on hedge being preserved). No new CONTESTED citations to submit to citation-verifier; no citation here is factually disputed, the issue is inferential overreach in how positional/statistical evidence is being narrated as gene-level support.
