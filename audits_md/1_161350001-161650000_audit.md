## Genes marked CONTESTED or WEAKENED by red-team-skeptic (verbatim reasoning preserved)

### HSPA6 — CONTESTED

"The researcher's own summary ('generic, highly inducible stress-response gene... no
COPD-specific literature') is honest, not overclaimed. But I'd go further than 'weak': the one
piece of *functional* annotation actually available for the lead variant rs2099684 (Yazar et al.
2026, PMID 42102734) is an eQTL for the *neighboring* gene FCGR3A, not HSPA6 — that's not merely
absent evidence for HSPA6, it's a specific finding that actively points the regulatory mechanism
at this exact variant away from HSPA6. Combine that with Open Targets' cross-trait L2G (blood
counts, albumin, eGFR, Takayasu, hematocrit, macrophage/GTEx QTLs) never once naming HSPA6 as
top gene despite tagging the same locus repeatedly, and the case that HSPA6's prioritization is
nearest-gene/PoPS/ABC artifact rather than true biology becomes fairly strong. The
irritant-inducibility data (cigarette smoke condensate in BEAS-2B, sulfur mustard in A549) is
real but is generic heat-shock-family stress response (HSPA6 is one of many genes induced under
xenobiotic/oxidative stress in almost any epithelial cell line) — it doesn't distinguish HSPA6 as
a lung-specific or COPD-specific mechanism versus a nonspecific stress-response bystander. No
lung-function GWAS support compounds this (prior #2).

Verdict: CONTESTED. Not merely under-supported — there is a specific competing finding (the lead
variant's own published eQTL target is the neighboring gene, not HSPA6) that argues against
HSPA6 being the effector gene at this locus."

### FCGR3A — WEAKENED

"The researcher's hedged framing ('rests on plausible immune mechanism... plus an
eQTL/statistical-genetics link... rather than any direct COPD phenotype evidence') is
appropriately conservative and matches the underlying evidence — no overclaiming in the
researcher's own language. But several things deserve more skepticism than even that hedge
implies:

- Tissue mismatch. The rs2099684→FCGR3A eQTL (Yazar et al. 2026) is NK-cell-specific and, per the
  study's design (single-cell eQTL, sex-interacting), is derived from blood/PBMC data, not lung
  tissue. NK cells and CD16+ monocytes do traffic through lung, but a blood eQTL is not
  lung-tissue evidence of effect direction or magnitude in situ; this should be weighted as
  suggestive-of-mechanism, not disease-tissue-confirmed.
- Phenotype analogy problem. Takayasu arteritis (large-vessel autoimmune vasculitis) and
  malaria-antibody response are being used as supportive precedent for 'this variant matters for
  immune-mediated disease,' but neither is COPD or even a lung phenotype. Pleiotropy of an
  FCGR-cluster regulatory variant across autoimmune and infectious-disease traits establishes
  that the variant is immunologically active in general — it does not establish that its COPD
  association (if real) operates through the same mechanism, cell type, or even the same gene.
  This is an analogy, not corroborating disease evidence, and should be labeled as such.
- Within that analogy, the evidence is already shakier than presented: the Takayasu signal is
  explicitly reported as joint 'FCGR2A/FCGR3A,' not FCGR3A alone, and in the malaria literature
  the researcher notes rs2099684 itself was *not* the variant reaching significance in one of the
  two cited studies (Cretin et al., a different variant, rs1771575, was). Citing Cretin et al. as
  evidence 'for' rs2099684 is a soft overclaim worth flagging even though the citation is
  verified as existing and accurately quoted — the researcher's inclusion of it under 'FCGR3A
  evidence' risks being read as more supportive than it is.
- Paralog confounding is unresolved and is the single biggest problem. FCGR3A, FCGR3B, and
  FCGR2A are tandemly duplicated, highly homologous, and FCGR3B carries a common CNV — no cited
  paper cleanly isolates FCGR3A's effect from FCGR3B/FCGR2A at this variant. MAGMA (the only tool
  prioritizing FCGR3A internally) is a gene-based test that sums signal in a window and is known
  to perform poorly at distinguishing tightly linked paralogous genes — its prioritization of
  FCGR3A here could just as easily be capturing FCGR3B or FCGR2A signal.
- One mitigating point in FCGR3A's favor: no overlap was found with smoking-behavior GWAS
  signals, which argues against a smoking-neurobiology confound (prior #1) — if the signal is
  real, it's more likely immune/lung-cell-intrinsic than smoking-mediated. But the absence of any
  lung-function (FEV1/FEV1/FVC) association (prior #2) is a real gap that the researcher didn't
  downplay but that should be weighted explicitly against causality.

Verdict: WEAKENED. FCGR3A has a genuine molecular anchor (a specific, cell-type-resolved eQTL at
the lead variant) that HSPA6 lacks, which is a real point in its favor relative to HSPA6. But
'immune plausibility + eQTL in the wrong tissue + disease analogies from unrelated
autoimmune/infectious phenotypes + an unresolved paralog-confounding problem' does not amount to
COPD-relevant evidence, and the malaria/Takayasu citations should not be allowed to read as
COPD-supportive in synthesis even though they are individually accurate."

## Non-candidate gene flagged by red-team-skeptic: FCGR3B

"This is formally out of scope as a 'candidate' here, but I don't think it can be silently set
aside. Open Targets' own cross-trait L2G evidence and disease-association score
(genetic_association 0.433, highest at the locus) consistently favor FCGR3B over both listed
candidates, and FCGR3B is neutrophil-expressed — directly relevant to the
neutrophilic-inflammation axis of COPD (prior #3), which neither HSPA6 nor FCGR3A biology
addresses. Combined with HSPA6 never being top L2G gene in any indexed study and FCGR3A's
paralog-confounding problem, the aggregate picture is that the true causal gene at this locus may
not be on the candidate list at all. I'd flag this explicitly to synthesis-lead as a
candidate-gene-selection gap warranting a follow-up look at FCGR3B (and joint
FCGR2A/FCGR3A/FCGR3B CNV-aware fine-mapping literature) before finalizing this locus, rather than
forcing a choice between HSPA6 and FCGR3A alone."
