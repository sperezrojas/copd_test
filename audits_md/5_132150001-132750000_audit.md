## Genes/claims marked CONTESTED by red-team-skeptic (reasoning preserved verbatim)

### IRF1 — directional inconsistency in mechanistic literature (CONTESTED)

> I'm marking **IRF1's mechanistic-directionality narrative CONTESTED** (not IRF1's candidacy
> per se, which remains a live WEAKENED candidate): Hey et al. 2021 (enhanced Irf1 → excessive
> inflammation) and Veerati et al. 2020 (delayed/blunted IRF1-dependent antiviral induction in
> COPD donor cells) point toward opposite functional directions of IRF1 dysregulation in
> COPD-relevant tissue, and the researcher's synthesis does not flag this tension. This is not a
> new citation (both already VERIFIED) so nothing needs to go back to the citation-verifier — it's
> a synthesis-level contradiction, preserved here for the audit trail.

Specifically: Hey J, et al. Nat Commun 2021 (PMID 34764283) found enhanced Irf1 function/expression
in airway macrophages of Scnn1b-transgenic mice impairs efferocytosis and produces excessive
inflammatory responses (a gain-of-function-flavored, pro-inflammatory phenotype). Veerati PC, et
al. Front Immunol 2020 (PMID 32499788) found rhinovirus-induced antiviral gene induction
(including IRF1) is delayed, not deficient, in COPD/asthma donor airway epithelial cells relative
to healthy donors (a blunted-response phenotype). These point in opposite functional directions
and were not reconciled by any source found in this review.

> - Several IRF1 papers cited are explicitly asthma-oriented (Matsuzaki et al., Veerati et al.'s
>   COPD/asthma donor comparison, Koetzler et al.'s asthma/COPD framing).
