## 2026-09-11 ADDENDUM: THRA added after genome-build-error correction

**Background:** The study team discovered a genome-build error in the internal
MAGMA/PoPS gene-location merge file (`loci/copd-all-gene-prioritization.txt`) used to
generate candidate-gene lists. A corrected re-merge surfaced THRA (thyroid hormone receptor
alpha) as a candidate gene at this locus that was never evaluated in the original 13-gene
review.

**Step 1 — coordinate check (performed by orchestrator, not a subagent):** THRA
(ENSG00000126351) was independently confirmed via Ensembl coordinates (through the Open
Targets GraphQL API) to span chr17:40,058,290-40,093,867 (GRCh38). This lies entirely
inside the locus's defined 1 Mb window (17:39,150,001-40,150,000), confirming THRA is a
genuine physical resident of the locus and that the build-error correction is legitimate,
not spurious. However, THRA does NOT overlap the locus's actual fine-mapped credible-set
span: the nearest credible-set variant is rs7224129 (17:39,919,173, PIP=0.00062, annotated
to ORMDL3 as downstream_gene_variant), approximately 139 kb from THRA's start coordinate.
No credible-set variant is annotated to THRA. This places THRA in an evidentiary category
similar to CSF3/PSMD3 from the original review (window-resident, no credible-set variant),
though physically closer to the credible-set cluster (~139 kb vs. CSF3/PSMD3's ~300-450 kb).
Because THRA does plausibly belong at this locus (window overlap confirmed), the pipeline
proceeded to full researcher/citation-verifier/red-team-skeptic evaluation rather than
stopping at the coordinate check.

**Step 1b — Open Targets structured lookup (performed by orchestrator):** THRA has the
single highest COPD-association score of any gene queried at this locus (0.252, vs. GSDMB
0.192 and ORMDL3 0.131), essentially no asthma-association score (unlike every other
high-COPD-scoring gene here), a notable "bronchopulmonary dysplasia" top-hit (0.259, its
single highest score across 1836 diseases), and strong LOF constraint (gnomAD lof
oe=0.276, 90% CI 0.179-0.439). This structured database context (NOT a verified literature
claim) motivated the full researcher dispatch — see full detail in
`evidence_tables/_status/17_39150001-40150000/opentargets.md`.

**Steps 2-4 — researcher, citation-verifier, red-team-skeptic:** All 13 citations returned
by the THRA-focused researcher dispatch were VERIFIED by citation-verifier (no
retractions, no NOT_FOUND, no CLAIM_MISMATCH — see
`verified_citations_thra_addendum.md`). The red-team-skeptic then critiqued THRA and
explicitly assessed whether it changes the original ORMDL3-vs-GSDMB tension (see full
verbatim critique below and in `critique_thra_addendum.md`).

**Outcome:** THRA is marked CONTESTED (see below) and does NOT change the original
prioritized-gene call. ORMDL3 remains the prioritized gene, confidence LOW, unchanged from
the original review. See `evidence_tables/17_39150001-40150000.md` Prioritized gene section
for full reasoning.

## Genes marked CONTESTED (reasoning preserved verbatim from red-team-skeptic)

### PGAP3 — CONTESTED
> The case rests on (1) positional/ABC top-ranking, which the researcher already concedes
> doesn't distinguish PGAP3 from GSDMB/ORMDL3/ZPBP2 sharing the same regulatory block, and (2)
> one hedged SMR/TWAS paper (Wen 2025) that lists PGAP3 as one of four co-equal "putative"
> pleiotropy genes with an explicit "needs functional validation" caveat. No paper positively
> assigns causal COPD biology to PGAP3 specifically; Open Targets COPD score is the lowest of
> all high-PIP-adjacent genes (0.0067). GPI-anchor remodeling biology has no independent
> connection to lung/immune pathways. This is the textbook nearest-gene/LD-hitchhiker problem
> the priors ask us to watch for — a purely positional case with essentially no gene-specific
> mechanistic support. The researcher already reaches this conclusion; I agree and elevate it to
> an explicit CONTESTED rather than leaving it ambiguous, so synthesis-lead doesn't quietly
> promote PGAP3 on the strength of its ABC/PIP rank alone.

### JUP — CONTESTED
> No COPD or asthma literature at all; disease phenotype is exclusively
> cardiac/dermatological (Naxos disease/ARVC); strong LOF constraint argues against a common
> noncoding regulatory variant driving a haploinsufficiency-type mechanism. PoPS scores are
> similarity-network-based and can nominate genes with no direct disease-tissue evidence — that
> appears to be exactly what happened here. Purely circumstantial/positional case, better
> explained by JUP's established cardiac/skin biology being irrelevant to this locus's lung
> signal. The researcher reaches the same conclusion; I concur explicitly as CONTESTED.

### IKZF3 — CONTESTED
> Zero COPD-specific literature; Open Targets COPD score negligible; disease profile
> dominated by cancer/immunodeficiency; extreme LOF constraint. All cited functional papers are
> either allergic/Th2 mouse asthma models (Kilic 2024) or general T-cell immunology unrelated to
> lung disease (Ridley 2020) or a different gene entirely (IKZF1, cited only as paralog
> background). The researcher's own conclusion (Th2/ACOS-mediated at best) is right, but given
> the near-total absence of positive evidence I'd call this outright CONTESTED rather than a soft
> "low priority" — this looks like network/pathway-based prioritization noise from PoPS-style
> gene-set enrichment (T-cell activation category) rather than a real candidate.

### ERBB2 — CONTESTED
> No COPD, asthma, or lung-function evidence whatsoever; entirely an oncology gene (HER2
> amplicon). Textbook bystander/LD-block passenger. Researcher's own call is correct; I concur
> fully as CONTESTED (oncology-only).

### STARD3 — CONTESTED
> Missense variant studied only in an unrelated congenital urological condition
> (hypospadias); remaining literature is gastric-cancer amplicon biology and general
> cholesterol-trafficking cell biology with no lung data. The Open Targets "smoking initiation"
> score (0.151) is an unverified structured-DB signal with literally no literature found to
> substantiate or refute it — it should not be treated as evidence either way. Purely
> circumstantial/positional. CONTESTED.

### ZPBP2 — CONTESTED
> Notably, zero COPD evidence exists for ZPBP2 — the only functional data (Fang 2011) is an
> adult-onset asthma association in a Han Chinese cohort, and even that paper's own data argue
> against ZPBP2 acting independently: the risk allele's effect co-occurs with increased
> ORMDL3/GSDMB expression, consistent with ZPBP2's coding variant merely tagging the shared
> regulatory haplotype (reinforced by Verlaan 2009's domain-wide cis-effects). Canonical
> sperm-zona-pellucida-binding function has no plausible lung mechanism. This is a clean case of
> a missense variant that looks superficially like strong evidence (coding variant! disease
> association!) but the same paper's own expression data undercuts an independent-effect
> interpretation. CONTESTED — correctly self-diagnosed as a cis-regulatory marker by the
> researcher, and additionally weakened by having no COPD data of any kind.

### PNMT — CONTESTED
> Single relevant paper is an OVA-asthma mouse model (not COPD), plus a developmental
> lung-atlas paper (not disease-specific). Open Targets COPD score negligible, disease profile

### GSDMA — CONTESTED

### THRA — CONTESTED (added 2026-09-11 addendum)
> LD / physical-location bias. THRA is a legitimate window-resident (chr17:40,058,290-40,093,867,
> inside the 1 Mb region), so the build-error correction is not itself spurious. But THRA
> carries no credible-set variant — the nearest is rs7224129 at PIP=0.0006 (essentially a
> non-signal), already annotated to ORMDL3, and it sits ~139 kb away. This is functionally the
> same evidentiary position as CSF3/PSMD3 in the original 13-gene review (window-resident,

> but proximity alone is not fine-mapping evidence, and nothing in the statistical-genetics
> search (no THRA-specific GWAS, coloc, TWAS, or MR hit) closes that gap. The Sakornsakolpat "up
> to 17 genes" observation is a dangling thread — unresolved whether THRA is even among them —
> and should be weighted as approximately zero evidence until resolved, not partial support.
>
> Smoking-mediation. No direct evidence either way was surfaced (no THRA-smoking-trait GWAS, no
> smoke-exposure expression study analogous to the ORMDL3 Chen et al. papers). This is a genuine
> blind spot rather than a resolved null — THRA's case can be neither strengthened nor weakened
> on smoking grounds from what's presented.
>
> Contradicting evidence. The strongest signal in this dossier is actually negative evidence:
> the human THRA-loss-of-function syndrome (RTHα, OMIM 614450), characterized across four
> independent case series (PMID 26585273, 28471274, 30747412, 38744258), reports no
> pulmonary/respiratory phenotype despite a well-documented multi-system clinical picture
> (growth, dysmorphism, neurodevelopment, GI, cardiac). This is a meaningful contradiction: if
> germline THRA dysfunction had a strong lung-structural or lung-function effect, a rare but
> clinically well-studied human LOF disorder is a reasonable place to expect it to surface, and
> it hasn't. Combined with three negative/inconclusive RCTs and a Cochrane review finding no
> thyroid-hormone-supplementation benefit for BPD (PMID 11076326, 18590560, 16625632), the
> mechanistic case for THRA-specific (as opposed to generic thyroid-axis) lung biology is
> thin-to-negative, not just "absent." I'd flag the RTHα phenotype papers as the single most
> decision-relevant piece of evidence here, and it cuts against THRA.

> Overclaiming check. The researcher's own bottom line is appropriately hedged ("weak and
> speculative... would not prioritize THRA... cannot be dismissed outright") and matches the
> underlying evidence — no overreach detected. The Means et al. 1997 CaMKIV/thymus claim and the
> Wu et al. 2017 TSH-receptor paper are correctly flagged by the researcher as non-lung/
> off-target respectively; no inflation of these into COPD-relevant immune evidence occurred.
> This is a well-calibrated writeup.
>
> Thoroughness. The researcher's search was reasonably thorough for a single new gene
> (documented 0-hit search strings, explicit statement of what couldn't be found), and
> appropriately separated reverse-causation-prone adult NTIS/COPD literature from germline
> evidence. I don't think this gene needs a redo before synthesis, modulo the search-budget
> caveat above.
>
> Verdict: CONTESTED (leaning toward effectively unsupported). The contradiction is not a rival
> paper making a competing genetic claim (none exists), but rather the absence of a lung
> phenotype in the direct human LOF disease model plus negative RCT evidence on the mechanism
> synthesis-lead would need (thyroid hormone signaling → lung structure/function) — both
> already-verified, so nothing new needs flagging to citation-verifier as unverified. I am not
> asserting THRA is disproven — a single-gene, single-population LOF syndrome could plausibly
> mask a subtle lung phenotype, especially since alveolarization effects might be too subtle for
> the case reports' focus — but the "no credible-set variant + no statistical-genetics support +
> negative-leaning mechanistic evidence" combination means this is materially weaker than the
> prior 13 candidates, not on par with them.
>
> Does THRA change the ORMDL3-vs-GSDMB tension? (c) — it does not materially change the
> original tension. THRA introduces no fine-mapping evidence at all (no credible-set variant,
> PIP≈0 for the nearest one), whereas the ORMDL3/GSDMB tie-break problem is precisely that both
> genes sit under real credible-set signal in a shared cis-regulatory block. THRA cannot unseat
> a gene from a tie-break it isn't even competing in on the same evidentiary axis. The one number
> that makes THRA look competitive — Open Targets COPD score 0.252 vs GSDMB's 0.192 and ORMDL3's

> researcher could not corroborate it with any primary genetic or mechanistic study specific to

> negative-leaning human-disease-model signal is not a basis to reweight a tie-break that rests

> candidacy is essentially proximity/database-driven rather than fine-mapping- or
> mechanism-driven, underscoring how crowded and non-specific this locus's gene-attribution
> problem is.
>
> Flag on the Open Targets score discrepancy: Yes — the 0.252 THRA COPD score is at meaningful

> evidentiary domains the pipeline otherwise requires (statistical genetics: zero hits;

> Bottom line: THRA does not unseat ORMDL3 and does not resolve the ORMDL3-vs-GSDMB tie-break;

## Genes marked WEAKENED (reasoning preserved verbatim, included for full transparency even though the pipeline only mandates verbatim preservation for CONTESTED)

### KRT15 — WEAKENED
> The mouse mechanistic literature (Krt15-KO worsened smoke-induced emphysema/EMT via
> MMP-9; impaired basal-cell regeneration) is genuinely COPD-specific and smoke-exposure-mediated
> (not smoking-behavior/neurobiology confounded — this is direct pulmonary-toxicity/repair
> biology, a category the smoking prior treats favorably). However, the genetic linkage is weak:
> MAGMA-only prioritization, no coding/splice credible-set variant, and — critically — the
> researcher never establishes whether the MAGMA gene-based signal for KRT15 is driven by the
> same credible-set variants anchoring GSDMB/ORMDL3/PGAP3 or is an independent, weaker secondary
> signal in the gene-dense window. MAGMA aggregates SNP-level p-values across a gene body/window
> and can pick up bystander significance from a neighboring strong locus without implying KRT15
> itself carries the causal variant. There's also no FEV1/FEV1-FVC concordance cited for KRT15
> (prior 2 — absence should be weighed). The inference that KRT15 is a strong candidate "despite
> modest genetic prioritization" overreaches: compelling biology cannot substitute for an
> unestablished genetic link to this specific credible set.

### GSDMB — WEAKENED (this is the important one)
> This is where I think overclaiming risk is highest, addressing prompt point (a) directly.
> Strip out what's actually COPD-specific: the Sakornsakolpat 2019 and Shrine 2019 papers support
> the *locus* (17q12-21) as COPD/lung-function-relevant at the level of hundreds of loci and note
> explicit asthma-sharing for 14 of them — this is locus-class evidence, not gene-specific, and
> doesn't distinguish GSDMB from ORMDL3/ZPBP2/GSDMA/PGAP3. The two directly-characterized
> credible-set variants (rs2305480 missense, rs11078928 splice-acceptor abolishing pyroptotic
> activity) come from Panganiban 2018, which is an asthma-risk paper (GERA/EVE), not COPD. The
> mouse GSDMB-overexpression model (Das/Miller/Broide 2017) is explicitly an asthma model. So "2
> of 5 required credible-set coding/splice variants directly experimentally characterized" is
> true, but the characterization is 100% asthma-outcome, 0% COPD-outcome — the researcher's
> framing of this as GSDMB's "deepest gene-specific mechanistic literature at this locus" for
> COPD purposes conflates asthma mechanism with COPD relevance. Given (i) this locus is one of
> the most robustly replicated childhood-onset-asthma loci in human genetics, (ii) asthma is a
> well-established epidemiological risk factor for COPD (asthma-COPD overlap, reduced lung-growth
> trajectory), and (iii) the COPD GWAS is not smoking-adjusted nor asthma-history-adjusted, a
> fully sufficient alternative explanation is that GSDMB's COPD-GWAS signal is entirely
> asthma-history-mediated with no distinct adult/smoke-driven GSDMB biology at all. The
> researcher does flag this gap explicitly ("no paper cleanly dissociates a COPD-specific GSDMB
> mechanism from asthma") — good — but the overall assessment paragraph still leads with
> "deepest mechanistic literature at this locus," which risks being read by synthesis-lead as
> stronger COPD support than it is. Verdict: evidence is real and well-verified, but the causal
> inference for COPD specifically (as opposed to asthma, with COPD association plausibly a
> downstream artifact of asthma-history confounding) overreaches.

### ORMDL3 — WEAKENED
> This gene has the best case for genuinely COPD/smoke-specific (not just asthma-shared)
> biology in the entire set: Chen 2025 and Chen 2021 (JACI) show ORMDL3 induction by cigarette
> smoke in COPD/smoker airway epithelial and smooth-muscle cells, with knockdown blunting
> smoke-induced ER stress/mucus/inflammation — this is pulmonary biology triggered by smoke
> exposure, not smoking-initiation neurobiology, and is a genuinely distinct narrative from the
> childhood-asthma/sphingolipid story. That said, three real limitations temper this: (1) No
> credible-set coding/splice variant is annotated to ORMDL3 itself — nearby variants are
> downstream_gene_variant only — so causal-gene assignment within this three/four-gene
> regulatory block (Verlaan 2009's CTCF/cis-regulatory domain spans GSDMB and ORMDL3 jointly) is
> exactly as circumstantial for ORMDL3 as it is for GSDMB; the researcher's framing risks
> implicitly granting ORMDL3 more causal-gene certainty than the fine-mapping data support. (2)
> The smoke-induction expression studies are correlative/disease-state observations in
> patient-derived or exposed cells — they establish that ORMDL3 expression rises with smoke
> exposure and that this contributes to injury in vitro, not that the GWAS germline risk allele
> itself modulates this smoke-response (no genotype-stratified induction or eQTL-by-smoking
> interaction data cited). Elevated expression in diseased tissue could be a consequence of
> COPD/smoke exposure rather than a cause tied to the risk variant. (3) On prompt point (c):
> Balantic 2013 (P=0.045, 493 Slovenian adults, CC genotype 37% vs 26%, not smoking-adjusted,
> marginal single p-value, conflated with "asthma without rhinitis" in the same comparison) is
> weak, hypothesis-generating evidence at best and should not be treated as human genetic
> confirmation of an ORMDL3-COPD link — if synthesis-lead cites this as corroborating human
> evidence rather than a marginal single small study, that would be overweighting it. Net:
> promising and appropriately smoking-relevant mechanistic story, but causal-gene assignment
> within the LD block and the genotype-to-phenotype chain are not yet nailed down, and the one
> human COPD genetic-association result is too weak to lean on.

### CSF3 — WEAKENED (addresses prompt point b directly)
> This is the strongest standalone biology in the entire gene list — a
> genome-wide-significant neutrophil-count GWAS hit at 17q21.1 (Okada 2010) plus a direct,
> smoking-cohort-specific CSF3 promoter-variant association with protection against
> lung-function decline (He 2008, OR 0.73, multiple-comparison-corrected, in a
> continuously-smoking NHLBI cohort) — genuinely satisfying both the smoking-relevance and
> spirometry-concordance priors better than any other gene here. But it should not be treated as
> a peer candidate to the PIP/MAGMA/PoPS/coloc-nominated genes: it was added by the researcher
> via independent Ensembl-window enumeration, carries zero credible-set variants, and sits
> ~300-450kb from the high-PIP cluster — outside the core fine-mapped signal span the credible
> set actually supports. Neither Okada's rs4794822 nor He's CSF3 promoter variant is shown to be
> in LD with rs1495100 or with the fine-mapped variants; this could easily be a second,
> independent GWAS signal co-resident in the same 1Mb extraction window rather than the same
> locus's causal gene. The researcher flags this caveat themselves, which is good practice, but
> I'd elevate it: until LD/r² between rs1495100 (or the credible-set variants) and the
> CSF3/PSMD3 signal is directly checked, CSF3 should be presented to synthesis-lead as a flagged
> hypothesis-generating side-finding requiring verification, not folded into the ranked
> candidate-gene list for this locus. This is a genuine open action item, not a closed critique —
> recommend the researcher (or synthesis-lead) run that LD check before finalizing.
