## Genes/findings flagged CONTESTED or WEAKENED by red-team-skeptic (verbatim reasoning preserved)

### NFIB — verdict: WEAKENED (not CONTESTED; no independent contradicting paper was found, this is a critique of inference/emphasis and unresolved attribution, not of citation validity)

First red-team-skeptic dispatch (verbatim, search budget exhausted before any WebSearch
query returned — critique based on internal analysis only):

> Gene-specific vs. nearest-gene+borrowed-biology reasoning. There's no competing
> protein-coding gene in this interval, so the classic "LD block, several plausible genes"
> problem doesn't apply structurally. But a related and, I think, more serious version of
> the concern is present: nearly all of the "pulmonary" evidence marshaled for NFIB is
> borrowed from contexts that don't actually establish an adult COPD/emphysema mechanism.
> Hsu 2011, Steele-Perkins 2005 (via Campbell 2008), Lajoie 2014, and Pais 2013 are all
> complete-knockout or conditional-knockout mouse studies of fetal/perinatal lung
> maturation — Nfib-null mice die at birth from failed saccularization. That is a
> fundamentally different biological regime (organogenesis, complete loss of function)
> from a common noncoding GWAS variant with PIP=0.568 exerting a partial dosage effect on
> adult airflow obstruction. The researcher does not show — and I don't think the
> literature currently shows — any mechanistic bridge from "Nfib is required for late
> fetal lung maturation" to "this NFIB haplotype increases adult COPD risk." The one piece
> of evidence that touches adult, smoke-exposed pulmonary biology (Gao et al. 2007,
> NFI-A/B binding the LOX promoter, smoke-condensate-modulated) is in rat fibroblasts and
> concerns promoter occupancy, not a disease-outcome or expression-QTL link back to the
> GWAS signal. It's suggestive, not load-bearing. The researcher itself confirms zero
> PubMed hits for "NFIB COPD," "NFIB emphysema," "NFIB airway epithelium" — i.e., there is
> no direct NFIB-COPD mechanistic study at all. The prioritization of NFIB is therefore
> resting on (a) it being the sole protein-coding gene physically in the region plus (b)
> general evidence that NFIB is an important, dosage-sensitive transcription factor in
> some tissues (developing lung, brain, bone tumors) — which is closer to "only gene here
> + strong unrelated biology" than to gene-specific COPD biology.
>
> Smoking-mediation vs. pulmonary biology. This is the key point and I think the
> researcher's framing under-weights it. Open Targets shows comparable-magnitude COPD
> (0.783, 0.702) and smoking-initiation (0.571, 0.571) L2G scores at NFIB, and zero
> FEV1/pulmonary-function-measurement credible-set evidence. Per prior #2, a COPD
> association with no spirometric concordance is exactly the pattern that should raise
> suspicion of a non-pulmonary (here, smoking-behavior) mechanism. [...] That asymmetry in
> emphasis, even without any single false claim, risks leaving a reader with more
> confidence in a direct pulmonary mechanism than the underlying statistical evidence (no
> FEV1 support, matched-magnitude smoking score) actually warrants. I'd flag this as the
> central overclaiming concern for this locus — not a fabricated citation, but a
> narrative-weight mismatch relative to the statistical genetics.
>
> Competing pleiotropy — Malan syndrome. NFIB haploinsufficiency causing a
> neurodevelopmental/macrocephaly syndrome (Schanze 2018, Zenker 2019) is a genuinely
> important alternative hypothesis, not just background color. [...] this locus could be
> acting on smoking behavior via CNS/neurodevelopmental NFIB biology, with the COPD signal
> downstream of smoking exposure rather than independent pulmonary pathology. This
> alternative should be explicitly given to synthesis-lead, since the researcher assembled
> the pieces but didn't state the hypothesis.
>
> Verdict: WEAKENED. The gene call itself (NFIB is the only plausible protein-coding gene
> physically in the region, and it's genuinely LOF-constrained and dosage-sensitive)
> holds. But the inference that this reflects direct pulmonary/COPD-specific disease
> biology overreaches [...]. Not CONTESTED — no independent contradicting paper was found
> because no independent search could be run (budget exhausted); this is a critique of
> inference/emphasis, not of citation validity.

## Search-coverage limitation (orchestrator note)

Both red-team-skeptic dispatches on this locus encountered an exhausted WebSearch budget
(reported as 200/200) before returning any results, which appears to be a session-wide cap
rather than one caused by this locus's task specifically. The second dispatch obtained
partial independent coverage via WebFetch against Europe PMC's REST API, GWAS Catalog, and
Semantic Scholar, but was blocked by CAPTCHA/paywall barriers from directly inspecting the

lung-function (FEV1/FEV1-FVC) support. This is flagged here as the single most valuable
follow-up check for a human reviewer with direct PDF/supplementary-table access, before
treating the pulmonary-vs-smoking-mediated mechanism question for NFIB as resolved in
either direction.
