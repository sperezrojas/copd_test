## Excluded citations (RETRACTED / NOT_FOUND / CLAIM_MISMATCH)

**PMID 14991826** — Healy et al., "A functional polymorphism regulating dopamine beta-hydroxylase
influences against Parkinson's disease," Annals of Neurology, 2004.

- **Verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH.**
- Researcher's claim (as originally written): "functional promoter variant rs1611115 (-970C>T) regulates
  plasma/serum DBH enzymatic activity and modulates Parkinson's disease risk."
- Citation-verifier's finding (verbatim): "Healy et al. abstract describes 'A functional --1021C -->
  T polymorphism in the dopamine beta-hydroxylase gene' but claim states rs1611115 (-970C>T) — different
  coordinates and no rs number confirmed for the -1021 position in abstract."
- **Disposition:** the underlying paper is real and does support a functional DBH promoter variant
  modulating Parkinson's disease risk via altered enzyme activity — but the specific variant identifier
  and coordinate attributed to it in the researcher's write-up (rs1611115, -970C>T) do not match what the
  paper actually reports (a -1021C>T variant, with no rs1611115 identifier confirmed in the abstract).
  Excluded from the evidence table as written; not usable to support the rs1611115/-970C>T claim without
  further verification of which SNP the paper actually studied.

## Gene marked CONTESTED by red-team-skeptic

### FAM163B — CONTESTED

Red-team-skeptic's reasoning, verbatim:

> The credible-set/L2G evidence (7/9 noncoding variants, L2G 0.70/0.68) is purely positional/statistical,
> and the aggregated COPD score (0.32) is explicitly noted as "almost no literature-mining signal (0.01)"
> — i.e., near-total absence of independent support, which the researcher does honestly flag. But
> there's a more serious problem the findings don't fully reckon with: **this locus's own lead variant,
> rs3025343, is DBH's well-established, independently-discovered smoking-cessation SNP**, replicated
> across five separate cohorts/studies by groups with no connection to this GWAS or to FAM163B. That is
> a specific, mechanistically-grounded alternative causal-gene account for the exact same lead variant
> that FAM163B's L2G score is built on. L2G algorithms substantially weight eQTL/molecular-QTL density,
> and FAM163B has QTLs across six tissues (brain, testis, liver, nerve, heart, iPSC) — that could easily
> make it statistically "win" a nearest-gene/QTL-density contest without being the causal gene. This is
> close to the textbook nearest-gene/LD-block bias the task asks me to watch for: strong statistical
> prioritization for FAM163B sits right next to a variant that already has a well-replicated,
> independently-discovered functional home (DBH) with a plausible biological story. The three
> mechanistic citations found for FAM163B (brain-restricted neuronal protein, skeletal-muscle/UPR
> co-expression, TCGA breast-cancer XGBoost feature) provide literally zero lung/airway/immune
> connection and are each in a different, non-lung tissue.
>
> **CONTESTED** — not because a paper argues "FAM163B is wrong," but because the independently-verified
> DBH/rs3025343 smoking-cessation literature constitutes a credible, mechanistically superior alternative
> explanation for the same lead variant that FAM163B's candidacy is built on. Synthesis-lead should not
> treat FAM163B's L2G lead as settling gene assignment at this locus.

## Additional skeptic caveats (genes WEAKENED, not CONTESTED — included here for transparency, not required by pipeline rules but preserved for completeness)

### DBH — WEAKENED

> This is the more interesting candidate, but the researcher's summary ("best genetically supported gene
> in cluster") overstates it in one respect: by the pipeline's own L2G metric, DBH is the **minority**
> gene (0.06-0.09 vs FAM163B's 0.70/0.68) at the credible sets Open Targets scored. DBH's strength is not
> statistical/positional — it's an independently-assembled mechanistic/replication case... That last
> point cuts both ways: an effect on cessation *within already-diagnosed COPD patients* is at least as
> consistent with DBH acting as a disease-course/behavioral modifier... as with a lung-pathobiology
> mechanism, and this is a reverse-causation/confounding possibility the findings don't explicitly rule
> out. The Lutz 2015 post-bronchodilator FEV1 hit... was found entirely within smoker cohorts..., so
> residual confounding by smoking dose/duration (not just status) is not excluded... **WEAKENED** — the
> smoking-cessation mechanism is well-supported and directly ties to this locus's own lead variant, but
> "best genetically supported gene in cluster" overstates the fine-mapping/L2G picture..., and the FEV1
> signal, while suggestive of something beyond pure cessation-behavior, doesn't yet rule out
> smoking-dose confounding or a reverse-causation (disease-modifier) interpretation.

### ABL1 — WEAKENED, bordering on exclusion

> This candidacy is weak and I think the findings actually understate how weak. Three compounding
> problems: (1) physically 2.7-3.0 Mb outside the locus interval with zero GWAS/L2G evidence (count=0)
> — MAGMA and PoPS are not fine-mapping tools; MAGMA gene-level tests are known to be biased toward
> large, SNP-dense genes... and PoPS is intended to prioritize among genes near an implicated interval...,
> not to reach 2.7-3 Mb outside it. (2) All the positive mechanistic literature is pulmonary-fibrosis
> biology..., not COPD/emphysema... biology, and the drugs used... are multi-target kinase inhibitors
> hitting Src/PDGFR/BCR-ABL simultaneously — none isolate ABL1 specifically. (3) There is a genuine
> directional tension the findings surface but under-weight: ABL1-inhibitor drugs *cause* interstitial
> lung disease/hypersensitivity pneumonitis as an adverse effect... Combined with essential-gene status
> (oe=0.115, most-constrained bin) and a cardiotoxicity liability, the overall picture argues against
> common regulatory variation at ABL1 having a plausible large effect on COPD risk even if it were
> physically at this locus. **WEAKENED**, bordering on exclusion... I'd recommend synthesis-lead treat
> ABL1 as a low-confidence, likely-noise candidate for this locus rather than a co-equal contender with
> FAM163B/DBH.
