# Audit appendix — locus 12:89250001-89450000 (DUSP6; POC1B evaluated and ruled out)

## Targeted re-opening (2026-09-11)

The original review of this locus (DUSP6 only, final call DUSP6/LOW confidence) was
re-opened after the study team discovered a genome-build error in the MAGMA/PoPS
gene-location merge (`loci/copd-all-gene-prioritization.txt`). A corrected re-merge
surfaced POC1B as a candidate gene at this locus that had never been evaluated. This
audit documents the full process of checking POC1B, including the independent
physical-overlap verification, structured Open Targets lookup, researcher literature
search, citation verification, and red-team critique, per the re-opening instructions.

### Step 1 — independent physical-overlap check (performed before any literature dispatch)

Confirmed via Open Targets/Ensembl GraphQL (same GRCh38 build as the locus file):
- POC1B (ENSG00000139323): chr12:89,419,718-89,526,135, minus strand.
- Locus window: 12:89,250,001-89,450,000.
- **Result: POC1B DOES physically overlap the locus window**, but only at its extreme
  downstream (3') edge — the segment 12:89,419,718-89,450,000 (~30kb, the last portion of
  the gene body, since the gene's TSS/promoter sits at the higher coordinate, 89,526,135,
  on the minus strand) falls inside the window. The bulk of POC1B, including its
  promoter/TSS, lies outside the window entirely.
- **POC1B does NOT overlap the fine-mapped credible-set span** (12:89,327,388-89,382,708,
  the min-max of all 30 ABF-fine-mapped variant positions at this locus). The nearest
  credible-set variant is >35kb from POC1B's nearest boundary. No credible-set variant is
  annotated gene=POC1B.
- Because this is not the "doesn't overlap at all" case (which per the re-opening
  instructions would have stopped the review at this step), POC1B was carried forward to
  full literature research, with this proximity gap flagged explicitly for the researcher
  and downstream synthesis.

### Step 2 — Open Targets structured lookup for POC1B (full detail in
`evidence_tables/_status/12_89250001-89450000/opentargets.md` addendum)

Key findings (structured database lookup, NOT literature, not sent through
citation-verifier/red-team-skeptic):
- Genetic constraint: LoF oe=0.705 (0.522-0.967, mildly constrained) — notably less
  constrained than DUSP6 (LoF oe=0.23).
- Full list of all 221 diseases associated with POC1B genome-wide retrieved: COPD,
  smoking initiation, lung function, and asthma do not appear anywhere in this list, not
  even at a score of 0. Top association: cone-rod dystrophy (0.77).
- POC1B does not appear as an L2G-predicted gene in any of the same 47 independent
  GWAS/QTL credible sets where DUSP6 scores 0.78-0.86 at the lead variant rs704074
  (including a direct COPD-GWAS hit, L2G=0.807 for DUSP6).
- A BLUEPRINT neutrophil splicing-QTL cluster exists within the POC1B gene body at
  12:89,459,718-89,466,770, downstream of both the locus window and the credible-set span.

### Step 3 — researcher literature search for POC1B (full detail in
`evidence_tables/_status/12_89250001-89450000/researcher.md`, "GENE: POC1B" section)

The researcher investigated all four requested angles (established ciliogenesis/centriole
biology and any airway-cilia relevance; COPD/lung-function/smoking GWAS; PCD/ciliopathy
respiratory-phenotype literature specifically; immune/inflammation relevance) and found,
in the researcher's own words: "no PubMed-indexed literature connecting POC1B to COPD,
lung function/spirometry, smoking behavior, airway epithelium, mucociliary clearance,
primary ciliary dyskinesia specifically, or any immune/inflammatory cell type or pathway."

Key negative findings, each the result of a dedicated search (not merely unsearched gaps):
- "POC1B airway epithelium": 0 hits.
- "POC1B primary ciliary dyskinesia airway cilia": 0 hits.
- "POC1B lung function COPD spirometry": 0 hits.
- "POC1B COPD chronic obstructive pulmonary disease": 0 hits.
- "POC1B smoking": 0 hits.
- No paper mentioning both "POC1B" and "DUSP6" together, and none discussing "POC1B" and
  "12q23"/this locus specifically, was found — i.e., no one has explicitly evaluated or
  debated POC1B as an alternative candidate causal gene at this locus.

POC1B's established biology (22 citations, see below) is centered on: (1) centriole-lumen/
"inner scaffold" structural protein function required for centriole integrity and PRIMARY
(non-motile, signaling) ciliogenesis, evidenced across Tetrahymena, zebrafish, and human
cultured cells — never in multiciliated airway epithelium; (2) autosomal-recessive
cone-rod dystrophy and rarer syndromic ciliopathy (Joubert syndrome + polycystic kidney
disease) in humans, with no respiratory phenotype ever reported, including in the most
severe multi-organ case; (3) sperm-centriole/flagellum biology; and (4) incidental
GWAS/eQTL hits in unrelated diseases (cardiovascular biomarker, pancreatic cancer),
reflecting eQTL pleiotropy at a gene-dense chromosomal region rather than COPD relevance.

## Contested / weakened / not-supported gene verdicts

### DUSP6 — WEAKENED (red-team-skeptic overall verdict, both original pass and re-opening)

The skeptic's full original critique is preserved verbatim below (also saved at
`evidence_tables/_status/12_89250001-89450000/critique.md`).

---

Note from red-team-skeptic: web search budget for this session was exhausted, so this
critique relies entirely on the dossier as provided (the task allowed this — "if useful,"
not required).

**1. Overclaiming given absence from both GWAS and undifferentiated L2G**

Yes, this is real overclaiming risk that the dossier itself surfaces but doesn't
sufficiently foreground. DUSP6 is absent from the genome-wide-significant loci in Cho et
al. 2014 (PMID 24621683) and absent from the discussed/exemplar gene lists in Shrine et
al. 2023 (PMID 36914875), the largest lung-function GWAS to date, despite that study
naming 559 causal genes across 1,020 signals — a very low bar to clear if DUSP6 had real
spirometric support. The researcher is honest that the full 559-gene supplementary list
wasn't checked, so this is not proof of absence, but the burden should sit with a positive
claim, not a null one — "not found among discussed/exemplar genes" is weak-to-moderate
evidence against, not neutral. Combined with the Open Targets pattern (L2G ~0.78-0.86
essentially undifferentiated across 47 unrelated credible sets spanning Kallmann syndrome,
ADHD, cannabis use, insomnia, atrial fibrillation, eGFR, hernia, and COPD alike, with no
lung-function trait even appearing among those 47), the most parsimonious explanation is
that DUSP6 is a strongly-constrained (LoF oe 0.23), 3'UTR-variant-harboring gene that wins
L2G/nearest-gene algorithms by default at this locus regardless of the phenotype being
fine-mapped — i.e., a "greedy nearest-gene" artifact rather than trait-specific causal
biology. This is exactly the LD/nearest-gene bias prior #1 warns about, and it should be
stated explicitly and prominently, not left implicit.

**2. Smoking-mediated vs. direct pulmonary mechanism — genuinely unresolved, and this matters**

The dossier's own synthesis is right to call this "genuinely ambiguous," but this should be
pushed further toward flagging it as a live threat to the whole locus rather than a
background caveat. Key facts: Open Targets scores smoking initiation (0.387/0.636)
essentially tied with COPD (0.371/0.603) for this gene; DUSP6's single strongest disease
association by a wide margin is Kallmann syndrome/GnRH-neuron biology (0.71, a
CNS/neuroendocrine developmental pathway, independently corroborated by two candidate-gene
screening papers, PMID 40508017 and PMID 38593951); other top hits are ADHD, cannabis use,
substance abuse, and insomnia — a strikingly neurobehavioral cluster. Meanwhile no
lung-function GWAS signal was found for this locus at all. This pattern (strong
behavioral/neuro pleiotropy, tied smoking-initiation score, absent spirometry concordance)
is close to the profile the standing priors describe as the signature of a
smoking-initiation-mediated locus rather than a pulmonary-biology one. The researcher could
not confirm or rule out genome-wide significance for rs704074 in GSCAN (PMID 30643251) —
this is the single most important missing fact and should be labeled as such rather than
glossed as "open." Until that is resolved, this should not be called "SUPPORTED as a
direct pulmonary gene."

**3. Immune evidence — over-broad extrapolation to "COPD-relevant innate immunity"**

None of the four immune papers is lung tissue, airway, or COPD-specific: endothelial
ICAM-1/neutrophil recruitment in sepsis-model acute lung injury (PMID 29493888/29682902)
is a systemic vascular-endothelial phenomenon, not airway/alveolar biology; the arthritis
myeloid paper (PMID 41217493) is joint-specific; the vitamin-D/PBMC paper (PMID 39042613)
is correlative and mixed-cell-population; the T/B-cell proteasome paper (PMID 30416500)
explicitly concludes DUSP6's role is non-essential in that system. Taken together this is a
scattershot of contexts in which DUSP6 modulates inflammation in both pro- and
anti-inflammatory directions depending on cell type — exactly the "mixed and
context-dependent" picture the researcher honestly flags, but it is not evidence for a
neutrophilic-COPD mechanism specifically. The BLUEPRINT neutrophil/monocyte sQTL
colocalization (structured Open Targets data, not literature, and not lung tissue) is the
only thing tying DUSP6 biology to the right cell types, and it stops well short of
demonstrating airway relevance. This section should not be read as supporting prior #3's
neutrophilic/innate-immune COPD hypothesis beyond "plausible, unproven, non-specific."

**4. TUG1/DUSP6 airway-remodeling paper (PMID 31557398) — real but should not be over-weighted**

This is legitimately the most on-topic paper in the dossier (COPD sputum/lung tissue,
cigarette-smoke mouse and cell models, FEV1 correlation), but it has real limitations that
should temper its weight: (a) it is a single research group/single paper, unreplicated;
(b) the mechanism is an lncRNA-miRNA-sponge (TUG1→miR-145-5p→DUSP6) — a class of mechanism
with a well-documented history of irreproducibility and overfitting in the lncRNA
literature; (c) DUSP6 here is a downstream rescue/overexpression target in an epistasis
experiment, not itself the object of genetic perturbation matching the GWAS allele;
(d) directionally, increased DUSP6 expression is pro-remodeling/harmful in this model,
which is difficult to reconcile with the ERK-activity-reducing (gain-of-phosphatase-
function) missense variants studied elsewhere (PMID 22155192) — this inconsistency
deserves more weight as a coherence problem, not just a footnote. Overall: supportive but
should be treated as hypothesis-generating single-paper evidence, not confirmatory
mechanistic proof.

**5. Other overclaiming / missing alternatives**

- The variant-level evidence is almost entirely off-topic for COPD (NEC in premature
  infants, bipolar disorder, lung cancer SNP associations, mandibular prognathism) — none
  of it should be read as pulmonary-function support, and the dossier appropriately labels
  these tangential, but a synthesis table should not let the sheer volume of DUSP6
  citations create a false impression of convergent pulmonary evidence.
- Saferali et al. (PMID 31269066) found no colocalized blood sQTL for this locus among
  COPD loci tested — a direct, COPD-cohort-specific null result that argues against a
  peripheral-blood regulatory mechanism, and deserves more prominence as contradicting
  evidence rather than being listed alongside merely "not among genome-wide-significant
  loci" facts.
- The CLAIM_MISMATCH exclusion (PMID 25017204, direction of regulation reversed from what
  was claimed) is a useful integrity flag: it suggests the initial literature pull for
  DUSP6 required correction on a directional claim, which is a mild caution flag about the
  overall care taken in characterizing this gene's expression direction — worth noting for
  synthesis-lead as a pattern, not just a single fixed error.
- No paper was found addressing DUSP6 in alveolar/emphysema biology, protease-antiprotease
  balance, or airway epithelial development — a genuine biological gap, not just a
  search-thoroughness gap, since Shrine et al. is a comprehensive, well-powered spirometry
  resource that would very likely have caught a true direct pulmonary-biology gene at this
  credible-set's fine-mapping precision (PIP=0.20 is itself modest, consistent with a
  diffuse/uncertain signal).
- No paper was found (within dossier or the skeptic's budget-exhausted search) explicitly
  proposing an alternative gene at this 12q22-23 locus as a rival causal candidate for
  COPD/lung function — this would be a valuable search for the researcher to complete
  before final synthesis, since 28/30 credible-set variants annotating to DUSP6 is not the
  same as functional proof, and Open Targets' "always wins" pattern above raises the
  alternative-gene question without answering it. **[This item was addressed by the
  targeted re-opening below.]**

**Overall verdict: WEAKENED**

The statistical/database and mechanistic evidence for DUSP6 is real (strong, consistent
L2G; neutrophil/monocyte splicing QTL; one directly relevant cigarette-smoke/airway-
remodeling mechanistic paper; endothelial/myeloid pro-inflammatory biology), but the
inference that this represents a COPD-specific direct pulmonary or innate-immune mechanism
overreaches the evidence in three concrete ways: (1) DUSP6 is absent from both the 2014
COPD GWAS and the exemplar/discussed gene set of the 2023 definitive lung-function GWAS,
and no lung-function GWAS signal was found at all for this credible set; (2) its L2G
dominance is undifferentiated across dozens of unrelated traits and its top disease
association (Kallmann syndrome/GnRH-neuron biology) plus near-tied smoking-initiation
score argue at least as strongly for a neurobehavioral/smoking-initiation or off-target
developmental mechanism as for pulmonary biology, and the single most decisive fact needed
to adjudicate this (rs704074/DUSP6 genome-wide significance in GSCAN) remains unresolved;
(3) the immune evidence, while genuinely showing DUSP6 can be pro-inflammatory, comes
entirely from non-lung, non-COPD systems (sepsis-model endothelium, arthritis myeloid
cells, PBMC correlative data) and should not be read as COPD-neutrophil-specific. This
should not be labeled CONTESTED (no paper was found that names a rival causal gene at this
locus or reports a clean null for DUSP6-COPD specifically, aside from the Saferali sQTL
null and the GWAS absences, which are omissions rather than active refutations), but it
clearly falls short of SUPPORTED. Recommend treating DUSP6 as a plausible-but-unresolved
candidate whose mechanism (pulmonary vs. smoking-behavioral vs. off-target neuro/immune) is
not yet distinguishable from the current evidence base, and flag the unresolved
GSCAN/rs704074 smoking-initiation-significance question as the single highest-value
follow-up before any stronger claim is made.

### POC1B — NOT SUPPORTED (red-team-skeptic verdict, targeted re-opening)

The skeptic's fresh critique (targeted re-opening, addressing both POC1B on its own terms
and whether it changes DUSP6's "sole candidate" status) is preserved verbatim below (also
saved at `evidence_tables/_status/12_89250001-89450000/critique.md`).

---

Note from red-team-skeptic (re-opening dispatch): web search budget was again exhausted
before independent spot-checks could be run. This critique is a re-analysis of the
dossier as provided (researcher findings + citation-verifier verdicts + Open Targets
structured data for both genes), not an independently re-verified one.

**POC1B — critique of the negative evidence**

Is the negative conclusion defensible? Largely yes, but with two soft spots worth flagging
rather than a clean "proven absent":

1. Physical/statistical case is solid and not really in dispute — POC1B's gene body sits
   35kb+ from the nearest credible-set variant, its TSS/promoter is at the far end of the
   gene, and it is not L2G-predicted at rs704074 in any of 47 independent credible sets —
   including the direct COPD-diagnosis GWAS where DUSP6 scores 0.807. This is the single
   best piece of evidence in the whole dossier and it's hard to argue with.
2. The regulatory/enhancer-hijacking angle was not explicitly tested, and the researcher
   didn't claim it was. The BLUEPRINT neutrophil sQTL cluster for POC1B exists just
   10-15kb downstream of the window/credible-set boundary; TAD boundaries don't respect
   our 200kb cutoff, and no Hi-C/TAD-boundary check or LD check between this sQTL's lead
   SNP and rs704074 was performed. This is a minor, disclosed gap rather than concealment
   — the prior for a >35kb-distant sQTL cluster with no phenotype linkage to COPD in its
   own right materially outweighing an on-variant, direct-COPD-GWAS L2G hit of 0.807 is
   very low, but it is not formally excluded.
3. "Absence of evidence" was actively tested, not just assumed: dedicated negative
   searches (POC1B+COPD, POC1B+spirometry, POC1B+smoking, POC1B+PCD/airway-cilia) each
   returned zero hits, and the most obvious alternative hypothesis (POC1B as a
   motile-cilia/PCD gene) was explicitly considered and rejected on mechanistic grounds
   (centriole-lumen structural protein for primary/photoreceptor cilia vs. axonemal dynein
   arms for motile cilia — a real, citable distinction). This is exactly the right way to
   establish a negative; no overclaiming detected.
4. One thing slightly underweighted: POC1B's oe=0.705 (mildly constrained) vs DUSP6's
   oe=0.23 (strongly constrained) is mentioned only as a database fact, not folded into
   the argument, but it is a modest additional point against POC1B being disease-relevant
   via LoF-type mechanisms.

**Does POC1B change DUSP6's "sole candidate" status?** No — POC1B fails to clear even a
minimal bar for consideration as a rival candidate at this locus. This is not a toss-up and
not really a "two-gene locus with DUSP6 favored" either, because that framing implies
POC1B has some non-trivial independent support, and by the dossier's own numbers it has
essentially none: no physical/fine-mapping overlap (0/30 credible-set variants vs 28/30
for DUSP6); no L2G signal in any of 47 credible sets vs DUSP6's 0.78-0.86 including a
direct COPD GWAS hit; zero PubMed literature connecting POC1B to COPD, spirometry, or
smoking behavior; a phenotype spectrum mechanistically distinct from airway/motile-cilia
biology with no respiratory phenotype even in its most severe syndromic presentation; and
no paper anywhere proposing POC1B as an alternative gene at this locus. The correct
characterization for synthesis is: the build-correction surfaced a statistical/positional
artifact, not a biological rival. DUSP6 remains the sole candidate with any defensible
mechanistic or statistical claim at this locus; POC1B should be documented (so the
artifact and the process of ruling it out is auditable) but not carried forward as a
competing hypothesis.

**Verdict for POC1B: NOT SUPPORTED.** Every axis of evidence (fine-mapping overlap, L2G,
molecular QTL within the credible-set span, disease-association literature, mechanistic
plausibility) points the same direction — away from POC1B. The one loose thread (the
neutrophil sQTL cluster just outside the window) is a hypothesis the researcher didn't
fully close off, but it is speculative and unsupported by any COPD/lung phenotype linkage
in POC1B's own extensive disease-association data. This is about as clean a negative as
this pipeline is likely to produce.

**Does this change DUSP6's own WEAKENED verdict?** Partially, and in a specific, narrow
way — a modest upgrade in confidence on gene identity, not on mechanism. What it
strengthens: the prior critique's biggest structural worry about DUSP6 was that its L2G
dominance might be a "greedy nearest-gene artifact." Having now explicitly tested the
next-nearest plausible gene (POC1B) and found it does not win L2G for anything, anywhere,
near this variant (0/47 credible sets) is modest evidence against pure positional/
nearest-gene default-behavior. This does not fully answer the artifact worry, since DUSP6
could still be winning by default against gene deserts/regulatory-poor neighbors on the
other side rather than by true causal biology. What it does NOT strengthen: none of the
substantive concerns from the prior critique are touched by this new information — the
absence from the 2014 and 2023 GWAS, the smoking-initiation/COPD score tie, the Kallmann
syndrome/bipolar-disorder pleiotropy, and the unresolved GSCAN question all stand exactly
as before. **Net effect: keep the overall DUSP6 verdict as WEAKENED rather than upgrade to
SUPPORTED/HOLDS — gene identity and causal mechanism are separate questions, and this
exercise only resolved the former.**
