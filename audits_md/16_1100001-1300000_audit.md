## CONTESTED gene: CACNA1H

Skeptic's full reasoning (verbatim from `evidence_tables/_status/16_1100001-1300000/critique.md`):

> The fine-mapping case (PIP 0.767 lead + 0.98 cumulative credible-set mass, two missense variants) is
> the strongest statistical signal at this locus, but the researcher's own search found **zero**
> literature connecting CACNA1H to COPD, lung function, or airway/immune biology — this is a real and
> important negative, not just an absence of positive evidence. Everything else known about CACNA1H is
> neuro-endocrine (T-type calcium channel; familial hyperaldosteronism gain-of-function, epilepsy
> pharmacogenomics), and the one credible-set missense variant with a phenotype association
> (rs61734410, ethosuximide response in absence epilepsy, Glauser 2017) **failed to replicate** in
> Myers 2021 — a direct non-replication of the only variant-specific phenotypic claim in the credible
> set. The OT context sharpens the concern further: no genetic_association-datatype COPD score exists
> for CACNA1H (only clinical/literature text-mining, which likely reflects nearest-gene assumption in
> curated databases rather than independent genetic evidence), and the actual molecular-QTL
> colocalizations at this variant are with eosinophil/leukocyte counts and, notably, **smoking
> cessation** (h4 0.92–0.95) — not lung function or COPD. Given CACNA1H is a calcium channel with
> plausible neurobiological roles in reward/addiction pathways, this coloc pattern is a live
> alternative explanation (smoking-behavior mediation, prior #1) that the researcher flagged but could
> not corroborate with peer-reviewed literature. That gap cuts both ways: it's not confirmed, but it's
> also not refuted, and "no literature found" for smoking-CACNA1H is a weaker disconfirmation than an
> actual negative study.
>
> Overclaiming check: the researcher did not overclaim — they explicitly reported the null PubMed
> search and the non-replication. The risk is downstream, if synthesis-lead over-weights CACNA1H
> purely on PIP without registering that literally none of the human phenotype literature for this
> gene is pulmonary, and the one credible-set-variant phenotype claim didn't replicate.
>
> **Verdict: CONTESTED.** Not because a paper argues "CACNA1H is not the COPD gene here," but because
> the combination of (a) exclusively neuro-endocrine established biology, (b) failed replication of
> the only variant-level phenotype claim, (c) OT coloc pointing to smoking-cessation/blood-cell-count
> traits rather than lung function, and (d) zero genetic_association-based COPD/lung evidence
> anywhere, together constitute credible grounds to contest treating high fine-mapping PIP as
> equivalent to pulmonary-disease relevance at this locus.

## WEAKENED gene: TPSG1

Skeptic's full reasoning (verbatim):

> Important correction to a possible overclaiming pattern, in the researcher's favor: Hansbro 2014's
> Prss31 knockout is genuinely the mouse ortholog of TPSG1 (TPSG1's protein product, transmembrane
> tryptase gamma, is also called PRSS31), so this is legitimate gene-specific evidence, not
> family-level bait-and-switch. However, it is a single mouse loss-of-function study with a
> **double-edged phenotype**: protective for smoke-induced macrophage infiltration/fibrosis but
> harmful (increased) for baseline airway hyperreactivity. A knockout that improves one COPD-relevant
> endpoint while worsening another cannot cleanly support a directional hypothesis ("TPSG1 loss is
> protective" or "risk-increasing") for human COPD — this needs to be stated plainly to synthesis-lead
> rather than folded into a single "most mechanistically relevant" framing.
>
> The surrounding tryptase-family literature (Beckett 2013 on mast cell protease-6, Siddhuraj 2022 on
> CPA3, Wang 2025 review, Liu 2026 on general tryptase+ mast cells) genuinely does **not** test TPSG1
> — these are different genes/proteins (beta-tryptase, MCPT6, CPA3) in a related but distinct pathway.
> The researcher labeled this correctly as "family-level, not gene-specific," which is appropriate,
> but there is a real risk that a downstream reader treats five tryptase-pathway citations as
> convergent evidence for TPSG1 when only one (Hansbro) actually tests the gene. This should be
> flagged as a **volume-vs-specificity overclaiming risk** even though the researcher's own framing
> was careful.
>
> Structurally, TPSG1 has **no credible-set variant** at all (PoPS is an orthogonal,
> expression/similarity-based method, discordant here with fine-mapping, which puts 98% of PIP mass on
> CACNA1H). This PoPS-vs-fine-mapping conflict is a genuine, unresolved tension that should be
> surfaced to synthesis-lead rather than implicitly resolved by treating TPSG1 as a co-equal candidate.
> OT confirms TPSG1's pulmonary-disease and COPD associations are animal_model + literature only, with
> no genetic_association datatype support, and the gene is not LoF-constrained (oe=2.00,
> tolerant-looking), which is at least weakly consistent with being a less essential/less
> disease-driving gene, though not dispositive on its own.
>
> **Verdict: WEAKENED.** The single Hansbro mouse study is real, gene-specific, and worth reporting,
> but the inference that TPSG1 is a strong candidate overreaches: the phenotype is double-edged, there
> is zero human genetics support, the surrounding tryptase-family papers don't test this gene and
> shouldn't be counted as corroboration, and PoPS discordance with the fine-mapping (which
> overwhelmingly favors CACNA1H) is unresolved.
