## Excluded citations

### 1. PMID 30804561 / DOI 10.1038/s41588-018-0342-2 — Sakornsakolpat P et al., Nat Genet 2019

**Verdict: NOT_FOUND (regarding the specific claim attributed to it).**

Researcher's original claim: this major COPD GWAS (UK Biobank + International COPD Genetics
Consortium, 35,735 cases/222,076 controls) identifies 82 COPD risk loci, and the researcher
could not confirm whether the 14q24.1 region (this locus, near ACTN1/RAD51B) is among the 82
named loci.

Citation-verifier's verbatim finding: "According to PubMed, the article exists and is not
retracted. Abstract confirms '82 loci...35 were novel discoveries.' Full text accessed via
PMC6546635 confirms the 82 loci exist but does not list them by chromosomal position in the
body text. The 82 loci are referenced in supplementary tables (indicated as citations but not
provided in full text). The paper specifically mentions '4q24' and other loci but does NOT
explicitly mention chromosome 14:68.8-68.95 Mb (14q24.1, ACTN1/RAD51B region) in the
accessible portions. Claim cannot be verified without access to complete supplementary tables
listing all 82 named loci."

Disposition: excluded from the evidence table because the specific claim this citation was
meant to support (that this locus is one of the 82 GWAS-significant loci from this paper)
could not be confirmed. The paper's existence and general findings (82 loci, 35 novel) are
not in dispute, but that generic fact does not constitute gene-specific evidence at this
locus and would not belong in the evidence table even if verified. A reviewer with access to
the paper's full supplementary tables could resolve this by checking whether 14:68.8-68.95Mb
appears among the 82 reported loci.

### 2. PMID 36187339 / DOI 10.1155/2022/6431164 — Dong C et al., Oxid Med Cell Longev 2022

**Verdict: RETRACTED.**

Researcher's original claim: this paper lists MAGOH3P as one of six genes overexpressed in
endometrial cancer in a TCGA-UCEC bioinformatics/RNA-methylation-regulator analysis. The
researcher itself flagged this article as retracted per PubMed metadata before passing it to
citation-verifier.

Citation-verifier's verbatim finding: "According to PubMed, retraction confirmed. PubMed
metadata shows: 'Retracted Publication. Retraction in Oxid Med Cell Longev. 2023 Sep
27;2023:9825804.' The article claims MAGOH3P overexpression in endometrial cancer, but this
is from a retracted publication and unreliable. Verdict: RETRACTED regardless of underlying
claim quality."

Disposition: excluded from the evidence table. This was, in any case, the only citation
identified anywhere in the literature search that named MAGOH3P specifically, and it is (a)
retracted and (b) about an unrelated cancer type with no lung/COPD relevance even before
considering the retraction. Its removal leaves MAGOH3P with zero surviving literature
evidence of any kind — see CONTESTED reasoning below.

### ACTN1 — WEAKENED

"The credible-set variant (rs117951468, PIP=0.98) does not VEP-annotate to ACTN1 — it
annotates to MAGOH3P. ACTN1's candidacy rests on 'nearest protein-coding gene in the window,'
which is a weaker claim than nearest-gene-to-the-actual-fine-mapped-variant.

The verified evidence splits into two very different tiers. PMID 23434115 and PMID 34502272
are strong, well-replicated human genetics — but for a different, dominant Mendelian
phenotype: ACTN1-related autosomal dominant macrothrombocytopenia, a megakaryocyte/platelet-
cytoskeleton disease. This is a serious competing explanation, not corroborating evidence:
ACTN1 is a highly conserved, functionally important cytoskeletal gene with an established
non-pulmonary human disease mechanism. A COPD GWAS signal (or an Open Targets
constraint/genetic-signal score) near ACTN1 is entirely consistent with the locus tagging
platelet/megakaryocyte biology — which has well-known cross-talk with inflammation and could
easily surface in a COPD GWAS without any lung-intrinsic mechanism — rather than a distinct
pulmonary pathway. The researcher's writeup, as summarized, does not appear to have weighed
this alternative explicitly.

The COPD/lung-specific evidence is thin by comparison: PMID 31937048 is a single small
Chinese-language microarray bioinformatics study (unreplicated, hypothesis-generating at
best), and PMID 25666808 is a generic dendritic-cell cytoskeleton mechanism paper — actinin
is a ubiquitous cytoskeletal protein, so 'actinin matters for cytoskeleton in some immune
cell type' is expected background biology for almost any ACTN-family gene, not COPD-specific
support.

Verdict: WEAKENED. The verified citations are real, but the strongest and best-replicated
ones support an alternative (platelet/megakaryocyte) mechanism rather than a pulmonary one,
and the COPD-specific evidence is a single small unreplicated study plus a nonspecific
mechanism paper. Calling ACTN1 the locus's causal gene overreaches what this evidence shows;
at minimum the platelet-pleiotropy alternative should be presented alongside it, not
implicitly discounted by 'nearest gene' framing."

### RAD51B — WEAKENED

"RAD51B is the only candidate with internal-method convergence (FLAMES/MAGMA/PoPS), but it
sits ~70kb outside the locus window, on the flank opposite ACTN1/MAGOH3P — i.e., its gene
body is not close to the PIP=0.98 variant. Convergence of three prioritization heuristics is
a real signal worth taking seriously, but these are model-based/proximity-and-functional-
genomics-weighted tools, not independent replication, and none of them substitutes for direct
statistical-genetics evidence tying RAD51B to COPD or spirometry.

On the literature side as you've described it, there is no surviving gene-specific COPD or
lung-function evidence for RAD51B: PMID 26635082 (Soler Artigas lung-function GWAS) is
explicit negative/absence evidence — RAD51B is not named in it. PMID 31095684 is about
asthma, not COPD. PMID 30956756 is a lung-cancer genetic-interaction study reflecting
RAD51B's dominant literature footprint (DNA double-strand-break repair / oncology, notably
also a known breast-cancer GWAS locus gene) — a biological domain with no established
connection to airway inflammation, remodeling, or airflow obstruction.

The Open Targets FEV1/FEV1-FVC gwas_credible_sets scores (0.63-0.72) cannot rescue this: they
are unverified structured-DB context, and critically, the null variant/study identifiers mean
it cannot be confirmed this is even the same credible set as rs117951468. Given RAD51B sits
70kb away, this could easily be an entirely separate LD block/association near RAD51B's own
gene body, unrelated to the signal being evaluated at this locus — which would argue RAD51B
has independent lung-function biology but say nothing about whether RAD51B explains this
credible set.

Verdict: WEAKENED. The prioritization-tool convergence is a genuine, non-trivial
computational signal, but in the absence of any verified literature directly linking RAD51B
to COPD/lung function, and given its dominant biology is DNA-repair/oncology rather than
pulmonary/immune, treating RAD51B as the resolved causal gene overreaches. This is closer to
'plausible computational candidate, unconfirmed by biology' than 'supported.'"

### ZFP36L1 — WEAKENED

"This candidate was added by the researcher, is not flagged by FLAMES/MAGMA/PoPS, and has
zero statistical-genetics evidence of any kind — its case is proximity (closer to the locus
boundary than RAD51B) plus a narratively constructed 'plausible T-cell immune mechanism.'

The verified citations (PMID 36269839, 34276705, 25299049) are general T/B-cell or myeloid
immunology with no COPD or lung-function specificity. ZFP36L1 (a tristetraprolin-family
mRNA-decay regulator) has broad roles in T-cell quiescence, B-cell development, and
hematopoiesis generally — this is exactly the profile where 'a plausible immune mechanism'
could be constructed post hoc for almost any immune-adjacent gene near almost any
inflammatory-disease locus. Per the prior on inflammatory complexity, this evidence should be
labeled nonspecific, not COPD-relevant, and the writeup should say so rather than let the
mechanism narrative imply specificity it doesn't have.

Verdict: WEAKENED. Physical proximity plus a generic, non-lung, non-COPD immunology
literature does not constitute evidence for candidacy — it constitutes a hypothesis. It
should not be ranked above RAD51B (which at least has convergent computational
prioritization) or presented with confidence comparable to ACTN1's Mendelian-disease
evidence. I did not find (nor could I search for) literature contradicting a ZFP36L1-COPD
link — there simply isn't literature establishing one, which is a different, weaker problem
than contradiction, but should be stated as plainly as the researcher's own framing allows."

### MAGOH3P — CONTESTED

"This is the gene VEP actually assigns as nearest to the PIP=0.98 credible-set variant, but
it is an unprocessed pseudogene with no known protein product and no established regulatory
function anywhere in the literature. The one citation retrieved for it was RETRACTED by the
citation-verifier.

VEP's 'nearest gene' call for an upstream_gene_variant is a naive distance-based annotation
and carries no functional weight on its own — pseudogenes are routinely the nearest annotated
feature to noncoding variants without being functionally relevant. Once the retracted
citation is removed, MAGOH3P has literally zero surviving evidence, positive or negative. The
retraction itself should raise suspicion rather than be treated as a minor housekeeping note
— it means an attempt to substantiate this candidate failed verification, which is the
citation-verifier doing exactly its job of catching a weak/false lead.

Verdict: CONTESTED. Not because there's positive evidence against MAGOH3P, but because the
sole evidentiary claim for it failed verification (retraction), leaving nothing behind it
besides a distance-based VEP annotation. It should be reported as effectively
unsupported/excluded rather than carried forward as a live candidate alongside the other
three."

### Locus-level issues raised by red-team-skeptic (verbatim)

"LD/proximity ambiguity. This is a genuinely hard locus to resolve: a single noncoding,
MODIFIER-impact upstream variant with no missense/splice biology of its own, sitting in a
~150kb window with four plausible genes on both flanks (RAD51B outside the window entirely).
None of the four candidates has reported eQTL colocalization evidence in what you've
summarized — that is a conspicuous absence for a purely regulatory-annotation credible set,
and its absence should be treated as a real gap, not a neutral non-finding.

Smoking-mediation confound. The absence of smoking-initiation associations for any candidate
should not be read as confirmation of a direct pulmonary mechanism. Open Targets' coverage of
smoking-behavior GWAS datasets (e.g., GSCAN-derived smoking initiation/cessation/heaviness)
is not exhaustive, and neither the researcher nor Open Targets appears to have done a
dedicated lookup of rs117951468 or this window against a dedicated smoking-initiation GWAS
(e.g., Liu et al. GSCAN). Absence of a hit under limited datasource coverage is weak evidence
of true absence. This should be flagged as an artifact-of-search-coverage risk, not treated
as supporting 'this locus acts through pulmonary biology rather than smoking behavior.'

Open Targets FEV1/FEV1FVC scores — appropriate treatment. You are right to treat these as
unverified DB context only, and the null variant/study identifiers are a real identifiability
problem: without confirming these Open Targets credible-set rows correspond to the same LD
block/signal as rs117951468, the FEV1 scores for ACTN1 (0.70) and RAD51B (0.63-0.72) cannot
be cited as evidence that either gene explains this COPD signal specifically. This caveat
should be stated prominently wherever these scores are used in synthesis, not buried as a
footnote.

Thoroughness flag for synthesis lead. Based on what's described, the evidence bases for
RAD51B and ZFP36L1 both look thin/tangential (an explicit negative GWAS result, an asthma
paper, and a cancer paper for RAD51B; three generic immunology papers with no lung context
for ZFP36L1). Before finalizing any ranking among the four candidates, I'd recommend the
synthesis lead confirm whether the researcher specifically searched for (1) lung-tissue
eQTL/colocalization data for RAD51B and ACTN1 at this locus, (2) a targeted smoking-
initiation/GSCAN lookup for rs117951468, and (3) lung-specific or single-cell expression data
for ZFP36L1 (e.g., Human Lung Cell Atlas), since none of these appear in the evidence
summarized to me."
