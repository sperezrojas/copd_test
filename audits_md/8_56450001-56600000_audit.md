# Audit trail (excluded citations / contested findings): locus 8_56450001-56600000 (PENK)

This is the excluded/contested appendix for `evidence_tables/8_56450001-56600000.md`. It exists so the evidence
table can be handed to a reviewer without also handing out the full audit trail. Full stage checkpoints are at
`evidence_tables/_status/8_56450001-56600000/` (opentargets.md, researcher.md, verified_citations.md,
critique.md).

## Excluded citations (RETRACTED or CLAIM_MISMATCH verdicts — not used in the evidence table)

### 1. CLAIM_MISMATCH — Saunders GRB, Wang X, Chen F, et al. "Genetic diversity fuels gene discovery for tobacco
and alcohol use." Nature, 2022. PMID 36477530; DOI 10.1038/s41586-022-05477-4.

- **Claim as originally proposed by researcher:** This multi-ancestry (3.4M individuals) GWAS of smoking/alcohol
  traits highlights a fine-mapped/TWAS gene for the "DrnkWk" (drinks per week) phenotype described as "involved
  in cortical development as well as the processing of several neuroendocrine peptides, including neurotensin
  and substance P, and may also have a role in amyloid-beta processing," generating "peptides such as BAM 12
  (which shows mu-opioid receptor selectivity) and BAM 22 (which shows delta-opioid receptor selectivity)" — the
  researcher believed, based on BAM-12/BAM-22 being canonical proenkephalin(PENK)-derived peptides, that this
  passage describes PENK, but flagged that the gene symbol itself was stripped/redacted in the full-text
  extraction tool used, so the attribution could not be confirmed from the text alone.
- **Citation-verifier finding:** The article itself is real and exists as described (3.4M-individual multi-
  ancestry GWAS, 3,823 associated variants across 2,143 loci). However, the verifier could not independently
  confirm from the accessible abstract/text that PENK is the specific gene named for the DrnkWk phenotype.
  Verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH — article exists, but the specific gene-identity claim attached to it (that this is
  about PENK) is unconfirmed, not verified.
- **Disposition:** Excluded from the evidence table. This remains an open, unresolved lead (not a
  disqualified one) — see "What would resolve the tie" in the Prioritized gene section of the evidence table for
  the specific follow-up recommended (checking GSCAN/Saunders supplementary tables or GWAS Catalog directly for
  the named gene at this locus).

### 2. RETRACTED — Retraction Note for "Long noncoding RNA LINC00968 inhibits proliferation, migration and
invasion of lung adenocarcinoma through targeting miR-22-5p/CDC14A axis" (original DOI
10.1007/s13205-021-02981-8). Retraction Note PMID 37033386; DOI 10.1007/s13205-023-03531-0.

- **Claim as originally proposed by researcher:** This was cited only as a data-integrity flag (i.e., the claim
  IS that the underlying mechanistic paper was retracted), not as positive evidence for LINC00968 biology.
- **Citation-verifier finding:** Confirmed as an official Retraction Notice; explicitly retracts the original
  article (DOI 10.1007/s13205-021-02981-8). Verdict: RETRACTED.
- **Disposition:** Per pipeline rule (only VERIFIED citations go in the main evidence table), this retraction
  notice itself is excluded from the table proper, even though its content (documenting a retraction) is
  accurately characterized. It is preserved here as an explicit caution: a meaningful fraction of the
  LINC00968 mechanistic cancer-biology literature has known data-integrity problems (see also the Expression of
  Concern for a separate LINC00968 paper, which DID receive a VERIFIED verdict as a real EoC document and is
  included in the main evidence table as a data-integrity caveat row, not as positive biological evidence).

## Genes flagged WEAKENED by red-team-skeptic (reasoning preserved verbatim from critique.md)

The skeptic did not use a binary CONTESTED flag for this locus; instead it returned a "WEAKENED" verdict against
all three candidate genes' framing, with specific reasoning per gene. Full text is reproduced below verbatim
from `evidence_tables/_status/8_56450001-56600000/critique.md`.

### Search-budget disclosure (applies to entire locus)

> Before critiquing, a disclosure per the search-budget requirement: this session's web-search budget was
> already exhausted (200/200) by the time I was invoked, so I was unable to run any independent contradiction
> searches for PENK, XKR4, or LINC00968 — not even a shallow 1-2-query pass on any of the three. My critique
> below therefore rests entirely on internal consistency/logic checks of the researcher's own (already-verified)
> evidence, not on new literature. Flag for synthesis-lead: this locus received zero independent adversarial
> search coverage from the review step. If search budget is renewed, the highest-value queries would be (a)
> XKR4 + smoking-initiation/cigarettes-per-day GWAS supplementary tables specifically, since the researcher
> already suspected but couldn't confirm this, and (b) PENK/PENK-AS1 + lung-function/COPD specifically, to make
> sure a negative Open Targets lookup isn't missing a paper-only association.

### PENK — WEAKENED

> The researcher's own evidence table is more equivocal than the synthesis sentence "PENK is the strongest
> candidate gene at this locus" suggests... The two flagship human smoking-behavior GWAS (Liu 2019/GSCAN,
> Furberg 2010/TAG) — the most authoritative sources available — do not name PENK or XKR4 at all. So the Open
> Targets 0.438 genetic_association score for smoking initiation is not literature-confirmed to be about PENK
> specifically; it's a database-level locus-to-gene mapping, which is exactly the kind of nearest-gene/LD
> assignment this review is supposed to be skeptical of... The single citation that would have been the
> strongest direct link (Saunders 2022) failed gene-identity verification and is explicitly excluded. That was
> the closest thing to a real human-genetics tie between a PENK-like gene and a substance-use phenotype, and
> it's gone. What remains supporting a "substance-use neurobiology" narrative is a rodent prodynorphin-KO/
> ethanol paper (Femenia & Manzanares) — mouse CNS, not human, not smoking, not COPD — plus the
> uncertain-provenance Open Targets score. The immune/tissue-repair citations... are correctly and consistently
> labeled non-lung by the researcher; they function only as "absence of lung evidence, presence of other-tissue
> evidence"... not positive mechanistic support... The pig backfat-thickness GWAS naming XKR4 and PENK together
> is essentially irrelevant (different species, different trait, likely reflects synteny/LD in the pig genome)
> and should not be counted as evidence for either gene — flag this as tangential, not corroborating.
>
> Net effect: PENK is the most biologically plausible named gene in a 150kb window with no protein-coding genes,
> by elimination and by its neuropeptide/CNS-reward biology — but "strongest candidate" overstates the actual
> evidentiary weight, since zero of the cited papers show a confirmed, literature-verified PENK-specific tie to
> smoking, COPD, or lung function, and the one paper that seemed to was excluded. The claim that this locus is
> "more consistent with prior #1 (smoking-mediated) than prior #2 (direct pulmonary biology)" is really a claim
> that prior #2 has zero support and prior #1 has weak, mostly unconfirmed support — both should be stated as
> evidentiary vacuums rather than the framing implying prior #1 is well-evidenced. Recommend downgrading
> language from "strongest candidate" to "most plausible candidate by elimination and biological rationale, with
> no confirmed disease-genetics support in either direction."

### XKR4 — WEAKENED

> The zero-hit result for COPD/FEV1/FVC/smoking traits... is a genuine, well-documented negative and is fairly
> reported. The efferocytosis hypothesis is properly and explicitly hedged by the researcher... This labeling is
> fair and not overclaimed — credit where due. The bigger unaddressed problem is distance and the unexamined
> intervening gene set: XKR4 sits ~0.9-1.4Mb from the credible set, with eight protein-coding genes (PLAG1, LYN,
> CHCHD7, TGS1, MOS, RPS20, SDR16C5, TMEM68) in between that were never evaluated at all. PoPS scores are not
> distance-limited the way nearest-gene calls are, so long range alone doesn't disqualify XKR4 — but the
> complete absence of any comparative check against those eight intervening genes... means XKR4's PoPS
> prioritization hasn't been shown to beat plausible alternatives, only asserted. This is a thoroughness gap, not
> just a strength-of-evidence gap — flag for synthesis-lead: the intervening protein-coding genes at this locus
> were not screened before XKR4 was accepted as the PoPS candidate. The LoF-constraint contrast (XKR4 oe~0.23 vs
> PENK oe~0.99) is interesting but low-relevance to a common noncoding regulatory locus — constraint metrics
> speak to sensitivity to loss-of-function, not to whether a common regulatory variant acting through this gene
> would produce disease-relevant dosage effects. Shouldn't be weighted as meaningful discriminating evidence
> either way.

### LINC00968 — WEAKENED (dismissal under-supported on the counter-side too)

> The negative case against LINC00968 is solid: no genetic-association evidence for any COPD-relevant trait, and
> its only disease literature is oncology-biomarker work, partially retracted/flagged for image integrity —
> correctly not treated as supporting COPD relevance. However, the researcher's alternative explanation — that
> LINC00968 is "a transcript body incidentally containing regulatory elements near PENK" — is asserted without
> any cited ABC/Hi-C/enhancer-linking or eQTL evidence connecting these specific credible-set variants to the
> PENK promoter. That's a hypothesis of exactly the same epistemic status as the PENK "strongest candidate"
> claim: plausible, consistent with the well-known FTO/IRX3-type precedent of intronic variants regulating a
> neighboring gene, but not demonstrated here. The fact that ABC didn't flag LINC00968 doesn't tell us whether
> ABC (or any enhancer-linking method) flagged PENK from this specific credible-set region either — that
> comparison isn't in the findings as presented. Flag for synthesis-lead: request explicit confirmation of
> whether ABC/coloc/Hi-C evidence links the LINC00968-resident credible-set variants (rs1866823, rs2246873)
> specifically to PENK regulatory elements, versus PENK being named only via FLAMES nearest-gene logic. Without
> that, the choice between "LINC00968 is functional" and "LINC00968 is a passenger over a PENK enhancer" is
> currently undetermined, not resolved in PENK's favor — this is squarely the LD/nearest-gene-bias concern the
> review protocol asks to check for, and it isn't fully resolved by the evidence presented.

### Overall thoroughness note (verbatim)

> This locus is unusual: a 150kb credible-set window with no protein-coding genes at all, flanked by a proximal
> noncoding-adjacent gene (PENK) and a distal PoPS gene (XKR4) separated by eight unexamined protein-coding
> genes. The researcher was appropriately transparent about negative results (no FEV1/COPD/smoking evidence for
> any of the three genes) and about the failed Saunders attribution — that transparency is good practice and
> should be preserved in synthesis rather than smoothed over. But the overall gene-prioritization case at this
> locus is weaker than the "PENK is strongest candidate" framing implies: it is essentially a plausibility
> ranking among three genes none of which has direct, verified COPD/lung-function/smoking-trait literature
> support. Synthesis should present this locus as low-confidence/mechanistically undetermined rather than as a
> PENK-led smoking-pathway locus, and should note the two specific gaps above (intervening XKR4-neighborhood
> genes unscreened; no explicit ABC/coloc link from the LINC00968 credible set to PENK) as open items rather
> than resolved.
