## CONTESTED / WEAKENED genes — verbatim red-team-skeptic reasoning

### PHACTR4 — WEAKENED
> The researcher's own citations are the contradiction: every single VERIFIED paper on PHACTR4
> places it in a non-pulmonary context — prostate cancer TWAS, type 1 diabetes immune-eQTL,
> neural tube closure, a multi-system congenital syndrome explicitly noted to have *no
> pulmonary phenotype*, hepatocellular carcinoma IL-6/STAT3, lupus nephritis biomarker. That
> last set (T1D, lupus, HCC) is broad pleiotropy across unrelated tissues, which is a yellow
> flag for a gene whose PIP is being carried mainly by "it's nearest and contains the missense
> variant" rather than any lung mechanism. The missense variant itself (rs61785974, PIP=0.028)
> falls in the same RPEL3 domain implicated in the Torti et al. congenital-syndrome variants,
> but those are different variants with a distinct (non-overlapping, non-pulmonary) phenotype —
> this is not evidence the credible-set missense variant is functional, let alone lung-relevant.
> No smoking-behavior GWAS paper corroborates the Open Targets smoking-initiation signal. Given
> a 159-variant diffuse credible set with no dominant variant (max PIP 0.042), "nearest gene" is
> a weak prior here, and the literature doesn't rescue it.
>
> **Verdict: WEAKENED.** Positional/nearest-gene status only; no disease-relevant literature;
> the multi-tissue pleiotropy (prostate cancer, T1D, lupus, HCC, congenital syndrome without
> lung involvement) argues actively against a lung-specific mechanism rather than merely
> failing to support one.

### RCC1 — CONTESTED
> Question 2 asks whether "constrained essential housekeeping gene" is legitimate reasoning or
> circular. It's circular as stated. LOF-constraint (upperBin6=0) tells you RCC1 is essential
> for cell viability generally (it's the sole RanGEF, required for nucleocytoplasmic transport
> and mitotic spindle assembly in every dividing cell) — this is exactly the kind of gene
> MAGMA/PoPS are prone to falsely prioritizing at gene-dense loci, because (a) housekeeping
> genes tend to have broad, high-expression eQTLs that inflate PoPS's expression-similarity
> features, and (b) MAGMA aggregates SNP-level signal by proximity, which is confounded by gene
> density — this locus has eight genes packed together. The researcher's own conclusion
> ("proximity/genic-density effects rather than a true causal mechanism... remains genetically
> unresolved") is the correct read, and I'd go further: constraint is *evidence against* a
> common noncoding COPD-risk variant acting through partial loss of RCC1 function specifically,
> because a gene this essential would be expected to show strong purifying selection against
> any expression-perturbing regulatory variants too, not just coding ones — though this is a
> weaker argument and cuts both ways (constrained genes can still have viable hypomorphic
> regulatory alleles). The prostate-cancer TWAS (Fu et al.) linking both RCC1 and PHACTR4
> expression to an unrelated cancer phenotype at this same locus is a genuine alternative
> explanation: it suggests this locus harbors a broadly-acting eQTL/regulatory hub whose
> downstream phenotype consequence is context/tissue-dependent and not demonstrated to be
> pulmonary.
>
> **Verdict: CONTESTED.** Not because there's a specific paper arguing against RCC1, but
> because (a) zero disease-relevant, lung-relevant, or immune-relevant literature exists for
> this gene despite it being the top statistically-prioritized candidate, (b) the researcher's
> own assessment states the causal role is "genetically unresolved," and (c) there's a
> plausible, evidenced alternative (genic-density/eQTL-hub artifact, with the pleiotropic
> prostate-cancer TWAS hit as supporting precedent for "this locus's regulatory signal reaches
> genes with no COPD connection"). Statistical prioritization without any corroborating biology
> at a gene-dense locus should not be reported as if it were a resolved causal-gene call.

### OPRD1 — CONTESTED
> This is the clearest case of overclaiming risk in the set. The Open Targets COPD
> genetic-association score (0.277) is a database aggregate, not independently verified, and
> the two literature studies that actually test OPRD1 directly — Sharafshah 2021 (nicotine
> dependence, intron-1 variants) and Annis 2024 (persistent post-surgical opioid use) — are
> **both null**. A domain-expert narrative that frames OPRD1 as "the smoking-mediation
> candidate" on the strength of the OT score, while the two specific studies retrieved are
> negative, is an inference that outruns its own evidence: it would be overclaiming to say
> OPRD1 "is associated with" or "mediates" smoking/nicotine-dependence risk at this locus. The
> Gusakova review supports only a generic opioid-receptor-family immunomodulation point, not an
> OPRD1-specific or lung-specific one. Zero airway/lung-epithelium literature exists at all for
> OPRD1.
>
> **Verdict: CONTESTED.** Should be explicitly flagged: the two most relevant, most direct
> candidate-gene studies found are negative results for the exact hypothesis (OPRD1 variants →
> nicotine dependence/persistent opioid use) that the smoking-mediation narrative would need to
> hold. If OPRD1 is retained as a candidate, it should be framed as "biologically plausible
> target class (opioid receptor, addiction pathway) with a supportive OT aggregate score, but
> the two specific genetic studies directly testing this hypothesis for OPRD1 were both null" —
> not as an established smoking-mediated mechanism. This is a case where the priors document's
> guidance ("distinguish signals plausibly mediated through smoking from those supported by
> biology") argues for *caution*, not confidence, because even the smoking-mediation evidence
> is weak/negative.

### SNHG3 — CONTESTED
> Question 4: this is thin support. The chain of inference is: ABC enhancer-promoter score
> (computational annotation, not independently corroborated by any eQTL/coloc literature cited)
> → one acute sepsis-lung-injury mouse/endothelial model (mechanistically about acute
> inflammatory permeability, not chronic airflow obstruction/emphysema/remodeling) → two
> retracted lung-cancer mechanism papers that, had they stood, would have been about cancer,
> not COPD anyway. There is no non-retracted paper connecting SNHG3 to COPD, emphysema, or any
> chronic-lung-disease phenotype. The retraction pattern itself (2 of the only 3 lung-related
> papers found are retracted for data/image manipulation) is worth flagging as a pattern per the
> reviewer instructions — it suggests the SNHG3-lung mechanistic literature broadly (at least
> the cancer-context portion) is compromised, which should lower rather than merely neutralize
> confidence in the surviving sepsis paper's generalizability.
>
> **Verdict: CONTESTED.** An ABC-only regulatory link (no credible-set variant of its own)
> combined with acute-injury-only, non-chronic-COPD mechanistic support, in a literature area
> where the majority of adjacent findings were retracted for research misconduct, is not
> sufficient to sustain SNHG3 as a serious causal candidate. It should be reported as
> "computationally linked via enhancer-promoter prediction only; no verified disease-relevant
> mechanistic literature; treat retraction pattern as a negative signal for the broader
> SNHG3-lung literature quality, not merely as two absent data points."

### SESN2 — CONTESTED for locus causal-gene assignment (biology itself not in question)
> This is the central scope-integrity issue in the whole write-up. Both genes [SESN2 and
> YTHDF2] have genuinely strong, mechanistic, disease-specific COPD literature (SESN2: causal
> mouse LOF-prevents-emphysema data plus human COPD tissue/CT correlation; YTHDF2: two
> independent causal cigarette-smoke-pathway mechanistic papers). But neither has *any*
> statistical connection to this GWAS signal: no credible-set variant, no MAGMA/PoPS/ABC flag,
> and for SESN2, it sits ~160kb outside the fine-mapped credible-set span entirely. They were
> found only because the researcher expanded scope to "genes physically present in the region,"
> which per this pipeline's own design (genes are the unit of evidence *as implicated by the
> credible set/prioritization methods*, not "everything in a 750kb window") is arguably beyond
> the intended scope.
>
> The risk is exactly what the researcher flags in their own hedge: presenting compelling COPD
> biology for a gene under a locus write-up creates a strong pull toward reader inference that
> "this GWAS signal implicates SESN2/YTHDF2 in COPD," which is not demonstrated. GWAS signal and
> gene-level mechanistic plausibility are two independent lines of evidence, and conflating them
> (even unintentionally, by co-location in the same document) is a classic locus-to-gene
> overclaiming failure mode — arguably worse here than the reverse (RCC1: statistically
> implicated, no biology) because a reader skimming for "the best story" will gravitate to
> SESN2/YTHDF2's clean mechanistic narrative and mentally assign it the GWAS signal's
> statistical credibility it did not earn.
>
> **Verdict: CONTESTED for causal-gene assignment at this locus specifically** (not contested as
> biology — the underlying SESN2/YTHDF2-COPD mechanistic literature itself is not in question
> and should not be re-litigated). Recommend explicit labeling: "SESN2 and YTHDF2 are
> biologically-compelling COPD genes located near/adjacent to this locus but are NOT supported
> by this locus's fine-mapping, MAGMA, PoPS, or ABC signal, and should not be reported as
> candidate causal genes for this GWAS association without independent statistical evidence
> (e.g., a conditional/secondary credible set or independent eQTL colocalization specifically
> implicating them)." This should be surfaced prominently to synthesis-lead as a "these are
> interesting but out-of-scope; do not let them anchor the locus verdict" note, given how much
> more compelling they read than the actually-prioritized genes.

### YTHDF2 — CONTESTED for locus causal-gene assignment (biology itself not in question)
(See SESN2 entry above — the red-team-skeptic addressed both genes together under the same
reasoning. Skeptic's per-gene summary verdict: "YTHDF2: CONTESTED for locus causal-gene
assignment (biology itself not in question) — same issue as SESN2; compelling

## Locus-wide caveats (verbatim from critique, preserved for the record)

> 1. The 159-variant diffuse credible set (max PIP 0.042) is itself the strongest piece of
> evidence in the whole write-up, and it argues against confident single-gene assignment at
> this locus altogether. This should be stated up front by synthesis-lead as a ceiling on
> confidence: with no variant carrying even 5% PIP, fine-mapping has not meaningfully narrowed
> the causal gene, and every one of the gene-level inferences above is downstream of a
> statistically weak, unresolved signal. Any synthesis verdict stronger than "candidate gene
> unresolved at this locus" would be overclaiming relative to the fine-mapping evidence itself,
> independent of the individual gene critiques above.
>
> 2. The Fu et al. prostate-cancer TWAS (PMID 42323493) implicating both RCC1 and PHACTR4 is a
> genuine alternative-phenotype explanation that the write-up correctly surfaces but doesn't
> fully reckon with. If this chr1 locus's regulatory/eQTL architecture reaches into
> prostate-cancer-relevant expression changes for two of the same genes nominated here for
> COPD, that is evidence of a broadly pleiotropic regulatory hub, not evidence for or against
> COPD relevance per se — but it does mean "RCC1/PHACTR4 expression is regulated by this locus"
> (which is plausible) is separable from "and that regulation causes COPD" (unestablished).
>
> 3. Smoking-mediation prior (prior #1) is under-resolved for the whole locus. Open Targets
> shows only weak/modest smoking-initiation signals for five genes and none for
> RCC1/SNHG3/TAF12-DT, and the one gene with a plausible smoking/addiction mechanism (OPRD1) has
> null direct genetic support. No gene at this locus has any FEV1/FEV1-FVC/emphysema signal in
> Open Targets, and neither agent could confirm whether Shrine et al. 2019 (the major
> lung-function GWAS, PMID 30804560) even reports a signal here. Per prior #2, a COPD
> case-control association *without* pulmonary-function support "warrants consideration of
> alternative pathways" — that consideration has not actually been fully done for this locus.
>
> 4. Overclaiming risk in this write-up is less about individual sentence-level exaggeration
> (the researcher's prose is unusually well-hedged throughout) and more about document-level
> framing/salience: presenting SESN2/YTHDF2's strong biology and OPRD1's OT score prominently,
> next to appropriately hedged caveats, risks a time-pressured synthesis erasing the hedges
> while retaining the compelling narrative.
>
> 5. Coverage is reasonably thorough and appropriately depth-tiered; no gene was under-searched
> to a degree requiring a re-do before synthesis. The one true unresolved fact-finding gap is
> the Shrine et al. 2019 locus-overlap question, which neither agent was able to resolve (the
> red-team-skeptic's WebSearch budget was exhausted before any independent verification query
> could run this session) — flagged here as a genuine open item for any future follow-up on
> this locus, not resolved by this review.
