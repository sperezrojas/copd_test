### Pass 1 (reasoning-based; search budget exhausted before contradiction search)

**Disclosure of my search limitation (read first):** My WebSearch tool budget for this session was already exhausted (200/200) before either of my two contradiction-search queries ("LINGO1 COPD lung function GWAS" / "TBC1D2B COPD lung function GWAS") could execute — both returned zero results due to quota, not zero hits. I therefore performed **no independent literature search** for either gene; my critique below is based entirely on the detailed evidence summary supplied in the task prompt (which itself derives from the researcher/verifier/OpenTargets files) plus domain reasoning applying the stated priors. I was also unable to directly open the local files (`researcher.md`, `verified_citations.md`, `opentargets.md`) because my toolset here contains only WebSearch/WebFetch (no Read/Bash), and WebFetch cannot resolve local filesystem paths. **This means neither gene received an independent contradiction-search pass from me — flag this gap to synthesis-lead and re-run this critique step with search capacity restored before finalizing.**

#### LINGO1 (MAGMA/PoPS-prioritized)

The prioritization here is a purely statistical/positional call (MAGMA gene-based test + PoPS similarity score), and it is not corroborated by a single piece of direct pulmonary evidence: no COPD-specific literature, no spirometry/FEV1 association, and a credible set that is 100% intergenic, sitting ~93kb from the gene's own boundary with no coding, splice, or even gene-body variant. Per prior #2, the total absence of spirometric concordance is itself a warning sign, not a neutral gap — for a locus this large (84 variants) and this statistically confident, the absence of any FEV1/FEV1-FVC signal is conspicuous.

LINGO1's own best-attested genetic association (essential tremor) fails to consistently replicate in a closely related CNS phenotype (negative association with Parkinson's disease in the largest meta-analysis) — this is a red flag about the general robustness of LINGO1 GWAS signals, independent of COPD. Meanwhile, Open Targets shows a moderately confident (L2G ~0.65-0.67) signal for **smoking initiation** near LINGO1, with **zero** COPD GWAS/L2G support. Given LINGO1's actual known biology (Nogo-receptor co-receptor, oligodendrocyte/myelination and neurite-outgrowth regulation — a CNS gene through and through), the single most parsimonious reading of this locus, applying prior #1, is that it reflects smoking-behavior/neurobiological pathway confounding in an un-adjusted-for-smoking COPD GWAS, not a direct pulmonary mechanism. The 16-child pilot methylation study (PMID 41012410) is too underpowered and mechanistically indirect (methylation, not genetic association; pediatric; n=16) to shift this.

The BK-channel citations are a specific overclaiming risk: one is a general review, and the other (PMID 36738787) is about **LINGO2**, a paralog — not LINGO1. Any functional airway/BK-channel narrative built on these citations would be a paralog-conflation error; the citations as verified do not test LINGO1 itself in any airway or lung-biology assay.

**Verdict: CONTESTED.** The evidentiary picture for a direct pulmonary/COPD role is essentially empty, the gene's own signal-replication track record is shaky even in its home phenotype domain, and the more coherent story (smoking-initiation-adjacent CNS biology, unadjusted-for-smoking case-control GWAS) argues this is more likely a smoking-mediated confound than a lung-biology candidate. This should be reported to synthesis-lead as the top statistical prioritization candidate being the weakest-supported gene at the locus.

#### TBC1D2B (nearest gene)

The single OT COPD signal (L2G 0.426) is undermined on multiple counts: it comes from an MTAG study that **conflates COPD with colon polyp** (a trait-mixing method known to inflate/distort signal attribution for either component trait), it is centered on a lead variant ~37kb outside this locus's actual credible-set window (an LD/nearest-gene-bias concern per instruction #1 — this may not be the same causal signal as rs12899237 at all), and even within that same MTAG-derived locus, OT's own colocalization ranks **SH2D7** higher (0.735) than TBC1D2B (0.426) — i.e., the pipeline's own comparator metric prefers a gene that isn't even a candidate under consideration here.

The lung-tissue functional citation (PMID 31719531) is NSCLC/metastasis biology — a mechanistically distinct context (invasion/motility pathways) from COPD's inflammatory/emphysematous/remodeling processes, and extrapolating it to COPD is a leap the researcher appropriately did not make strongly (per the prompt's framing, this was already flagged as suggestive, not established — appropriate caution). The eQTL-sharing argument with the 15q25 CHRNA5/IDH3A/PSMA4 smoking-and-lung-cancer locus (PMID 29486777) is ~490kb away; if anything real is being captured there, it more plausibly reflects the extremely well-established CHRNA5 nicotinic-receptor/smoking-heaviness biology than a TBC1D2B-specific pulmonary mechanism — this is actually a point in favor of prior #1 (smoking-mediation) rather than direct lung biology, though the physical distance genuinely does argue against simple LD tagging as the researcher noted.

The most important piece of **actual contradicting evidence**, which deserves more weight than the prompt's framing gives it: seven independent Mendelian case-report papers document biallelic TBC1D2B loss-of-function causing NEDSGO (a neurodevelopmental/craniofacial syndrome) with **no reported pulmonary phenotype in any patient**. Complete LoF is a far stronger perturbation than a common noncoding regulatory variant; the absence of any pulmonary finding across seven case series is genuine negative evidence against TBC1D2B having a major, penetrant role in lung structure or function — this should be treated as an argument against, not merely "no mention."

Finally, the immune-cell eQTL breadth (invoked under prior #3) is not informative on its own: broad multi-tissue/multi-celltype eQTL activity is common and non-specific, and the one nominally "immune" citation (PMID 37900130, thyroid ophthalmopathy bioinformatics signature) does not test TBC1D2B individually or in any COPD-relevant immune context. Citing eQTL breadth as supportive of an immune mechanism without any functional immunology data specific to TBC1D2B would be an overclaim.

**Verdict: CONTESTED.** The COPD evidence is built on a trait-conflated MTAG signal at a different locus where the pipeline's own metric prefers a different gene, a distant and speculative eQTL argument, cancer-context (not COPD-context) functional data, and is actively counter-weighted by Mendelian LoF case series showing no pulmonary phenotype.

#### Locus-level observations for synthesis-lead (Pass 1)

- **Both** candidate genes at this locus are CONTESTED under my reading — neither has spirometry concordance, direct COPD-specific mechanistic data, or an uncontradicted case. This is an unusual outcome (the pipeline's own top statistical pick and the nearest gene both failing), and it's worth synthesis-lead explicitly considering the alternative that **neither flanking gene is the true effector** — an 84-variant, fully intergenic credible set sitting 70-93kb from both genes' bodies is also consistent with a distal regulatory element (enhancer/insulator) acting on a gene outside this window entirely, which would require chromatin-conformation (Hi-C/promoter-capture Hi-C) data to resolve. I did not see evidence in the material provided that such data was checked; flagging this as a possible thoroughness gap.
- Given the un-adjusted-for-smoking design of the underlying COPD GWAS (per the pipeline's stated prior #1) and LINGO1's much stronger circumstantial link to smoking-initiation biology than to lung biology, I'd weight the smoking-mediation hypothesis for this whole locus as more likely than either gene acting through direct pulmonary mechanism.
- **New citations identified by me:** none — I was unable to run any searches due to budget exhaustion, so no new citations require routing to citation-verifier from this pass.
- **Recommendation:** re-run the contradiction-search step for both LINGO1 and TBC1D2B once search budget is available, and consider tasking the researcher (or a structural-biology follow-up) with checking chromatin-interaction data (e.g., lung/airway epithelial Hi-C or promoter-capture Hi-C) for this credible-set interval before synthesis-lead finalizes gene assignment at this locus.

#### What I found, by search

**1. LINGO1 + COPD / lung function / FEV1** — Zero genuine hits. Europe PMC returned candidate matches only via keyword coincidence (e.g., a COPD Mendelian-randomization drug-repurposing paper (PMID 38660492) that does not actually list LINGO1 among its hits; a head-and-neck-cancer differential-expression paper (PMID 40067528) where LINGO1 appears in an unrelated tumor gene list, not lung tissue). No paper links LINGO1 to COPD, spirometry, FEV1, or FEV1/FVC. This independently confirms the researcher's "essentially zero" finding rather than just reflecting under-searching.

**2. TBC1D2B + COPD / lung function / pulmonary** — Also zero direct hits for a COPD/spirometry mechanism. But one indirect, potentially relevant item turned up (already known to this pipeline): PMID 29486777 — TBC1D2B is named as one of only five genes whose GWAS signal is shared across all three major lung cancer histological subtypes (LUAD/LUSC/SCLC), alongside CHRNA5, PSMA4, IDH3A, and RP11-650L12.2. CHRNA5 and PSMA4 are the canonical genes of the well-known 15q25.1 nicotinic-receptor/smoking-heaviness locus, whose pan-histology lung cancer signal is widely attributed substantially to nicotine dependence/smoking behavior rather than tumor-intrinsic biology. TBC1D2B being grouped with these genes in an independent disease context (lung cancer, not COPD) is circumstantial but relevant supporting evidence for the smoking-mediation hypothesis at 15q25 broadly. **Caveat, flagged for citation-verifier and for whoever checks this further:** I could not confirm from the abstract alone whether the TBC1D2B signal in that paper physically falls inside this locus's 15:77,800,001–78,050,000 window — the CHRNA5-CHRNA3-CHRNB4-IREB2-PSMA4 cluster sits roughly 78.7–78.95 Mb (hg38), i.e., several hundred kb to ~1 Mb telomeric of this credible set's boundary, in a region known for unusually extended LD (a large inversion polymorphism). It's plausible TBC1D2B's cited GWAS hit is driven by LD tagging into that block rather than a signal at the current locus, or it could be a distinct/more proximal TBC1D2B-specific tag — I cannot distinguish these from the abstract. [Orchestrator note: this paper is already the same PMID 29486777 already surfaced by the researcher and already VERIFIED — no separate/duplicate verification was performed, and the "Zhu et al." attribution in this pass is an error; correct authorship per citation-verifier is O'Brien TD et al.]

**3. Primary literature naming a candidate gene for this exact locus (rs12899237 / 15:77.8–78.05 Mb) in a COPD or lung-function GWAS** — Zero hits. Direct search on "rs12899237" returned no Europe PMC records at all. A combined search for (COPD OR lung function) AND 15q25 AND (LINGO1 OR TBC1D2B) also returned zero. This is a genuine empirical absence, not just a narrow query artifact — I tried several phrasings. No published COPD or spirometry GWAS paper appears to discuss this specific sub-locus by name or nominate either gene as a candidate independently of this pipeline's own MAGMA/PoPS/nearest-gene machinery.

**4. Chromatin interaction / Hi-C data for this region** — Inconclusive, not a real hit. The query returned three European Society of Human Genetics conference-abstract compilations (PMC7705415, PMC11627200, PMC6778823) with no abstract text retrievable — almost certainly noise from broad co-occurrence of "lung" and "15q25" somewhere in large multi-abstract PDFs, not a specific Hi-C study of this locus. I could not confirm or refute the existence of chromatin-conformation data connecting this credible set to a distal gene. This remains a genuine open gap, not evidence either way.

#### Effect on the CONTESTED verdicts

**LINGO1 — CONTESTED stands, and is reinforced.** The independent search adds no new supporting biology and no new contradicting paper beyond what the prior critique already reasoned from (essential tremor as the dominant established signal, smoking-initiation L2G competing at Open Targets, no COPD/spirometry L2G). What it does add is a firmer empirical basis for the "zero literature" claim: this is now a confirmed true absence across two independent search approaches, not merely an artifact of an exhausted search budget. The inference that LINGO1 drives COPD risk through lung biology rests entirely on statistical gene-prioritization (MAGMA/PoPS) with no corroborating genetic-association or functional literature, against a background where the same region has a well-documented smoking-initiation signal. CONTESTED is appropriate.

**TBC1D2B — CONTESTED stands, with an added (unverified) reason to doubt a direct lung mechanism.** No direct COPD/lung-function evidence was found (confirming the researcher). The new finding — TBC1D2B recurring alongside CHRNA5/PSMA4 as a pan-histology lung cancer susceptibility gene — is suggestive that this gene may be swept up in the broader 15q25 smoking/addiction-associated genomic neighborhood's pleiotropy rather than acting through an independent lung-intrinsic mechanism, which is consistent with (and arguably strengthens) the smoking-mediation prior. However, this is a newly-surfaced framing of an already-verified citation whose positional relevance to the actual 77.8–78.05 Mb window is unconfirmed, so it should be treated as a lead worth checking, not a confirmed strike against the gene. Combined with the pre-existing points (Mendelian LOF syndrome with no pulmonary phenotype, NSCLC-metastasis-only lung relevance, Open Targets' own L2G preferring SH2D7 over TBC1D2B at the one COPD-adjacent variant, and now this smoking-locus-adjacent lung cancer signal), the case against a direct TBC1D2B-to-COPD mechanism is, if anything, somewhat stronger than in the prior pass.
