## Full red-team-skeptic critique (verbatim)

### 1. Is "sole protein-coding gene" strong enough positional evidence?

It's real evidence, but it answers a narrower question than the researcher/orchestrator's framing
implies. Sole-protein-coding-gene status rules out the classic "LD block with several plausible
protein-coding neighbors" problem — that's a genuinely strong structural feature of this locus and
better than the typical nearest-gene call. But it does **not** establish that the credible-set
variants are *cis*-regulatory for CD44 specifically, as opposed to (a) acting on the neighboring
lncRNAs CD44-AS1/CD44-DT (which are themselves candidate regulators of CD44 or independent
trans-acting transcripts — not interrogated), or (b) participating in a long-range enhancer
contacting a gene outside the credible-set window entirely (TAD-hijacking is a known failure mode
of "nearest gene" logic even when only one protein-coding gene sits at the locus). The ABC-model
pick for CD44 is reassuring on this front but ABC predictions are themselves biased toward
proximal genes and shouldn't be treated as independent confirmation.

More importantly: Open Targets shows no L2G, no colocalization, no curated genetic_association
score at all for this variant, and two major, well-powered consortium papers covering essentially
the same phenotypes (Shrine 2019, 279 lung-function signals; Sakornsakolpat 2019, 82 COPD loci)
do not include this locus. That is a nontrivial negative data point, not merely "absence of
evidence" — these are large, well-powered scans of the same trait space. This locus's own
discovery statistics (p-value, effect size, replication) are not available to this review; without
it we don't know if this is a genuinely novel, well-powered locus or a borderline signal that
happened to clear threshold in a newer/larger meta-analysis. As written, the case for CD44
causality rests on elimination + mechanistic plausibility, not on any gene-specific
statistical-genetics finding — this should be stated explicitly and not blurred into "CD44 is
supported by convergent evidence" language, since the convergence is entirely on the biology side,
not the statistical-genetics side.

### 2. Does the directional inconsistency undermine the mechanistic story?

Cell-type-specific, context-dependent CD44 biology (down on efferocytic macrophages, down on
ASMC, up on inflamed submucosal neutrophils) is biologically plausible and not inherently
contradictory — COPD is well known to involve divergent, even opposing, receptor regulation
across cell compartments. This should not be treated as a fatal contradiction on its own.

However, none of these directional findings have been connected to the direction of effect of the
risk allele at rs353642 (i.e., does the COPD risk allele increase or decrease CD44 expression, and
in which cell type?). No eQTL data is presented at all. Without that anchor, the "convergent
mechanistic story" is actually a set of independently-observed CD44 phenotypes in COPD tissue that
have not been tied to the GWAS signal — it's pattern-matching across disconnected studies
(different cohorts, different cell types, different comparisons), not a single mechanistic thread
traceable back to the credible-set variants. This is a real gap, not just a stylistic one: it
means we cannot currently say whether the GWAS locus, if causal, would predict more or less CD44
activity, and therefore cannot adjudicate which (if any) of the discordant phenotypic observations
is the relevant one.

### 3. CONTESTED or prioritize, and at what confidence?

Not CONTESTED in the strong sense (no paper argues for a specific alternative causal gene at this
locus, and structurally there is no other protein-coding gene competing for the signal — the
"contesting" evidence here is exclusion from two major consortium GWAS papers, which is a
negative/absence finding, not a rival positive claim). But the inference as currently framed
overreaches if presented as "convergent evidence supports CD44 as a COPD gene at this locus"
without heavy qualification, because:
- Zero gene-specific statistical-genetics support (no TWAS/coloc/MR/eQTL finding naming CD44
  causal).
- Explicit exclusion from Shrine 2019 and Sakornsakolpat 2019.
- Open Targets literature-only score with no curated genetic association.
- The mechanistic/immune evidence, while real, establishes CD44 as a plausible COPD-relevant gene
  in general (true of a great many inflammation/tissue-remodeling genes), not that this specific
  locus acts through those mechanisms.

**Verdict: WEAKENED.** Prioritize CD44 by elimination (it is the only defensible protein-coding
candidate, and the credible set is clean/tight around it), but confidence should be flagged
low-to-moderate, clearly separating "positional/mechanistic plausibility" from "gene-level causal
statistical genetics," which does not currently exist for this locus.

### 4. Other overclaiming / missing alternatives

- The Liao et al. 2014 dmGWAS network finding is soft evidence — network-propagation methods pull
  in many genes via protein-protein-interaction proximity to a phenotype-associated network, not
  because of a genetic signal specific to CD44 itself. Should be weighted accordingly and not
  cited as if it were a CD44-specific genetic association.
- Piao 2023, Lennon & Singleton 2011, Bell 2018, Klagas 2009, Noguera 2012, Di Stefano 2009 are
  all legitimate CD44-biology-in-lung-disease papers, and the researcher appropriately did not
  overstate any of them as causal-variant evidence (e.g., Piao's "CD44-dependent" claim is
  correctly scoped to a mouse/cell mechanism, not extended to claim GWAS causality). Lu 2018 and
  Huang 2024 were already appropriately downweighted by the researcher for methodological/journal-
  quality reasons — good practice, no overclaiming there. The Wu 2024 medRxiv preprint is
  correctly flagged as unpublished/non-peer-reviewed and should remain excluded from any
  confidence-bearing claim until published.
- Missing: no PheWAS/GWAS-Catalog pleiotropy check for rs353642 itself (CD44 variants have
  documented associations with blood cell traits, osteoarthritis, and other traits in GWAS
  Catalog) — this would help distinguish a lung-specific signal from a pleiotropic
  hematologic/immune-cell-count signal that could confound the COPD association (e.g., via
  eosinophil/neutrophil count pathways rather than direct pulmonary tissue biology). The skeptic
  was unable to run this search (web search budget exhausted before reaching it this session) —
  flagged as a follow-up for future work, not resolved here.
- The smoking-mediation question is correctly left open by the researcher rather than assumed
  either way — appropriate given prior #1, since no smoking-trait GWAS overlap data was found for
  this locus specifically.
- Alternative regulatory-target hypothesis (enhancer acting on a distal gene despite CD44 being
  the only local protein-coding gene) is not explicitly discussed by the researcher and should be
  named as a residual possibility, even though the skeptic regards it as low-likelihood given the
  ABC/FLAMES/PoPS/MAGMA unanimity.

### Bottom line

The gene-elimination logic (sole protein-coding gene, tight 12-variant intronic credible set,
unanimous computational-pick concordance) is sound and CD44 should remain the prioritized
candidate at this locus. But the inference should not be presented as supported by "convergent
evidence" in a way that implies gene-level statistical-genetics support — there is none (confirmed
absence from two major consortium GWAS/lung-function papers, no Open Targets curated genetic
association, no eQTL/coloc/TWAS/MR finding). The structural and immune mechanistic literature
establishes CD44 as a biologically plausible COPD-pathway gene in general, with a real but
currently unreconciled directional inconsistency across cell types, and without any data tying a
direction of effect to the risk allele. This should be presented as "prioritized by elimination +
mechanistic plausibility, not by gene-specific statistical genetics," with follow-up work
(rs353642 PheWAS/GWAS-Catalog lookup, and sourcing this locus's own discovery-GWAS effect
size/novelty context) flagged before treating CD44 as a high-confidence causal gene.
