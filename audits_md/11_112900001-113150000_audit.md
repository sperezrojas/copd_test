# Audit trail — locus 11:112,900,001-113,150,000 (NCAM1)

This is the excluded-evidence and contested/weakened-verdict appendix for `evidence_tables/11_112900001-113150000.md`. It is kept separate so the evidence table can be shared without also sharing the full critique trail.

## Excluded citations (RETRACTED / NOT_FOUND / CLAIM_MISMATCH)

**Mo C, Ye Z, Hatch K, Zhang Y, Wu Q, Liu S, Kochunov P, Hong LE, MA T, Chen S. "Genetic Fine-mapping with Dense Linkage Disequilibrium Blocks: genetics of nicotine dependence." bioRxiv preprint. 2020. DOI: 10.1101/2020.12.10.420216.**
Verdict: **CLAIM_MISMATCH** — excluded from the evidence table.
Reason: The preprint exists (deposited 2020-12-11; authors confirmed) and its general topic (dense-LD-block genetic fine-mapping of nicotine dependence in UK Biobank) matches what was claimed, but its accessible abstract does not name NCAM1, TTC12, ANKK1, DRD2, or chromosome 11q23 at all, and therefore does not support the specific claim (raised by red-team-skeptic as a candidate citation) that this work favors NCAM1 over the other 11q23 cluster genes as the likely causal gene. Full text was not accessible to citation-verifier for deeper checking. Publication status: still an unpublished bioRxiv preprint as of September 2026 (no peer-reviewed version found). This citation should not be used to support any NCAM1-vs-TTC12/ANKK1 claim unless the specific gene-level claim can be independently re-confirmed from full text.

No citations in this locus's review were found to be RETRACTED or NOT_FOUND. All other 13 citations reviewed (11 from the researcher's initial batch, 2 of the 3 new citations identified by red-team-skeptic) returned VERIFIED — see `evidence_tables/_status/11_112900001-113150000/verified_citations.md` for full detail.

## Genes marked CONTESTED / WEAKENED by red-team-skeptic

NCAM1 was **not** marked CONTESTED — no source reviewed argues against NCAM1 as the physically/statistically implicated gene at this locus, or nominates a specific alternative gene as more likely causal for the COPD association. However, the skeptic explicitly returned a verdict of **WEAKENED** on the researcher's synthesis/interpretation (not on the gene assignment itself), reasoning preserved verbatim below:

> **Verdict: WEAKENED.** The gene-assignment (NCAM1 over DRD2/ANKK1/TTC12 as the physically/statistically implicated gene) holds up reasonably well across four convergent methods and Open Targets L2G. But the researcher's synthesis overreaches in two specific ways: (a) treating FEV1/FVC concordance as evidence for a direct-lung-biology hypothesis competing with smoking-mediation, when under the paper's own biological prior #1 that concordance is exactly what smoking-mediation predicts; and (b) declaring the structural/developmental-biology domain exhaustively negative when at least one directly relevant, mechanistic (non-marker) paper on NCAM1 in pulmonary vascular smooth muscle biology was findable. The CD56+ immune evidence should be explicitly downgraded from "competing/complementary hypothesis" to "unlinked biological plausibility, no genetic evidence" in the final write-up. Recommend the structural/developmental domain be re-searched by the researcher before synthesis-lead finalizes this locus.

Full critique detail (reproduced verbatim from the red-team-skeptic subagent) is preserved below for traceability:

> **1. Is "smoking-mediated neurobiology via NCAM1" actually established as a single shared mechanism?**
>
> Not conclusively. The flat PIP distribution (lead PIP 0.05 across ~63 intronic variants) is being used by the researcher to support "a regulatory/expression-modulating mechanism," but a diffuse credible set is genuinely ambiguous evidence — it is equally consistent with (a) one true regulatory variant poorly resolved by LD/imputation, (b) several independent weaker regulatory signals within the same gene body, or (c) two distinct causal variants (one smoking-behavior, one lung-function) sitting in partial LD within the same large gene, which a flat PIP distribution would not distinguish from a single pleiotropic variant. The Open Targets GWAS-GWAS colocalization (h4 0.85-0.92) is the only piece of evidence that would actually speak to "same causal variant," and it is explicitly flagged as structured-database background, not literature-verified, and critically there is *no molecular QTL (eQTL/sQTL) colocalization* to tie the signal to NCAM1 expression in either brain or lung tissue. The researcher's hedge ("substantially, though perhaps not exclusively, mediated") is appropriately cautious in tone but the underlying evidentiary basis for "one shared mechanism via NCAM1" is thinner than the confident gene-naming implies — it should be described as the best-supported hypothesis given available data, not as an established mechanism.
>
> **2. NCAM1 vs. TTC12/ANKK1 as the driver of smoking pleiotropy — LD/gene-boundary artifact risk.**
>
> This is a legitimate concern the researcher surfaced but did not fully resolve. Gelernter 2006 and Yang 2008 (candidate-gene era, family-based/case-control, pre-GWAS) both localize the strongest addiction-phenotype signal to TTC12/ANKK1, with NCAM1/DRD2 as weaker flanking associations. NCAM1 is a very large gene (>400kb), so intronic credible-set variants landing inside its boundaries could simply reflect that NCAM1 occupies more physical/LD real estate in this locus than TTC12/ANKK1/DRD2, rather than NCAM1 being the true regulatory target — a classic large-gene absorption artifact. However, in searching for corroborating or contradicting material I found independent, more recent evidence that should be weighed against Gelernter/Yang: (i) a 2022 paper reporting that fine-mapping of the shared genetic basis of cannabis/cigarette smoking and schizophrenia "implicates NCAM1 and neuronal abnormality" specifically (not TTC12/ANKK1), and (ii) a biorxiv preprint (2020.12.10.420216) that reportedly favors NCAM1 over the other cluster genes using denser LD-block fine-mapping. Both are candidates for tempering (not resolving) the TTC12/ANKK1 concern, but neither has been through citation-verifier [at time of this critique], and the biorxiv item is a preprint of unknown publication status and should be weighted accordingly. Net effect: the modern statistical-genetics literature trends toward NCAM1, the older candidate-gene literature trends toward TTC12/ANKK1 — this is a genuine, unresolved tension that should be reported as such rather than implicitly settled in NCAM1's favor.
>
> *[Orchestrator's note: citation-verifier subsequently VERIFIED the Song et al. 2022 paper (PMID 35235886) but returned CLAIM_MISMATCH on the bioRxiv preprint, which does not, per its accessible abstract, actually name NCAM1 or the other cluster genes. So the "tempering" evidence for NCAM1 over TTC12/ANKK1 is weaker than the skeptic's critique implied at the time it was written — only the Song et al. verified citation should be relied upon, not the preprint.]*
>
> **3. CD56+ cytotoxic lymphocyte evidence — is it appropriately downweighted?**
>
> The researcher's "marker vs. mechanism" caveat is correct as far as it goes, but the final synthesis sentence still lists this as a "narrow but real...immune thread in COPD lung tissue" positioned as a competing/complementary hypothesis alongside smoking-mediation — which risks giving it more standing than it has earned. Freeman 2014 and Marquardt 2017 use CD56 purely as a flow-cytometry gating marker to define NK/NK-T populations; neither paper varies, measures, or discusses NCAM1 genetic variation, expression dosage, or eQTL effects at all. There is zero genetic linkage between the credible-set variant and this immune phenotype — and the Open Targets query explicitly found no lung eQTL/molecular-QTL colocalization to bridge that gap. This "thread" is therefore pure biological plausibility (CD56 happens to be the protein product of NCAM1, and CD56+ cells are relevant to COPD severity), not evidence bearing on this locus at all. It should be labeled speculative/hypothesis-generating in the synthesis, not placed on comparable footing with the multiply-replicated smoking-behavior GWAS signal.
>
> **4. Should NCAM1 be marked CONTESTED?**
>
> I would not go as far as CONTESTED — no source directly argues against NCAM1 as the locus gene or nominates a specific alternative gene as causal for the COPD signal; all four prioritization methods and Open Targets L2G converge on NCAM1 over DRD2/ANKK1 even within the COPD-labeled and FEV1/FVC-labeled credible sets specifically. But the case should be **WEAKENED**, for two independent, additive reasons: [see summary verdict above].
>
> **5. Other confounds/overclaims.**
>
> - None of the four general COPD/lung-function GWAS landscape papers name NCAM1/11q23 among their reported loci (confirmed by citation-verifier). This is a mild negative data point against "this is an established, well-replicated direct lung-function locus" — plausibly just a power/discovery issue in earlier, smaller GWAS, but worth stating explicitly rather than only implicitly, since the locus's strongest and most repeated literature footprint is in addiction/psychiatric genetics (nicotine dependence, PTSD, cannabis/schizophrenia), not pulmonary genetics.
> - Reverse-causation/collider framing: given COPD case ascertainment is almost entirely in smokers, a locus that shifts smoking initiation/heaviness will mechanically generate a COPD GWAS signal without any direct lung mechanism — this is the single most parsimonious explanation and is likely under-emphasized relative to how confidently "NCAM1" is named as a biological actor versus "this locus, acting through smoking behavior however it does so within the NCAM1/TTC12/ANKK1/DRD2 cluster."
