# Audit appendix — locus 17_45850001-46100000 (MAPT/17q21.31)

This is the audit trail for evidence_tables/17_45850001-46100000.md: excluded citations
(RETRACTED / NOT_FOUND / CLAIM_MISMATCH — none found at this locus, see below) and every gene
the red-team-skeptic marked CONTESTED, with reasoning preserved verbatim from the checkpoint
files. This locus required an unusually large pipeline effort (956-variant credible set, 8
candidate genes, 2 red-team-skeptic passes, 2 citation-verifier passes) given its position in
the 17q21.31 MAPT/KANSL1/CRHR1/STH/SPPL2C inversion (H1/H2) haplotype block — the largest
credible set in the dataset.

## CONTESTED gene determinations (verbatim reasoning from critique.md)

### MAPT — CONTESTED
"Real lung-tissue eQTL evidence (Morrow 2018) and an independent large lung-function GWAS
gene-call (Shrine 2019 Table 1) both point to MAPT, but MAPT's dominant, better-established
biology is neurobehavioral (smoking initiation, risk-taking, neuroimaging per Open Targets),
and its physical/LD centrality in the credible set is expected regardless of causality in this
near-complete-LD block. Two other major papers touching this locus give two different other
gene answers (KANSL1, CRHR1)."

From Pass 1 (search-budget-exhausted internal-consistency critique): "Real evidence (Morrow
2018 lung-tissue eQTL/network-integration top hit) exists, but it's a single small study
(n=117) without stated independent replication, and the researcher is transparent that
disambiguation from MAPT's dominant neurobehavioral biology is unresolved. Two of the citations
used as supporting context (Shrine 2019, Sakornsakolpat 2019) are explicitly flagged by the
citation-verifier as not confirmed to name this locus at all — presenting them alongside
Morrow 2018 as part of MAPT's evidentiary weight, even loosely, risks overclaiming... Alternative
explanation: MAPT's OT genetic-association component is higher for smoking initiation (0.366)
and risk-taking (0.431) than for COPD (0.226), and it is 'frequently top L2G for
neuroimaging/sleep traits' — consistent with a smoking/behavior-mediated pathway rather than
direct pulmonary biology... LD confound: MAPT hosting the lead variant and majority of the
956-variant credible set is exactly what near-complete LD across an inversion block predicts
regardless of which gene is causal — this is not independent evidence of MAPT's causal role...
WEAKENED — real mechanistic hint, but the inference of MAPT as the pulmonary-relevant gene
overreaches given unconfirmed GWAS-naming citations, dominant competing neurobehavioral signal,
and LD centrality being non-diagnostic."

### KANSL1 — CONTESTED
"Best single trait-specific/mechanistic finding (Wain 2015 FEV1-extremes + COPD + CNV-dosage,
in a smoking-stratified design) and Koolen-de Vries clinical literature shows a real (if
phenotypically distinct) developmental airway effect of KANSL1 haploinsufficiency — but this is
contradicted by contemporary Open Targets aggregate scoring (lowest COPD score, highest
smoking-initiation score of the window) and by Shrine 2019's own gene call favoring MAPT
instead."

From Pass 1: "Wain et al. 2015 (UK BiLEVE, n=50,008, smoking-stratified design) is the most
COPD/FEV1-trait-specific finding at this locus... However, this is a 2015 paper that predates
modern fine-mapping; 'novel locus' naming conventions from that era typically reflect
nearest-gene assignment within an LD block, not proof that KANSL1 specifically (versus MAPT or
SPPL2C, all in the same near-complete-LD inversion) is functionally causal — the paper cannot
distinguish among locus genes. There is also an unresolved, quantitatively stark internal
contradiction: Open Targets gives KANSL1 the lowest COPD association score of any gene in the
window (0.027)... while giving it the highest smoking-initiation score (0.345) — the opposite
pattern from what the 'smoking-independent pulmonary mechanism' narrative would predict...
Calling this 'the single strongest primary-literature finding at this locus for a direct,
smoking-independent pulmonary mechanism' is an overclaim given that a single 2015 study, however
well-designed, is contradicted by more recent aggregate genetic-association scoring for the very
same trait... WEAKENED — the Wain et al. finding is real and the best FEV1-trait-specific
evidence at the locus, but the researcher's superlative framing overreaches; the LD-block-
attribution problem is not solved by a pre-fine-mapping paper, and the OT score tension is a
legitimate unresolved contradiction that pulls in the opposite direction, not just a footnote."

From Pass 2 (follow-up, Koolen-de Vries syndrome check): "KdVS shows KANSL1 haploinsufficiency
does perturb airway structure/function (tracheomalacia, recurrent respiratory infections),
lending some plausibility to a developmental role consistent with Wain 2015's CNV-dosage
finding — but the phenotype (structural malacia in a severe, complete-loss-of-one-copy
syndrome) is not COPD-like airflow obstruction, so it doesn't transfer cleanly to a
common-variant, adult-onset airflow-obstruction mechanism."

### SPPL2C — CONTESTED, bordering artifact
"Highest Open Targets COPD genetic-association component of any gene at this locus despite
minimal (3 low-PIP) variant support and no gene-specific (as opposed to paralog-family)
mechanistic literature; the cited mechanistic review explicitly says SPPL2C is the
least-characterized paralog and attributes the relevant immune mechanism to SPPL2A/B instead."

From Pass 1: "Most internally inconsistent gene at the locus: highest COPD-specific Open
Targets genetic-association component (0.329) despite only 3 low-PIP variants and zero direct
COPD/lung/smoking literature. The one relevant mechanistic citation (Mentrup & Schröder 2021)
explicitly attributes the plausible immune mechanism (CD74/MHC-II cleavage) to the PARALOGS
SPPL2A/B and calls SPPL2C 'the least functionally characterized' of the three — the source cited
to support SPPL2C's immune plausibility actually undercuts it. This pattern (high aggregated
statistical score, minimal direct variant/PIP support, no gene-specific mechanism, discordant
functional literature) is classic for an eQTL-colocalization or LD-driven statistical artifact
rather than true causal-gene evidence — highly plausible in a 956-variant, near-complete-LD
credible set... WEAKENED, bordering CONTESTED — I'd treat the OT COPD score for SPPL2C as more
likely an LD/statistical artifact than genuine gene-specific signal until a mechanistic study
exists."

### CRHR1 — newly elevated third candidate (from Pass 2)
"Newly elevated by Pass 2 to a genuine third candidate via Sakornsakolpat 2019's own
nearest-gene annotation, but has no assigned credible-set variant in this locus's own 250kb
window, the weakest Open Targets COPD score of any gene reviewed, and no direct COPD/airway
mechanistic literature (its three surfaced citations are CNS/behavioral, not airway-tissue
biology). Best read as a plausible-but-thin third candidate, not a well-supported one."

Verbatim Pass 2 finding: "the paper's pulmonary-fibrosis cross-phenotype-overlap discussion
states verbatim: 'three segments shared with pulmonary fibrosis identified in the previous
study (FAM13A, DSP, and the 17q21 locus – here nearest CRHR1).' This is a significant,
previously-unrecorded finding: the dedicated 82-locus COPD case-control GWAS's own nearest-gene
annotation for this region, in its pulmonary-fibrosis-overlap analysis, is CRHR1 — a THIRD
candidate gene distinct from both MAPT and KANSL1. This directly weakens confidence in any
single-gene call at this locus: three major papers touching this region give three different
'implicated gene' answers (Wain 2015 -> KANSL1, Shrine 2019 -> MAPT, Sakornsakolpat 2019 ->
CRHR1), consistent with LD-block/nearest-gene-annotation instability rather than convergent
evidence."

## Genes NOT contested (consistent weak/no support across both critique passes)

- **MAPT-AS1** — "No credible evidence ties MAPT-AS1 specifically to COPD, lung function,
  smoking behavior, or lung immune biology. The one well-controlled functional study available
  argues against MAPT-AS1 meaningfully regulating MAPT expression at all (at least in neurons).
  Low-priority candidate." (researcher.md, confirmed HOLDS by both critique passes)
- **STH** — "STH has essentially no independent evidentiary support as a COPD candidate gene
  distinct from its embedding within the MAPT locus; the credible-set variants assigned to it
  are best interpreted as reflecting the H1/H2 haplotype structure rather than STH-specific
  biology. Low-priority candidate." (researcher.md, confirmed HOLDS by both critique passes)
- **PRR15L** — "PRR15L is a MAGMA statistical hit with essentially no supporting functional or
  disease-association literature in any domain. Lowest-priority candidate at this locus; most
  parsimoniously interpreted as a passenger gene captured by the extended LD block rather than
  an independently plausible COPD gene." (researcher.md, confirmed HOLDS by both critique passes)
- **PNPO** — "PNPO has no COPD/lung-function genetic association evidence, and its one relevant
  tissue-expression study argues against (rather than for) a strong lung-specific role, with
  only weak lung expression reported relative to liver/muscle/kidney. Low-priority candidate."
  (researcher.md, confirmed HOLDS by both critique passes — this is a case of genuine
  contradicting evidence being reported honestly rather than suppressed)

## Methodology notes / process transparency for this locus

- The researcher's first-pass checkpoint file (researcher.md) was verified against its own
  completion summary before proceeding: the summary claimed all 8 requested genes were
  researched (MAPT, MAPT-AS1, KANSL1, STH, SPPL2C, PRR15L, PNPO, CRHR1), and the checkpoint file
  was confirmed to contain a complete `## GENE:` section for each, consistent with the summary's
  specific claims (KANSL1's UK BiLEVE finding, MAPT's Morrow 2018 finding, SPPL2C's statistical/
  functional divergence, CRHR1's light-touch scope) — no gaps found, no re-dispatch needed.
- The first red-team-skeptic pass on this locus exhausted its search budget (200/200 calls
  already used elsewhere in this session) before running any independent searches, and
  disclosed this explicitly rather than silently presenting an unsupported critique. A second,
  fresh red-team-skeptic instance was dispatched specifically to complete the three follow-up
  investigations that pass 1 had flagged as needed (Shrine 2019 locus-naming, Sakornsakolpat
  2019 locus-naming, KANSL1/Koolen-de Vries pulmonary phenotype). All new citations this second
  pass surfaced (PMID 30804560 Table 1 detail, PMID 30804561 CRHR1 passage, GeneReviews NBK24676,
  PMID 37479688) were independently verified by a second citation-verifier dispatch before being
  incorporated into the evidence table and this audit.
- Consistent with pipeline scope rules, only the lead variant (rs242559) and the missense/
  splice credible-set variants (5 in MAPT, 3 in KANSL1, 1 in STH) received any variant-specific
  literature search; the remaining ~950 credible-set variants (predominantly intronic) were
  rolled into gene-level evidence per gene, not searched individually.
