## Excluded citations

### CLAIM_MISMATCH

**Kamran M, Laighneach A, Bibi F, Donohoe G, Ahmed N, Rehman AU, Morris DW. "Independent
Associated SNPs at SORCS3 and Its Protein Interactors for Multiple Brain-Related Disorders and
Traits." Genes (Basel). 2023. PMID 36833409 / DOI 10.3390/genes14020482.**

Researcher's claim: systematic GWAS-catalog review identifies 72 genome-wide-significant
SNP-phenotype associations at SORCS3 spanning 25 distinct neuropsychiatric/behavioral/cognitive
phenotypes, none pulmonary.

Citation-verifier's verdict: **CLAIM_MISMATCH**. Verbatim reasoning: "According to PubMed, the
abstract confirms extensive SORCS3 GWAS associations stating 'Analysis of association signals
at SORCS3 showed individual SNPs to be associated with multiple neuropsychiatric and
neurodevelopmental brain-related disorders and traits.' However, the abstract does not specify
'72 genome-wide-significant SNP-phenotype associations spanning 25 distinct
neuropsychiatric/behavioral/cognitive phenotypes,' nor does it enumerate these counts
explicitly. Furthermore, the abstract does not explicitly state the negative claim about
absence of 'spirometric, lung-function, COPD, or smoking-behavior traits.' The numerical
specificity claimed by the researcher is not present in the abstract."

Important context the reader should weigh: the researcher's checkpoint states these exact
figures ("72" associations, "25" phenotypes) were drawn from the PMC full text, not the
abstract — the citation-verifier checked the abstract only, which is a real but narrower check
than a full-text read. The red-team-skeptic separately attempted to independently verify via
full text, could not retrieve it (PubMed access blocked), but judged the qualitative
characterization (SORCS3 gene-set enriched for schizophrenia/bipolar/intelligence/education,
synapse-structure ontologies, no pulmonary phenotype) "consistent with what the researcher
claims" based on other available search summaries. Net: the paper itself is real and not
retracted, and its qualitative claim (extensive neuropsychiatric associations, no pulmonary
phenotype found) is plausible and uncontradicted — but the specific "72/25" figures should NOT
be cited as confirmed numbers without a human reviewer independently pulling the full text.
Excluded from the main evidence table on this basis; the underlying paper and its general
characterization can still inform a human reviewer's judgment, with this caveat attached.

## Thoroughness gap flagged by red-team-skeptic (not a citation exclusion, but relevant to audit)

The red-team-skeptic flagged that AS3MT, CYP17A1, NT5C2, and C10orf32/BORCS7 — all
independently enumerated by the researcher as physically within the same 10q24.32 LD block
(under GRCh37 coordinates) but not flagged by any pipeline method (FLAMES/MAGMA/PoPS/ABC) —
were not given a full citation-backed literature search by the researcher, who explicitly
stated this was "given resource constraints" and that some characterizations (e.g., AS3MT as a
schizophrenia gene) were from "general domain knowledge, NOT independently cited/verified in
this pass." Verbatim skeptic recommendation: "AS3MT/CYP17A1/NT5C2/C10orf32-BORCS7 should get a
minimal confirmatory literature pass (a few targeted searches each) before the synthesis lead
treats their absence-of-evidence as settled — not a full research cycle, but enough to convert
'not searched' into 'searched, negative.'"

Synthesis-lead's partial response: rather than re-dispatching a full researcher pass (which the
skeptic explicitly said was not required), synthesis-lead ran a supplementary Open Targets
structured-data cross-check (not a literature search) for all four genes' aggregate COPD
association scores and genetic constraint. Results: AS3MT, CYP17A1, and C10orf32/BORCS7 all
returned zero Open Targets COPD-association rows; NT5C2 returned a near-null score (0.0037,
literature-datatype only, no genetic_association contribution) similar in magnitude to CNNM2's.
None of the four show meaningful genetic constraint either. This converts "not searched" into
"checked against one structured database, found null" as the skeptic requested, but is
explicitly NOT a substitute for the targeted PubMed literature searches (e.g. "AS3MT COPD,"
"NT5C2 lung," "CYP17A1 lung function," "BORCS7 COPD") the skeptic also recommended — a human
reviewer or a follow-up researcher pass should still run these before fully closing the book on
these four genes. See `evidence_tables/_status/10_104600001-104950000/opentargets.md` for full
detail.

## Build-coordinate discrepancy (not an exclusion, but a material caveat carried through)

The researcher independently discovered, and the red-team-skeptic corroborated, that CNNM2's
GRCh38 coordinates (chr10:102,918,286-103,090,222) do not physically overlap this locus window
(10:104,600,001-104,950,000) at all — CNNM2 only overlaps under GRCh37 coordinates
(10:104,678,050-104,849,978). Since the credible-set variants driving this locus's fine-mapping
are themselves in GRCh38, this raises the possibility that CNNM2's designation as a
MAGMA/PoPS-prioritized "physically overlapping" gene for this locus is a build-mismatch
artifact in the upstream gene-prioritization pipeline, rather than genuine evidence tying
CNNM2's own credible-set variants to CNNM2. This is carried through explicitly in the main
evidence table and Prioritized-gene section rather than silently corrected or ignored, since
resolving it definitively (i.e., confirming which build the MAGMA/PoPS annotation pipeline
actually used) is outside what this literature-review pipeline can determine on its own.

## PMID discrepancy noted during Open Targets lookup (informational only, resolved)

During the Open Targets structured lookup, the COPD GWAS study record `GCST007692`
(Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019, Nat Genet) returned PMID 30804561. The researcher's own,
independent PubMed search for the same paper returned PMID 30804560 (differing by one digit)
and flagged this as unresolved. Synthesis-lead independently re-checked via WebSearch:
PMID 30804561 is confirmed correct (matches the paper's title "Genetic landscape of chronic
obstructive pulmonary disease identifies heterogeneous cell-type and phenotype associations,"
Nat Genet 2019;51:494-505, 35,735 cases / 222,076 controls). This PMID is used consistently in
`opentargets.md` and this audit; no citation in the main evidence table relies on the disputed
PMID 30804560.
