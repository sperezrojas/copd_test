## Excluded citations (RETRACTED / NOT_FOUND / CLAIM_MISMATCH)

1. **Wain LV, et al., Nat Genet 2017. PMID 28166213, DOI 10.1038/ng.3787.**
   Claimed: "Genome-wide association analyses for lung function and chronic
   obstructive pulmonary disease identify new loci and potential druggable
   targets" — large UK Biobank/SpiroMeta lung-function and COPD GWAS reporting
   RBMS3/3p24 among genome-wide significant loci.
   **Verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH.** Citation-verifier's full-text search of
   PMC5326681 found ZERO mentions of RBMS3, 3p24, or rs17666332; the paper's
   reported novel chromosome-3 signal is actually EEFSEC, a different locus.
   The researcher's claim of replication in this paper is not supported by
   the paper's actual content.

2. **Shrine N, et al., Nat Genet 2019 (researcher's tentative PMID 30804560).**
   Claimed: "New genetic signals for lung function highlight pathways and
   chronic obstructive pulmonary disease associations across multiple
   ancestries" — lung-function GWAS reporting RBMS3/3p24 as a significant
   locus.
   **Verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH.** Citation-verifier's full-text search of
   PMC6397078 found ZERO mentions of RBMS3, 3p24, or rs17666332 among its 279
   reported lung-function signals. Not supported.

3. **Sakornsakolpat P, et al. "Genetic landscape of chronic obstructive
   pulmonary disease identifies heterogeneous cell-type and phenotype
   associations." Nat Genet 2019. PMID 30804561.**
   Claimed: Major COPD case-control GWAS meta-analysis (International COPD
   Genetics Consortium) reporting 3p24/RBMS3 among genome-wide significant
   COPD loci.
   **Verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH.** Citation-verifier's full-text search of
   PMC6546635 found ZERO mentions of RBMS3, 3p24, or rs17666332 among its 82
   reported genome-wide-significant COPD loci. Not supported.

4. **PMID 41897845** (candidate RBMS3-height GWAS hit, researcher flagged as
   low-confidence/not reviewed in depth).
   **Verdict: NOT_FOUND** (as claimed). This PMID actually resolves to
   "Genome-Wide Identification of Genomic Regions Associated with Body Weight
   and Morphometric Traits in Awassi Sheep" — an unrelated sheep-genomics
   paper, not a human RBMS3-height association study.

## Genes/claims marked CONTESTED or flagged by red-team-skeptic

**RBMS3 as a validated/replicated COPD-GWAS locus: CONTESTED.**
Red-team-skeptic's full reasoning (verbatim, from
`evidence_tables/_status/3_29300001-29500000/critique.md`):

> This should carry near-zero incremental evidentiary weight. The prompt
> itself concedes this score derives from the same underlying GWAS
> meta-analysis that produced this pipeline's own credible set — it is not
> independent replication, it's the source data redescribing itself through a
> different interface. Worse, the citation-verifier's full-text checks show
> that three major, well-powered, independently published GWAS efforts that
> should have been positioned to detect this signal ... contain zero mentions
> of RBMS3, 3p24, or rs17666332. That is not merely "absence of a citation" —
> these are large, systematic locus-enumeration papers, so their silence is
> informative negative evidence, not a gap. ... That said, non-replication in
> 2017–2019 papers does not prove the signal is spurious: the pipeline's
> underlying meta-analysis may simply have larger sample size / different
> case definition / different smoking-adjustment than those three papers, and
> could be picking up a real but previously underpowered signal. The honest
> framing is "unreplicated in the pre-2020 literature, mechanism/robustness
> unknown" — not "robustly validated" (Open Targets framing) and not
> confidently "false positive" either.
>
> Verdict on the 0.83 score: WEAKENED to near-uninformative — circular with
> the pipeline's own input data, contradicted by silence in three major
> independent GWAS papers of record.

**Overall locus verdict (verbatim):**

> CONTESTED. The statistical-genetics case for RBMS3-COPD is weaker than it
> first appeared (unreplicated in three major papers, Open Targets score
> circular), the smoking-mediation question is raised but unresolved, and the
> mechanistic biology — while real and appropriately caveated by the
> researcher — is entirely non-lung/non-COPD in its direct evidence base.
> This gene should not be presented to synthesis as a strong, converging hit;
> it should be presented as a single-gene locus with a live,
> currently-unresolved question about whether this represents genuine
> pulmonary biology, an artifact of an unreplicated statistical signal, or
> smoking-behavior mediation.

**Competing hypotheses considered by red-team-skeptic (verbatim summary):**

> - Spurious/unreplicated statistical signal: plausible given the null
>   full-text results in three major prior meta-analyses named above. This is
>   the strongest alternative given current evidence, though not provable
>   from what's here.
> - Smoking-behavior-mediated (Prior #1): Open Targets flags "smoking
>   initiation" as an independently scored RBMS3 trait (0.264) ... However,
>   the researcher found zero PubMed hits combining RBMS3 with
>   smoking/nicotine/tobacco — so this hypothesis is flagged by the database
>   context but not corroborated by any literature citation. It should be
>   carried forward as an open, unresolved concern, not treated as confirmed
>   mediation.
> - Fibrotic/connective-tissue mechanism shared across ocular and pulmonary
>   tissue: the only independent, verified GWAS hit for RBMS3/3p24 outside
>   COPD is exfoliation syndrome (Aung 2017), a fibrotic ocular disease, and
>   the mechanistic literature independently converges on RBMS3 as a
>   stabilizer of fibrosis-associated transcripts ... This is a coherent,
>   testable hypothesis — but it is a bridge built by the reviewer/researcher
>   across disease contexts, not a claim any single cited paper makes about
>   lung. It is speculation, clearly labeled as such.
> - RBMS3 correct but mechanism unstudied in lung: possible ... But no paper
>   tests airway remodeling, emphysema, alveolar structure, or lung
>   inflammation directly. This is an evidence gap, honestly reported as such
>   by the researcher, not a finding.

**Explicit caution against overreach (verbatim):**

> There's an easy but unsupported rhetorical bridge from "RBMS3 restrains
> Wnt/β-catenin/EMT in cancer" to "therefore RBMS3 plausibly restrains
> EMT-driven airway remodeling in COPD." That bridge does not exist in any
> cited source and should be explicitly barred from the synthesis write-up
> unless a new citation is found.

**Red-team-skeptic's own disclosed limitation:** the skeptic reported it
could not run additional independent contradiction searches this session
(e.g., "RBMS3 lung fibrosis" or "RBMS3 emphysema") due to exhausted search
budget, and explicitly labeled its pass as "a shallow/incomplete
contradiction-search pass ... not a full pass" — flagged here for
transparency; this locus may warrant a follow-up targeted search in a future
review cycle.
