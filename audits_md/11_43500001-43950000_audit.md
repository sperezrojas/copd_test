## Excluded citations

### 1. Liu M, Jiang Y, Wedow R, et al. (GSCAN consortium). "Association studies of up to 1.2 million individuals yield new insights into the genetic etiology of tobacco and alcohol use." Nature Genetics, 2019. PMID 30643251; DOI 10.1038/s41588-018-0307-5.

**Verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH.**

Researcher's original claim: attempted to use this large tobacco/alcohol-use GWAS to argue that HSD17B12 is *not* discussed as a smoking-trait gene there, i.e., using the gene's apparent absence from this paper's findings as evidence against a neurobiological smoking-initiation mechanism for HSD17B12.

Citation-verifier's finding (verbatim): "According to PubMed abstract, this large GWAS of tobacco/alcohol use identified nicotinic, dopaminergic, and glutamatergic neurotransmission as dominant biological themes. However, HSD17B12 is not mentioned in the abstract as a smoking-trait gene, and no specific discussion of HSD17B12 was located despite the researcher's request to verify this claim." The paper itself is real and its general finding is accurate, but the verifier could not confirm or deny HSD17B12's presence or absence in the paper's full gene list (only the abstract was checked) — so the specific claim attributed to it (that HSD17B12 is notably absent) is unverified and should not be relied upon in either direction.

Red-team-skeptic's assessment (verbatim): "The GSCAN citation (#7) that the researcher tried to use to argue HSD17B12 is absent from smoking-trait gene lists was rejected by the verifier as CLAIM_MISMATCH — the researcher cannot confirm HSD17B12's absence from that paper. This citation should be treated as contributing nothing to the smoking-vs-pulmonary question in either direction, and the researcher's synthesis should not lean on it." ... "discard citation #7 (GSCAN) entirely per its CLAIM_MISMATCH verdict — it supports nothing about HSD17B12 specifically."

**Disposition:** excluded entirely from the evidence table. Not used to support any claim about HSD17B12's smoking-behavior mechanism (or lack thereof) in the final synthesis.

## Excluded gene-level evidence (no specific reviewable claim)

### ALKBH3 (flanking gene)

The researcher searched ALKBH3 (a flanking gene at this locus, identified via the Open Targets L2G neighbor comparison, L2G 0.05-0.11 vs. HSD17B12's 0.43-0.82 at this variant) and found no lung/COPD/immune connection in a title-level PubMed search ("ALKBH3 lung function COPD" returned zero hits). The researcher listed several PMIDs of general ALKBH3 DNA-repair/demethylase literature (41509214, 40874592, 33321288, 35400026, 31642493, 26863410, 26221185, 25944111) but explicitly did not review any of them individually or attach a specific, lung/COPD-relevant claim to any of them, stating: "Given no plausible lung-structural or immune connection surfaced and the Open Targets L2G data already substantially disfavor this gene at this locus, I did not pursue deeper individual-paper review — this is a gene where the biology (general DNA-repair/alkylation-damage function) does not make a clear COPD-relevant connection evident from available literature."

**Disposition:** excluded from the evidence table — no claim to verify, no evidentiary content for this locus's COPD prioritization. Not marked CONTESTED (no contradicting evidence exists; there is simply no supporting evidence).

## Genes considered and their skeptic disposition (none marked CONTESTED)

Red-team-skeptic's explicit finding on this locus (verbatim): "**None.** No gene at this locus has credible contradicting evidence (a paper arguing against it, a null finding, or a paper implicating a different gene at the same locus) in the material provided, and I was unable to independently surface any in this session due to search-budget exhaustion. HSD17B12 and MIR670HG are both WEAKENED (for the specific overreach reasons above), and ALKBH3/TTC17/ACCS simply have insufficient positive evidence to prioritize, with TTC17 particularly meriting a second look at the cilia-COPD hypothesis before final write-up."

For completeness, the WEAKENED (not CONTESTED) findings are preserved here as well, since they materially shaped how the evidence table frames each claim:

**HSD17B12 — WEAKENED** (skeptic's verbatim reasoning): "The core case (Loth FVC hit + Ashmore lung enzymology + Open Targets L2G distinguishing HSD17B12 from neighbors) is real and is the strongest gene-level case at this locus. But the inference overreaches in two specific ways that should be corrected before synthesis: (1) Sakornsakolpat 2019 is being allowed to imply direct COPD-locus replication that the verifier explicitly could not confirm — this should be stated as unconfirmed, not folded into a 'known COPD locus' framing; (2) the smoking-heaviness L2G signal at the same variant is substantial and should be foregrounded as a live competing/co-occurring mechanism rather than resolved by the Ashmore paper (which addresses initiation-vs-metabolism, not heaviness-mediation-vs-pulmonary-independence); (3) discard citation #7 (GSCAN) entirely per its CLAIM_MISMATCH verdict."

Also flagged by the skeptic: "the researcher did not appear to run a dedicated 'HSD17B12 COPD null / negative' or '11p11.2 COPD alternative gene' search — the absence of contradicting evidence here reads more like 'wasn't found by the searches that were run' than 'was actively searched for and absent.'" This is preserved as an open methodological caveat: absence of contradicting evidence for HSD17B12 should not be read as a confirmed clean negative.

**MIR670HG — WEAKENED, "bordering on not-evidence"** (skeptic's verbatim reasoning): "Given that Open Targets has zero gene-COPD evidence for MIR670HG and essentially no other data, and the credible-set PIP is concentrated in HSD17B12, I would characterize MIR670HG's immune evidence as WEAKENED at best, bordering on not-evidence for this specific locus's COPD mechanism. I do not think either individual paper needs a CONTESTED flag (both are accurately cited and the researcher didn't overstate them) — the concern is with how they might be used at synthesis, not with the citations themselves." The two supporting papers (Kupffer-cell/liver-macrophage phagocytosis, PMID 40293529; CNS-microglial neuroinflammation, PMID 34188516) are individually VERIFIED as accurately cited but are "0-for-2 on lung/airway tissue and 0-for-2 on COPD-relevant disease context." The skeptic explicitly warned against building a "superficially plausible 'MIR670HG -> macrophage/microglial immune regulation -> COPD innate immune involvement' narrative by analogy," calling this a cell-type/tissue-context overreach per the pipeline's prior #3.

An open structural-genomics question raised by the skeptic — whether MIR670HG is a host gene nested within HSD17B12 or a separate adjacent transcript — was resolved by the synthesis lead post-critique using Open Targets' `genomicLocation` field (a structured database lookup, not a literature claim): HSD17B12 spans chr11:43,680,509-43,857,741 and MIR670HG spans chr11:43,556,394-43,673,393 (GRCh38); these do not overlap (~7.1kb gap). MIR670HG is confirmed to be a separate, non-nested, non-overlapping adjacent gene. See `evidence_tables/_status/11_43500001-43950000/opentargets.md` addendum for full detail.

**TTC17 — insufficient evidence, not contested**, flagged by the skeptic as meriting a follow-up search on ciliary-dysfunction/mucociliary-clearance biology specifically in relation to COPD (a hypothesis the researcher's search did not directly test), though the skeptic does not expect this to change TTC17's bottom-line deprioritization given the Open Targets L2G disparity (0.05-0.11 vs. HSD17B12's 0.43-0.82).

**ACCS — insufficient evidence, not contested.** No lung/COPD/immune connection found; recently reclassified basic enzymology (cysteine S-conjugate sulfoxide β-lyase activity) with no established human physiological role.
