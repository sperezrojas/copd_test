## Excluded citations (RETRACTED / NOT_FOUND / CLAIM_MISMATCH)

## Gene marked CONTESTED by red-team-skeptic: GATA3

Verdict from red-team-skeptic (verbatim, full critique preserved in
`evidence_tables/_status/10_8900001-9250000/critique.md`):

> ### Verdict: CONTESTED (as a COPD-specific causal gene), with WEAKENED-BUT-SUPPORTED status only for "best
> available candidate gene by physical elimination"
>
> GATA3 remains the only plausible protein-coding candidate physically/regulatorily nearby, so it should not be
> dropped from consideration. But the claim that this locus reflects COPD-specific or neutrophilic/structural
> COPD biology is directly contested by: (a) negligible COPD-specific Open Targets score and asthma-only L2G
> assignment, (b) h4>0.99 colocalization indicating one shared asthma/FEV1/COPD signal rather than independent
> COPD support, (c) the CRISPR null effect of the exact fine-mapped lead variant on GATA3 expression (Kumagai
> 2024), (d) the Th2-low ACOS paradox (Hirai 2017), (e) the SB010 trial's failure to improve lung function
> despite eosinophil reduction (Greulich 2018), (f) old negative candidate-gene replication (Suttner 2009), and
> (g) absence of this locus from the one GWAS designed specifically to separate asthma- from COPD-predominant
> ACO signals (PMID 35104449, new/UNVERIFIED at time of critique, subsequently VERIFIED). The most parsimonious
> reading, consistent with the researcher's own synthesis, is that this is an asthma/Th2-allergic pleiotropy
> signal detected in the COPD GWAS via shared genetic liability and/or ACOS-phenotype contamination, not a
> COPD-intrinsic mechanism — synthesis-lead should present it accordingly and flag the underlying-cohort
> phenotype-definition question as unresolved.

### Full critique reasoning (verbatim, preserved for traceability)

**1. Is GATA3 overclaimed as "the" COPD gene here?** Yes, materially, though the researcher's own framing
already discounts this. The COPD-specific statistical genetics is thin by every available metric: Open
Targets' aggregate COPD disease-association score for GATA3 is negligible (not top 500/4855) vs. a strong
asthma score; the only locus-specific L2G assignment is to "allergic asthma" (0.615), not COPD or FEV1/FEV1-FVC;
the COPD credible set colocalizes (h4>0.99) with FEV1 and with asthma/allergic credible sets, which is
statistical evidence for one shared signal, not independent COPD support layered on top of the asthma signal;
and the single COPD/lung-function-specific GWAS citation (Shrine 2023) could not be confirmed to include GATA3
among its 559 prioritized genes. The dedicated ACO GWAS (John et al. 2022, Chest, PMID 35104449) found eight
ACO-associated loci (FAM105A, GLB1, PHB, TSLP, IL17RD, C5orf56, HLA-DQB1, one chr5 intergenic signal) in
~8,068 ACO cases, and GATA3/10p14 is not among them — circumstantial (the ACO GWAS is underpowered relative to
Shrine 2019 asthma or Shrine 2023 COPD/lung-function GWAS) but a real absence-of-replication data point in the
one study design built specifically to separate asthma-predominant from COPD-predominant signals.

**2. Physical distance + null functional effect of the lead variant.** GATA3 is ~930kb from the locus
interval; the "only protein-coding gene in the interval" argument is an elimination argument, not positive
evidence of mechanism. LINC02676 and other lncRNAs in the interval were not seriously evaluated as alternative
regulatory targets. Kumagai 2024 (PMID 38923989) is the only functional/CRISPR study of this exact variant, and
it found the PIP=0.997 lead SNP itself has no effect on GATA3 expression in Th2 cells — a different nearby SNP
(rs725861) does. Statistical fine-mapping (PIP) and functional causality are not the same thing, and here they
diverge in the one experiment available. Confidence that GATA3 (rather than a neighboring lncRNA, a different
distal gene, or no clean single-gene explanation at all) is the functional target of this regulatory element
should be lower than the "GATA3 is the only plausible candidate" framing implies.

**3. Contradicting evidence.** Beyond Hirai 2017's Th2-low ACOS paradox, Suttner 2009's negative candidate-gene
replication, and Kumagai's null CRISPR result, the Greulich 2018 SB010 trial (PMID 29615049) deserves more
critical weight: this was a mechanistic on-target test in the exact population most likely to show a
GATA3-driven COPD effect (COPD patients pre-selected for sputum eosinophilia), the drug did engage the target
(eosinophils decreased), and it still did not improve lung function or symptoms — a direct negative result on
the clinically relevant endpoint (airflow/lung function) for GATA3-axis modulation in COPD, not merely a
different-disease-model caveat.

**4. Alternative hypotheses not fully explored.** Pure ACOS-misclassification artifact: the researcher raises
this as their own synthesis but does not test it against the phenotype definitions of the underlying COPD GWAS
cohorts (self-report/ICD vs. GOLD-spirometry-confirmed case ascertainment) — this should be flagged to the
study team as an evaluable follow-up, not treated as resolved. Wrong regulatory target entirely (lncRNA
in-interval, or a gene further away than the interval boundary) was not seriously considered. Smoking-mediation:
the researcher found no smoking-trait GWAS links, appropriately reported as absence-of-evidence rather than
evidence-of-absence given they could not confirm from Shrine 2023 full text whether this locus was among the
smoking-driven signals excluded.

**5. Where the researcher over-rescued vs. was appropriately cautious.** Appropriately cautious: flagging the
Jiang et al. cluster as "disproportionately one research group," explicitly labeling Jogdand/Greulich as a
minority endotype, catching and fixing a Faas/Sasaki citation mismatch from the original briefing, flagging
Stadhouders' ILC2-vs-adaptive-Th2 distinction, and citing Li 2026 keratoconus pleiotropy as evidence against
COPD-specificity rather than for it. Slight overreach: treating the He 2022 (PM-exposure rat) and Han 2022
(ozone+P. aeruginosa mouse) papers as part of the COPD-relevant evidence base at all, given neither is a
smoking model and both are fairly distant from human COPD pathophysiology; and the Greulich trial's null
lung-function result reads more as evidence against a GATA3-airflow pathway than as a neutral feasibility
caveat.

## Other researcher-flagged caveats preserved for traceability

- **Citation correction caught by the researcher itself:** the orchestrator's original briefing (drawing on an
  Open Targets/UniProt function annotation) attributed a claim about GATA3's role in "IL33-driven macrophage
  transcriptional reprogramming" to Sasaki et al. 2013 (PMID 23824597). The researcher read the actual paper
  and found it describes Th2 T-cell identity maintenance, not macrophage biology — a mismatched citation in the
  UniProt annotation as relayed. The researcher located and substituted the correct supporting paper (Faas et
  al. 2021, Immunity, PMID 34644537). Both citations are retained in the evidence table, each with the accurate
  claim attached; citation-verifier independently confirmed this correction was accurate.
- **Research-group concentration:** several of the ILC2/Notch-GATA3/AECOPD papers (Jiang et al., PMIDs
  34354706, 31320835, 31572551, 39015676) originate from what the researcher describes as "disproportionately
  one research group," which would benefit from independent replication before being weighted heavily.
- **Non-smoking exposure models:** He et al. 2022 (PM-exposure rat) and Han et al. 2022 (ozone + P. aeruginosa
  mouse) are COPD models built on non-smoking environmental/infectious exposures; red-team-skeptic flagged
  their inclusion as padding the apparent volume of "COPD-relevant GATA3 evidence" without adding much
  probative weight for smoking-driven human COPD specifically.
- **Preprint status:** Flayer et al. (PMID 38045302) remains an unpublished medRxiv preprint as of September
  2026, not yet peer-reviewed, per both the researcher's and citation-verifier's independent checks.
- **Unresolved question flagged for the study team:** whether the COPD GWAS cohorts underlying this locus's
  signal required spirometry-confirmed (GOLD-defined) COPD case ascertainment versus self-report/ICD-code-based
  definitions was not resolved by the researcher or red-team-skeptic, and bears directly on how much of this
  locus's COPD association could reflect asthma-COPD misclassification versus genuine shared or COPD-intrinsic
  biology.
