This file preserves everything excluded from the main evidence table, plus the full

## CONTESTED gene: TCF4

Red-team-skeptic verdict: **CONTESTED**. Full reasoning preserved verbatim:

> Not because a paper directly refutes TCF4, but because the case for prioritizing TCF4 at
> *this* locus rests on (a) a MAGMA/PoPS gene-level call at a distance (1.77Mb) that
> plausibly exceeds standard assignment windows in a known gene-desert region,
> unaccompanied by any independent second signal or regulatory-linkage evidence, and (b) a
> single very recent, non-COPD-specific, unreplicated composite-liability paper. TCF4
> should be treated as a statistically-flagged but mechanistically-unconfirmed candidate
> pending clarification of the MAGMA/PoPS window used in this pipeline, and not presented
> as comparably well-supported to a gene with clean physical overlap.

Supporting detail on the distance concern:

> Standard MAGMA gene-based windows are typically far smaller (commonly ±0-35kb, sometimes
> extended to ~50-100kb); standard PoPS implementations (Weeks et al.) typically restrict
> gene assignment to within ~1Mb of the lead signal. A 1.77Mb gap exceeds even generous
> versions of these windows, which raises a real possibility of a gene-assignment/LD-window
> artifact rather than true biological signal — particularly plausible here because this is
> a known large gene desert on 18q21 with DCC as the sole coding gene for nearly 1.2Mb, a
> region prone to extended LD/recombination coldspots that can inflate apparent long-range
> statistical gene-prioritization.

Three alternative explanations were identified as not adjudicable from the data available
to this review: (a) LD-driven MAGMA/PoPS artifact (judged most likely given the desert
architecture), (b) a genuinely independent second COPD signal nearer TCF4 not captured in
this DCC-centered credible set (speculative — no second credible set or independent lead
SNP near TCF4 was reported), (c) a bona fide long-range regulatory element (distal
enhancer) regulating TCF4 despite the distance (plausible in principle, entirely
unevidenced here — no Hi-C or lung enhancer-target data was found).

On the Tu et al. 2026 finding (PMID 42653451), the skeptic's verbatim assessment:

> It is a single, ~1-month-old, unreplicated source; the TCF4 finding is a regulon
> enrichment (pDC/B cell identity programs) within a composite cross-disease
> "gAirwayDisease" liability factor spanning COPD+asthma+bronchiectasis+CRS — not a
> COPD-specific signal, and not explicitly tied back to this credible set/locus (no
> statement that this locus's COPD signal specifically drove the regulon call). Given
> TCF4's extreme constraint (oe=0.037) and broad pleiotropy across neuropsychiatric and
> multi-disease composite analyses, it plausibly surfaces in many trait-agnostic
> multi-omic screens without that reflecting a COPD-specific causal role. This is not
> enough, on its own, to prioritize TCF4 over DCC for this specific locus; it is a
> hypothesis-generating flag at best.

Mitigating point in TCF4's favor, preserved for balance:

> TCF4 has 2 GTEx lung transcript-usage (sQTL-type) records and 3 stimulated-monocyte
> eQTLs, i.e. some direct lung/immune-tissue molecular evidence — thin, but nonzero,
> whereas DCC has zero lung molecular QTLs at all. This tension should be weighed
> explicitly rather than letting physical distance alone settle the comparison.

## WEAKENED gene: DCC

Red-team-skeptic verdict: **WEAKENED** (not excluded from the main table, since it remains
the prioritized gene, but its mechanistic claims are explicitly downgraded). Full reasoning
preserved verbatim:

> The gene assignment (physical overlap) holds, but the mechanistic case overreaches:
> smoking-mediation is plausible but built on an absence-of-confirmation citation plus a
> circular Open Targets ranking rather than locus-specific evidence; there is no COPD/FEV1
> evidence and no lung eQTL for DCC itself; and the oncology/developmental literature almost
> entirely implicates the ligand (netrin-1) or other receptors, not DCC. State this as
> "smoking-behavior-plausible, lung-mechanism-absent" rather than a confirmed
> smoking-mediated COPD gene.

Specific overclaiming concern flagged and corrected:

> The two primary smoking-behavior GWAS (Liu 2019, Brazel 2018) are cited only as general
> architecture context — the researcher explicitly could not confirm DCC/NTN1 is named as
> an implicated gene in either paper. That is not evidence *for* DCC; it is an absence of
> evidence that should not be doing rhetorical work in the smoking-mediation argument. ...
> Open Targets association scores are themselves derived from other GWAS' own
> locus-to-gene assignments, which for a gene sitting in the middle of a large gene desert
> are subject to the same nearest-gene/window heuristics being scrutinized here — so this
> is suggestive, not confirmatory, and somewhat circular as "independent" support.

## Search-budget limitation on red-team review

The red-team-skeptic disclosed the following limitation verbatim, which the study team
should weigh when assessing how much independent adversarial scrutiny this locus actually
received:

> I attempted one shallow contradiction-search pass covering both DCC and TCF4 together
> (query 1: "18q21.2 COPD GWAS locus DCC TCF4 lung function chromosome 18"; query 2:
> "rs12965339 OR 18q21.2 lung function FEV1 GWAS catalog"). Both failed — the session's
> WebSearch budget (200/200) was already exhausted before my calls executed, apparently
> from upstream agent activity in this same session. I was not able to complete any
> independent search for either gene. My critique below is therefore based entirely on the
> researcher's own reported findings... I'd flag to synthesis-lead that if search budget
> can be raised, a targeted search for "18q21.2 lung function locus" against the GWAS
> Catalog / recent COPD multi-ancestry GWAS (e.g., any 2023-2025 update to
> Sakornsakolpat/Shrine) would be the single highest-value follow-up, specifically to check
> whether a more recent, larger COPD or spirometry GWAS has assigned this exact region to a
> third gene not considered here (the region is a large gene desert between DCC and TCF4,
> and desert regions are classic sites for LD-based misassignment).

This means the red-team critique above is based on internal-consistency review of the
researcher's own reported findings (including the researcher's own already-extensive
zero-result contradiction searches), not on a fully independent literature re-search. This
is a genuine limitation of this locus's review and is flagged here rather than silently
omitted.
