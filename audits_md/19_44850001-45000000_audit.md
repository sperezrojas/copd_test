## Excluded citations

### CLAIM_MISMATCH — excluded from evidence table

**Wang R, Cukerman E, Heng HH, Liew CC. "Identification of a locus of zinc finger genes in
human chromosome 19q13.1-q13.3 region by fluorescence in situ hybridization." Somat Cell Mol
Genet. 1996. PMID 8914611, DOI 10.1007/BF02369916.**

Researcher's original claim: "This is the original paper identifying and physically mapping
the ZNF180 and ZNF181 gene loci on chromosome 19q13.1-q13.3 via FISH; it establishes ZNF180's
genomic location and its general description as a zinc-finger-coding gene but reports no
functional, disease, lung, or immune data whatsoever."

Citation-verifier's verdict (verbatim): "CLAIM_MISMATCH — Wang R et al., Somat Cell Mol Genet
1996, PMID 8914611. Article title and existence confirmed; however, the abstract explicitly
states: 'genomic rearrangements were shown to be accompanied by various developmental
abnormalities, DNA repair deficiencies, and cellular malignancies.' The claim states the
paper 'reports no functional, disease, lung, or immune data' — this is inaccurate; it does
report disease-associated data and functional implications."

Red-team-skeptic's assessment (verbatim): "Citation-mismatch flag (process concern):
Citation-verifier caught that the researcher's claim of 'no functional, disease, lung, or
immune data' for Wang 1996 (PMID 8914611) is not accurate — the abstract does discuss disease
context (developmental abnormalities, DNA-repair deficiencies, malignancies) for the broader
19q13 rearrangement literature. This is a genuine instance of overclaiming-by-omission (the
researcher stripped disease context to strengthen a 'ZNF180 has nothing' narrative), correctly
caught by the verifier. That said, the disease context in question is about the rationale for
studying 19q13 chromosomal rearrangements in general — not gene-specific ZNF180 function or
COPD/lung relevance — so the substantive conclusion (ZNF180 has no lung/COPD-relevant
functional literature) still stands; only the 'the paper is completely silent on disease'
framing was wrong."

**Disposition:** Excluded from the main evidence table per pipeline rules (only VERIFIED
citations are used in the table). The paper is real and correctly identifies ZNF180/ZNF181's
genomic location, but should not be cited for any claim about absence of disease relevance.
The substantive downstream conclusion (ZNF180 has no lung/COPD-relevant literature) is
unaffected, since it rests on other evidence (the negative literature search overall, and the
CRC-specific PMID 39054954 finding).

No citations received a RETRACTED or NOT_FOUND verdict. 32 of 33 citations submitted were
VERIFIED (see `evidence_tables/_status/19_44850001-45000000/verified_citations.md` for the
full verbatim verifier output).

## CONTESTED / WEAKENED gene-level verdicts (full reasoning preserved)

### APOE — Verdict: WEAKENED (red-team-skeptic)

Full verbatim critique from red-team-skeptic:

> **1. The central inferential problem — Prior #2 applies directly, and the researcher flags
> but doesn't resolve it.** This credible set exists because rs429358/APOE region reached
> genome-wide significance in a COPD case-control GWAS (Open Targets genetic_association
> score 0.72, high-confidence L2G at 2 independent loci). Yet the researcher explicitly could
> not find a headline COPD or spirometry GWAS reporting this as a genome-wide-significant
> finding, and could not confirm colocalization with an independent FEV1/FEV1-FVC signal. Per
> Prior #2, "a COPD association without supporting pulmonary-function evidence warrants
> consideration of alternative pathways" — this is exactly that scenario, at one of the
> best-powered loci in all of human genetics (APOE is saturated with signal from Alzheimer's,
> lipid, and longevity GWAS of huge sample size, so if there were a robust independent
> spirometric effect it is plausible it would have been noticed by now). The researcher's own
> "thin-to-absent" characterization is honest, but the write-up doesn't push hard enough on
> what that absence implies: this pattern is more consistent with the association being
> carried by lipid/metabolic/mortality pleiotropy (or survivorship bias, per Li 2025 / Oh
> 2025) than by direct pulmonary mechanism. This should be surfaced to synthesis-lead as a
> first-order concern, not a caveat buried at the end.
>
> **2. Directionality is missing and matters.** Nothing in the findings states whether the
> COPD-risk allele at this locus corresponds to the epsilon4-tagging (rs429358-risk) or
> epsilon2-tagging direction. Given APOE's known epsilon4-association with mortality,
> cardiovascular disease, and Alzheimer's, the direction of effect is diagnostic: an
> epsilon4-concordant COPD "risk" direction is exactly what you'd expect from generic
> inflammaging/mortality/comorbidity pleiotropy rather than lung-specific biology, while a
> discordant direction would be more interesting. This is an omission worth flagging for
> follow-up rather than a reason to downgrade on its own.
>
> **3. Comorbidity/pleiotropy papers are being asked to do too much work.** Items 4 (You
> 2025), 5 (Zhang Z 2026), and 6 (Murrin 2026) are all cross-trait comorbidity studies
> (lung-GI, COPD-GI, COPD/asthma-musculoskeletal) that place APOE in a "pleiotropic gene set"
> or note pQTL/inflammatory-lipid pathway support. None of these isolate a COPD-specific or
> lung-autonomous mechanism — by the researcher's own admission for #4, and structurally true
> for #5 and #6 as well (comorbidity signal is not equal to lung-tissue causal mechanism).
> This is appropriately hedged in the write-up, but synthesis-lead should not let three
> separately-cited papers create an impression of convergent independent evidence when
> they're really three flavors of the same underlying observation.
>
> **4. Non-specificity of the knockout-mouse emphysema phenotype.** Cabanski 2015 explicitly
> states Apoe-/- is one of 5 smoke-susceptible strains sharing a generic
> NF-kB/TLR4/TNF-alpha pathway — i.e., the susceptibility is not uniquely ApoE-dependent,
> it's a strain-level phenotype from a shared innate-immune pathway. Combined with Moos
> 2024's finding that genetic Apoe deletion had "limited impact on inflammation and
> mortality" in a fibrosis model, there is direct internal tension in the knockout literature
> about whether ApoE loss is mechanistically necessary versus an incidental strain background
> effect. Only Goldklang 2012 and Arunachalam 2010 are both non-industry-funded and directly
> implicate ApoE loss-of-function; that is a much thinner evidentiary base than "13-plus
> knockout papers" suggests at first read.
>
> **5. Conflict of interest is substantial and should be stated plainly, not softened.** 8-9
> of the ~13 Apoe-/- emphysema papers come from a Philip Morris International systems-
> toxicology program using Apoe-/- mice purely as a convenient dual atherosclerosis/COPD
> comorbidity host to compare conventional vs. "modified-risk" tobacco products. These were
> not designed to test whether ApoE itself matters mechanistically (they don't include an
> ApoE-sufficient vs. -deficient direct comparison arm testing that question; ApoE-null is
> simply the host strain), and the funder has a direct financial interest in results showing
> reduced harm from its own products. This doesn't necessarily invalidate the emphysema
> phenotype itself, but it substantially inflates the apparent volume of "independent"
> support for ApoE lung biology.
>
> **6. LD caveat, weaker than usual but not zero.** The missense variant sitting
> unambiguously inside APOE is a reasonable basis for not exhaustively re-litigating
> TOMM40/APOC1/APOC2/NECTIN2/BCAM as alternative genes. But it's worth noting explicitly for
> the record that this is a famously long-range, high-LD haplotype block
> (APOE-TOMM40-APOC1), and a coding variant's presence doesn't by itself fully exclude that
> the disease-relevant mechanism operates through a co-inherited regulatory effect on a
> neighboring gene rather than through the amino-acid change itself.
>
> **7. Smoking-mediation is explicitly unresolved, not negative.** Given Zhang 2020's
> rs429358 x smoking-status interaction and Coppens 2021's nicotine-abstinence EEG effect in
> epsilon4 carriers, there is a live, unresolved possibility that this locus's apparent COPD
> association is smoking-behavior-mediated rather than pulmonary. Present as an open
> alternative hypothesis, not a ruled-out one.
>
> **Verdict: WEAKENED.** [full text preserved in evidence_tables/19_44850001-45000000.md
> Prioritized-gene section and evidence_tables/_status/19_44850001-45000000/critique.md]

### ZNF180 — Verdict: HOLDS (not contested; low-confidence conclusion endorsed)

Red-team-skeptic explicitly endorsed the researcher's low-confidence treatment of ZNF180 as
correct and not an overreach: "ZNF180 has zero COPD/lung/immune literature..., no Open
Targets L2G/coloc support, and imprecise constraint. Its candidacy rests entirely on PoPS...
plus physical proximity, while the sole credible-set variant is a missense change
unambiguously inside APOE — a much stronger and more direct form of variant-to-gene evidence
than PoPS alone. The researcher's inference — treat ZNF180 as low-confidence relative to
APOE — is well-supported by what's actually in hand and is not an overreach." Included here
for completeness/traceability, not because ZNF180 is contested.
