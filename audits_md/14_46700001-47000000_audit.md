## Excluded citations (CLAIM_MISMATCH or NOT_FOUND per citation-verifier)

### CLAIM_MISMATCH (2)

1. **Heinzman JT, Hoth KF, Cho MH, et al. "GWAS and systems biology analysis of depressive symptoms among smokers from the COPDGene cohort." J Affect Disord. 2018;243:16-22.** PMID 30219690, DOI 10.1016/j.jad.2018.09.003.
   - Researcher's claim: In a GWAS of depression among heavy smokers in the COPDGene cohort, a sub-genome-wide-significant SNP near MDGA2 (rs17118176, p=3.52x10⁻⁶) was identified; MDGA2-containing gene networks were enriched for synaptic transmission with strong co-expression in basal ganglia.
   - Verifier finding: the SNP/p-value association is confirmed, but the "strong co-expression in basal ganglia" claim is NOT directly supported by the paper — basal ganglia is mentioned in the tissue analysis generally, but MDGA2 itself is not specifically highlighted for that tissue.
   - Why excluded: the compound claim as stated by the researcher partially mismatches the source; only the confirmed portion (a depression-phenotype, sub-threshold SNP near MDGA2 in a COPD-ascertained-but-not-COPD-outcome cohort) would be usable, and even that has limited relevance (outcome is depression, not COPD/spirometry).

2. **Hettema JM, van den Oord EJCG, An SS, Kendler KS, Chen X. "Follow-up association study of novel neuroticism gene MAMDC1." Psychiatr Genet. 2009;19(4):213-214.** PMID 19440164, DOI 10.1097/YPG.0b013e32832cec22.
   - Researcher's claim: replicates a prior GWAS finding linking MDGA2 to neuroticism.
   - Verifier finding: the paper actually reports a FAILED replication (P>0.6 in the follow-up sample) — the opposite of what the researcher's summary implied.
   - Why excluded: direct contradiction between the researcher's characterization and the paper's actual finding. Note for synthesis: this is informative on its own — it is now the second/third documented failed-replication result touching MDGA2 across unrelated phenotypes (alongside Noth et al. 2013's IPF stage-3 non-replication), a pattern worth carrying forward qualitatively even though this specific citation is excluded from the evidence table.

## Contested/weakened gene assessment

The red-team-skeptic explicitly declined to mark MDGA2 CONTESTED — there is no competing gene at this locus to contest against (MDGA2 is the sole candidate: nearest gene, FLAMES-prioritized, and physically spans the entire credible-set window). Instead the skeptic rated the evidence base **WEAKENED** relative to the researcher's original framing. Full critique preserved verbatim below.

## Full red-team-skeptic critique (verbatim)

Note: my web-search budget for this session was exhausted before I could run independent confirmatory/contradicting-evidence searches (e.g., MDGA2 + lung function, rs12889080 + smoking-initiation GWAS). The critique below is therefore based entirely on the researcher's reported findings and the verifier's annotations, plus the standard priors — flagging this so synthesis-lead knows an independent contradicting-evidence search was not completed here.

### MDGA2 — critique

**Citation reliability.** 8 of 16 citations (50%) are either CLAIM_MISMATCH (2) or NOT_FOUND/unverifiable-paywalled (6). That is a high failure rate and several of the unverifiable ones are doing real interpretive work in the narrative — most notably the "CNS-restricted expression... outside the nervous system" claim (Litwack 2004, NOT_FOUND) that anchors the "no structural/developmental lung biology" conclusion, and the mouse body-weight phenotype (Fertan 2023, NOT_FOUND) that touches the adiposity hypothesis. These should be treated as unconfirmed background, not evidence, in synthesis. The two CLAIM_MISMATCH cases (Hettema 2009 actually *failed* to replicate, P>0.6; Heinzman 2018's "basal ganglia co-expression" claim unsupported) are correctly caught by the verifier, but note the pattern: this is now the *second and third* documented failed/non-replication for MDGA2 associations (alongside Noth 2013's IPF stage-3 failure), which is itself informative — MDGA2 signals across unrelated phenotypes have a track record of not holding up. That pattern should be stated explicitly in synthesis, not buried.

**Prior #1 (smoking-mediation) — overclaimed.** This is the most important catch. The researcher's own header calls Lai et al. 2025 "genome-wide significant," but the verifier found the actual reported value is P=4.52x10⁻⁷ — which does **not** meet the conventional genome-wide significance threshold of 5x10⁻⁸ (it is ~9-fold short of it). The verifier's note that "the qualitative claim holds under either value" is itself questionable: "genome-wide significant" vs. "suggestive, sub-threshold" is not a cosmetic difference, it's the difference between a robust and a marginal signal. Separately, even taking the association at face value, it is for a pooled cross-substance "substance-related disorder" phenotype (alcohol/cannabis/opioid/tobacco combined), not a tobacco/nicotine-specific trait — and the researcher explicitly confirms no smoking-initiation, cessation, or pack-years GWAS hit exists for this SNP. Labeling this "supporting prior #1 (smoking/addiction-behavior pathway)" therefore overstates a marginal, non-tobacco-specific finding as if it were solid smoking-mediation evidence. The more defensible characterization is: weak, sub-genome-wide-significant evidence for general addiction/reward-circuit vulnerability, not smoking specifically.

**Prior #2 (spirometry) — genuine, clean absence.** No FEV1/FEV1-FVC or COPD literature at all, across multiple targeted searches. This is correctly reported as a real gap rather than force-fit, and correctly weighted as evidence against a direct pulmonary mechanism per the prior's own logic (COPD association without spirometric concordance warrants skepticism).

**Prior #3 (inflammation) — correctly characterized as weak/indirect.** The Hellquist 2009 SLE/monocyte signal is verified but modest (OR 1.29-1.35, p=0.001-0.003) and the claimed Chinese-cohort replication (Wu 2011) is unverifiable and, per the researcher's own account, only replicated a subphenotype (rash/photosensitivity), not overall SLE risk — i.e., even the unverified corroborating source is itself a partial-replication story. No COPD-relevant airway inflammatory program (neutrophilic, CD8+, Th1/Th17, macrophage) evidence exists. This is honestly reported, not oversold.

**Adiposity/BMI alternative hypothesis — appropriately flagged, not oversold.** The researcher correctly labels this an Open Targets structured-database pattern (122/122 credible-set loci, mostly anthropometric) rather than literature evidence, explicitly notes it's outside the 3 standard priors, and does not claim a mechanism. This is good practice and should be passed to synthesis-lead as a genuinely open alternative — obesity/adiposity is biologically plausible for COPD-relevant systemic inflammation and lung mechanics, so this shouldn't be dismissed either.

**A missing alternative hypothesis: gene-size/pleiotropy artifact.** MDGA2 is ~430kb, one of the larger genes in the genome, and fills the entire credible-set window by itself. Its appearance across 122 unrelated GWAS credible sets (SLE, IPF, depression, fibromyalgia, neuroticism, carnitine metabolites, colorectal cancer, anthropometric traits, substance use) could reflect genuine broad pleiotropy — or could simply reflect that large genes mechanically accumulate more SNPs and are more likely to overlap any given credible set/LD block by chance (a well-known confound in gene-based GWAS analyses, which is why MAGMA and similar tools include gene-length correction). The researcher never raises this as a candidate explanation for MDGA2's promiscuous multi-trait footprint, and it should be flagged to synthesis-lead as a live alternative to "true broad pleiotropy."

**LD/nearest-gene bias.** Not really applicable here in the usual sense — there is no competing gene in the window (no MAGMA/PoPS/ABC hits, no other protein-coding genes), so MDGA2's *identity* as the locus gene is not in doubt. What's in doubt is whether MDGA2's *validated* biology (CNS synaptic, autism/epilepsy, addiction-adjacent) has anything to do with why this locus associates with COPD risk in an unadjusted-for-smoking GWAS.

**CONTESTED vs. WEAKENED.** I would not call this CONTESTED — that label implies credible evidence specifically arguing against MDGA2's role or implicating a different gene at the locus, and there is no competing gene here to contest against. What we have instead is a case where every line of "supporting" evidence, once the verifier's corrections are applied, turns out weaker than the researcher's framing suggests (sub-threshold p-value called "genome-wide significant," a non-tobacco-specific pooled trait invoked for a tobacco-specific prior, an unverifiable citation anchoring the "no lung expression" claim, a modest partially-non-replicating autoimmune signal). The researcher's own final paragraph is honest about the weakness, but the structured "SMOKING/SUBSTANCE-USE FINDING" header and "supporting prior #1" framing oversell the Lai 2025 result relative to what it actually shows.

**Most defensible interpretation of this locus:** MDGA2 is the only plausible gene physically at this locus, but there is no credible COPD-specific, spirometry-concordant, or COPD-relevant-immune evidence for it, and the strongest "biological" signal — a sub-genome-wide-significant, non-tobacco-specific pooled substance-use-disorder association at the lead variant — is weaker than the researcher's own header claims once the verifier's P-value correction is applied. MDGA2's best-validated biology is CNS synaptic/neurodevelopmental (dosage-sensitive epileptic-encephalopathy gene, autism/neuroticism/fibromyalgia associations), which is mechanistically disconnected from lung pathophysiology. The locus is most honestly reported as **low-confidence and mechanistically unresolved**, with three competing, none well-supported, hypotheses on the table: (1) marginal smoking/addiction-neurobiology mediation (weaker than stated), (2) an unaddressed adiposity/anthropometric pathway (Open Targets-flagged, no literature), and (3) a possible gene-size/pleiotropy artifact inflating MDGA2's apparent cross-phenotype relevance. None of these should be presented as the locus's likely mechanism with confidence.

**Verdict: WEAKENED** — the underlying citations are for the most part genuine (where verified), but the researcher's framing overreaches specifically on the Lai 2025 "genome-wide significant" / prior #1 smoking-mediation claim, and leans on unverifiable citations (Litwack 2004) to assert "no lung expression" as settled fact. Recommend synthesis-lead treat this locus as weak/non-specific evidence overall, correct the "genome-wide significant" language to "suggestive, non-tobacco-specific," and carry forward the adiposity and gene-size-artifact alternatives as explicitly unresolved.
