## Genes marked CONTESTED by red-team-skeptic

### HTRA1 — CONTESTED
Verbatim skeptic reasoning: "The credible set's top variants are non-coding, MODIFIER-impact,
intronic/downstream — annotation to HTRA1 reflects nearest-gene/VEP convention, not functional
attribution, in a locus with three physically overlapping genes (HTRA1, ARMS2, PLEKHA1) in tight
LD. Critically, the one place this exact three-gene ambiguity has been substantively adjudicated
in the literature — AMD — the resolution has trended *away* from HTRA1: Jakobsdottir 2005 favored
PLEKHA1/ARMS2 over HTRA1 coding variants, and later mechanistic work (Fritsche 2008, Kanda 2007,
both already cited by the researcher for ARMS2) argues the risk haplotype affects ARMS2 expression
specifically, not HTRA1 — undermining rather than supporting 'HTRA1 is the best-characterized gene
in the region' as a reason to prioritize it. Layered on top: no lung-specific mechanistic
literature exists at all (all ECM/TGF-β evidence is cerebral small-vessel disease; all
macrophage-polarization evidence is retina/choroid); the OT macrophage eQTL colocalizes with
height, not COPD/FEV1-FVC; two earlier lung-function GWAS explicitly exclude this locus; and the
only COPD-tissue paper (Zuo 2021) is a correlative 18-subject WGCNA study, not genetic or
functional evidence. The researcher's own hedge ('leans toward caution... not citable') is
honest, but the one-line 'highest-PIP gene at the locus' framing risks being read by
synthesis-lead as stronger than it is. This is a case where absence-of-lung-evidence plus a
genuinely contradictory precedent (AMD field explicitly disfavoring HTRA1 vs. its neighbors)
rises above ordinary 'weak evidence.'
**Verdict: CONTESTED** — not because a COPD-specific paper argues against HTRA1 (none exists),
but because the AMD literature that established this locus's biology explicitly argues the
regulatory effect belongs to ARMS2, not HTRA1, directly undercutting the nearest-gene/highest-PIP-
non-coding-variant inference used to prioritize HTRA1 over its neighbors at this locus."

### PLEKHA1 — WEAKENED
Verbatim: "PIP ~100-fold lower than the top HTRA1 variants — the statistical support within this
COPD credible set is weak on its face. It does have real causal precedent at this exact LD block
for AMD (Jakobsdottir 2005), but that precedent is itself contested by later ARMS2-favoring work,
and zero COPD/FEV1/FVC literature exists for PLEKHA1 anywhere. Notably, PLEKHA1 is the only one
of the three genes with a lung-tissue molecular QTL credible set, and that credible set's
colocalization was reviewed against 107 partner traits with none reaching COPD/FEV1-FVC — a
genuine (if OT-context, non-literature) negative test that argues against a lung mechanism for
this gene specifically.
**Verdict: WEAKENED** — the researcher correctly reports near-total absence of evidence, but the
low-PIP inclusion in the candidate list, combined with a real (if disease-mismatched)
colocalization test against COPD/FEV1-FVC coming up empty, means PLEKHA1 should be weighted lower
than its bare presence in the credible set might imply."

### PPAPDC1A/PLPP4 — WEAKENED
Verbatim: "No credible-set variant annotated to it; PoPS prioritization is a polygenic-similarity
method, not a fine-mapping/proximity signal, and can prioritize genes with no direct genetic
evidence at the locus. Zero OT evidence, zero relevant literature (only unrelated cancer biology).
**Verdict: WEAKENED** — effectively no support beyond a similarity-based prioritization
algorithm; should not be treated as a serious causal candidate without independent confirmation."

### ARMS2 — WEAKENED
Verbatim: "Excluded from the credible set entirely, but this may be an artifact of variant-to-gene
annotation rather than genuine absence of a regulatory relationship: ARMS2 is a small,
primate-specific gene physically nested within/adjacent to HTRA1's introns, and standard
VEP-style annotation pipelines are known (per the AMD literature) to under-annotate variants to
ARMS2 in favor of the larger overlapping HTRA1 transcript, even when the functional/expression
effect is on ARMS2. No COPD-specific evidence exists for ARMS2 either (confirmed independently).
This is a case where a gene lacking direct credible-set annotation may still deserve more
attention than it received, purely because of a known annotation-bias phenomenon documented in
the AMD literature.
**Verdict: WEAKENED** — under-explored rather than actively refuted; flag for synthesis-lead that
the 'HTRA1 has the credible-set annotation, ARMS2 doesn't' framing may reflect annotation-tool
limitations rather than biological reality, and that no gene at this locus (HTRA1, ARMS2, or
PLEKHA1) has direct COPD-specific support to adjudicate between them."

### DMBT1 — HOLDS (correctly deprioritized)
Verbatim: "Outside the credible set, ~23kb from nearest signal, and the one relevant paper (Weste
2022) is a direct negative finding (studied COPD bronchial secretions, did not find DMBT1
involvement). The researcher's implicit conclusion (not a plausible candidate) is well supported
by its own negative citation.
**Verdict: HOLDS** — correctly deprioritized, no overreach."

### BTBD16 — HOLDS (correctly deprioritized)
Verbatim: "Mostly outside the window, no credible-set proximity, no literature at all. Correctly
excluded.
**Verdict: HOLDS** — correctly deprioritized, no overreach."

## Structural issues flagged by red-team-skeptic (locus-wide, not gene-specific)

**(a) Locus-membership uncertainty.** Verbatim: "The researcher could not confirm that this
region is actually among Sakornsakolpat 2019's 82 reported COPD loci or Shrine 2019's 279
lung-function signals — the only evidence is the negative inference from Open Targets' own
missing credible set. If this signal isn't genuinely genome-wide-significant in either source
GWAS, the entire 'which gene is causal' exercise may be moot. Flag for synthesis-lead: confirm
locus membership in the source GWAS tables before treating this as an established COPD hit."

**(b) LD-block gene assignment is unresolved even for the disease this locus is famous for.**
The skeptic searched independently for COPD-specific ARMS2/HTRA1/PLEKHA1 causal-gene literature,
COPD-AMD shared genetic architecture, and smoking-GWAS membership, finding: no paper anywhere
argues for or against a specific gene at this locus for COPD specifically (the causal-gene debate
that exists is entirely about AMD); a COPD→AMD comorbidity study (Bair et al. 2021, PMID
34302051, VERIFIED) that is epidemiological, not genetic-pleiotropy, evidence and should not be
used to argue for shared germline causality at this locus; and a book chapter (Kortvely &
Ueffing 2016, PMID 26427389, VERIFIED) making explicit that AMD risk haplotypes at 10q26 affect
ARMS2 expression specifically, not HTRA1 — a point the original researcher's write-up did not
surface despite already citing the underlying primary papers (Fritsche 2008, Kanda 2007) for
ARMS2. On smoking-mediation: neither the GSCAN paper nor further searches could confirm or deny
whether this locus is a smoking-behavior locus — an unresolved evidence gap, not a resolved
negative finding.

## Bottom line for reviewers

No citation was rejected (RETRACTED/NOT_FOUND/CLAIM_MISMATCH) at this locus — the entire
evidentiary complication here is that the *strongest* verified literature (the AMD mechanistic
and causal-gene literature) is for a different disease at the same genomic locus, and it does not
unambiguously support HTRA1 over its neighbors ARMS2 and PLEKHA1. Treat the "Prioritized gene"
call in the main evidence table as provisional pending an LD lookup between this locus's lead
variant and the classic AMD variants, and pending confirmation of whether this locus is genuinely
genome-wide-significant in the published COPD/lung-function GWAS.
