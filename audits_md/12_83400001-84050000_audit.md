### TMTC2 — red-team-skeptic verdict: WEAKENED (verbatim reasoning)

> This is a textbook "nearest-gene-by-elimination" call, and the researcher's own findings
> supply most of the ammunition against it. TMTC2 sits 265-950 kb outside the actual
> credible-set window; the window itself is a gene desert containing only lncRNAs. TMTC2 is
> named as candidate not because any functional, coding, or regulatory evidence ties it to
> this specific credible set, but because it is the only protein-coding gene within 1.5 Mb —
> that is a distance-based default, not a mechanistic finding, and Open Targets' L2G
> algorithm is itself distance-weighted, so "TMTC2 is the only gene with any L2G signal" is
> partly circular (there's no competing protein-coding gene for L2G to assign probability
> mass to).
>
> The strongest genuine evidence here is the FEV1/FVC colocalization (h4 > 0.9), which under
> prior #2 is real and should be credited — this locus almost certainly harbors a genuine
> pulmonary-function-associated variant. But h4 > 0.9 colocalization tells you the *locus*
> affects lung function; it does not tell you *TMTC2* is the mediating gene, especially
> when: zero lung eQTL/sQTL exists for TMTC2 in GTEx v8 despite robust thyroid/liver eQTLs
> (actively arguing against a lung-tissue regulatory mechanism), the gene's only validated
> human disease phenotype (autosomal-dominant hearing loss) operates through an unrelated
> coding variant, and its molecular function (ER O-mannosyltransferase on
> cadherins/protocadherins) has no demonstrated lung-remodeling role. The PM2.5-methylation
> EWAS is non-genetic, correlative, potentially reflects reverse causation from exposure,
> and its own pathway enrichment points away from lung biology (neurodegenerative/cancer
> pathways) — this should not be treated as reinforcing the genetic signal.
>
> On smoking-mediation (prior #1): the moderate L2G for smoking-initiation/cessation
> (~0.57) and only-moderate colocalization between smoking traits and FEV1/FVC (h4
> ~0.49-0.69, well below the FEV1-FVC intra-trait h4>0.9) means the researcher is correct
> that this doesn't look like a clean smoking-behavior-only signal — but it is also nowhere
> near clean enough to declare a "direct lung mechanism" independent of smoking. Given the
> locus's broader phenome-wide pattern — educational attainment, occupational prestige,
> walking pace, frailty, age at first birth/sex, alongside CRP/WBC/lipids/eGFR/glaucoma —
> this reads much more like a general socioeconomic/behavioral/constitutional pleiotropy hub
> than a lung-specific locus. Educational attainment and occupational-prestige
> colocalization in UK Biobank is a well-known red flag for residual confounding
> (assortative mating, social stratification, healthcare-seeking behavior, and
> smoking-correlated SES all travel together), and this pattern is exactly the kind of
> confounded architecture prior #1 warns about. The cross-species cattle body-size/
> conformation signal reinforces that TMTC2 (or this haplotype) behaves like a generic
> growth/constitutional locus rather than a lung-specific one.
>
> The absence of any PubMed-indexed COPD or spirometry GWAS/fine-mapping paper naming TMTC2
> is flagged by the researcher as "a search gap, not necessarily true absence" — I'd push
> back on how much benefit of the doubt that deserves. Large spirometry GWAS (e.g., Shrine
> et al. 2019, Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019) report gene-desert loci by lead-SNP position and
> their own nearest-gene assignment, which may not even be "TMTC2" in their supplementary
> tables — a keyword search on "TMTC2" would systematically miss this. This is a genuine
> thoroughness gap: I recommend synthesis-lead flag this locus for a position-based (not
> gene-name-based) re-check of the major published spirometry/COPD GWAS supplementary loci
> tables at chr12:83.4-84.4 Mb, since gene-name search is known to fail at gene-desert loci
> precisely like this one.
>
> Verdict: WEAKENED. The locus-level spirometry association (FEV1/FVC h4>0.9) is credible
> and should be retained as evidence that *something* at this locus affects lung function,
> but the specific attribution to TMTC2 overreaches what the evidence supports — it is a
> distance-based default candidate with no lung eQTL, no lung-specific mechanism, only
> moderate (not high) L2G, ambiguous smoking-mediation, and a phenome-wide pleiotropy
> signature more consistent with a generic constitutional/behavioral hub than a
> COPD-specific pulmonary mechanism. I do not find this rises to CONTESTED, because no
> source actively argues against TMTC2 or convincingly substitutes a named rival gene — but
> the case is borderline, and calling TMTC2 "the candidate gene" for this locus without
> heavy hedging would be overclaiming.

### SLC6A15 — mentioned but not substantively researched (verbatim reasoning)

> Mentioned only in passing as flanking the atopic-march (pediatric eczema-to-asthma)
> signal rs993226, which itself does not identify a causal gene. SLC6A15 encodes a
> neuronal amino-acid transporter previously linked to hippocampal volume/major depressive
> disorder in other GWAS — no more lung-specific than TMTC2, and (per the researcher's own
> statement that TMTC2 is "the only gene with any Open Targets L2G signal") apparently
> carries no L2G support at this locus either. It does not deserve promotion above TMTC2 on
> current evidence, but the researcher's writeup should explicitly state *why* it was
> excluded (checked and found L2G-null) rather than leaving it as an unexplained aside —
> this is a minor thoroughness gap, not a case for reprioritization. I would not assign it a
> verdict of its own; it was not substantively evaluated and shouldn't be silently dropped,
> but nor should it be elevated on the thin thread of a phenotypically distinct (pediatric
> atopic disease vs. adult smoking-related COPD) signal that doesn't name a gene at all.

## Process/tooling limitations affecting this locus's evidence base (disclosed for transparency)

1. **Researcher's WebSearch budget was exhausted** before locus 12_83400001-84050000 was
   reached in the broader session; all researcher findings for this locus rely on PubMed,
   the Ensembl REST API, and the GTEx Portal API (via WebFetch) only — WebSearch could not
   be used as a fallback. The researcher explicitly flagged this gap.
2. **bioRxiv/medRxiv preprint search** was not usable for this locus because the
   `search_preprints` tool only supports date-range/category filtering, not keyword search;
   no TMTC2-specific preprint literature was checked.
3. **Red-team-skeptic had no filesystem access** in this dispatch and could not independently
   open the researcher's or orchestrator's checkpoint files, nor run its own web-search
   contradiction-checks (its WebSearch budget was also exhausted). Its critique above was
   produced entirely from a detailed itemized summary of the researcher's and orchestrator's
   findings supplied directly in its dispatch prompt (verified by the orchestrator against
   the underlying checkpoint files before dispatch). The skeptic itself flagged this
   limitation and recommended synthesis-lead independently confirm its reasoning against the
   source files — this has been done by the orchestrator in assembling this audit and the
   main evidence table.
4. **No Human Lung Cell Atlas / LungMAP single-cell lookup** was performed for TMTC2
   expression, flagged by the researcher as an open, unexplored (not completed-negative)
   avenue.
5. **No dedicated large spirometry/COPD GWAS meta-analysis paper (e.g., Shrine et al.
   2019-type)** was identified and checked by position (chr12:83.4-84.4 Mb) against its own
   nearest-gene/candidate-gene call for this locus — only gene-name keyword search was used,
   which the red-team-skeptic argues is likely to miss gene-desert loci like this one. This
   remains an open item for the study team.
