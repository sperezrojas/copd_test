## Flagged citation: Meyer et al. 2017 ARDS conference abstract (DNAJC1) — VERIFIED but downgraded

**Verdict: VERIFIED**, but treat with caution — reproduced here in full because the
context matters more than the bare verdict:

The citation itself checks out: existence confirmed (DOI resolves, Oxford Academic host),
no retraction, and the claim as stated is accurately quoted — the abstract does report SNP
rs10764330 in DNAJC1 as the top hit for sepsis-associated ARDS risk (OR 0.66, 95% CI
0.55-0.79, p=9.24x10^-6) in European- and African-ancestry subgroups.

However, two things weaken how much this should count as supporting DNAJC1 at the COPD
locus under review:
1. **It is a conference abstract, never confirmed as a full peer-reviewed manuscript** on
   this specific finding — lower evidentiary tier by design of the citation-verifier's own
   protocol notes.
2. **Citation-verifier found, during the verification pass (not part of the original
   researcher output, surfaced incidentally), that the same research group appears to have
   published a full peer-reviewed follow-up multi-ancestry GWAS of the same sepsis-associated
   ARDS phenotype** — Guillen-Guio et al. 2020, Lancet Respiratory Medicine, PMID 31982041,
   DOI 10.1016/S2213-2600(19)30368-6 — and in that later, full study, the gene
   prioritized as the primary ARDS-associated locus was **FLT1/VEGFR-1, not DNAJC1**. This
   was not independently re-verified line-by-line by citation-verifier (it fell outside the
   scope of what was dispatched), but is flagged because it is plausible, if unconfirmed,
   evidence that the 2017 DNAJC1 finding was a preliminary/underpowered signal that did not
   hold up once the full study was completed. Also note ARDS (acute lung injury) is
   mechanistically distinct from chronic COPD airflow obstruction — even a fully replicated
   DNAJC1-ARDS association would not directly transfer to COPD causal-gene status without
   further work.

Also note a minor attribution correction: the red-team-skeptic's original surfaced citation
misattributed first authorship to "Sipe" — citation-verifier corrected this to Nuala J.
Meyer (verified via the resolved abstract's author list).

## Genes/inferences marked WEAKENED by red-team-skeptic (verbatim reasoning)

Full critique text is in `evidence_tables/_status/10_21300001-22050000/critique.md`. Key
excerpts:

### CASC10 / MIR1915HG — WEAKENED

> "This is the locus's real interpretive crux and the researcher handles it about as
> honestly as the evidence allows: zero OT genetic-association evidence for any disease
> despite carrying the plurality of PIP mass is a striking negative, and the researcher
> explicitly raises (without being able to confirm) the alternative hypothesis that the
> ABC-assigned enhancer may regulate a neighboring gene's promoter rather than reflecting
> MIR1915HG's own causal product... Because ABC-based gene assignment is well known in the
> field to sometimes mis-assign an enhancer to the nearest gene body rather than its true
> regulatory target, and because there is literally no independent genetic-association
> evidence to check that assignment against, the case that MIR1915HG itself (as opposed to
> a neighbor whose expression it may regulate) is the causal gene is weaker than the raw
> PIP-mass figure alone would suggest to a reader who didn't read the caveat carefully."
>
> **Verdict: WEAKENED** — not because the researcher overclaimed (they didn't; the
> ambiguity is explicitly flagged), but because a reader relying on "carries ~0.397 of the
> PIP mass, ABC-prioritized" as a headline could easily overweight this gene without
> registering that this PIP mass is agnostic as to which downstream gene the regulatory
> element actually acts on.

### DNAJC1 — WEAKENED

> "1. Unverified primary source. The OT FEV1 score of 0.811 could not be traced to a
> primary GWAS/fine-mapping paper... this figure should be treated as provisional, not as
> independent corroborating evidence of comparable weight to a verified literature
> citation.
>
> 2. Physical/statistical disconnection from the credible set. Cumulative PIP across all 11
> DNAJC1-annotated variants is ~0.008, and they sit 300-400kb from the lead variant and the
> high-PIP flanking mass. This is a substantial red flag for treating DNAJC1 as the same
> causal signal as rs12770228/MIR1915HG rather than a second, statistically independent
> (and much weaker) signal that happens to fall inside the same wide window...
>
> 3. All immune-mechanism evidence is cross-tissue. Thyroid Treg infiltration, glioblastoma
> macrophage/ECM remodeling, periodontal mucosal methylation — none lung tissue, none
> COPD-relevant disease context...
>
> Given (1)-(3), plus the smoking-initiation signal being DNAJC1's 4th-highest broad OT
> disease association (unaddressed in the narrative)... I think 'warrants serious
> consideration as an alternative candidate' overstates the current evidentiary basis."
>
> **Verdict: WEAKENED** — the individual facts cited are real and verified, but the
> synthesis into "serious alternative candidate" outruns what an unverified
> quantitative-trait score, a statistically disconnected credible-set signal, and
> exclusively cross-tissue immune mechanism data can support. Recommend synthesis-lead
> treat DNAJC1 as a flagged open question requiring primary-source tracing (not a competing
> candidate on par with MLLT10/MIR1915HG)...

## Unresolved lead not sent for formal citation verification

The researcher speculated, without making a discrete attributable claim, that the Open
Targets FEV1 genetic-association score for DNAJC1 might derive from "one of the large
multi-ancestry lung-function GWAS meta-analyses (e.g., Shrine et al. 2023 Nat Genet, PMID
37749248)" but explicitly could not confirm DNAJC1-specific locus-level results in the text
reviewed. This was not sent to citation-verifier because there is no discrete claim to
check correspondence against (only "a paper like this might be the source"). This remains
an open item: identifying the actual primary GWAS/credible-set study underlying Open
Targets' DNAJC1-FEV1 score would materially change how much weight that finding deserves,
and is flagged here as follow-up work for the study team rather than resolved.
