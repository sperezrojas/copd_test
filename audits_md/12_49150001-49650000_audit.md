Critique follows, gene by gene.

---

**KCNH3**
The researcher's own hedge ("cannot be proven definitively") is appropriate, but the
inferential chain still has a hole: absence of pulmonary literature is not evidence *for*
a smoking-mediated mechanism, it is simply absence of evidence for a pulmonary mechanism.
These are not the same claim, and the write-up drifts toward treating them as
interchangeable. Ion channels expressed predominantly in CNS tissue by current annotation
can still have low-abundance, unstudied roles in airway smooth muscle, pulmonary
neuroendocrine cells, or vagal afferents innervating the lung — this is a real category of
channel biology (c.f. other Kv/Nav channels with dual CNS/airway roles) and simply hasn't
been looked for here. So the "smoking mediation" framing is a plausible hypothesis, well-
motivated by the OT aggregate score contrast (FEV1 0.30 vs smoking-initiation 0.55, both
non-trivial, smoking somewhat higher) and by GSCAN being cited — but the specific claim
that a 12q13/KCNH3 smoking-initiation GWAS hit exists is *not* confirmed (researcher
admits this, citation-verifier could not confirm it from supplementary tables). That is a
meaningful gap: the smoking-mediation hypothesis is currently supported only by (a)
absence of lung literature and (b) an OT aggregate score that itself may be driven by the
same neurobiology literature being reviewed here (circularity risk — OT's smoking-
initiation score for KCNH3 may derive from the same de novo neurodevelopmental/CNS papers
already cited, not from an independent smoking GWAS locus). I'd call this a case of
**underclaiming on the "unstudied peripheral role" alternative** and **mild overclaiming
on "smoking mediation"** being treated as the leading explanation rather than one of two
open possibilities (unstudied peripheral pulmonary role vs. smoking neurobiology) that
current evidence cannot adjudicate between. The FEV1 OT score of 0.30 (not zero) is also
somewhat in tension with "no lung literature anywhere" and deserved more direct
engagement — it wasn't sourced to any specific paper found by the researcher, which itself
is worth flagging as unexplained.
**WEAKENED** — real evidence (9 verified citations, all CNS/neuronal), but the leap to
"consistent with smoking-mediation" is one of two live hypotheses, not the favored one;
the researcher should present it as such rather than as the more probable interpretation,
and should reconcile the unexplained OT FEV1=0.30 signal.

**KMT2D**
This is the most important overreach in the packet. The chain of inference is: (1) gene
lies *outside* the ABF credible set — i.e., the statistical fine-mapping does not
implicate it at this locus at all, only MAGMA's gene-based test does, which is well known
to be vulnerable to LD/gene-density inflation, especially for a large gene like KMT2D; (2)
the mechanistic support is a single bioRxiv preprint, NOT peer reviewed, in a knock-in
mouse model of a catalytically-dead allele (a near-null, developmental, homozygous-type
perturbation) showing lung sacculation defects; (3) the human evidence is case
reports/small case series in Kabuki syndrome, a rare, highly penetrant monogenic disorder
caused by (typically) loss-of-function KMT2D variants with a completely different genetic
architecture (large-effect, rare, developmental-syndrome-causing) than a common noncoding
GWAS variant with a modest odds ratio for adult-onset COPD. Rare monogenic-syndrome organ
phenotypes are a weak prior for common-variant regulatory effects on the same gene —
dosage, developmental timing, and effect size are all qualitatively different, and this is
a well-recognized pitfall (the "Mendelian gene = GWAS gene" fallacy) that the researcher
does not explicitly name, even though it correctly notes the credible-set/MAGMA
discrepancy. The researcher's own framing ("genuinely compelling... independent
developmental lung biology support") risks reading as an endorsement of KMT2D's candidacy
when the correct framing is: interesting biology, essentially no statistical-genetic
support at this specific locus, and a category error risk in extrapolating from Kabuki
syndrome/preprint knock-in mice to this common variant. This needs to be stated far more
bluntly to synthesis lead.
**WEAKENED** — the developmental lung biology is real and worth reporting, but (a) the
preprint is unreviewed and should be labeled as such every time it's invoked, not just
once, (b) the case-report/Kabuki evidence is a rare-variant/monogenic-syndrome analogy
that does not straightforwardly transfer to common-variant GWAS causality, and (c) the
gene sits outside the credible set — this should be foregrounded as the single most
important caveat on KMT2D, not a secondary note.

**DNAJC22**
I agree this is being underweighted relative to its statistical genetics. It carries the
second-highest-PIP variant at the entire locus (0.055, roughly 15% of KCNH3-lead's PIP but
still second overall out of >15 genes' worth of variants), yet the write-up treats
"unstudied" as functionally equivalent to "no evidence for candidacy," when it should be
treated as a **known unknown** — a gene where the prior from fine-mapping is nontrivial
but literature simply hasn't caught up (DNAJC proteins are a large, functionally diverse
Hsp40 co-chaperone family; a DNAJC being "unstudied" is common and uninformative about
biological plausibility one way or the other). The researcher's own phrase ("genuine
evidence gap rather than a negative finding") is exactly right, but downstream synthesis
should not let the qualitative absence-of-papers outcome cause DNAJC22 to be effectively
dropped from consideration relative to genes like SPATS2 or MCRS1 that have *more*
literature but literature that is entirely irrelevant (cancer biology) or non-replicated.
On a like-for-like basis, "unstudied, PIP 0.055" arguably deserves comparable or greater
weight than "extensively studied but always in oncology contexts having nothing to do
with lung, PIP <0.03" (SPATS2) or "single non-replicated candidate-gene mining study, PIP
<0.01" (MCRS1). Flag for synthesis lead: DNAJC22 should not be ranked below SPATS2/MCRS1
purely because it has fewer papers.
**HOLDS** as a fair characterization of the literature search (the search was thorough —
macaque cold-adaptation and PD burden hits are clearly tangential and correctly
characterized), but the *relative prioritization* implied by the write-up (treated as one
of the weaker candidates) is not well justified given its PIP rank; this is a weighting
issue for synthesis, not a citation-accuracy issue.

**SPATS2**
The "LD spillover from KCNH3" hypothesis is reasonable and appropriately hedged as a
hypothesis rather than a conclusion. It's consistent with the structural facts: SPATS2
sits immediately adjacent to KCNH3, has a large number of variants but none individually
compelling (PIP ceiling ~0.028, i.e., an order of magnitude below the KCNH3 lead), all its
direct literature is orthogonal (HCC/immune-infiltration-in-tumor biology, not lung
immune biology — worth noting explicitly that "immune infiltration" in the Lin 2023 HCC
paper is tumor-microenvironment immunology and should not be casually conflated with
COPD-relevant pulmonary immunology even though both use the word "immune"), and the OT
smoking-initiation-only signal (0.31, no FEV1/COPD) is consistent with a
secondary/correlated signal rather than an independent causal pulmonary mechanism. This is
a sound example of appropriately flagged uncertainty rather than overclaiming.
**HOLDS**.

**MCRS1**
Appropriately downweighted, and the researcher did good diligence here — explicitly
catching that one candidate citation (Xu 2012) is about the *paralog* MCRS2, not MCRS1,
which is exactly the kind of gene-identity confusion this review process exists to catch,
and correctly identifying that the sole COPD-specific citation (Vishweswaraiah 2018) is a
small, non-GWAS, data-mining candidate-gene study flagging three genes (MCRS1, HJURP,
TLR8) as "novel" with no independent replication cited. The LoF-constraint statistic
(pLI-like 0.97) is a genome-wide essentiality metric, not a lung-specificity metric, and
the researcher correctly declines to let it substitute for mechanistic or genetic
evidence. This is a case where OT's "literature COPD score" could mislead a naive reader,
and the researcher's skepticism is warranted.
**HOLDS**.

**PRKAG1 / PRPF40B**
Both correctly characterized as having essentially no locus-relevant evidence beyond
their respective prioritization-method scores (PoPS, ABC), with only tangential, non-lung
tangential literature found. No overclaiming here — these read as honest null results.
**HOLDS** for both.

**TUBA1C / PRPH / FAM186B / TROAP-AS1 / HIGD1AP9**
All low-PIP, weak-or-absent literature, appropriately characterized as weak candidates
without overreach. TUBA1C's ependymal-cilia (brain ventricle) citation is correctly
distinguished from airway cilia, which matters — a careless synthesis could conflate
"cilia gene" with "airway cilia gene," and the researcher avoided that trap. No concerns.
**HOLDS** for all five.

**Point 7 — does fine-mapping alone favor KCNH3 regardless of the literature gap?**
Yes, on the statistical-genetics axis alone, KCNH3 is clearly favored: >85% of
credible-set PIP mass sits in two KCNH3 intronic variants (0.358 + 0.020), versus
DNAJC22's single 0.055 variant and SPATS2's PIP mass diffused across dozens of variants
each <0.028 (a pattern more consistent with either non-causal LD tagging or a genuinely
small-effect/omnigenic contribution than a single strong causal variant). Fine-mapping
PIP concentration is itself informative independent of literature — a single dominant
high-PIP variant in one gene's intron is the kind of pattern fine-mapping is designed to
surface as the most probable causal gene at a locus, all else equal. However, "all else"
is not equal here: KCNH3's PIP dominance says only that *a KCNH3 intronic/regulatory
variant is most likely the causal variant at this locus statistically* — it says nothing
about *which trait's biology* that variant acts through. The complete absence of
pulmonary/airway literature for KCNH3, despite it being a reasonably well-studied channel
gene (9 verified citations exist, none pulmonary), is a real, not merely an
absence-of-search, gap — this is different from DNAJC22, which is absent from the
literature because almost nothing has been published on it at all. For KCNH3, biology
*has* been actively characterized (epilepsy, circadian SCN neurons, neurodevelopmental
disease, behavioral pharmacology) and lung/airway biology never surfaces, which is
modestly stronger negative evidence against a direct pulmonary mechanism than DNAJC22's
blank slate. Net: fine-mapping strongly favors KCNH3 as the causal gene at this locus for
*some* trait; it does not by itself resolve whether that trait-mechanism is pulmonary or
neurobehavioral/smoking-related, and the balance of literature (extensive neuronal
characterization, zero pulmonary hits, OT smoking score > OT FEV1 score) leans toward the
latter without fully excluding an unstudied peripheral pulmonary role. Synthesis lead
should present this as: statistically the strongest candidate gene at the locus, but
mechanistically ambiguous between direct pulmonary biology and smoking-behavior mediation,
with current literature leaning (not proving) toward the latter.

## Gene marked CONTESTED (addendum)

**TUBA1A's immune-domain sub-claim is marked CONTESTED.** Four citations (Saini 2011 PMID
21414808, Weber 2012 PMID 22289485, Xu 2016 PMID 26844425, Baskaran 2014 PMID 24906794)
describe Th17-mediated autoimmunity against a lung self-antigen named "K-alpha1-tubulin"
in post-lung-transplant bronchiolitis obliterans syndrome, including one paper (Baskaran
2014) directly associating clearance of this antibody with reduced FEV1 decline. This is
the single most COPD-mechanistically-adjacent evidence in the entire TUBA1A packet.
However:

- The original TUBA1A citation-verifier pass could not determine, from the four papers
  themselves or from a broader literature search, which of the nine human alpha-tubulin
  genes (TUBA1A, TUBA1B, TUBA1C, TUBA4A, TUBA8, etc.) encodes "K-alpha1-tubulin." All four
  papers name the antigen historically, without a modern gene symbol or accession.
- Red-team-skeptic, reviewing this finding, raised a specific (self-flagged as
  unconfirmed) hypothesis: that "K-ALPHA-1" may be the historical alias for **TUBA1B**, not
  TUBA1A — based on the skeptic's recollection of 1980s-era tubulin gene-family
  nomenclature, in which "Kα1" denoted a broadly/ubiquitously-expressed alpha-tubulin
  isotype, as distinct from the more neuron-restricted TUBA1A isotype implicated in
  lissencephaly. The skeptic could not confirm this before their own search budget ran out
  and explicitly flagged it as a high-priority open item requiring dedicated follow-up.
- The orchestrator dispatched a **dedicated follow-up citation-verifier pass** specifically
  to resolve this question (NCBI Gene/HGNC/UniProt alias records; the original
  tubulin-nomenclature/cloning literature; the lung-transplant papers' own methods
  sections). After 45 tool uses, this follow-up pass reached the same conclusion as the
  first: **the gene-identity mapping remains genuinely unresolved in the literature
  itself** — not a search-effort shortfall likely to be closed by more querying. The
  primary lung-transplant papers cite the antigen by historical name and, in one case, a
  commercial antibody source (Chemicon), never by modern gene symbol or accession; tracing
  the original 1980s nomenclature papers that assigned the "K-alpha-1" name to a specific
  locus was not achievable with the tools available.

**Net verdict (red-team-skeptic, adopted for the final table): these four citations are
retained as VERIFIED for existence/accuracy of what they report, but are marked CONTESTED
for their applicability to TUBA1A specifically, and must be treated as generic
alpha-tubulin/airway-autoimmunity evidence of unconfirmed paralog specificity — not as
confirmed TUBA1A-specific mechanistic evidence — until the gene identity question is
resolved (e.g. by direct correspondence with the original research group, or by locating
the original tubulin-nomenclature cloning papers).** This is the single largest open
evidentiary uncertainty in the TUBA1A packet and the reason TUBA1A's overall verdict is
"WEAKENED, bordering on CONTESTED for its single strongest sub-claim" rather than a
cleaner WEAKENED-only call.

## Full red-team-skeptic critique (verbatim, TUBA1A addendum)

**Note on search limitations (from red-team-skeptic, this pass):** I attempted two
targeted searches — (1) to independently check whether "K-alpha1-tubulin" (Kα1T) can be
mapped to a specific alpha-tubulin gene, and (2) to check for any missed TUBA1A-COPD/
spirometry/smoking literature — but the web search budget for this session was exhausted
before either returned results. The critique below is based on the researcher's reported
findings plus reasoning/domain knowledge, with one hypothesis explicitly flagged as
**UNVERIFIED** rather than silently assumed (subsequently investigated by a dedicated
follow-up citation-verifier pass — see above).

### 1. Statistical genetics: no independent signal, weaker than KMT2D's positional case
TUBA1A has zero credible-set variants (nearest is ~17.5kb away, in TUBA1C), zero
GWAS/TWAS/MR/coloc literature, and zero OT associations across COPD, FEV1, FEV1/FVC,
smoking initiation, and nicotine dependence — a genuinely null profile, not just an
unremarkable one. Its candidacy is 100% a MAGMA/PoPS positional artifact of a
build-correction, i.e. one rung below even KMT2D (which at least sits in the same general
evidentiary tier but has some, if weak, human Mendelian-disease literature engaging with
the same biology repeatedly). This is the weakest statistical-genetics case of any
candidate gene reviewed at this locus so far.

### 2. Mendelian-architecture mismatch — the KMT2D fallacy, but sharper
TUBA1A is missense-depleted far beyond background (mis-z 8.75, oe=0.031) and near-fully
LoF-intolerant (pLI-like 0.9998) — the textbook signature of a dominant-negative,
dosage-sensitive neurodevelopmental disease gene where de novo coding mutations cause
lissencephaly/tubulinopathy. This mutational mechanism (rare de novo missense disrupting
microtubule polymerization in migrating neurons) has no obvious route to producing a
common noncoding variant with a modest population-level effect on COPD risk. This is the
same "Mendelian gene ≠ GWAS gene" concern flagged for KMT2D in the original review, but the
constraint metrics here are more extreme, and unlike KMT2D there isn't even a positional or
credible-set anchor to fall back on — making this mismatch more, not less, concerning for
TUBA1A.

### 3. Structural/developmental domain: appropriately hedged, but thin
Credit to the researcher: every developmental citation was framed with real caution
(Saidin's respiratory insufficiency correctly labeled a secondary hypotonia/brainstem
complication, not primary lung pathology; Proepper 2026's n=1 TUBA1A patient explicitly
separated from the LIS1/DCX subgroup percentages; Liu 2026 correctly notes TUBB4B, not
TUBA1A, is functionally dominant in ependymal cilia; Lin 2026 correctly framed as
pathway-adjacent, not functionally interchangeable). No overclaiming here — if anything
this is a model of correctly-hedged reporting. But stripped of the hedges, there is no
primary airway/lung-structural or ciliary phenotype directly attributable to TUBA1A
anywhere in the literature. Fioretto 2026 (lung-tissue proteomic co-expression in a
maternal-protein-restriction rat model) is the single citation placing TUBA1A expression
changes in lung tissue itself, and it's non-mechanistic, non-COPD, and confounded by
TUBA1A's near-ubiquitous cytoskeletal expression (the researcher's own caveat, which I
agree with).

### 4. Immune/inflammatory domain: this is where I'd push back hardest
The four transplant-rejection papers (Saini, Weber, Xu, Baskaran) describing Th17-mediated
anti-"K-alpha1-tubulin" autoimmunity in bronchiolitis obliterans are the most
mechanistically COPD-adjacent citations in the whole TUBA1A packet (airway fibrotic
remodeling, FEV1 decline) — and the citation-verifier already flagged that the antigen's
gene identity could not be confirmed as TUBA1A specifically.

I want to sharpen that caveat rather than just repeat it: to the best of my recollection,
**"K-ALPHA-1" is the historical/NCBI gene-alias name for *TUBA1B*, not TUBA1A** (the
ubiquitously-expressed alpha-tubulin isotype cloned and named "Kα1" in the 1980s
mouse/rat tubulin-gene literature, orthologous to human TUBA1B, as distinct from the more
neuron-restricted TUBA1A isotype whose mutations cause lissencephaly). If that's correct,
the entire immune-domain evidence base for TUBA1A is very likely misattributed and
actually belongs to a different paralog gene (TUBA1B) that isn't even on this locus's
candidate list. This is exactly the kind of tubulin-paralog confusion you'd expect given
nine near-identical human alpha-tubulin genes and antibody/ELISA reagents named by
historical convention rather than by current HGNC symbol.

**[ORCHESTRATOR RESOLUTION, added after this critique was received]:** a dedicated
follow-up citation-verifier pass investigated this specific hypothesis and could NOT
confirm it — the TUBA1B hypothesis remains plausible but unconfirmed. See "Gene marked
CONTESTED" section above for full detail. This is treated as an open risk requiring the
downstream handling instruction given there, not as a refutation or a confirmation of
either the TUBA1A or TUBA1B identity.

### 5. No contradicting "TUBA1A is not it" paper — but not because anyone looked and it held up
I did not find (nor did the researcher report) a paper explicitly arguing against TUBA1A
at this locus — but that's unsurprising given TUBA1A was never previously proposed as a
COPD candidate for anyone to argue against. Absence of rebuttal literature here is not
exoneration; it reflects that TUBA1A has essentially never been tested against
COPD/spirometry phenotypes at all (zero OT rows, zero PubMed smoking hits). This is a case
of an evidence vacuum, not a case that survived scrutiny.

## Verdict for TUBA1A: WEAKENED, bordering on CONTESTED for its single strongest sub-claim

The gene-level inference "TUBA1A is a plausible causal COPD gene at this locus" overreaches
what the evidence supports. Every domain, taken on its own terms, is either (a) absent
(statgen, smoking), (b) rare-monogenic-disease evidence poorly suited to explain a
common-variant signal (structural/developmental), or (c) built on a lung-relevant antigen
whose gene identity as TUBA1A specifically has not been established and may well belong to
a different paralog (immune) — a sub-claim marked **CONTESTED pending verification** (now
investigated by a dedicated follow-up pass, which also could not resolve it — see above).
The researcher's own hedging throughout is appropriately conservative and their final
"weak candidate" framing is fair; the skeptic goes slightly further and calls this the
weakest candidate gene reviewed at this locus to date, weaker than KMT2D.

## Does TUBA1A change the locus's prioritized-gene call?

**No — KCNH3 remains the pick, and if anything TUBA1A's addition modestly reinforces
rather than weakens that call.**

Reasoning (red-team-skeptic, verbatim):
- TUBA1A carries zero credible-set variants and zero fine-mapping PIP mass. Its addition
  doesn't touch the core statistical fact that drove the original KCNH3 call: >85% of the
  credible-set PIP mass sits in KCNH3 introns. That fine-mapping dominance is completely
  unaffected by adding a positional-only neighbor gene.
- TUBA1A does not create a genuine three- or four-way ambiguity with KMT2D and DNAJC22.
  It's better understood as a second MAGMA/PoPS-type positional candidate in the same weak
  tier as KMT2D — but on the evidence assembled here, it's weaker than KMT2D (no
  independent disease-cohort literature repeatedly engaging the same gene the way
  Kabuki-syndrome/KMT2D literature does) and clearly weaker than DNAJC22 (which has actual
  credible-set statistical support, PIP=0.055, second-highest locus-wide). Adding a third
  weak positional candidate to a list that already had one doesn't change the ranking
  logic; it just means "the credible-set-external candidates are crowded and none of them
  individually rival KCNH3's fine-mapping evidence."
- TUBA1A does not address or resolve the original core ambiguity in the KCNH3 call —
  whether its mechanism is pulmonary or smoking-behavior/neurobehavioral. TUBA1A has no
  smoking literature at all, so it can't even serve as a tie-breaker on that axis; it's
  simply orthogonal to the question that was actually driving KCNH3's LOW confidence
  rating.
- One process point worth flagging to the synthesis-lead: TUBA1A surfaced only because of
  a genome-build correction in the MAGMA/PoPS merge. It's worth confirming whether that
  same build correction was checked against *all* other candidate genes at this locus
  (e.g., did PRKAG1, SPATS2, MCRS1, etc. also shift under the corrected build?), or
  whether TUBA1A was added in isolation. If the latter, that's a thoroughness gap — not
  because TUBA1A itself needed more searching, but because an unsystematic re-check of
  only one gene after a build fix risks missing whether other genes' positional scores
  also moved. **This process point is preserved here as an open item for the study team;
  it was not further investigated by the orchestrator, as it falls outside the scope of
  this locus's literature-review pipeline.**

**Net effect on confidence:** KCNH3 remains the prioritized gene, confidence still LOW
(unchanged — the mechanism-ambiguity that justified LOW originally is untouched by
TUBA1A), with KMT2D and DNAJC22 as runners-up as before, and TUBA1A now added as a third,
weaker positional runner-up whose immune-domain evidence carries a specific,
investigated-but-unresolved identity-verification risk (K-ALPHA-1 = TUBA1A vs TUBA1B) that
should be borne in mind by any reviewer relying on the transplant-rejection/Th17 literature
as TUBA1A evidence.

- **Red-team-skeptic's own note:** web search budget was exhausted before independently

  citation-verifier pass subsequently investigated the identity question (45 tool uses)
  and also could not resolve it — this appears to be a genuine gap in the published

- **Process point (unresolved, flagged for study team, not investigated further by this
