  supplementary gene/locus tables were not accessible to the researcher. This is flagged as

- **Internal-pipeline fine-mapping annotation** (all 26 credible-set variants mapping to

## 3. Red-team-skeptic critique — full verbatim output

**Disclosed limitation (critic's own words):** "all four of my independent

the broader 3q21 locus/alternative-genes query) failed to execute — the session's

**This means the critique below is an internal-consistency/reasoning review of the material
already supplied (researcher findings + citation-verifier verdicts + Open Targets context),
not a fresh independently-searched contradiction check.** This is a genuine, disclosed gap
in this locus's review and should be weighed accordingly by the study team — it is not
silently absorbed into the LOW-confidence prioritization call above, but is a contributing
reason for that low confidence.

### HACD2 — Verdict: WEAKENED

> The locus-assignment evidence for HACD2 is genuinely strong on its own terms (26/26
> credible-set variants, consistent OT L2G ranking 0.61-0.76 at this exact position). But
> that evidence is entirely about *which gene sits under the statistical signal*, not about
> *what the signal does biologically* — and on the latter question there is a total vacuum:
> zero COPD literature, zero FEV1/FVC literature, and the one disease-relevant claim in Open
> Targets (smoking initiation, score 0.465) could not be independently verified in primary
> literature at all.
>
> Two things deserve more skepticism than the researcher gave them:
> 1. The OT fine-mapping evidence being leaned on is for body-fat-percentage, not COPD.
>    Using "HACD2 is top L2G gene at this variant" as support for a COPD-prioritization
>    decision silently substitutes a different phenotype's fine-mapping for this one's...
>    Combined with HACD2's top disease hits being obesity/T2D/corneal-neovascularization/
>    sinusitis (not respiratory), a live alternative hypothesis is that this locus is
>    fundamentally an adiposity/metabolic locus, and its COPD-GWAS signal... could reflect
>    obesity's mechanical effects on spirometry (restrictive pattern lowering FVC,
>    confounding FEV1/FVC ratio) rather than any obstructive-lung mechanism.
> 2. gnomAD pLoF oe=0.41 with CI 0.20-0.94 is appropriately described as wide/uncertain by
>    the researcher — good, no overclaiming there.
>
> **Verdict: WEAKENED.** The gene-assignment (HACD2 = the gene under the credible set)
> holds up; the *inference that this represents lung-relevant COPD biology* does not — it
> rests on statistical fine-mapping borrowed from an unrelated trait (adiposity) plus an
> unverified single-database smoking-initiation flag. This is not CONTESTED in the sense of
> a paper actively arguing against HACD2 — no such paper was found or (given my search
> failure) searched for — but the case for a lung mechanism is essentially unsupported and
> an adiposity-mediated alternative is at least as plausible as anything currently on the
> table.

### MYLK — Verdict: CONTESTED

> MYLK has a real, verifiable literature base (asthma genetics, ALI/ARDS, sepsis, nmMLCK
> mouse knockouts, endothelial permeability biology), and the researcher did not overclaim
> this — it explicitly said "biologically plausible" rather than "causal for COPD," and
> explicitly separated asthma/acute-lung-injury biology from chronic-airflow-obstruction
> biology... none of the seven MYLK citations concern COPD or chronic airflow obstruction.
> They cluster in an acute/allergic inflammatory paradigm... that is mechanistically and
> temporally distinct from COPD's chronic, often smoking-driven, structural remodeling and
> neutrophilic/CD8+ inflammatory biology described in the priors. Generalizing "MYLK affects
> airway/vascular smooth muscle and permeability in asthma and ALI" to "MYLK is a COPD gene"
> would be an unwarranted leap.
>
> More importantly, there is a genuine internal contradiction the researcher surfaced but
> didn't push hard enough on: MYLK's nomination rests entirely on FLAMES/MAGMA/PoPS
> gene-level scores, with zero credible-set variant overlap and zero eQTL/Hi-C/ABC link to
> the HACD2 interval — and Open Targets' own independent fine-mapping/L2G data at this exact
> lead variant (across multiple traits) never ranks MYLK among candidates at all,
> consistently favoring HACD2/ADCY5/PDIA5/CCDC14/ROPN1 instead. This is a structural, not
> merely absence-of-evidence, contradiction... A known limitation of PoPS/MAGMA-style
> methods is that they can be biased toward genes with rich pre-existing disease/pathway
> annotation (MYLK has abundant asthma/ALI literature feeding into feature similarity) even
> when that gene is not the true regulatory target of the local variants — which is exactly
> the pattern here.
>
> **Verdict: CONTESTED.** The contradiction is that Open Targets' own independent
> fine-mapping/L2G evidence at the exact lead-variant position directly disfavors MYLK...
> while favoring HACD2 and several unexamined neighbors... Combine that with the fact that
> MYLK's entire literature support is for a different disease context (asthma/ALI, not
> COPD/FEV1), and the case for MYLK at this locus is weaker than the researcher's
> "biological plausibility" framing suggests, even though I could not run new searches to
> find an explicit "MYLK is not the causal gene at 3q21 for COPD" paper.

### Alternative genes at this locus (ADCY5, PDIA5, CCDC14, ROPN1) — not evaluated, flagged as coverage gap

> This is a real gap, and it exists independent of anything the researcher did wrong — these
> genes were never evaluated because they are outside the pipeline's internal
> candidate-gene list, yet Open Targets' own fine-mapping ranks them as L2G alternatives to
> HACD2 at the exact lead variant for the (admittedly different, adiposity-related) traits
> it has ingested. I was not able to search these genes at all (budget exhausted before I
> could query ADCY5/PDIA5/CCDC14/ROPN1 individually). ADCY5 in particular is worth flagging
> by name to synthesis-lead/researcher for a follow-up pass: from general domain knowledge
> (not a verified search result — flag as needing verification) ADCY5 is a well-established
> GWAS locus for fasting glucose/T2D/birth weight, which would be consistent with the
> adiposity/metabolic-pleiotropy hypothesis raised above for HACD2, but I have no verified
> evidence either way on a direct lung-function link. This should be logged as an explicit
> coverage gap: the candidate-gene list itself may be incomplete at this locus, and
> synthesis-lead should decide whether to route ADCY5/PDIA5/CCDC14/ROPN1 back to the
> researcher for a proper pass before finalizing HACD2-vs-MYLK as the only two contenders.

>   "Researcher's overall judgment" paragraphs for both genes are appropriately hedged and
