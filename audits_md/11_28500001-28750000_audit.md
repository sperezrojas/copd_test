### Red-team critique — locus 11_28500001-28750000 (METTL15), verbatim

Note from red-team-skeptic: web search budget was exhausted during this session, so this
critique relies on the evidence already assembled by the researcher and citation-verifier, plus
the Open Targets structured context provided. No new contradicting-evidence citations were
obtained from literature search — that is itself a limitation flagged below (point e), not
something silently omitted.

**(a) L2G score 0.52 — overclaimed as "the only gene with any prediction," should not be read as
decisive.** An L2G of 0.52 is a low-moderate score — well below the ~0.7-0.8 range typically
treated as confident gene assignment in L2G literature, and only marginally above a coin flip.
The researcher's synthesis does not explicitly flag this as merely moderate; it should. Being the
*only* gene with any L2G prediction at this credible set is a low bar (it just means no other
gene scored *at all*, which can happen with sparse annotation in a gene-poor-ish, intergenic
region) and should not be conflated with strong confidence in METTL15 specifically. This should
be stated in the evidence table as "weak-to-moderate, uncontested by default rather than by
competition."

**(b) The PheCode-496 credible set (lead rs11030399) is not demonstrated to be the same causal
signal as this locus's own lead rs10835362.** These are two independent fine-mapping exercises
(different source GWAS — MVP PheCode-496 vs. this pipeline's own COPD meta-analysis) with lead
variants ~40kb apart in the same broad LD block. Without an explicit pairwise LD (r²) figure or
formal colocalization between the two COPD-related signals, "same broad LD block" is suggestive
but not proof of a shared causal variant — this is precisely the LD/nearest-gene bias the review
process is supposed to interrogate. The researcher (and the OT context) treats the MVP credible
set's L2G score as if it transfers directly to this locus's own signal; that inferential step is
not fully justified and should be flagged as an assumption, not a finding. This weakens, but does
not eliminate, the gene-level relevance of the L2G evidence.

**(c) "Smoking-mediated, not pulmonary" is stated too strongly given what's actually missing vs.
what's actually negative.** The researcher is appropriately careful in the synthesis paragraph
(explicitly notes wheeze/asthma H4=0.868/0.768 "leaves open an airway-relevant alternative"), but
the framing of "no colocalization found" for FEV1/FEV1-FVC is being used as if it were evidence
of absence, when the record actually shows this trait pair was simply never tested/reported among
the 134 COLOC_PIP_ECAVIAR tests supplied — an absence of a lookup, not a null result. This is an
important distinction under Prior #2: a true null spirometric colocalization would be meaningful
negative evidence; an untested trait pair is not. The synthesis should be reworded to say
"spirometric colocalization data unavailable in this dataset" rather than implicitly treating it
as supporting the "not pulmonary" conclusion. Given asthma/wheeze H4 in the 0.77-0.87 range
(comparable in strength to the neuropsychiatric colocalizations the researcher is emphasizing),
an airway-inflammatory/atopic pathway is at least as well supported by the colocalization data as
the smoking-behavior pathway is, and the researcher's headline framing understates this.

**(d) Mitochondrial dysfunction/oxidative stress alternative hypothesis is acknowledged but not
seriously engaged, and this is a real gap.** COPD pathophysiology does have a well-established
mitochondrial dysfunction/oxidative stress component (airway epithelial senescence, ROS-driven
protease-antiprotease imbalance), and Lv et al. 2024 (verified) shows METTL15 deficiency
increases ROS and reduces mitochondrial membrane potential — a mechanistically plausible link to
COPD that the researcher's synthesis dismisses too quickly by leaning on "ubiquitous, not
lung-specific" and the mouse KO's absence of a lung phenotype. Absence of a lung phenotype in the
KO mouse is meaningful but not dispositive: the Averina 2022 KO study did not apparently *assess*
lung/airway phenotypes at all (per the researcher's own note — "NO lung/airway phenotype reported
**or apparently assessed**"), so this is again an absence of evidence, not evidence of absence,
and should not be leaned on as strongly as it currently is in the synthesis paragraph. A more
calibrated statement: "the mitochondrial-oxidative-stress mechanism is biologically plausible and
consistent with general COPD pathophysiology, but it is unproven for METTL15 specifically because
no study has tested lung/airway tissue directly."

**(e) Other overclaiming / missing alternatives:**
- The Kimbrel 2023, Deng 2024, and Zhao 2025 papers are all genuinely strong (VERIFIED, on-point),
  but note they converge on *neuropsychiatric/behavioral* traits (suicidality, ADHD) rather than
  smoking specifically — the researcher's synthesis folds these into a
  "smoking-behavior/neuropsychiatric-mediated pathway" single bucket, which is a reasonable
  grouping but somewhat blurs two distinct mechanistic hypotheses (nicotine-dependence-specific
  vs. broader externalizing/impulsivity-related neurobiology). Worth noting for the synthesis
  lead that this locus's strongest replicated signal may be a general neurobehavioral pleiotropy
  locus rather than smoking-initiation specifically — the GSCAN 2019 (Liu et al.) link to smoking
  initiation itself is CLAIM_MISMATCH/unconfirmed, so the "smoking initiation" colocalization
  rests on the OT structured data alone (H4=0.914), not on a verified literature source describing
  this specific locus.
- The circ-METTL15/PD-L1 lung cancer immune-escape paper (Zhang 2021) is correctly flagged as
  likely non-applicable — circRNA mechanism in a xenograft cancer model has no clear bridge to
  non-malignant COPD airway inflammation across the epithelial/immune cell types relevant to Prior
  #3. This should not be counted as immune-system evidence for METTL15 in COPD at all; treating it
  as "the one immune-adjacent hit" (even while discounting it) risks giving it more weight in the
  table than it deserves. Recommend explicitly excluding it from the evidence tally rather than
  listing it as a discounted positive.
- No search was conducted (or reported) for literature directly disputing METTL15 as causal at
  this locus or proposing an alternative gene — the "contradicting evidence" search specified in
  this pipeline's methodology appears not to have been done as an active, targeted step (as
  opposed to noting what didn't confirm). Given the intergenic, non-coding nature of the entire
  29-variant credible set and the modest L2G score, an active search for alternative candidate
  genes in the 11:28.58-28.62 Mb interval (and for any COPD/lung-function fine-mapping paper
  naming a different gene there) is warranted before this locus is finalized. This search was not
  completed by the red-team-skeptic itself due to exhausted web-search budget this session —
  flagged explicitly as an unresolved thoroughness gap for the synthesis lead, not silently passed
  through.

**Verdict: WEAKENED**

The molecular biology of METTL15 (mitochondrial rRNA methyltransferase, SSU mitoribosome
assembly) is solid and well-verified, but it is generic housekeeping biology with no lung-specific
or COPD-specific data (no lung tissue expression/eQTL emphasis reported, no lung phenotype tested
in the KO mouse). The statistical genetics case rests on (i) a moderate (0.52) L2G score from a
*different* credible set/lead variant than this locus's own lead SNP, whose transferability has
not been demonstrated, and (ii) colocalization evidence that is at least as strong for
asthma/wheeze as for smoking-behavior traits — undermining the researcher's headline framing that
this is predominantly a smoking-mediated, non-pulmonary locus. The absence of FEV1/FEV1-FVC
colocalization data should be described as untested, not as negative evidence, and the
mitochondrial-oxidative-stress-in-COPD alternative hypothesis deserves more explicit engagement
rather than being waved off via an underpowered/incompletely-phenotyped mouse KO. This gene is not
CONTESTED in the sense of having a credible rival paper naming a different causal gene (none was
found or actively searched for), but the inference as currently written overreaches on
directionality (smoking vs. pulmonary) beyond what the colocalization data actually show.
Recommend the synthesis lead treat METTL15 as a low-confidence candidate with an
ambiguous/mixed (neurobehavioral-and/or-airway-inflammatory) rather than a confidently
"smoking-mediated, not pulmonary" mechanism, and flag for a follow-up targeted search (once search
budget resets) for (1) any direct FEV1/FEV1-FVC GWAS/colocalization data at this locus, and (2)
alternative candidate genes/papers in the 11:28.58-28.62 Mb interval.
