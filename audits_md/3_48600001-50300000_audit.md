## Genes flagged CONTESTED or WEAKENED by red-team-skeptic (full critique, verbatim)

The red-team-skeptic reviewed the researcher's synthesis and did not endorse "SUPPORTED"
for any of BSN, MST1, RNF123, GPX1, RHOA, or DAG1 — every one of these six was judged
WEAKENED relative to the researcher's original framing, with RHOA judged to border on
CONTESTED specifically for its FAM13A cross-reference. Only APEH, NICN1, and AMT were left
at a provisional SUPPORTED status (for a "no evidence found, consistent with low priority"
claim), and even that carries the caveat that the skeptic did not independently re-search
these three genes (WebSearch budget was exhausted partway through the critique; PubMed
WebFetch was used as a partial substitute for 6 of 9 genes).

### Preliminary disclosure — search coverage for this critique

The WebSearch tool was unavailable for the skeptic's entire review (session's search budget

do not draw on the WebSearch budget) as a partial baseline pass. It was able to get an

Independent PubMed queries returned literal "no results" for "BSN + COPD," "MST1/RON +
COPD," "RNF123 + COPD/lung," and "RHOA + COPD + GWAS/fine-mapping" — confirming the
researcher's own finding that none of these genes have direct human lung-genetic

researcher's report that were subsequently sent to citation-verifier (see above; 2 of 3
verified, 1 CLAIM_MISMATCH and excluded).

### Cross-cutting structural issue

This locus spans BSN, MST1, RNF123, and (per L2G/ABC) GPX1 within a fairly narrow physical
interval, and three separate genes here (BSN, MST1, RNF123) are each credited with an
"independent" IBD/Crohn's association.

- The researcher's own citations show Latiano 2010 (PMID 20024904) is cited as evidence for
  BOTH BSN (via rs9858542) and MST1 (via rs3197999). If those two variants are in strong LD
  with each other (highly likely in a single candidate-gene/regional association paper
  examining the same IBD-associated haplotype), then this is not two independent
  confirmations of two separate causal genes — it is one association signal being counted
  twice, once under each neighboring gene's name. The researcher's framing does not address
  whether these are LD-independent hits within that paper, and should not be presented as if
  they are.
- This 3p21.31-adjacent region is well known in IBD genetics as a locus where the
  causal-gene identity among MST1/RNF123/GPX1/neighbors has itself been debated (multiple
  genes sit in one strong-LD haplotype). Importing "MST1 is the best candidate" into COPD
  inherits an unresolved problem from the IBD-genetics literature and presents it as
  resolved.
- This supports treating the locus as a plausible shared immune-regulatory hub rather than
  crowning a single causal gene. The "convergent IBD evidence across three genes" should be
  read as weaker, more ambiguous support for any one of them than the researcher's
  individual gene write-ups imply.

### Per-gene critique and verdicts

**BSN — WEAKENED.** The neuronal Mendelian-disease biology (Chen 2025, Ye 2022, Conroy
2014, Ghiglieri 2010) is solid and genuinely supports "no known lung mechanism." But the
"smoking-behavior mediation" causal narrative rests heavily on unverified GWAS-Catalog
scrapes. Educational attainment and mathematical ability are traits with near-universal
polygenic/pleiotropic overlap across the genome — citing them as evidence of a specific
"neurocognitive pathway to smoking uptake" is overclaiming; almost any large-effect locus in
the genome will show some educational-attainment GWAS-Catalog hit. The smoking-cessation
hit on the missense variant (rs34762726, p=2e-18) is more specific and more compelling if it
holds up, but it is also unverified and its LD-independence from the IBD-associated
haplotype hasn't been checked. Recommend downgrading "may be partly or wholly mediated
through behavioral/neurocognitive pathways" to "cannot exclude a direct lung role;
smoking/behavioral mediation is a plausible, unproven, testable alternative."

**MST1 — WEAKENED.** Every piece of MST1's mechanistic evidence (Häuser 2012, Morrison
2004, Lutz 2003, Tsutsui 2005, Huang 2020 review) is gut/IBD/PSC or CNS-autoimmune — zero of
it has ever been tested in lung or COPD tissue (independently confirmed: literal no-results
for MST1+COPD/RON+COPD). MST1 is ranked top candidate largely on (a) a real missense
variant + pQTL + IBD replication, and (b) the absence of a smoking-behavior GWAS-Catalog
hit — but (b) is absence-of-evidence treated as evidence-of-absence, and is itself an
unverified/context-only check. The researcher's ranking logic is internally inconsistent:
strong statistical/functional-immune evidence in a non-lung tissue is treated as more
convincing for MST1 than equivalent non-lung-specific evidence is discounted for
RHOA/GPX1. Downgrade "best combination of statistical and mechanistic immune evidence" to
"strongest coding-variant/functional signal, but with no lung-tissue evidence at all —
inference to a pulmonary mechanism is currently speculative."

**RNF123 — WEAKENED** (matches researcher's own hedged framing). IBD/Crohn's evidence (Liu
2026) plus multi-disease pleiotropy (cardiac hypertrophy, retinopathy, wound-healing NF-κB)
reads as a generically inflammatory/proliferative ubiquitin-ligase role, not lung-specific.
Independent check found zero RNF123-COPD/lung literature. The educational-attainment
GWAS-Catalog flag is fair and appropriately hedged by the researcher as "weaker version" of
the BSN concern.

**GPX1 — WEAKENED, not SUPPORTED as "among the most compelling."** The mouse in-vivo
smoke-exposure mechanistic data (Geraghty 2013, 2016) is genuinely the most disease-specific
direct experimental evidence at this entire locus. But GPX1 has no credible-set variant at
all — its candidacy rests entirely on ABC regulatory-annotation, a much less direct line of
statistical evidence than a fine-mapped PIP. Calling it "among the most functionally
compelling genes at this locus" conflates strength-of-mechanism with
strength-of-genetic-linkage-to-this-specific-GWAS-signal. Skeptic found a
candidate-gene-era human association study not cited by the researcher — Korytina et al.
2009 (PMID 19705749) — but citation-verifier subsequently found this paper's own conclusion
does NOT credit GPX1 with the COPD association (CLAIM_MISMATCH; excluded above). Net
verdict: WEAKENED — real, disease-specific mechanism, but genetic linkage to this GWAS
signal is the locus's weakest point among "real" candidates; superlative framing overreaches.

**RHOA — WEAKENED, bordering on CONTESTED on reasoning grounds.** This is the clearest case
of "mechanism so pleiotropic it could support almost any locus." RhoA/ROCK signaling is
involved in essentially all cell types cited (macrophage efferocytosis, airway smooth
muscle contractility, diaphragm muscle atrophy, fibroblast collagen production) — none of
these papers study RHOA genetic variation or this GWAS locus; they study the RhoA/ROCK
pathway pharmacologically or in cell/mouse models unrelated to this fine-mapped signal.
Independent PubMed check for "RHOA + COPD + GWAS/fine-mapping/credible set" returned zero
results, confirming no genetic literature connects RHOA specifically (as opposed to the
pathway generically) to this or any COPD locus. Combined with near-null fine-mapping (all
PIP <0.008) and second-lowest L2G score (0.081) at this locus, RHOA's inclusion as "among
the most compelling" is not supportable. The FAM13A cross-reference (Corvol 2018) is a
non-sequitur for RHOA's own candidacy: it shows RhoA is downstream of a different,
independently-established COPD gene at a different locus, which says nothing about whether
RHOA itself is the causal gene here. Reclassify RHOA as "pathway-plausible but statistically
and specifically unsupported" rather than one of the locus's stronger candidates.

**DAG1 — WEAKENED** (consistent with researcher's own "thin/contested" framing, with one
addition). No COPD or GWAS-based lung-function literature exists for DAG1 (confirmed
independently). The muscular-dystrophy/respiratory-failure literature (Nascimento 2026, Luo
2025, Ishigaki 2025) is a mechanistically distinct pathway (respiratory pump muscle
weakness, not airway/alveolar disease) — correctly flagged by researcher as a mismatch.
Skeptic surfaced two airway-relevant dystroglycan papers not in the researcher's report:
Shareef et al. 2021 (Inflammation; beta-dystroglycan in airway smooth muscle/eosinophils in
allergic asthma) and White et al. 2001 (alpha-dystroglycan in airway epithelial wound
repair) — both subsequently VERIFIED by citation-verifier and added to the main evidence
table. These modestly soften "no lung biology at all" to "some airway-tissue biology
exists, but in asthma/Th2-eosinophilic and epithelial-repair context, not
COPD/neutrophilic/smoke-response context" — still a mechanistic mismatch with COPD
specifically. Overall verdict unchanged: thin relative to its PoPS score.

**APEH, NICN1, AMT — not independently re-searched (disclosed above); treated as
provisionally SUPPORTED.** The researcher's finding of essentially no disease biology is
consistent with low PIP and (for NICN1/AMT) low L2G; skeptic has no basis to contest the "LD
passenger" call, but flags this as a shallow-to-none search tier — absence of contradicting
evidence here is genuinely absence-of-search, not evidence of a clean bill of health.

### Answers to the five specific questions posed to the skeptic (summary)

1. BSN smoking-confound argument: partially overclaimed. Education/math-ability hits are
   weak, non-specific pleiotropy evidence, not diagnostic of a smoking-behavior mechanism;
   the smoking-cessation missense-variant hit is more specific but unverified and its
   independence from the co-located IBD haplotype is unchecked.
2. MST1 favored via absence of smoking signal: yes, this is absence-of-evidence reasoning,
   compounded by MST1 having zero direct lung/COPD experimental data — an evidentiary gap as
   large as BSN's, just not framed that way.
3. RHOA "among the most compelling": not reasonable given near-null PIP, second-lowest L2G,
   and pathway evidence so generic it would implicate RhoA at nearly any inflammatory-disease
   locus. Clearest instance of non-specific mechanism being over-weighted.
4. GPX1 "mechanism looking for a locus": yes, in the sense that it has no credible-set
   variant at all; its case is genuinely strongest mechanistically (direct smoke-exposure in
   vivo lung data) but weakest statistically tied to this exact signal; superlative language
   should be tempered.
5. Shared immune-hub alternative hypothesis: well supported by the data as presented —
   BSN/MST1/RNF123 all tagging IBD, with at least one shared source paper (Latiano 2010)
   cited for two different genes, is a red flag for LD-based double-counting rather than
   three independent confirmations. This locus should be presented as a multi-gene
   regulatory/immune hub with an unresolved single-causal-gene identity, rather than a locus
   with one clear winner.
