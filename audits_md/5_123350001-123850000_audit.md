## Excluded citation: CLAIM_MISMATCH

**PMID 37547106 — Dong Y et al., 2023, Frontiers in Pediatrics, DOI: 10.3389/fped.2023.1102639**

- Researcher's original claim (as submitted to citation-verifier): "Clinical/genetic case
  series of 36 children with Joubert syndrome, noting that JBTS patients commonly show
  'breathing abnormalities' as part of the clinical phenotype (oculomotor apraxia plus
  episodic hyperpnea/apnea) and multi-organ involvement. This particular series's genetic
  findings were in other JBTS genes, not CEP120 specifically — cited only as general
  JBTS-phenotype context, not as CEP120-specific genetic evidence."
- Citation-verifier verdict: **CLAIM_MISMATCH**. Verbatim: "Paper exists with correct DOI
  (10.3389/fped.2023.1102639) and is a JBTS case series documenting breathing
  abnormalities. However, the claim states 'genetic findings were in other JBTS genes, not
  CEP120 specifically.' This is inaccurate: the abstract explicitly reports identification
  of a CEP120 variant (c.214 C>T, p.Arg72Cys) as one of their novel findings. This is a
  gene-specific genetic finding in CEP120."
- Red-team-skeptic's assessment of this mismatch: "This is not a case of the researcher
  overclaiming; if anything they under-claimed and mischaracterized the paper in the
  *opposite* direction (making CEP120 look less implicated than it is in that paper).
  However, this paper still only adds another rare-variant JBTS case, not COPD/FEV1/
  common-variant evidence — so correcting the claim doesn't change the overall verdict on
  the architecture-gap problem. It should be re-submitted to the citation-verifier with a
  corrected claim ('Dong et al. report a CEP120 p.Arg72Cys variant in a JBTS proband with
  breathing abnormalities, part of a broader ciliopathy case series') before being used at
  all, and even then it remains rare-disease evidence, not GWAS-bridging evidence."
- Disposition: excluded from the main evidence table per pipeline rule (only VERIFIED
  citations are used). If a future pipeline run wants to use the corrected claim above, it
  must be re-submitted to citation-verifier as a fresh, accurately-worded claim before
  inclusion — it is not carried forward here as verified.

## Gene-level skeptic verdicts: WEAKENED / CONTESTED-adjacent

### CSNK1G3 — WEAKENED

Verbatim from red-team-skeptic: "The researcher did the right thing here: ran five
targeted dedicated searches and got zero hits on every axis (COPD, lung function,
epithelial development, immune/inflammatory, smoking). All four verified citations are
genuinely off-target — kidney-stone MR, a Han-Chinese positive-selection scan, bovine
milk-protein GWAS, and a cancer-cell-line kinome screen. None of these establish a lung
mechanism, and none were stretched by the researcher into a lung claim (no overclaiming
detected). The Open Targets scores independently corroborate this: COPD L2G 0.096, FEV1
L2G 0.081/0.079 — all weak... intronic location is not evidence of function on the
harboring gene; introns of one gene very commonly contain regulatory elements (enhancers,
etc.) acting on a neighboring gene (classic FTO/IRX3 pattern), so this doesn't resolve the
question either way. The absence of any missense/splice variant in the credible set
removes the strongest form of direct-coding evidence for CSNK1G3... Verdict: WEAKENED. The
verified literature is real but entirely irrelevant to lung biology; the statistical (Open
Targets) evidence is also weak for this gene specifically. I found no contradicting paper
naming a different mechanism for CSNK1G3 (nor could I search independently), so this isn't
CONTESTED in the sense of an active counter-claim — it's a case of a nearest/intronic gene
with no supporting mechanism despite genuine effort to find one."

### CEP120 — WEAKENED, bordering on CONTESTED

Verbatim from red-team-skeptic (key excerpts; full text also in
`evidence_tables/_status/5_123350001-123850000/critique.md`):

"**Architecture mismatch.** Every verified functional citation for CEP120 (Shaheen 2014,
Joseph 2018, Roosing 2016, Tsai 2019) is about **rare, biallelic, loss-of-function or
hypomorphic missense mutations** causing a distinct monogenic ciliopathy spectrum (JATD,
Joubert syndrome, Meckel syndrome). That is a fundamentally different genetic architecture
from the credible-set signal actually being evaluated here: a common noncoding variant
(rs35390568, PIP 0.092, downstream_gene_variant, MAF presumably common in a population
GWAS). None of the four Mendelian-disease papers touch common variation, heterozygous
dosage effects, expression modulation, or any regulatory mechanism at this specific locus.
The literature does not bridge that gap — it is an analogy the researcher is implicitly
relying on ('ciliogenesis relevant to airway mucociliary biology'), not a demonstrated
mechanism.

**Phenotype mismatch, not just architecture mismatch.** JATD is a **restrictive**
thoracic-cage disorder (small thorax constrains lung growth) — this is mechanistically
distinct from COPD, which is airflow obstruction from airway/parenchymal disease. Citing
JATD as 'supporting a pulmonary mechanism' without noting that the established human
phenotype is restrictive rather than obstructive physiology is a meaningful gap in the
researcher's narrative...

**Point 3 — alternative explanation for the L2G scores.** L2G-type models are trained on
features like variant-to-gene distance, VEP consequence, chromatin interaction, and eQTL
overlap — not literature-curated mechanism. A high L2G score for CEP120 could easily
reflect CEP120 simply being the closest/only annotated protein-coding gene with a usable
TSS-distance or chromatin-loop feature in that specific sub-region... The wide range of the
14 FEV1 L2G rows (0.052-0.840, with 4/14 rows below 0.73) is itself worth noting: this is
not full consensus across whatever underlying studies/tissues feed that aggregation... That
heterogeneity is evidence the signal is not unambiguous.

**Point 5 — unexplored alternative: KRT18P16 / unannotated regulatory element.** The
researcher notes the CEP120-proximal credible-set variants sit near a pseudogene.
Pseudogenes can host regulatory elements (enhancers, ceRNA function) independent of the
nearby protein-coding gene's identity. Neither the researcher nor I could evaluate whether
the region regulates KRT18P16, a currently unannotated element, or acts at a distance on
CSNK1G3 via a chromatin loop. This alternative is not ruled out and should be named
explicitly as an open possibility, not assumed away by the FLAMES/ABC prioritization (both
of which have known false-positive-to-nearest-annotated-gene tendencies in gene-dense or
pseudogene-adjacent regions).

**Verdict: WEAKENED, bordering on CONTESTED.** I am not marking it fully CONTESTED because
I did not find (nor was I able to search for, given exhausted budget) a specific paper
arguing FOR a different gene at this locus or presenting a null COPD/CEP120 finding — the
'contest' here is internal to the evidence: a real statistical prioritization signal (Open
Targets L2G, FLAMES, ABC) sits alongside a genuinely disconnected literature base (rare
monogenic ciliopathy, restrictive not obstructive, no common-variant/regulatory bridge, one
CLAIM_MISMATCH). The inference 'CEP120 causal via ciliopathy/mucociliary mechanism' as
currently written overreaches: it should be reframed as 'CEP120 is the better-supported
gene by aggregated statistical fine-mapping (L2G/FLAMES/ABC), with plausible but
*unverified* mechanistic rationale from a distinct rare-disease phenotype; no literature
currently connects the common regulatory variant at this locus to CEP120 expression,
airway ciliary function, or COPD/FEV1 directly.'"
