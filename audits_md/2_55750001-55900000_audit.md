## Full red-team-skeptic critique (verbatim)

### Search-budget disclosure
Before critiquing, the skeptic attempted the mandated baseline contradiction-search pass
across both candidate genes ("EFEMP1 COPD emphysema lung function GWAS variant" and "PNPT1
lung disease pulmonary phenotype"). Both queries failed — the session's web-search budget
(200/200) was already exhausted, apparently by prior agents in this pipeline before this
invocation. The skeptic was unable to run any independent search for either EFEMP1 or
PNPT1. Everything in the critique is therefore a critique of the internal evidence and its
logical structure only, not an independently-verified absence-of-contradicting-literature.
This is a real gap: neither gene at this locus received an independent contradiction-search
pass. If search budget can be raised, this locus should be prioritized for a re-run of that
pass, given EFEMP1 is the pipeline's apparent front-runner.

### GENE: EFEMP1

**What holds up well:** The researcher's write-up is unusually well self-hedged already —
it flags the FVC-vs-FEV1/FEV1FVC distinction (cite 1), the composite-phenotype caveat
authors themselves raised (cite 3), the non-lung tissue caveats (cites 11, 14), and
explicitly rates the immune domain as weak/absent rather than padding it with the single
contested case report (cite 15). That's good practice, not overclaiming.

**Where the inference overreaches or needs qualification:**

1. The COPD-specific case is thinner than the FEV1/FEV1FVC case, and that distinction gets
   blurred. Of the 4 verified statgen citations, none is a direct COPD-case/control or
   FEV1/FEV1FVC GWAS hit for EFEMP1: #1 is FVC (volume, not obstruction), #2 is CT-emphysema
   conditional on smoking status in a replication context (not a novel signal in that
   paper), #3 is a composite mvLung phenotype the authors themselves say shouldn't be read
   as pure pulmonary-function genetics, and #4 is hernia. The much stronger, more consistent
   FEV1/FEV1FVC L2G (0.69-0.91 across >=5-10 studies) and the COPD colocalization with FEV1
   (h4~0.99) live only in the Open Targets database context — which is explicitly
   unverified, not a literature citation. So the verified-literature evidence base alone
   under-supports a direct COPD/obstruction mechanism; the strongest quantitative support
   for "this is really about airflow obstruction" is currently resting on unverified
   structured data, not on the 20 verified papers. Synthesis should not present
   COPD-specific confidence as equivalent to the FEV1/FEV1FVC confidence implied by Open
   Targets.

2. Smoking-interaction finding (Nyberg 2026) needs precise framing, not directional
   smoothing. The emphysema association appearing only in smokers (null in the
   51%-never-smoker stratum) is not evidence of neurobehavioral smoking-initiation
   confounding (consistent with Open Targets' finding of no colocalization with
   smoking-behavior GWAS among top traits) — but it IS evidence that the phenotypic effect
   is smoke-exposure-conditional, i.e., a gene-by-environment interaction. Combined with
   cite #13 (fibulin-3 is a preferred substrate of MMP-12, the classic cigarette-smoke/
   macrophage-elastase emphysema protease), there's a coherent, mechanistically specific
   hypothesis: smoke-induced MMP-12 degrades a fibulin-3 matrix already destabilized by
   risk-allele dosage. This is a good alternative to prior #1's default "smoking confound"
   framing, but it remains a hypothesis assembled from disparate papers (a GWAS interaction
   result + an unrelated biochemistry paper), not a demonstrated mechanism in lung tissue.
   Should be labeled as such, not as established biology.

3. Direction-of-effect complication (Stanton et al. 2017, cite #14) is a genuine tension,
   correctly surfaced but under-weighted. In Efemp1-KO mice, loss of Efemp1 was protective
   against cigarette-smoke-induced pathology (retina). This cuts against a simple "less
   EFEMP1 -> more smoke-induced damage" story, even though it's a different tissue/disease.
   It should be explicitly retained in the audit trail as a data point that argues for
   caution about assuming a single universal direction of effect for EFEMP1 loss-of-function
   across tissues, rather than treated as irrelevant because it's non-pulmonary.

4. The human pathological evidence for EFEMP1->emphysema is n=1 and confounded by systemic
   disease. Mol et al. 2024 (cite #10) is correctly identified by the researcher as the only
   direct human path evidence, but the same patient had diaphragmatic hernia and bowel
   diverticula — i.e., emphysema co-occurs with, rather than is isolable from, a generalized
   connective-tissue fragility syndrome in this individual. This is consistent with (not
   independent support for) the ECM-pleiotropy story, and shouldn't be cited as if it were a
   clean single-organ replication.

5. Variant-to-gene assignment is a database call, not a literature-established one.
   rs75550771 is intergenic with PIP=0.9999 — very strong statistical fine-mapping
   confidence in the variant, but none of the 20 verified citations perform fine-mapping/
   eQTL/functional work that distinguishes EFEMP1 from neighboring genes at this specific
   variant. The EFEMP1-over-PNPT1 call rests on FLAMES prioritization and Open Targets L2G
   (unverified), not on the verified literature base. This should be flagged transparently
   in the evidence table as a database-derived (not literature-derived) gene assignment —
   LD/nearest-gene-bias risk is only partially mitigated by the fact that PNPT1 is
   genomically outside the stated locus window.

6. Extreme phenotypic pleiotropy (hernia, prolapse, diverticulosis, BP traits, drusen,
   myopia, scoliosis) is mechanistically plausible (systemic elastic/connective-tissue
   fragility manifesting differently by organ) but also means the COPD signal could be
   capturing a general connective-tissue-fragility subtype rather than a COPD/emphysema-
   specific or inflammation-specific pathway — worth flagging under prior #3's call to
   consider disease-subtype heterogeneity: this may be a "structural-fragility COPD"
   mechanism distinct from neutrophilic/adaptive-immune COPD biology, not a competing-gene
   problem, but a subtype-specificity caveat for synthesis.

**Verdict: WEAKENED.** The gene-level structural/developmental biology (ECM/elastic-fiber
gene, KO phenotypes, MMP-12 substrate) is real, verified, and mechanistically coherent with
emphysema pathophysiology (loss of elastic recoil), and the absence of smoking-behavior
colocalization argues against simple confounding. But the inference that this is a
well-established, COPD/obstruction-specific pulmonary signal overreaches what the 20
verified citations show: the direct COPD/FEV1-FEV1FVC evidence is thin in the literature set
itself (FVC only, smoker-conditional emphysema replication, and a caveated composite
phenotype), the strongest quantitative support lives in unverified database context, the
human pathological evidence is n=1 and confounded, and there's a genuine (if
tissue-mismatched) directional contradiction in mouse data (cite #14) that shouldn't be
dropped from the record.

### GENE: PNPT1

**Assessment:** Correctly and conservatively characterized by the researcher already —
lower COPD and FEV1/FEV1FVC L2G than EFEMP1 (~4x lower), weaker constraint, zero lung/COPD-
specific literature among the 3 citations, and the one genuinely interesting mechanism
(MDA5/type-I-interferon activation from mitochondrial dsRNA accumulation, cites #19-20) is
explicitly and appropriately labeled speculative/non-pulmonary by the researcher, not
oversold as a COPD innate-immune mechanism. No overclaiming detected here.

**Scope flag for synthesis-lead:** PNPT1 sits ~117 kb from the lead variant and, per
Ensembl coordinates, is entirely outside the stated locus window
(2:55,750,001-55,900,000) — it was not in the original locus candidate list and was pulled
in only because it's the Open Targets L2G runner-up. Synthesis-lead should decide
explicitly whether to include an out-of-window gene in the final gene table at all, rather
than let its presence here imply it's a co-equal candidate; as evaluated, it is clearly
subordinate to EFEMP1 on every axis (L2G, constraint, literature depth).

**Verdict: HOLDS** for the modest claim actually made ("PNPT1 is a substantially weaker
candidate than EFEMP1; its interferon biology is speculative and non-pulmonary") — this
claim is accurately scoped to the evidence and not overreached. Recommend synthesis-lead
treat PNPT1 as out-of-scope for the primary gene table given the window issue, carrying
this critique forward rather than dropping it silently.

## Note on PNPT1 candidate-gene scope

PNPT1 is not listed as a candidate gene in the original locus input file
(`loci/2_55750001-55900000.md`) — the file lists only EFEMP1 (nearest gene and FLAMES-
prioritized). PNPT1 was added to the research scope by the orchestrator solely because it
was the only other gene with a non-trivial Open Targets L2G score at this locus's
fine-mapped credible sets. Per Ensembl coordinates (queried by the researcher), PNPT1
(2:55,634,061-55,693,863) lies entirely outside the stated locus window
(2:55,750,001-55,900,000), ~117kb from the lead/credible-set variant. It is retained in the
evidence table for completeness and transparency (since Open Targets' own gene-assignment
model considered it), but the study team should weigh this scope caveat when deciding
whether PNPT1 belongs in any downstream candidate-gene list for this locus.
