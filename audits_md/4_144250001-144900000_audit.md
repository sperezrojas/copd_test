## Excluded citations (CLAIM_MISMATCH)

### PMID 26416757 — Band G et al. 2015, "A novel locus of resistance to severe malaria in a region of ancient balancing selection." Nature. DOI 10.1038/nature15390.

- **As offered by researcher (gene: FREM3):** "identifies a malaria-resistance haplotype in
  the glycophorin gene cluster (GYPA/GYPB/GYPE) region of chr4q31, in which FREM3 (or
  FREM3-proximal regulatory variation) is the implicated gene, conferring ~33% protection
  against severe P. falciparum malaria via effects on erythrocyte-surface glycophorins."
- **Citation-verifier verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH** — "Abstract identifies malaria-resistance
  locus 'close to a cluster of genes encoding glycophorins' with 33% protection. However,
  the abstract does NOT explicitly name FREM3 as the implicated gene. The researcher claims
  'FREM3 (or FREM3-proximal regulatory variation) is the implicated gene,' but the abstract
  attributes the signal to the glycophorin region (GYPA/GYPB/GYPE), not FREM3. This is a
  mismatch between the claim and the abstract text."
- **Why excluded:** the paper is real and unretracted, but it does not support the specific
  FREM3-causal-gene claim attributed to it; using it as FREM3 evidence would overclaim.
- **Orchestrator follow-up:** queried Open Targets directly for GYPA's genomic coordinates
  to check the red-team-skeptic's flag about whether the glycophorin cluster itself falls
  inside the stated locus window. Result: GYPA (ENSG00000170180) spans chr4:144,109,303-
  144,140,751 (GRCh38) — this is ALSO outside the stated locus window
  (4:144,250,001-144,900,000), lying ~110kb centromeric of the window start, consistent
  with the same "outside the stated boundary" pattern already noted for FREM3 and SMARCA5.
  GYPA/GYPB/GYPE are therefore not being newly proposed as candidate genes for this locus.

### PMID 39500888 — Li S et al. 2024, "USP3 promotes DNA damage response and chemotherapy resistance through stabilizing and deubiquitinating SMARCA5 in prostate cancer." Cell Death Dis. DOI 10.1038/s41419-024-07117-3.

- **As offered by researcher (gene: SMARCA5):** included only as general (non-pulmonary)
  cancer/DNA-repair context; researcher itself flagged that "this abstract also states
  SMARCA5 loss of function can cause a neurodevelopmental disorder and (per this abstract's
  own text) 'Williams syndrome' — the latter attribution should be treated cautiously since
  Williams syndrome is classically a 7q11.23 microdeletion syndrome unrelated to SMARCA5's
  chr4 locus."
- **Citation-verifier verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH** — "The abstract states: 'Loss of function of
  the SMARCA5 can cause neurodevelopmental disorder and Williams syndrome.' This claim is
  erroneous/imprecise. Williams syndrome is a well-established 7q11.23 microdeletion
  syndrome (involving LIMK1, ELN, STX1A, and other genes), NOT an SMARCA5-related disorder
  on chr4. The abstract conflates two distinct genetic syndromes. SMARCA5 loss may cause
  neurodevelopmental disorder, but attributing Williams syndrome to SMARCA5 is incorrect."
- **Why excluded:** the underlying paper's own background statement contains a verifiable
  factual error; it is not used as evidence for any COPD/lung-relevant SMARCA5 biology
  regardless (it is a prostate-cancer DNA-repair mechanism paper with no lung relevance),
  and is excluded entirely from the evidence table rather than cited for an unrelated,
  reliable portion of its content.

## Genes marked CONTESTED by red-team-skeptic

Reproduced (condensed) from `evidence_tables/_status/4_144250001-144900000/critique.md`;

### FREM3 — CONTESTED

> "Not because of a specific new contradicting paper on FREM3 itself, but because (a) the
> only citation offered as support was reassigned by the verifier to a different gene
> entirely, (b) the gene body sits far outside the stated locus boundary, consistent with a
> MAGMA LD-bystander effect rather than direct implication, and (c) there is no independent
> lung/COPD evidence of any kind. This should be weighted as very weak relative to HHIP,
> and the synthesis lead should not treat 'MAGMA-prioritized' as biological evidence on its
> own here."

Supporting detail from the skeptic: FREM3's gene body (chr4:143,577,302-143,700,675) lies
~550kb-1.1Mb outside the stated locus window (4:144,250,001-144,900,000); the researcher's
own independent Ensembl cross-check (in `researcher.md`) found the same discrepancy
independently. No COPD/lung GWAS, TWAS, MR, or colocalization evidence was found for FREM3
by the researcher ("FREM3 COPD" returned zero PubMed results).

### SMARCA5 — CONTESTED

> "This is the weakest of the three candidates: no lung/COPD literature, a gene body
> outside the stated window, and both supporting citations either off-target (paralog-based
> NDD paper) or actively unreliable (a paper the verifier caught misattributing an unrelated
> syndrome). The researcher's write-up is appropriately restrained here (it doesn't
> overclaim), but I'd flag to synthesis lead that this candidate has essentially zero
> disease-relevant evidence and its PoPS nomination alone should not be treated as
> meaningfully competing with HHIP."

Supporting detail from the skeptic: SMARCA5's gene body (chr4:143,513,445-143,557,486) also
lies outside the stated locus window, at a comparable distance to FREM3. PoPS nominates
genes by feature similarity (expression/pathway/PPI), not direct regulatory linkage to
credible-set variants, which the skeptic notes is a known general limitation applying with
particular force here given the coordinate discrepancy. Zero COPD/lung PubMed hits were
found for SMARCA5.

## HHIP — not contested, but two caveats the red-team-skeptic raised (both retained, with caveats, in the main evidence table rather than excluded)

1. **Single-study immune mechanism should not be over-weighted.** The skeptic found the
   researcher's overall-assessment language bundled the single-study immune finding (Wang
   et al. 2023, PMID 36822205 — VERIFIED) into the same evidentiary tier as the multi-decade
   statistical-genetics/spirometry evidence. The skeptic recommends describing this as "an
   emerging, single-study mechanistic lead" rather than a co-equal pillar. This caveat is
   reflected in the main evidence table's skeptic-verdict column and in the Prioritized-gene
   section's confidence discussion.
2. **Direction-of-effect gap.** None of the assembled evidence establishes whether the
   COPD-risk allele at this locus increases or decreases HHIP expression, or whether that
   direction is concordant with the Hhip+/- mouse loss-of-function-causes-emphysema
   phenotype. This is a real, unresolved gap in an otherwise strong case, noted explicitly
   in the Prioritized-gene section rather than treated as disqualifying.
3. **Search-coverage limitation disclosed by red-team-skeptic**: the skeptic's own
   WebSearch budget was exhausted before it could run independent contradiction searches
   (e.g., for a paper disputing HHIP as the 4q31 causal gene, or nominating an alternative
   gene). Its critique of HHIP is therefore reasoning over the researcher's provided
   material rather than a fully independent adversarial literature search. This is flagged
   here for transparency; it did not prevent the skeptic from applying the biological
   priors and structural/coordinate reasoning described above, and no specific
   counter-evidence was found or suppressed — the gap is an absence of an independent check,
   not a positive finding either way.
