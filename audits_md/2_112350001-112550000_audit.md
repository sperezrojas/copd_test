### TTL — verdict: WEAKENED (verbatim from red-team-skeptic, original pass; unchanged on reopening)

"The researcher's own findings are unusually well-hedged already, and the Open Targets
structured context (L2G 0.861 exclusively to TTL at rs3811038 across 12 credible sets,
h4~0.999 colocalization with 14 smoking/behavioral traits and zero spirometric traits,
TTL's own top disease association being smoking initiation) is internally consistent
and points to one conclusion: this is very likely a smoking-behavior locus, not a
pulmonary-biology locus, that happens to manifest in the COPD GWAS because the GWAS is
unadjusted for smoking (prior #1 applies cleanly and is not being fought here — good).

Where I'd push back:
- The structural/developmental section leans on TTL-null mouse neuronal phenotypes
  (PMID 15899979, 26773468) and a Parkinson's paper (PMID 41107317) as "pathway"
  context, but these are CNS papers with no lung phenotype reported. The researcher is
  appropriately explicit that there is 'no direct evidence TTL itself... has any role in
  airway ciliary motility' — this is correctly hedged, not overclaimed. Good practice:
  they distinguished TTL from the TTLL1 paralog (Vogel et al., PMID 20442420) that
  actually does have a respiratory ciliary phenotype, and explicitly declined to use it
  as TTL evidence. That paralog-discrimination is exactly the kind of check this
  pipeline wants; if anything it strengthens confidence in the researcher's discipline
  here, not a red flag.
- The Amara et al. bladder-cancer paper (PMID 30642841) is included as 'weak/indirect
  smoking-exposure-context evidence' — correctly labeled weak/indirect and not
  overreached.
- The genuinely important residual gap: neither Saunders 2022 (PMID 36477530) nor
  Brazel 2018 (PMID 30679032) was confirmed to actually name TTL among reported loci.
  This means the mechanistic link between 'TTL is the L2G/colocalization gene' and 'TTL
  is a literature-established smoking-behavior gene' rests entirely on the Open Targets
  pipeline output, not on a paper someone can point to that discusses TTL biology in a
  smoking-GWAS context. That's a meaningful evidentiary gap that should be flagged to
  synthesis-lead, not silently treated as closed — the researcher does flag it, which is
  correct, but it means the 'TTL = smoking gene' story is currently a database-inference
  chain, not a literature-confirmed one.
- No inflammatory/immune literature at all beyond an uninterpreted eQTL. This is a true
  empty domain, correctly reported as such rather than padded.

Net assessment: the researcher does not overclaim — if anything the write-up is
conservative and honest about gaps. The strongest evidence (Open Targets L2G +
colocalization pattern) argues this locus is smoking-behavior-mediated per prior #1,
with no spirometric concordance (prior #2 directly invoked and unmet) and no immune
signal (prior #3 not applicable — domain is empty, not contested). I did not find, and
could not search for, independent contradicting evidence (e.g., a paper arguing FOR a
direct pulmonary mechanism of TTL, or against the smoking-mediation reading) — this is a
genuine gap in my review, not a clean bill of health.

Verdict: WEAKENED. Not because the researcher misrepresented anything, but because the
inference chain 'TTL is the causal gene at this locus, and this locus reflects
smoking-behavior neurobiology' is well-supported by database colocalization but not yet
corroborated by any literature source that names TTL specifically in a smoking-genetics
or lung-disease context — the case rests on unconfirmed inference through the
Saunders/Brazel loci-list gap. Synthesis-lead should treat this as
smoking-mediated-locus-of-moderate-confidence, and should flag (as the researcher did)
that the TTL-specific literature link to smoking behavior is still an open confirmation
task, not a closed one."

### ANAPC1 — verdict: CONTESTED (verbatim from red-team-skeptic, original pass)

"This gene has a more serious structural problem than the researcher's write-up fully
foregrounds: the Ensembl coordinates place ANAPC1 at 2:111,611,639-111,884,690, which
does not overlap the stated locus window (2:112,350,001-112,550,000) at all — it sits
several hundred kb to ~900kb outside it. If that coordinate discrepancy is real (I could
not independently re-verify GRCh38 coordinates via search this session, so I'm flagging
it as reported/unresolved rather than confirmed), then ANAPC1's presence in this
candidate list is itself questionable on physical-overlap grounds, independent of any
literature evidence. This is a more fundamental issue than typical 'LD/nearest-gene
bias' — it would mean the PoPS-based inclusion criterion (stated as physical overlap
with the locus) may not actually be satisfied, and the input file itself may contain an
error or be using a different overlap definition (e.g., TAD-based or regulatory-window
PoPS scoring rather than literal gene-body overlap). Synthesis-lead should get this
resolved (confirm build/transcript coordinates) before treating ANAPC1 as a real
candidate at this locus at all, because if the coordinate discrepancy holds, the entire
'PoPS-prioritized gene' framing for ANAPC1 is built on a premise that doesn't hold, not
on weak-but-real evidence.

Separately from the coordinate issue, on the merits of the evidence presented:
- ANAPC1 never receives an L2G score at this variant (0/12 credible sets), and COPD does
  not rank in its top 25 diseases. This is a strong negative signal from the same
  database that positively identifies TTL.
- The immune evidence is a pattern I'd call classic nonspecific 'hub gene' bioinformatics
  noise: HIV Vpr degradation of APC1 in T cells/macrophages (unrelated disease),
  Alzheimer's T-lymphocyte proliferation (unrelated disease), an ARDS blood-transcriptome
  bioinformatics reanalysis where ANAPC1 is one of four correlative 'hub genes' with no
  functional validation and where a different gene (PSMD2) was the top hit, and a
  COVID-19 'retesting-positive' transcriptome study where ANAPC1 was merely
  down-regulated in one exploratory comparison, not a hub gene. None of these show
  COPD-specific or COPD-relevant inflammatory-program specificity (prior #3: this is
  precisely the 'nonspecific/generic' category the prior tells us to distinguish, and the
  researcher does correctly label it as such — good).
- The only lung-relevant hit is a lung cancer pharmacogenomic SNP (chemotherapy
  response, PMID 26893365) — unrelated to COPD risk biology and correctly labeled as such
  by the researcher.
- ANAPC1 is a core cell-cycle/APC-C scaffold gene, broadly essential across tissues — the
  kind of gene where genome-wide bioinformatics 'hub gene' hits in arbitrary disease-
  transcriptome studies are expected by chance/housekeeping ubiquity, not because of
  COPD-specific biology. The researcher's framing of this as 'broadly essential, not
  lung/immune-restricted' is the right caveat and should be weighted heavily against any
  of the immune citations being read as COPD-supportive.

Verdict: CONTESTED. Two independent grounds: (1) the coordinate/physical-overlap
discrepancy, which — if accurate as reported — undermines ANAPC1's basic eligibility as
a candidate gene at this locus regardless of any downstream evidence; and (2) even
setting that aside, the literature and database evidence assembled (zero L2G scoring at
the causal variant, no COPD/asthma/spirometric GWAS hit, only generic multi-disease 'hub
gene' bioinformatics correlations and one unrelated lung-cancer pharmacogenomic SNP)
affirmatively argues against ANAPC1 rather than merely failing to support it. I did not
find, and could not independently search for, any paper defending ANAPC1 as a COPD gene
— the researcher's zero-hit targeted searches are consistent with genuine absence, but
this should be understood as 'actively searched and came up empty/negative,' which is
different from and stronger than 'not yet searched.'"

**Orchestrator's independent follow-up (original pass):** The coordinate discrepancy
flagged above by both the researcher and the red-team-skeptic (as unresolved/
unconfirmed) was independently re-checked directly against the Open Targets
`genomicLocation` field (not merely re-quoting the researcher's Ensembl REST result):
ANAPC1 = 2:111,611,639-111,884,690 (GRCh38, minus strand); TTL = 2:112,482,113-
112,541,739 (GRCh38, plus strand); stated locus window = 2:112,350,001-112,550,000.
This confirms the discrepancy is real: ANAPC1 does not overlap the stated locus window
(gap of ~465,311 bp), while TTL sits entirely within it. This finding is carried into
the evidence table's Open Targets summary and Prioritized-gene sections.

## POLR1B — verdict: CONTESTED (verbatim from red-team-skeptic, reopening pass)

**Independent-search disclosure (verbatim):** "Before assessing, I attempted three
targeted independent searches (POLR1B + COPD/emphysema/lung-function; Treacher Collins
lower-airway/parenchymal phenotype; Pol I/ribosome biogenesis + lung epithelial
senescence). All three failed — the session's web search budget was already exhausted
(200/200) before I could run them. So I was not able to independently verify or refute
the researcher's 'zero hits' claims, or independently test the alveolar-senescence/
ribosome-biogenesis hypothesis. This is a real gap; flagging it explicitly per
instructions rather than silently treating my critique as equivalent to a fresh
literature check. What follows is a critique of the inference structure of the evidence
as presented, which is still doable without new searches."

**Critique (verbatim):**

1. "LD/nearest-gene bias — this is the central problem, and it's severe. POLR1B fails
   the basic test for locus relevance twice over: (a) its overlap with the window is a
   boundary artifact — only the first ~8kb of a 38kb gene falls inside
   2:112,350,001-112,550,000, with the bulk of the gene body extending outside it; (b)
   the lead/only credible-set variant rs3811038 sits inside TTL, ~59.3kb from POLR1B's
   nearest edge, and POLR1B never receives an L2G score at that variant across all 33
   credible sets tested (12 GWAS + 21 molecular QTL) — a unanimous null, not just a low
   score. Combined with the ~176bp tandem adjacency to TTL, this is about as clean a
   case as one could ask for that POLR1B's candidacy is a window/merge artifact rather
   than independent statistical-genetic evidence. The researcher's own 'artifact of
   tandem physical adjacency' framing is the correct read, not an overclaim."
2. "Smoking-mediation prior — not applicable here, in POLR1B's favor and disfavor
   simultaneously... Absence of smoking-mediation evidence here isn't a point in
   POLR1B's favor; it's just one more category where POLR1B has no evidence of any
   kind."
3. "Contradicting/undermining evidence found by the researcher (correctly weighted).
   The 'Chronic Obstructive Asthma' score (0.190) is the one data point that could
   superficially look like support, but the researcher/orchestrator correctly traced it
   to a gwas_credible_sets evidence entry with null variant/study fields — i.e.,
   unattributable to this locus, and inconsistent with POLR1B's unanimous zero-L2G at
   rs3811038. Treating this as supporting evidence would be overclaiming; the
   researcher did not do so, and neither should synthesis. The absence of COPD from the
   full 223-disease list (vs. TTL's rank 6/811) is a stronger and cleaner negative
   signal than a low rank would be."
4. "Mechanistic plausibility for lower-airway disease is weak, and the researcher's
   OSA-vs-parenchymal distinction is sound... The general ribosomopathy reviews
   (Orgebin, Bielczyk-Maczyńska) supporting 'no lung phenotype documented across
   ribosomopathies' is a reasonable negative-evidence citation, though I could not
   independently confirm no ribosomopathy-lung link exists in more recent (2023-2026)
   literature given my search-budget failure — this is a residual unknown, not a
   refutation."
5. "Generic/essential-gene profile, correctly characterized. LoF oe=0.263 (top
   constraint decile) is textbook core-machinery essentiality (Pol I subunit), not a
   disease-specific signal... No immune/inflammatory literature at all, again correctly
   characterized as analogous to ANAPC1's 'generic housekeeping gene' problem."
6. "Overclaiming check... I did not find a place where a hedged database pattern or a
   general-disease-review citation was translated into a causal or COPD-specific
   claim. This is the same discipline shown in the original TTL/ANAPC1 review."
7. "Thoroughness... I flag only that my own inability to independently confirm these
   zero-hit claims (search budget exhausted) means this pass should be treated as a
   partial/incomplete independent check, not a full one."

**Verdict (verbatim):** "The evidence does not support POLR1B as an independent
candidate at this locus; it actively undermines its own candidacy. Physical overlap is
a boundary artifact (8kb of 38kb, wrong end of the gene from the lead variant),
fine-mapping/L2G support is a unanimous zero across every credible set tested, COPD is
entirely absent (not just low-ranked) from its disease association profile, its one
plausible-looking signal (Chronic Obstructive Asthma) is untraceable to this locus, and
its established disease mechanism (Treacher Collins, upper-airway/craniofacial-
developmental) is mechanistically disconnected from adult obstructive lung disease.
This is a distinct failure mode from ANAPC1 (which had zero physical overlap at all)
but converges on the same practical conclusion: no genuine independent evidence links
this gene to the locus's COPD signal."

## Effect on locus-level call (verbatim from red-team-skeptic)

"POLR1B does not create a tie or push the locus to INSUFFICIENT confidence, and it does
not overturn TTL — if anything it reinforces TTL as the (still weak) best-supported
gene at this locus. TTL retains exclusive, unanimous L2G support at the fine-mapped
lead variant across 12 GWAS credible sets and a real (if smoking-associated rather than
spirometry-associated) disease-association profile. POLR1B has none of that: no L2G,
no COPD entry at all in Open Targets, no lung/spirometry/smoking literature, and a
mechanistically distant disease mechanism. A 'tie' would require two candidates with
comparably strong-but-different weaknesses; here the weaknesses are wildly asymmetric —
TTL's problem is which pathway (smoking vs. lung) explains a real, strong signal, while
POLR1B's problem is that it has no signal to explain. My recommendation: retain TTL as
the locus's best-supported gene at LOW confidence (unchanged from the original
verdict), explicitly note POLR1B was evaluated and found to be a boundary/tandem-
adjacency artifact with no independent supporting evidence (CONTESTED), and flag to
synthesis-lead that a full independent contradiction-search for POLR1B could not be
completed this round due to web-search-budget exhaustion — if the orchestrator wants
stronger assurance before finalizing, a follow-up pass with restored search budget on
'POLR1B lung/emphysema/ribosome biogenesis senescence' would be the one gap worth
closing, though based on the structural/statistical evidence alone I do not expect it
to change the conclusion."

No new citations were introduced by the skeptic in this addendum (no independent search
was possible this round either — search budget exhausted before any query completed,
