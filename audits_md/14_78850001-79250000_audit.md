### Critique: NRXN3 (14_78850001-79250000)

**1. LD / nearest-gene bias — not applicable here, and that's a genuine strength.**
This is about as clean a single-gene locus as GWAS produces: NRXN3 physically spans virtually the entire credible-set interval, the ~70 intronic credible-set variants sit inside the gene body, and nearest-gene, FLAMES, MAGMA, and PoPS all converge on it with no competing paralog or neighbor in the region (ABC = NA, consistent with no enhancer-hijacking alternative candidate surfacing). There is no "which gene in the LD block" ambiguity to litigate. That said, one methodological wrinkle worth naming for the record: MAGMA (and to a lesser extent PoPS, which uses MAGMA-derived features) is known to be biased toward large genes with many SNPs, and NRXN3 is one of the largest protein-coding genes in the genome (~1.5+ Mb). Given the credible set already sits entirely inside NRXN3, MAGMA/PoPS naming it isn't really independent confirmation so much as a near-automatic consequence of gene size and physical overlap — this doesn't undermine gene identity (there's nothing else it could be), but it means "four independent methods agree" should not be read as four independent lines of mechanistic evidence; it's really one structural fact (the whole locus is NRXN3) confirmed four ways.

**2. Smoking/behavioral mediation — this is the crux, and the evidence strongly favors an indirect route.**
The Open Targets colocalization pattern is about as clear a "smoking/behavior/adiposity" signature as you'll see at a COPD locus: H4 0.6-0.93 with smoking initiation, smoking cessation, substance use disorder, BMI/weight/body fat, age at first sex, and daytime napping — a classic impulsivity/reward/addiction-and-metabolic cluster consistent with NRXN3's canonical neuronal presynaptic cell-adhesion biology (Südhof). Against that, across all 30 overlapping GWAS credible sets there is zero colocalization or L2G signal for FEV1, FEV1/FVC, or any spirometric trait. Under this pipeline's own prior #1 and #2, that asymmetry is close to a textbook case: strong smoking/behavioral concordance, no pulmonary-function concordance. The one MR study offered (Apalowo 2025, VERIFIED) reinforces this rather than rescuing a direct-lung story — it routes the NRXN3 obesity variant to COPD/asthma risk via body composition, not airway biology. Taken together, the case that NRXN3 is "causal for COPD via direct pulmonary mechanism" is weak to absent; the case that it's causal for the COPD GWAS signal via smoking initiation/heaviness and/or adiposity is well supported by structured colocalization data (though note: colocalization evidence here is Open Targets' structured output, not itself a verified literature citation, so it should be labeled as such in synthesis).

**3. Contradicting evidence.**
Two things already surfaced by the citation-verifier function as contradicting evidence and should be weighted as such, not filed away as mere technicalities:
- Sakornsakolpat 2019 (PMID 30804561) CLAIM_MISMATCH: this is a large prior COPD GWAS meta-analysis, and per the verifier's full-text check, 14q24/NRXN3 is not among its 82 genome-wide-significant loci. The researcher used it as "prior COPD-GWAS context" for this locus, which the verifier correctly caught as unsupported. This is mild non-replication evidence — the locus either emerged only with larger downstream sample sizes, is population/cohort-dependent, or is a borderline/unstable signal. It doesn't kill the locus (GWAS discovery growing with N is normal) but it's a data point against treating NRXN3-COPD as a long-established, robustly replicated finding, and it should be reported as non-replication in an early major COPD GWAS, not silently dropped.
- Shrine 2023 lung-function GWAS (PMID 36914875) — unresolved, not neutral. This is arguably the single most decisive paper available: the largest spirometry GWAS to date, and if NRXN3/14q24 is genuinely absent from its 559 reported genes, that is close to direct disconfirmation of a pulmonary-function mechanism (complementing the Open Targets non-colocalization with FEV1/FEV1FVC already noted). The verifier flagged this as unresolved because the abstract doesn't settle it. I recommend this be treated as a priority follow-up before final synthesis — someone needs to check the Shrine 2023 supplementary gene table directly for NRXN3/14q24. Until that's done, "the field's largest lung-function GWAS is silent on NRXN3" should be treated as a live possibility, not swept into "gap."

I did not have search budget to hunt for additional papers explicitly arguing against NRXN3-in-lung-disease or proposing alternate mechanisms at 14q24, so I can't report a positive "I searched and found nothing more." That search should still be logged as done (or not done) by whoever finalizes this locus — don't let my inability to run it stand in for a completed contradicting-evidence search per the protocol's requirement.

**4. Overclaiming check — cannot fully verify, flagging as an open item.**
Based only on the summary given to me, the researcher's behavior looks honest: explicit "zero direct lung/airway/epithelial/emphysema" and "zero immune/inflammatory" findings, reported as genuine gaps rather than papered over, and the Apalowo MR claim as reported to me is correctly scoped to a body-composition-mediated pathway rather than inflated into a lung-mechanism claim. That's good practice. But since I could not read the actual one-sentence claims verbatim against the verifier's quoted abstract sentences for all 18 VERIFIED citations, I can't personally rule out subtler overreach (e.g., a domain-writeup sentence that says "NRXN3 contributes to COPD risk" without the smoking/behavioral qualifier attached). Recommend the synthesis-lead or a second pass explicitly re-check the final synthesis language against this distinction — "NRXN3 is associated with the COPD GWAS signal at 14q24, most plausibly via smoking-behavior/adiposity pathways, with no direct pulmonary or immune evidence" is the defensible claim; "NRXN3 is a COPD susceptibility gene acting in the lung" is not supported by anything summarized here and would be overclaiming.

**5. Thoroughness.**
The pattern described — a consistent, well-documented zero-hit result across two entire independent domains (structural/developmental lung biology and immunology/inflammation), contrasted with a rich, specific hit rate in the statistical-genetics/behavioral domain — looks like a real biological signal rather than under-searching. A sparse or lazy search typically produces scattered null results across all domains; here the nulls are domain-specific and complete, which is more consistent with "this gene truly has no lung/immune literature" than with a coverage gap. I don't flag this gene for additional researcher re-work on thoroughness grounds. The one exception is the Shrine 2023 follow-up in point 3, which is a citation-resolution task, not a re-search task.

**On your specific question — does the single-gene, no-LD-ambiguity structure mean the behavioral-genetics literature should carry more weight despite no lung evidence?**
It increases confidence in attribution (we can be confident NRXN3, not some neighbor, is the gene tagged by this credible set) but it does not — and should not — increase confidence in a pulmonary mechanism. If anything, the single-gene structure sharpens rather than resolves the problem: there's no neighboring gene to which the addiction/impulsivity/obesity biology could be offloaded while preserving a clean "NRXN3 does the lung part." It's one gene, one set of variants, doing one thing (modulating neuronal reward/behavioral circuitry and downstream smoking/adiposity phenotypes), and the parsimonious read is that the COPD association is a downstream consequence of that single mechanism acting through smoking exposure and/or body composition — not two coincidentally co-located mechanisms. So: trust the gene assignment fully; do not extend that trust to a pulmonary-biology inference the data doesn't support.

**Additional citations worth chasing (send to citation-verifier / researcher for explicit follow-up):**
- Shrine et al. 2023 lung-function GWAS (PMID 36914875) — explicit check of whether NRXN3/14q24 appears in the 559-gene list/supplementary tables (not just the abstract). This is the single highest-value open item.
- The predecessor Shrine et al. 2019 Nature Genetics lung-function GWAS (larger predecessor study) for the same NRXN3/14q24 question, if not already checked.
- GTEx / Human Protein Atlas lung-tissue expression data for NRXN3 (not a "citation" in the traditional sense, but worth pulling as structured data alongside the Open Targets facts already cited) — if NRXN3 is minimally expressed in lung/airway epithelium, that's independent supporting evidence against a direct pulmonary mechanism and should be stated plainly rather than left implicit.

**Determination: WEAKENED.**
The statistical-genetics and behavioral/addiction-genetics evidence for NRXN3 is real, extensive, and — given the single-gene locus structure — unambiguous in its gene attribution. But the inference that NRXN3 contributes to COPD through pulmonary or immune/inflammatory biology overreaches what's shown: there is no lung-function colocalization across 30 examined credible sets, no direct lung/airway literature, no immune/inflammatory literature, one prior major COPD GWAS (Sakornsakolpat 2019) that doesn't even replicate the locus, and an unresolved (not negative, but unresolved) status in the largest lung-function GWAS (Shrine 2023) that needs to be chased down before this locus is finalized. The defensible synthesis-level claim is that NRXN3 is very likely a smoking-behavior/nicotine-dependence and/or adiposity locus whose COPD GWAS signal is mediated through those pathways rather than through primary pulmonary pathology — a real and legitimate finding for a smoking-unadjusted COPD GWAS, but a substantially different claim than "NRXN3 drives COPD via lung biology," and the write-up should say so explicitly rather than let the volume of the addiction/obesity literature substitute for lung-relevant evidence that isn't there.
