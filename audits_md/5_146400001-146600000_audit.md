# Audit trail: 5:146,400,001-146,600,000 (nearest gene TCERG1)

This appendix lists everything excluded from the main evidence table
(`evidence_tables/5_146400001-146600000.md`), with reasoning preserved verbatim from the
citation-verifier and red-team-skeptic where available.

## Excluded citations (not VERIFIED)

**1. Liu M, Jiang Y, Wedow R, et al. "Association studies of up to 1.2 million individuals
yield new insights into the genetic etiology of tobacco and alcohol use." Nat Genet.
2019;51(2):237-244. PMID: 30643251. DOI: 10.1038/s41588-018-0307-5.**

- **Verdict: NOT_FOUND** (citation-verifier)
- **Reason (verbatim from citation-verifier):** "Paper exists and is the correct GSCAN
  tobacco/alcohol GWAS. However, the abstract specifies 566 variants across 406 loci but
  makes no mention of chromosome 5, rs73793188, TCERG1, or GPR151. Cannot confirm the
  specific locus attribution from available documentation."
- **Why this citation was sought:** the researcher flagged this as the likely source of
  the "smoking initiation" association Open Targets attributes (via its aggregate,
  non-locus-specific disease-association score) to TCERG1 (score 0.339) and GPR151 (score
  0.071). The paper itself is real and legitimate (this is not a fabricated/retracted
  citation), but the *specific claim* that this chr5:146.4-146.6Mb locus, or TCERG1/GPR151
  by name, is a reported genome-wide-significant hit in this paper could not be confirmed
  from available text. Per red-team-skeptic: "the smoking-initiation association
  currently rests only on an unverifiable OT aggregate score (0.339), not a checked GWAS
  paper" — this should be treated as an open question for the study team to check directly
  against GSCAN's supplementary locus tables or GWAS Catalog, not as an established
  smoking-behavior link for this locus.

No other citations returned by the researcher were RETRACTED or CLAIM_MISMATCH; 17 of 18
were VERIFIED (see `evidence_tables/_status/5_146400001-146600000/verified_citations.md`
for the full verbatim verifier output, including per-citation evidence quotes).

One VERIFIED citation carries an indexing caveat worth flagging here even though it was
not excluded: **Rios et al. 2026, bioRxiv preprint (PMID 42620318, DOI
10.64898/2026.08.02.742327)** — citation-verifier confirmed the preprint exists and its
claims match, but noted: "Unusual that a bioRxiv preprint has been assigned a PMID; this
is atypical indexing. However, the paper exists in PubMed as indexed." It remains in the
main evidence table as VERIFIED (with the preprint / non-peer-reviewed status noted in
the Skeptic verdict column), but the study team may wish to independently double-check
this indexing anomaly.

## Genes/claims marked CONTESTED or WEAKENED by red-team-skeptic

Full critique preserved verbatim in
`evidence_tables/_status/5_146400001-146600000/critique.md`. Key verdicts:

### TCERG1 — WEAKENED (not CONTESTED)

> "Fine-mapping genuinely and specifically implicates TCERG1 over its neighbors (dominant
> PIP mass, dominant L2G for the locus's actual colocalizing traits) — that part holds.
> But the inferential leap from 'TCERG1 is the right gene for this locus's blood-cell/
> height signal' to 'TCERG1 is a COPD gene via inflammatory/lung biology' is unsupported:
> no COPD/FEV1 literature, no neutrophil-mechanism literature, and the one lung paper is a
> different disease. Not CONTESTED (no paper argues against TCERG1), but the case for
> COPD-specific relevance is much thinner than the genetics alone would suggest."

Specific overclaiming risks flagged: chaining "dominant L2G for neutrophil/WBC count" +
"LoF-constrained" + "one lung-phenotype paper" into an unsupported neutrophilic-COPD
mechanism story; treating the BPD biomarker paper (PMID 41552916) as general "lung
evidence" for adult COPD; treating the Huntington's disease age-of-onset role as
disease-relevance "precedent" (a non sequitur); treating "TCERG1 has an established
smoking-initiation link" as confirmed when the supporting citation (PMID 30643251) was
NOT_FOUND by the verifier.

### GPR151 — metabolic/T2D sub-claim CONTESTED; overall COPD relevance WEAKENED

> "Gurtan et al. 2022 (PMID 35381001, already VERIFIED) directly contradicts
> Bielczyk-Maczynska et al. 2022 (PMID 36456565, already VERIFIED) on GPR151's metabolic
> role — this is a clean internal contradiction, already flagged by the researcher, and
> should be presented to synthesis-lead as unresolved, not as a positive
> metabolic-pleiotropy data point. For COPD relevance specifically, I'd call it WEAKENED
> rather than CONTESTED: no paper argues against a GPR151-COPD link because none proposed
> one to begin with; the case is a weak, indirect, behaviorally-mediated inference with
> minor fine-mapping support and one explicit null (peripheral-immune) finding cutting
> against the airway-immune version of the story."

Additional flag: the habenula-comorbidity-enrichment finding (PMID 30429765, pneumonia and
pulmonary fibrosis enriched among diseases comorbid with addiction) "could easily be
misquoted as 'GPR151 linked to pulmonary disease' — it's an enrichment analysis over
comorbidity gene-sets, not a GPR151-specific pulmonary finding." Also flagged: GPR151 is
architecturally a minor player at this locus (2 variants, PIP<0.001), so treating it as a
serious co-equal candidate to TCERG1 (except for the sleep-apnea signal, where it does
lead) is itself a mild overreach. A cross-check oddity was also noted: Open Targets'
aggregate smoking-initiation score is higher for TCERG1 (0.339) than GPR151 (0.071), even
though GPR151 has the stronger literature-based mechanistic case for smoking/nicotine
behavior — flagged as an unresolved discrepancy, not silently resolved either way.

### PPP2R2B — CONTESTED

> "Not because a paper directly refutes a PPP2R2B-COPD hypothesis (none was ever
> literally proposed in the literature), but because within the evidence provided there
> is a direct method-vs-method contradiction: MAGMA/PoPS nominate PPP2R2B while Open
> Targets' colocalization/L2G model gives it zero support and instead assigns the locus's
> real, credible-set-anchored signal entirely to TCERG1. That is exactly the 'different
> gene implicated at the same locus by a rival method' scenario the task asks to watch
> for, and it should be flagged to synthesis-lead as the central unresolved tension of
> this locus, not quietly reconciled in favor of either method."

Additional flag: citing the T-cell apoptosis/SLE mechanism (PMID 21746932) as
"immunology support" for a COPD role "would be a serious overclaim — it's a lupus T-cell
apoptosis mechanism, never tested in airway immunity, neutrophils, macrophages, or COPD,
and porting an SLE T-cell-survival mechanism onto COPD's inflammatory programs is an
unsupported disease-to-disease extrapolation." Red-team-skeptic further characterizes
PPP2R2B's MAGMA/PoPS nomination as likely "a proximity/gene-level-burden artifact... about
as clean a 'the credible set doesn't distinguish or even reach this gene' case as this
locus offers."

## Other caveats disclosed by red-team-skeptic

> "Search-budget disclosure up front: the session's web-search budget was already
> exhausted when I attempted the baseline contradiction-search pass (all three queries —
> TCERG1, PPP2R2B, GPR151 — returned 'budget exhausted' errors with zero results). So none
> of the three genes received an independent search from me; my critique below is
> analysis of the pasted evidence only, applying the standing priors, not additional
> literature discovery. Flag this explicitly to synthesis-lead: this is a genuine coverage
> gap, not a 'clean' review pass."

This means the red-team-skeptic's critique for this locus is based entirely on
analytical/logical scrutiny of the researcher's and Open Targets' findings as presented,
not on independent literature counter-search. The study team should weigh this as a
somewhat thinner adversarial pass than usual for this locus.

## Cross-cutting note

No candidate gene at this locus (TCERG1, PPP2R2B, GPR151) has any VERIFIED literature
citation or Open Targets structured association directly linking it to COPD, FEV1, or
FEV1/FVC. This is a locus-wide evidentiary gap, not specific to any one gene, and is
central to why the prioritized-gene call in the main evidence table is marked LOW
confidence.
