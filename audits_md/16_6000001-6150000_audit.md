# Audit appendix — Locus 16:6000001-6150000 (RBFOX1)

This appendix records every citation excluded from the evidence table (RETRACTED /
NOT_FOUND / CLAIM_MISMATCH) and every gene/claim the red-team-skeptic marked CONTESTED,
with reasoning preserved verbatim from the citation-verifier and red-team-skeptic

## Excluded citations (CLAIM_MISMATCH)

### 1. Liu M, Jiang Y, Wedow R, et al. "Association studies of up to 1.2 million individuals yield new insights into the genetic etiology of tobacco and alcohol use." Nature Genetics, 2019. PMID: 30643251; DOI: 10.1038/s41588-018-0307-5.
**Claim as originally made by researcher:** This GSCAN meta-analysis of up to 1.2 million individuals identified 566 variants in 406 loci associated with smoking initiation, cessation, cigarettes-per-day, and alcohol use; RBFOX1 is among the genomic regions implicated in this large smoking-behavior genetic architecture.
**Citation-verifier verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH.**
**Verbatim justification:** "Full-text search confirms RBFOX1 is not mentioned anywhere in the article despite the claim that 'RBFOX1 is among the genomic regions implicated.' The paper identifies 566 variants in 406 loci and discusses specific genes (CYP2A6, ADH1B, DRD2, CHRNA5, GRIN2A, HOMER2, etc.) but RBFOX1 does not appear."
**Why this matters:** This was the flagship citation intended to establish RBFOX1's link to smoking-initiation/heaviness behavior (as distinct from nicotine-dependence severity). Its disqualification is a major reason the red-team-skeptic downgraded the smoking-mediation hypothesis from "supported" to "weakened, one plausible hypothesis."

### 2. Sakornsakolpat P, Prokopenko D, Lamontagne M, et al. "Genetic landscape of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease identifies heterogeneous cell-type and phenotype associations." Nature Genetics, 2019. PMID: 30804561; DOI: 10.1038/s41588-018-0342-2.
**Claim as originally made by researcher:** The largest published COPD GWAS at the time (35,735 cases/222,076 controls); researcher explicitly flagged uncertainty whether RBFOX1 (chr16:~6.0-7.7 Mb) is among the 82 reported loci.
**Citation-verifier verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH.**
**Verbatim justification:** "Full-text search confirms RBFOX1 is not mentioned among the 82 identified COPD loci. Despite the explicit researcher question 'is RBFOX1 (chr16:~6.0-7.7 Mb) reported among the 82 loci?' — it is not."
**Why this matters:** This is the largest, most directly relevant COPD case-control GWAS available, and it affirmatively does not report RBFOX1 as a locus. Per red-team-skeptic: "a locus this large and LD-rich, in genes this well-studied for lung function, failing to appear in the largest lung-function/COPD GWAS to date is evidence weighing against, not neutral to, a direct pulmonary mechanism."

### 3. Shrine N, Guyatt AL, Erzurumluoglu AM, et al. "New genetic signals for lung function highlight pathways and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease associations across multiple ancestries." Nature Genetics, 2019. PMID: 30804560; DOI: 10.1038/s41588-018-0321-7.
**Claim as originally made by researcher:** Companion lung-function GWAS (400,102 individuals) defining 279 lung-function (FEV1/FVC) signals; researcher explicitly flagged uncertainty whether an RBFOX1 locus signal is among them.
**Citation-verifier verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH.**
**Verbatim justification:** "Full-text search confirms RBFOX1 is not mentioned among the 279 lung function signals. Despite the explicit researcher question 'is an RBFOX1 locus signal reported?' — it is not."
**Note:** The associated 2024 author correction to this paper (PMID 38641645, DOI 10.1038/s41588-024-01752-4) was separately checked and confirmed to exist and pertain to this paper (VERIFIED as a real correction), but this does not change the CLAIM_MISMATCH verdict on the underlying RBFOX1 claim.
**Why this matters:** This is the largest, most directly relevant lung-function GWAS available, and it affirmatively does not report an RBFOX1 signal.

No RETRACTED or NOT_FOUND citations were identified at this locus — all 14 citations checked were real, non-retracted papers; 3 of the 14 (plus the flagship smoking GWAS citation counted above) were CLAIM_MISMATCH, i.e., the paper exists but does not support the specific claim attributed to it.

## CONTESTED / WEAKENED verdicts from red-team-skeptic (verbatim)

### Claim: RBFOX1 has a direct pulmonary/structural lung mechanism
**Verdict: CONTESTED**
"Actively contradicted by confirmed absence from the two largest COPD/lung-function GWAS (Sakornsakolpat 2019, Shrine 2019), plus zero OT lung/COPD evidence, plus zero lung-specific structural biology found."

### Claim: RBFOX1's COPD association is smoking-behavior-mediated
**Verdict: WEAKENED**
"Plausible and worth reporting, but resting on 2 verified nicotine-dependence-specific (not smoking-initiation) citations after the flagship GSCAN citation was disqualified as a mismatch; should be framed as leading hypothesis, not established mediation."
Full reasoning: "The flagship citation (Liu et al. 2019 GSCAN, PMID 30643251) was a CLAIM_MISMATCH: RBFOX1 is not mentioned in that paper at all. That leaves two real citations — Chen et al. 2020 (PMID 31294817, nicotine dependence FTND/TTFC) and Chen et al. 2016 (PMID 27164557, cotinine/FTND/schizophrenia overlap). Both are legitimate but narrower than 'smoking initiation': they concern nicotine-dependence severity/quantity phenotypes (FTND, TTFC, cotinine), not smoking initiation (ever-smoked) or cessation. This matters because COPD risk is driven substantially by smoking initiation and duration/pack-years, not only dependence severity... neither paper reports the same lead variant (rs35459620) or even the same LD block as this credible set — both are gene-level 'RBFOX1 was among genes implicated' statements from independent GWAS, not fine-mapped/colocalized to this credible set."

### Claim: RBFOX1 has meaningful immune/inflammatory-pathway relevance to COPD
**Verdict: CONTESTED/WEAKENED**
"Evidence is non-lung, enrichment-level, or review-derived without organ specificity; no COPD- or airway-specific immune mechanism found."

### Claim: RBFOX1 is the only plausible candidate gene at this locus
**Verdict:** Not contested as a gene-assignment claim ("Reasonable given all 11 credible-set variants are intronic within RBFOX1 and it's the sole gene physically present") — but the skeptic explicitly warned: "this says only that RBFOX1 is the correct **gene assignment**, not that RBFOX1 has a validated **COPD mechanism** — these two claims should not be conflated in synthesis."

## Alternative explanations flagged by red-team-skeptic (not previously considered by researcher)

1. **Winner's-curse/LD-artifact false positive:** "RBFOX1 is one of the largest genes in the genome (~1.7 Mb), sits in a gene-poor, low-recombination region, and MAGMA and ABC both failed to prioritize any gene here — a notable disagreement with FLAMES/PoPS... Large genes have disproportionately more 'COPD-associated' intronic SNPs simply by virtue of covering more genomic real estate and more measured variants, independent of causal mechanism... a scattershot pleiotropy pattern that is at least as consistent with a statistical/LD magnet effect as with true multi-organ pleiotropy."

2. **Uncharacterized lung-specific isoform/splicing function:** "RBFOX-family paralogs (RBFOX2 in particular) do have described roles in epithelial/mesenchymal splicing programs, so a lung-relevant RBFOX1 isoform is not biologically implausible, merely unstudied. This is a real 'absence of evidence, not evidence of absence' case."

3. **Brain sQTL colocalization as a distinct hypothesis (not yet literature-confirmed):** The Open Targets CommonMind DLPFC brain-splicing-QTL cluster overlapping this locus "is a specific, testable mechanism... but it is database context, unconfirmed in peer-reviewed literature, and should not be casually merged into the smoking-mediation narrative as if it were verified support. It's a hypothesis-generating hint, not evidence."
