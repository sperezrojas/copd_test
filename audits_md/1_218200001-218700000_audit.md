## Excluded citations (verifier verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH / RETRACTED / NOT_FOUND)

### 1. Hobbs BD, de Jong K, Lamontagne M, et al. "Genetic loci associated with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease overlap with loci for lung function and pulmonary fibrosis." Nat Genet. 2017. PMID: 28166215. DOI: 10.1038/ng.3752.

**Verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH** (verified 2026-09-11).

Researcher's claim: this is a large COPD case-control GWAS (15,256 cases/47,936 controls +
replication) that reconfirms multiple known COPD loci including the region overlapping the
TGFB2/lung-function signal.

Verifier's finding: the paper's general subject matter is confirmed real (15,256
cases/47,936 controls, "22 loci associated at genome-wide significance" per abstract), but
the abstract text retrieved does **not** explicitly name TGFB2 among the confirmed loci.
The specific claim that this paper names/reconfirms TGFB2 could not be verified from the
abstract alone.

Disposition: excluded from the verified evidence table. Not necessarily false — TGFB2 is a
very plausible candidate to be among the 22 loci reported, and Open Targets itself cites
this same PMID (28166215) as a `gwas_credible_sets` evidence source for TGFB2 at this
locus (score 0.734, per the Open Targets summary) — but per pipeline rules, only
verifier-confirmed claims go in the evidence table. Full-text confirmation would resolve
this; recommended as a follow-up.

### 2. Sakornsakolpat P, Prokopenko D, Lamontagne M, et al. "Genetic landscape of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease identifies heterogeneous cell-type and phenotype associations." Nat Genet. 2019. PMID: 30804561. DOI: 10.1038/s41588-018-0342-2.

**Verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH** (verified 2026-09-11).

Researcher's claim: largest COPD GWAS to date (35,735 cases/222,076 controls, UK Biobank +
ICGC) identifying 82 loci, reconfirming the TGFB2 region among previously described
COPD/lung-function loci, with enrichment of COPD risk loci in lung tissue/smooth
muscle/specific lung cell types.

Verifier's finding: the paper's general subject matter is confirmed real (35,735
cases/222,076 controls, "82 loci" per abstract), but the abstract text retrieved does
**not** explicitly name TGFB2. The specific claim that this paper names/reconfirms TGFB2
could not be verified from the abstract alone.

Disposition: excluded from the verified evidence table. Same caveat as above — Open
Targets itself cites this same PMID (30804561) as a `gwas_credible_sets` evidence source
for TGFB2 at this locus (score 0.767, the highest of the three COPD-study scores), which
is independently consistent with the researcher's claim, but was not itself confirmed by
the citation-verifier from the abstract text. Full-text confirmation recommended as
follow-up.

### TGFB2 — Verdict: WEAKENED (verbatim reasoning from skeptic)

"The evidence base is real, multi-domain, and the COPD/spirometry concordance is genuinely
strong (satisfies prior #2 well) — TGFB2 remains the clear front-runner at this locus,
especially relative to RRP15. But the 'robustly supports...rather than smoking behavior'
framing [from the researcher's original summary] overreaches: two of the statgen citations
don't actually say what's claimed [Hobbs 2017, Sakornsakolpat 2019 Nat Genet — see excluded
citations above], the smoking-mediation alternative was not actually excluded (just
under-tested due to tool limits), the Mendelian LOF evidence has a real pulmonary-phenotype
gap that the summary elides, and the strongest functional/CRISPR evidence validates a
different variant [rs1690789, Parker 2019] than the credible set. Recommend synthesis-lead
present TGFB2 as well-supported but with these specific caveats intact, and flag the
smoking-GWAS lookup as a to-do rather than a closed question."

Specific points raised by the skeptic, preserved in full:

- **Mendelian pulmonary-phenotype gap:** "a systematic review of ~3,896 LDS cases across
  all subtypes found no pulmonary phenotype for LDS4 [Gouda et al. 2022, PMID 35662564]. If
  TGFB2 haploinsufficiency in humans caused emphysema-like lung disease, a cohort
  ascertained partly through connective-tissue/vascular surveillance in specialized clinics
  might still be expected to pick up an incidental pulmonary signal over decades of case
  reports. Ascertainment bias is a fair partial explanation but doesn't fully rescue the
  claim... Critically, the researcher never established whether the common COPD-risk allele
  at rs2799098 increases or decreases TGFB2 expression/activity, so it's not even clear the
  LDS4 (loss-of-function) model is the relevant one to invoke by analogy."
- **Direction-of-effect complications:** two Mendelian-disease papers (Al Maskari et al.
  2016, PMID 27782106 — a predicted-LOF missense variant paradoxically *increased*
  TGFB1/TGFB2 expression; Mumm et al. 2025, PMID 40204055 — a distinct missense variant
  causes a gain-of-function-like skeletal disease, Camurati-Engelmann type 2) "show that
  direction-of-effect for TGFB2 variants is not simple."
- **Functional evidence is gene-level, not variant-level:** "the CRISPR-validated
  regulatory variant (rs1690789) is 200kb downstream of TGFB2 and is not one of this
  locus's credible-set variants. It supports 'some regulatory element near TGFB2 controls
  TGFB2 expression in lung fibroblasts' (gene-level plausibility) but does not directly
  validate rs2799098 or the other credible-set SNPs as functional. No lung-tissue eQTL
  colocalization for the specific credible-set variants was reported anywhere in the
  findings."
- **Smoking-behavior alternative not excluded:** "the researcher explicitly says the
  PubMed-negative result for TGFB2 x smoking-initiation GWAS is an 'incomplete negative'
  caused by search-quota exhaustion, and recommends direct GSCAN/GWAS Catalog lookup that
  was never done. Despite that, the overall summary asserts the locus acts 'rather than
  through smoking behavior' — this is not earned by the evidence presented; it's an
  assumption dressed as a conclusion."
- **Fibroblast-enhancer enrichment not TGFB2-unique:** "Castaldi 2014's fibroblast-enhancer
  enrichment was the strongest signal across all 5 known COPD loci tested, not unique to
  TGFB2 — it's suggestive but somewhat generic support for a 'lung fibroblast/stroma'
  mechanism at COPD loci broadly, not a TGFB2-specific discriminator."
- **Immune evidence appropriately downweighted (not a criticism, a confirmation of good
  practice):** "The researcher found only one relevant paper (Wen et al. 2002), correctly
  identified it as asthma/Th2 airway-epithelium biology rather than COPD-specific, and
  explicitly stated they found no COPD-relevant macrophage/neutrophil/Th17/Tc17/CD8+
  literature for TGFB2, labeling this a 'secondary, less-supported' line... This matches
  prior #3's instruction to distinguish specific from generic/contested immune evidence and
  is not being smuggled in as stronger than it is."

### RRP15 — Verdict: INSUFFICIENT-EVIDENCE (effectively excluded as effector gene)

Verbatim reasoning from skeptic: "Zero COPD/lung-function GWAS or colocalization hits,
only generic ribosome-biogenesis/nucleolar-stress biology in non-lung, non-immune systems
(yeast, cancer cell lines, maize, mouse liver), corroborated by very low Open Targets L2G
scores (COPD ~0.05-0.07, FEV1/FVC ~0.10-0.16) and unremarkable gnomAD constraint, plus only
3.7% of credible-set PIP mass. This is not a case of contradicting evidence being found
against RRP15 (none was actively searched for by either the researcher or me, per the
disclosure above) — it's a case of a genuine absence of any supporting signal in a low-PIP
tail. I have no basis to call it CONTESTED (no specific rebuttal paper found) and it isn't
WEAKENED in the sense of 'real evidence being overstretched' — the researcher was
appropriately conservative and didn't oversell it... Should be treated as a low-priority/
background candidate relative to TGFB2, not a serious competitor at this locus."
