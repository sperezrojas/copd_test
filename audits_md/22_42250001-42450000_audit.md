**Citation-verifier verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH.**
Justification (verbatim from citation-verifier): "Paper confirmed to exist; reports 566

researcher correctly flagged this as an open question — the paper does NOT appear to

**Why excluded:** This is the GSCAN smoking/alcohol-use GWAS, the researcher's proposed
underlying primary-literature support for the Open Targets-reported "smoking
initiation" trait association for TCF20 (score 0.267). Because the citation-verifier
could not confirm this specific locus is among the 406 reported GSCAN loci, this
citation must NOT be used as locus-specific evidence in the main table. It is usable
only as general background on the existence/scale of large smoking-behavior GWAS
efforts, not as a claim that TCF20/this locus is a confirmed smoking-initiation hit.
**Open item for any future revisit of this locus:** check the actual GSCAN supplementary
loci table (or a newer/larger smoking-initiation GWAS, e.g. later GSCAN releases) for a
hit at chr22:42.25-42.45Mb — red-team-skeptic identified this as the single check most

### TCF20 — WEAKENED

"The fine-mapping case is strong and well-described... But the functional/disease case
built on top of it is where the overreach lives.

- Vetrini 2019 (verified, claim matches) establishes TCF20 haploinsufficiency causes a
monogenic neurodevelopmental syndrome. This is real but is evidence for a completely
different phenotype (developmental delay/ID) via a completely different presumed
mechanism (dosage-sensitive haploinsufficiency from severe LoF variants) than a common
noncoding intronic variant (rs8142378, MODIFIER impact) nudging COPD risk in a GWAS.
Citing a monogenic LoF-syndrome paper to support relevance of a common noncoding
regulatory variant is a category error common in these prioritizations — it establishes
the gene is biologically important and dosage-sensitive, not that this variant/mechanism
is relevant to airflow obstruction. This should be labeled explicitly as
constraint/precedent evidence, not mechanistic disease evidence.
- The Xu 2024 circRNA-Tcf20 citation is honestly flagged by the researcher itself as
mechanistically distinct (acute LPS sepsis/ARDS, not chronic COPD) and about a circRNA
transcribed from the locus rather than TCF20 protein. Good that this was caveated, but
its inclusion at all risks being read as 'TCF20 has an acute lung injury connection' when
it's really 'a transcript from this genomic region shows differential expression in an
unrelated acute lung injury model.' I'd downgrade this to near-irrelevant rather than
supportive-with-caveats.
- The GSCAN (Liu 2019) citation is the crux issue. The citation-verifier flagged
CLAIM_MISMATCH... The researcher's framing — 'TCF20 as smoking-behavior-mediated
candidate, but unverified' — is more hedged than an unqualified claim would be... That
hedge is appropriate and should stay in the writeup. However, the framing still risks
overclaiming in synthesis: calling TCF20 'the smoking-behavior-mediated candidate'
implies smoking-mediation is the leading mechanistic hypothesis for this locus, when in
fact the only actual evidence for that link is an aggregated Open Targets score (0.267,
itself downstream of unknown underlying GWAS hits, possibly including this exact GSCAN
study that failed verification) — not an independently confirmed genetic association at
this locus... That's a meaningfully weaker claim than 'candidate for smoking-mediated
mechanism,' and the synthesis lead should not upgrade this into 'TCF20 acts via smoking
behavior' without the GSCAN locus-level check being redone.
- Total absence of TCF20-COPD, TCF20-lung-function, and rs8142378 literature (zero
PubMed hits across all three targeted searches) is itself important negative evidence
given prior #2... This is a locus where 'we don't know' is the honest state, not
'probably smoking-mediated.'

Verdict: WEAKENED. The fine-mapping support for TCF20 as the causal gene is solid, and
constraint argues for a real functional gene at this locus. But the disease-mechanism
narrative overreaches... The honest summary is 'TCF20 is the fine-mapped gene at this
locus with no confirmed lung, COPD, or smoking-locus-level evidence; smoking-behavior
mediation is a plausible but currently unsubstantiated hypothesis, not a finding.'"

### SHISA8 — WEAKENED

"Zero credible-set variant overlap (PoPS-only) is a serious problem independent of
anything else — per the scope note, PoPS reflects genome-wide functional similarity, not
this locus's actual fine-mapping, so SHISA8's inclusion as a 'candidate' here is already
on much weaker footing than TCF20 or even NFAM1. All three cited papers... describe
exclusively neuronal, non-pulmonary, non-immune biology, and the researcher's own
summary judgment says this plainly. No overclaiming detected here — if anything the
researcher already appropriately deflated this gene... Verdict: WEAKENED (already
appropriately downgraded by the researcher, but flagging that its presence in the
candidate list at all should be clearly labeled as PoPS-genome-wide-similarity-only, not
locus-supported, in any synthesis table — a reader skimming gene names could mistake it
for having comparable statistical support to TCF20)."

### NFAM1 — WEAKENED

"The physical case is very weak: one synonymous variant at PIP=0.003 is essentially
noise-level in a credible set where TCF20 carries 86%+ of the mass — this variant would
not be expected to survive an LD/fine-mapping sanity check as the causal signal. That
needs to be stated unambiguously in any synthesis, not softened into 'plausible
immune-relevant candidate.'

On the biological side, per prior #3..., NFAM1 is an ITAM-signaling adaptor with genuine
immune biology, and the researcher is right to note the asthma-exacerbation
transcriptomic association (Liu 2025) as the only airway-disease-adjacent evidence. But
that study is asthma, not COPD — these are overlapping but distinct diseases with
different dominant inflammatory programs (Th2/eosinophilic vs. the
neutrophilic/CD8+/Th1/Th17 mix more typical of COPD per the priors)... The other three
immune citations... are generic immune-adaptor biology with no lung specificity at all —
they support 'NFAM1 is a real immune signaling gene' but not 'NFAM1 is relevant to COPD
lung pathology' specifically. The Open Targets 'upper respiratory tract disorder' score
(0.178) is explicitly caveated by the researcher as weak, non-COPD-specific, and
unsupported by any fine-mapping/colocalization (none exists at all for this region) —
appropriately hedged, not overclaimed.

Net assessment on NFAM1 as a competing candidate: the immune biology is real but diffuse
and non-COPD-specific, and it is undermined by the near-total absence of statistical
support at this locus (0.003 PIP, synonymous, non-lead). I do not think NFAM1 is
currently a credible alternative to TCF20 at this locus — it reads more like 'a gene
with interesting general immune biology that happens to sit near the locus,' which is
exactly the LD/nearest-gene-bias concern prior #1 warns about. The researcher's own
summary judgment already lands here appropriately.

Verdict: WEAKENED. The immune-relevance framing is fair as generic gene biology but
overstates NFAM1's case as a locus candidate — it should be presented as a
low-probability, non-specific alternative, not a competing candidate on par with TCF20.
No direct COPD evidence exists for NFAM1 at all (only asthma and non-pulmonary immune
contexts), and its statistical support at the locus is negligible."

### LINC01315 — WEAKENED / effectively ruled out

lung/immune/smoking signal anywhere. The researcher's 'likely reflects genomic

Verdict: WEAKENED / effectively ruled out (researcher's own characterization as
'weakest candidate' is accurate and not an overclaim — I'd call this HOLDS in the sense
that the modest, deflationary conclusion drawn matches the thin evidence)."

### Overall framing assessment (verbatim)

"As written by the researcher (with the 'but unverified'/'could not be traced to a
verified primary GWAS locus-level finding' hedge included), this is appropriately
cautious and should NOT be simplified upward in synthesis... the defensible synthesis
statement is: 'TCF20 is the dominant fine-mapped gene at this locus by PIP, is highly
constrained, and has strong monogenic neurodevelopmental-disease precedent; there is no
confirmed pulmonary-function or COPD-specific mechanistic evidence, and the
smoking-behavior-mediation hypothesis — while consistent with TCF20's known
neurobehavioral disease spectrum and an aggregate Open Targets smoking-initiation signal
— has not been independently confirmed at the primary-GWAS locus level (the GSCAN
citation intended to support it failed independent verification of chromosome/locus
relevance).' Anything shorter that drops the hedge (e.g., 'TCF20 likely acts through
smoking behavior') would be overclaiming relative to the actual evidence base, and I'd
flag that as a CONTESTED framing if it appears in synthesis without the caveat. As
currently written by the researcher, it holds because the hedge is intact — this is a
caution for the synthesis-lead's downstream compression, not a fault in the researcher's
own text.
