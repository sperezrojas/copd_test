This is the excluded-citation and contested-gene appendix for
`evidence_tables/5_95550001-95800000.md`. Reasoning from citation-verifier and
red-team-skeptic preserved verbatim (or near-verbatim) below.

**Targeted re-opening note (2026-09-11):** this locus was originally completed with SPATA9
(nearest gene) and RHOBTB3 (Open Targets L2G-surfaced context gene) as the only candidates
evaluated. A genome-build error in the study team's MAGMA/PoPS gene-location merge file was
subsequently discovered and corrected; the corrected re-merge surfaced a third candidate,

the locus was independently confirmed before any further work (see Open Targets summary in

therefore researched, verified, and critiqued in full, and is now documented below alongside
the original SPATA9/RHOBTB3 material, which was left untouched and not re-verified.

## Contested / weakened gene verdicts (red-team-skeptic, verbatim reasoning)

### SPATA9 — WEAKENED

> Exactly one citation makes a SPATA9-specific claim: Soler Artigas 2011, a discovery-era
> GWAS that names SPATA9 as nearest-gene at a genome-wide-significant lung-function locus.
> That is a 2011 nearest-gene call, not a fine-mapped or functionally validated assignment.
>
> Shrine 2019 is not SPATA9-specific — the researcher's own framing admits it's "a
> locus-class-level claim, not SPATA9-specific" (a GRS-level statement about hundreds of
> signals generalizing across smokers/never-smokers). Citing it under "SPATA9 evidence"
> already overstates what it shows.
>
> Shrine 2023 was explicitly flagged CLAIM_MISMATCH by the verifier — correctly excluded as
> SPATA9-specific evidence, but its presence in the table at all invites a reader to skim
> past the caveat.
>
> Zero TWAS/MR/eQTL/coloc paper in 15 years names SPATA9. Zero PubMed hits for the lead
> variant rs466748. No mechanistic/developmental/immune literature of any kind. Canonical
> function is spermatogenesis (Sertoli-cell/globozoospermia biology), and gnomAD shows
> near-zero LoF constraint (LOEUF ~1.40) — i.e., this gene is tolerant of loss-of-function,
> which is weak but real evidence against a highly penetrant dosage-sensitive lung
> mechanism (it doesn't rule out a regulatory/intronic expression effect, but it cuts
> against, not for, SPATA9 mattering).
>
> The Open Targets L2G score (0.604 SPATA9 vs 0.229 RHOBTB3) is being used as if it were
> independent statistical confirmation, but L2G models are partly built on distance-to-gene
> features — the same signal that generates "nearest-gene" bias in the first place. A
> 0.604/0.229 split (not 0.9+/0.05) is a modest lead, not a decisive one, and it is not
> independent of the nearest-gene prior that produced SPATA9 as the candidate in the first
> place.
>
> FLAMES/MAGMA/PoPS/ABC — the actual orthogonal gene-prioritization methods — were all NA
> at this locus. So there is literally zero non-nearest-gene, non-L2G statistical evidence
> favoring SPATA9 over any neighboring gene.
>
> On the never-smoker FEV1/FVC colocalization: this is real and useful evidence that the
> locus acts on lung function through a mechanism that persists in never-smokers (i.e.,
> argues against pure smoking-behavior mediation for this locus as a whole). But it is
> gene-agnostic — it colocalizes with the region, not with SPATA9's expression
> specifically, and it sits equally well (arguably better, given RHOBTB3's stronger
> biological plausibility for a hypoxia/vascular mechanism) with RHOBTB3 or an unannotated
> regulatory element. Using it to bolster confidence in SPATA9 specifically is a category
> error the researcher should not make, and the synthesis-lead should not inherit that
> conflation.

### RHOBTB3 — WEAKENED, bordering on CONTESTED as an affirmative causal claim

> Not in the locus file's own candidate gene list at all — it exists in this evidence base
> only because Open Targets' internal L2G model surfaced it as second-place (0.229) at the
> same credible-set variant. Its Open Targets COPD association score (0.0845) is markedly
> lower than SPATA9's (0.225) — the structured-database evidence, on its own terms, does
> not favor RHOBTB3.
>
> The three literature citations for RHOBTB3 (PMID 24923387, 26215701, 40311591) plus the
> JEM paper (41385111) establish a real, replicated RHOBTB3–PHD2/HIF-1α degradation axis —
> but every single functional demonstration is in renal carcinoma, breast cancer, or
> UV-irradiated skin keratinocytes. None is in lung, airway, alveolar tissue, or any COPD
> model. The mouse LacZ expression paper (PMID 24923387) is explicitly noted by the
> verifier as not addressing respiratory tissue — so even the one paper positioned to show
> lung relevance affirmatively does not.
>
> HIF-pathway biology is plausibly relevant to COPD (hypoxia, pulmonary vascular
> remodeling) as a prior, but "plausible given a well-known pathway" is exactly the kind of
> narrative bridging that should be labeled speculative rather than evidentiary.
>
> The JEM paper's immune-evasion finding (CXCL10/CD8+ T-cell recruitment) is explicitly
> noted as NOT mediated by RHOBTB3 itself (it's a parallel STAT1/IRF1 axis) — this citation
> is close to irrelevant to RHOBTB3's mechanism and should probably be dropped from the
> RHOBTB3 evidence table rather than left in as supporting context, since it risks being
> skimmed as "RHOBTB3 → immune evasion" when the paper says the opposite. [Retained in the
> evidence table but marked CONTESTED in the skeptic-verdict column per this reasoning —
> orchestrator's editorial choice to preserve traceability rather than silently drop it.]
>
> Verdict: WEAKENED (bordering on CONTESTED as an affirmative causal claim). The RHOBTB3–HIF
> story is real molecular biology but three tissue-contexts removed from lung/COPD (cancer,
> skin, and a tumor-immune-evasion pathway explicitly independent of RHOBTB3). It should be
> reported as a mechanistically-plausible-but-untested alternative, not as evidence that
> "RHOBTB3 is relevant to COPD."

### Cross-cutting gaps flagged by red-team-skeptic (verbatim, original pass)

> 1. LD/nearest-gene bias is essentially unresolved. With all four orthogonal
>    prioritization methods (FLAMES/MAGMA/PoPS/ABC) NA, and the L2G split only 0.604 vs
>    0.229, this locus has no method that convincingly separates SPATA9 from RHOBTB3 (or
>    other unexamined neighbors). This should be stated plainly in the final table, not
>    smoothed over by the "statistically best-supported" framing.
> 2. No lung single-cell or bulk-tissue expression data (e.g., GTEx, Human Lung Cell Atlas)
>    is reported for either gene. This is a basic, easily-obtainable check that appears
>    absent from the researcher's evidence base and would materially inform plausibility
>    (if SPATA9 is essentially untranscribed in lung/airway tissue, that alone would be
>    strong grounds to downgrade it regardless of statistical scores). Recommend
>    synthesis-lead request this before finalizing.
> 3. Smoking-behavior GWAS (GSCAN) overlap check did not resolve — this is honestly
>    disclosed as inconclusive rather than omitted, which is good practice, but it means
>    smoking-mediation for this locus remains genuinely untested via the behavioral-genetics
>    route; only the never-smoker FEV1/FVC colocalization argues (at the locus level, not
>    gene level) against pure smoking-mediation.
> 4. Overclaiming check: the researcher's synthesis sentence ("supports SPATA9 as the
>    statistically best-supported gene") is a fair characterization relative to RHOBTB3
>    given the L2G score ordering, but risks being read by a downstream synthesis step as
>    stronger confidence than the underlying record (one 2011 nearest-gene mention + a
>    modest, partially-circular L2G margin + all fine-mapping methods NA) actually supports.
>    I'd ask that any final write-up retain the researcher's own "genuine, unresolved
>    tension" language rather than compress it away.
>
> Both genes got only shallow, prompt-derived scrutiny in this review (no independent
> searches succeeded due to exhausted search budget) — synthesis-lead should treat this
> contradiction-search as effectively not done for either gene and weight that accordingly,
> especially before any final "SPATA9 is prioritized" determination is locked in.

# ADDENDUM (2026-09-11, targeted re-opening): RFESD

## Step 1 — physical overlap confirmation (orchestrator, before any further work)

Per the re-opening protocol, RFESD's coordinates were independently confirmed via Open
Targets' `target.genomicLocation` field before dispatching any further work: RFESD
(ENSG00000175449) spans chr5:95,646,718-95,684,773 (GRCh38, strand +1), entirely inside the
5:95,550,001-95,800,000 locus window. Critically, the locus's own lead credible-set variant,
rs466748 (PIP=0.334, 5:95,674,324, originally annotated `gene=SPATA9` by this pipeline's own
VEP-style annotation), falls physically INSIDE RFESD's gene body — this position is
intronic to both SPATA9 and RFESD, which overlap/sit immediately adjacent to each other at
this locus. **Verdict: genuine physical overlap, not a build-error false positive** — the
re-opening therefore proceeded to full researcher/citation-verifier/red-team-skeptic
evaluation rather than being ruled out at step 1. This was independently re-confirmed by the
researcher via a separate Ensembl REST lookup, in agreement with the Open Targets figure.

## Citation verification for RFESD (citation-verifier, verbatim/near-verbatim)

All 4 RFESD citations were **VERIFIED** as to existence/general claim match, with two
caveats noted below. None provide any lung-, COPD-, or pulmonary-function-relevant
evidence — all are non-pulmonary (fish gonad, hepatocellular carcinoma, maternal
blood/autism, general tobacco/alcohol behavior with no locus-specific confirmation).

1. **Mkulo et al. 2025** (PMID 40694934) — VERIFIED. Abstract confirms rfesd as one of
   several testis-biased DEGs in a teleost fish gonad transcriptome, exact match to claim.
2. **Wang et al. 2022** (PMID 35681134) — VERIFIED. Abstract confirms the WGCNA/746-HCC-
   sample analysis, the 11-hub-gene signature including RFESD, and explicitly: "knockdown
   of RFESD inhibited cell proliferation in HCC cells."
3. **Zhu et al. 2020** (PMID 33159718) — VERIFIED for the core claim (RFESD one of six
   genome-wide-significant DE transcripts in maternal blood, ASD vs. TD). **Caveat:**
   whether RFESD is specifically a member of the paper's separately-described
   adaptive-immune/histone-modification/RNA-processing-enriched WGCNA co-expression module
   — versus merely appearing in the unrelated six-transcript DE list — **could NOT be
   resolved from the abstract** (full-text review not performed). Do not treat RFESD as
   confirmed to be part of that immune-enriched module.
4. **Liu et al. 2019 (GSCAN)** (PMID 30643251) — VERIFIED as to paper identity/general

   406 tobacco/alcohol-use loci) is UNRESOLVED from abstract-level access — an open gap,
   identical in kind to the same unresolved gap already flagged for SPATA9 in the original
   review.

No RETRACTED or NOT_FOUND citations among the RFESD set.

## RFESD — red-team-skeptic verdict: WEAKENED, bordering on excludable (verbatim reasoning)

> **Physical overlap vs. statistical evidence:** rs466748 (PIP=0.334) sitting inside
> RFESD's gene body is real and independent of any prioritization model, but physical
> containment of a variant inside a gene's introns is extremely weak evidence on its own —
> SPATA9 and RHOBTB3 were also implicated largely through positional/LD arguments rather
> than functional fine-mapping, and RFESD's overlap doesn't escape that same critique; if
> anything it deepens the existing LD/nearest-gene ambiguity by adding a third transcript
> whose body the credible variant physically touches. Overlap answers "is this gene a
> plausible mechanical candidate for regulatory disruption," not "is this gene supported by
> association or biology." Containment inside a gene body is weaker evidence than either
> the L2G score or the FEV1/FVC colocalization already documented for this locus, neither
> of which point to RFESD at all.
>
> **Does Open Targets L2G's repeated non-selection of RFESD count as meaningful negative
> evidence?** Partially, with an important caveat. L2G was queried at 15 independent
> credible-set instances (9 at rs153916 including the exact COPD GWAS GCST007692 and all 5
> FEV1/FVC-ratio GWAS at this locus, plus 6 more at the lead variant rs466748 itself) and
> RFESD never surfaced even as a scored (non-winning) candidate. RFESD never appearing at
> all across 15 queries is a stronger, more structural absence than a simple low rank, and
> is corroborated by two further independent negative findings (EBI GWAS Catalog "no
> results" for RFESD across any trait; zero PubMed GWAS/eQTL hits for RFESD). Three
> independent negative signals converging is more informative than any one alone. However,
> L2G's feature set draws heavily on functional annotation, chromatin state, and eQTL/pQTL
> data, and a gene with an empty UniProt function field and no eQTL catalog entries (RFESD)
> may simply lack the input features L2G needs to score it at all — a "sparse-annotation
> blind spot" rather than a considered rejection. This is a real limitation on how much
> weight the absence can bear. But it cuts both ways: a gene this functionally
> uncharacterized and this absent from every annotation-based prioritization tool, every
> disease-association database (Open Targets, GWAS Catalog), and the entire pulmonary
> literature also has essentially no case built for it — the researcher found nothing
> pulmonary at all for RFESD, not even a suggestive nearest-gene mention in a spirometry
> paper (which SPATA9 at least has, weak as that is).
>
> **Contradicting/contextual evidence:** No paper argues explicitly against RFESD at this
> locus (there is essentially no RFESD-COPD literature to contradict), so nothing rises to
> CONTESTED in the adversarial sense. But the two functional citations that do exist (Wang
> 2022 HCC knockdown; Zhu 2020 ASD maternal-blood transcriptome) are non-pulmonary and only
> weakly generalizable — the same "distant tissue, different disease" problem already
> identified for RHOBTB3's HIF-pathway citations, but arguably worse, because RFESD's
> citations don't even establish a coherent biological pathway the way RHOBTB3-PHD2-HIF1α
> does (RFESD's Rieske Fe-S domain function is structurally inferred, not characterized).
> The Human Protein Atlas single-cell data (erythroid-cell cluster, low immune-cell
> specificity) argues mildly against a neutrophilic/CD8+/macrophage-centric COPD mechanism
> under prior #3, though HPA itself flags this data as unreliable and it should not be
> over-weighted either way.
>
> **Overclaiming check:** The researcher's own synthesis ("no meaningful statistical-
> genetics, structural/developmental, or immune/inflammatory evidence base... genuine
> negative finding") is appropriately calibrated — it does not overclaim exclusion (a
> formally negative/absence finding is not proof of non-causality, and the researcher
> doesn't claim it is) and does not overclaim support. This is the most honestly-scoped
> writeup of the three genes at this locus so far. No CLAIM_MISMATCH-style overreach
> detected.
>
> **Does this change the SPATA9-vs-RHOBTB3 tie?** No. It does not become a three-way tie
> and does not resolve the existing tie. RFESD is clearly the weakest of the three
> candidates: zero COPD/FEV1 association evidence in any database queried, zero L2G support
> across 15 independent queries, zero GWAS Catalog presence for any trait, and no
> lung-relevant functional literature at all — a strictly worse evidentiary profile than
> either SPATA9 (2011 nearest-gene GWAS call + 0.604 L2G score) or RHOBTB3 (real, if
> non-pulmonary, molecular pathway + 0.229 L2G score). The physical-overlap fact is
> genuinely new and worth recording — any final write-up should note the credible-set
> variant is genomically ambiguous between at least two protein-coding genes (RFESD and
> SPATA9) — but overlap alone does not elevate RFESD to parity with genes that have actual
> (if weak) association or pathway evidence.
>
> **Recommendation:** retain RFESD in the writeup for completeness and to record the LD
> ambiguity honestly, but rank it distinctly below both SPATA9 and RHOBTB3 — closer to
> "excludable pending any future functional/eQTL data" than to a genuine contender. Flag for
> synthesis-lead: this characterization rests on a record with no independent
> contradiction-search performed (by researcher or skeptic, due to exhausted search
> budgets) — a future pass with search budget available should specifically check for (a)
> any lung eQTL/sQTL catalog entries (GTEx, lung-specific eQTL studies) for RFESD that Open
> Targets' L2G might be missing, and (b) whether RFESD and SPATA9 share a bidirectional
> promoter or overlapping regulatory element that would make them mechanistically
> inseparable at this locus.
