## CONTESTED evidence (preserved verbatim reasoning)

### SERPINA1 — PiMZ heterozygote spirometric/COPD risk: CONTESTED

This is the single most important nuance for this locus, flagged independently by both the researcher and the red-team-skeptic. Reasoning preserved in full:

**From the researcher's original findings:**
> "North et al., 2016... found a genuinely discordant/contested data point relative to the Molloy et al. and Al Ashry/Strange findings above and should be reported as such — it may reflect cohort differences (general population/never- and ever-smokers combined vs. smoking-enriched or family/case-control cohorts), confounding by height/growth pathways, or selection effects, but it is a real published finding that complicates a simple 'Z allele dose = worse lung function' narrative at the population level."
>
> "These two small, older, contradictory studies [Tarján 1994 positive; McDonagh 1979 negative] illustrate that the 'is PiMZ a COPD risk factor' question has genuinely been debated over decades, with the modern consensus (per the 2017 Al Ashry & Strange review and the larger Molloy et al. 2014 family study) leaning toward 'yes, especially in smokers,' but not unanimous, and the North et al. 2016 large meta-analysis complicates this further."

**From the red-team-skeptic's critique (verbatim):**
> "Spirometry concordance — more mixed than a first read suggests, and this deserves emphasis for synthesis. The case-control COPD literature (dominated by severe ZZ-deficiency framing) and the population-based spirometric literature diverge more than the write-up's structure implies:
> - Thun 2012 SAPALDIA (population-based, n>4600): NO overall main effect of PiMZ on lung-function decline.
> - North 2016 (n>19,000 meta-analysis): PiMZ associated with increased FEV1 and FVC — the opposite direction from AATD-risk expectation, prompting the authors to say the elastase-inhibition-benefits-lung-function model 'may merit re-evaluation.'
> - Only the small, case-control-design studies (Tarján 1994, n=28 pairs; Al Ashry & Strange review noting case-control designs show stronger risk than population-based ones) support the classic risk direction for heterozygotes.
>
> The researcher already labeled this contested and preserved all the discordant citations rather than cherry-picking — that is good practice and should be credited. But there's an inferential subtlety worth surfacing explicitly for synthesis: the GWAS case-control signal at this locus, being an allele-dosage association in a population sample, is very likely driven predominantly by the far-more-common Z-allele heterozygotes (MZ), not by rare ZZ homozygotes. The 'classic monogenic cause of AATD/emphysema' framing is airtight for ZZ homozygotes but is the wrong reference class for interpreting the population GWAS beta — for the heterozygote population that actually generates most of the GWAS signal, spirometric concordance is genuinely mixed-to-null, and one large study finds the opposite-direction effect on lung function. This doesn't threaten the gene call (SERPINA1 is unambiguously correct), but it should temper any synthesis language implying the GWAS association itself has been shown to act uniformly through reduced spirometric function at the population level."

**Resolution for synthesis:** This CONTESTED status does not change the causal-gene assignment (SERPINA1 remains HIGH confidence per the missense credible-set variant, mechanistic/interventional evidence, and fine-mapping support at the rare-variant/ZZ level). It does mean that claims about the population-level (heterozygote-driven) GWAS association acting uniformly through reduced spirometric function should be tempered, and North et al. 2016 / Thun et al. 2012 should not be dropped from any downstream synthesis in favor of only the concordant studies.

### PRIMA1 — CONTESTED in the narrow sense of "why did PoPS pick this gene," not because of any found pulmonary role

**From the red-team-skeptic's critique (verbatim):**
> "PRIMA1 is marked CONTESTED only in the narrow sense that its status as PoPS's top pick is itself the contradicting signal that needs to be resolved/explained in synthesis (i.e., why did PoPS favor a neuronal gene with no locus-relevant biology over the textbook causal gene sitting on the credible-set variant) — not because I found literature arguing PRIMA1 has a pulmonary role."

## Other minor flags (not CONTESTED, but preserved for completeness)

- **Nakamura 2011 (PMID 21778810)** states SERPINA1 is "the only gene proven to influence COPD susceptibility" — this is a dated (2011, pre-large-COPD-GWAS-era) superlative claim. Both researcher and skeptic flagged that this should not be over-relied upon given many additional COPD GWAS loci have since been established; it is retained in the evidence table as a historically accurate statement about that era's evidence base, not a current-day exclusivity claim.
- **Malerba et al. 2005 (PMID 16284217)** immune/inflammatory evidence (elevated sputum neutrophils/IL-8 in PiMZ carriers) is a small, underpowered study (n=10/group). Both researcher and skeptic agree it is mechanistically plausible and supportive but should not be weighted as robust independent immune evidence.
- **DeLuca et al. 2019 (PMID 31298815)** — the only SERPINA1-related finding in this paper (asthma association) did not survive multiple-testing correction (adjusted P=.058); retained in the evidence table explicitly as a negative/non-robust finding for completeness, not as positive evidence.
