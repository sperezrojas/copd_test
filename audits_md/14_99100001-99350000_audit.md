# Critique: Locus 14:99100001-99350000 (BCL11B / C14orf177–LINC02914)

**Search-budget disclosure:** This session's web-search budget was already exhausted
(200/200) before I could run independent contradiction searches (queries on "BCL11B COPD
GWAS lung function" and "rs807734 smoking initiation GWAS" both failed to execute). My
critique below relies entirely on the researcher's reported findings and the
citation-verifier's annotations — I was not able to independently confirm or refute
anything beyond what's already in the record. This is a genuine coverage gap for this
locus; if a deeper independent check is wanted (esp. confirming rs807734 in GSCAN
supplementary tables, or checking LD/position overlap between the Xu 2025 pneumothorax
signal and the rs807734 credible set), it should be flagged to synthesis-lead as unresolved
rather than assumed done.

## 1. Smoking-mediated mechanism — is it overclaimed?

Yes, partially. The only variant-level claim (rs807734 genome-wide significant for smoking
status in PMID 30643251/GSCAN) is CLAIM_MISMATCH — unconfirmed against the primary paper's
own reported hits, resting only on the GWAS Catalog's structured database record. That
record and the Open Targets gwas_credible_sets smoking-initiation scores (0.48–0.89) are
not fully independent lines of evidence — GWAS Catalog curation and OT's credible-set
overlap scoring both typically trace back to the same underlying smoking-initiation GWAS
summary statistics (GSCAN or its successors), so this is closer to one data source counted
twice than two convergent ones. That said, the directional signal (smoking-initiation score
consistently higher than the FEV1/FEV1-FVC scores: 0.48-0.89 vs 0.42-0.56/0.79) is a real,
if uncertain, pattern worth reporting. The correct framing is "a plausible, not yet
independently confirmed, smoking-initiation association that is at least as strong as the
pulmonary-function association in the same database," not "BCL11B is a confirmed
smoking-mediated locus." As currently written the researcher's synthesis leans toward the
latter framing more than the evidence supports.

## 2. Is BCL11B's COPD/lung-function candidacy well-supported?

No — this is the weakest part of the case. Every direct locus-table citation tying
BCL11B/14q32.2 to COPD or lung function (Sakornsakolpat 2019, Shrine 2019) is
CLAIM_MISMATCH/unconfirmed from the abstract. Zhao 2020's WGS study abstract names only
PIAS1, RGN, FTO as novel loci and is silent on BCL11B — not proof of exclusion (it may
simply not have been "novel" if already reported elsewhere), but it is not supporting
evidence either, and the researcher was right to present it as non-supporting. No dedicated
BCL11B-COPD colocalization/TWAS/MR paper exists at all (researcher's own negative finding).
What remains is: (a) database-only Open Targets credible-set scores, (b) "BCL11B is the
nearest gene and all 35 credible-set variants are intronic to it" — a positional/LD
argument, not a mechanistic or association-confirmed one. This is real supporting
circumstantial evidence but is thinner than the volume of citations in the writeup implies;
the case currently rests on architecture (nearest gene, intronic variants) plus
unverified/database claims, not on a single independently-confirmed literature association
with COPD or spirometry.

## 3. Does the pneumothorax GWAS (Xu 2025) change relative prioritization?

This is the most consequential new fact in the record, and it cuts against BCL11B. A GWAS
of primary spontaneous pneumothorax — a structural/mechanical lung-integrity phenotype, at
least lung-relevant even if not COPD — reports a genome-wide-significant 14q32.2 locus that
the paper's own authors attribute to C14orf177, not BCL11B. This is exactly the "implicates
a different gene at the same locus" pattern the task explicitly asks reviewers to search
for, and here it surfaced from verification rather than a deliberate contradiction search.
Important caveats that limit how far this should be pushed: (i) "14q32.2" is a broad
cytogenetic band; there's no confirmation in the record that Xu 2025's lead variant is in
LD with, or physically overlapping, the rs807734 credible set — this needs an explicit
position/LD check before treating it as the same signal; (ii) the phenotype (pneumothorax)
is mechanistically distinct from airflow obstruction/COPD; (iii) no functional follow-up
was done on C14orf177 in that paper (the functional work was on CBX7 at a different,
independent locus); (iv) single population (Chinese Han). So: too weak/indirect to flip
prioritization outright, but strong enough that it should stop the pipeline from treating
BCL11B as the presumptive causal gene by default. It's independent, published,
author-asserted evidence for the alternative gene — a materially different evidentiary
status than "PoPS also nominates it."

## 4. Is the immune-program evidence for BCL11B genuinely COPD-relevant?

Mostly no — this reads as generic, high-quality basic immunology/developmental biology
being stretched toward disease relevance. T-lineage commitment, MAIT17, ILC1/ILC2/NK
development, and RelB-Bcl11b in ILC2s are all real, well-verified findings, but all in
mice, none in COPD or cigarette-smoke models, and several not even lung-specific (skin
keratinocyte TSLP paper; corticospinal-neuron papers, which the researcher appropriately
flagged as an unproven bridge to nicotine biology). The one asthma paper (Mao 2024) is a
Th2-driven, OVA-LPS model — a different endotype from the neutrophilic/Th1/Tc1/Th17
biology more typically associated with COPD, though the MAIT17/Th17-Tc17 connection is at
least plausibly relevant given the stated prior that COPD inflammatory programs are
heterogeneous and can include Th17/Tc17. One inferential overreach to flag explicitly: the
claim "Bcl11b is pro-ILC2/pro-type-2-lung-pathology when de-repressed" is an extension
beyond what PMID 32203192 (RelB restrains ILC2s by suppressing Bcl11b) literally
establishes — the paper is about ILC2 differentiation control, not lung pathology outcomes
per se. Net effect: BCL11B is a broadly pleiotropic, LOF-constrained master regulator
across T-cell, ILC, skin, and CNS development. That pleiotropy is exactly what should raise
suspicion that it could sit near GWAS signals for many traits without being causally
responsible for the lung-specific one — the OT top-hit list (schizophrenia, bipolar
disorder, autism, restless legs, its own Mendelian immunodeficiency syndrome) supports this
concern rather than allaying it.

## 5. CONTESTED determination

**BCL11B: CONTESTED.** Reasoning: (a) every direct COPD/lung-function locus-table citation
is unconfirmed (CLAIM_MISMATCH) by the citation-verifier; (b) one large WGS study's
abstract is silent on it where a novel-loci list is being made; (c) the single
variant-level smoking claim is also unconfirmed, and where it is asserted (OT credible-set
scores), it favors smoking-initiation over pulmonary function, undercutting a
direct-lung-mechanism narrative; (d) most damning, an independent, verified, lung-relevant
GWAS (Xu 2025, pneumothorax) attributes the 14q32.2 signal to a different gene (C14orf177),
which is precisely the "implicates a different gene at the same locus" criterion for
CONTESTED. This does not mean BCL11B is wrong — it remains a strong LOF-constrained
candidate with real immunology and biological plausibility — but the currently-assembled
evidence base does not support treating it as the confirmed causal gene, and there is now a
specific, citable counter-claim. Recommend synthesis-lead down-weight confidence
accordingly and flag the LD-overlap question (does Xu 2025's variant overlap the rs807734
credible set?) as an open item for anyone continuing this locus.

**C14orf177/LINC02914: WEAKENED (not fully CONTESTED).** The Xu 2025 pneumothorax finding
is a genuine positive data point in its favor, but the overall case is still very thin
(near-zero Open Targets disease association, no functional validation in Xu 2025, and the
AML amplicon paper (Abbas 2014) is a mild negative signal — same 14q32 amplicon showed no
elevated C14orf177 expression while BCL11B did, albeit in a different tissue/disease
context that may not transfer to lung). Kunkle 2016 also explicitly favored BCL11B over
C14orf177 mechanistically within the same linkage interval. This gene should not be
dismissed given it is the PoPS pick and now has one independent supporting GWAS, but it is
not strong enough on its own to be prioritized over BCL11B either — both genes currently
have real but incomplete/contested evidence, and I'd flag this locus overall as needing the
LD/positional overlap check before either gene is confidently ranked.
