## Excluded citations (RETRACTED / NOT_FOUND / CLAIM_MISMATCH)

### 1. Sakornsakolpat P et al. 2019, Nat Genet. PMID 30804561, DOI 10.1038/s41588-018-0342-2
**Verdict: NOT_FOUND** (for the specific claim attributed to it)
- Claim attempted: that SOX6/11p15.2 is among the 82 genome-wide-significant loci reported in this
  major COPD GWAS meta-analysis.
- Citation-verifier: paper exists, is not retracted, but the abstract does not mention SOX6 or
  chromosome 11p15.2; could not check the supplementary loci table with available tools.
- Red-team-skeptic follow-up (independent GWAS Catalog REST API query): **resolved this as a

  genome-wide-significant associations, none mapping to SOX6 or chr11:16.15–16.45 Mb. This
  upgrades the open question to a settled negative finding: this locus's COPD signal is not
  independently replicated in the flagship COPD GWAS discovery paper.

### 2. Shrine N et al. 2019, Nat Genet. PMID 30804560, DOI 10.1038/s41588-018-0321-7

### 3. Xu K et al. 2020, Nat Commun. PMID 33082346, DOI 10.1038/s41467-020-18489-3
**Verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH**
- Claim attempted: that this paper's smoking-status meta-analysis (MVP+GSCAN, N=842,717)
  identified rs7932056 near SOX6 as genome-wide significant (P=1e-9, OR=0.969) for smoking

- Citation-verifier: paper exists, not retracted, title/scope confirmed (13 loci reported for

- Red-team-skeptic: independently reproduced the underlying fact via the EBI GWAS Catalog REST API

  independently by red-team-skeptic); it is excluded from the formal evidence table only because
  the citation-verifier's text-matching standard was not met against the paper's own abstract.
  Reported instead as corroborating structured-database evidence in the main evidence table (see
  "Independently re-confirmed primary-database finding").

## CONTESTED gene/claim, with red-team-skeptic's reasoning preserved verbatim

**Gene: SOX6 — CONTESTED component: the "direct pulmonary mechanism" framing of the researcher's
synthesis (the SOX6-to-locus gene assignment itself is NOT contested and is well supported).**

Red-team-skeptic's verdict, in full:

> The gene-to-locus assignment (SOX6) itself is robust and not in dispute. What is contested is the
> mechanistic interpretation: the researcher's findings, read together with the structural/immune
> citations, leave open a "direct pulmonary developmental/immune mechanism cannot be ruled out"
> framing that overstates evidence which is exploratory (Pniewska, single PBMC study) or
> developmentally/temporally distant (Cao, embryonic patterning). Meanwhile, converging
> statistical-genetic evidence (8/8 credible sets = smoking/mood traits, independently reconfirmed
> GWAS Catalog record for smoking cessation at the exact lead SNP, and confirmed absence from both
> flagship COPD and lung-function GWAS) makes smoking-mediation the dominant, best-supported
> interpretation, not a co-equal alternative.
>
> CONTESTED — not on the SOX6 gene call, but on the "direct pulmonary mechanism" component of the
> researcher's synthesis. Recommend synthesis-lead write this locus up as: SOX6 robustly
> implicated, association most parsimoniously explained by a smoking-behavior/neurobehavioral
> pathway rather than direct lung biology; structural/immune evidence noted as weak, exploratory,
> and insufficient to establish an independent pulmonary mechanism.

Specific critiques of the two citations driving the contested "direct pulmonary mechanism" framing
(both citations remain in the main evidence table as VERIFIED, but flagged there with this
caveat):

- **Cao S et al. 2023, Cell Res. PMID 37085732** (SOX6 as a distal/alveolar epithelial patterning
  regulator in human embryonic lung scRNA-seq): "Embryonic distal/alveolar epithelial patterning
  is developmental biology; adult COPD pathophysiology (emphysematous destruction,
  protease-antiprotease imbalance, airway remodeling, mucus hypersecretion) is a distinct, largely
  acquired, smoking-triggered adult process. If SOX6 truly had an obligate role in distal lung
  patterning, the more biologically expected phenotype would be a rare congenital/structural lung
  anomaly ..., not adult-onset susceptibility to a smoking-driven disease decades later. The
  statement 'a direct pulmonary developmental mechanism cannot be ruled out' is technically
  defensible ... but is doing rhetorical work disproportionate to the evidence — it should be
  presented as speculative/unresolved, not as counterweight evidence to the smoking-mediation
  case."

- **Pniewska E et al. 2015, Int Arch Allergy Immunol. PMID 25634111** (SOX6 induced in COPD-patient
  PBMCs after LPS stimulation): "A single exploratory PBMC/LPS-stimulation differential-expression
  study cannot establish disease specificity or mechanism: PBMCs are not lung
  tissue/airway macrophages/epithelium; LPS/TLR4 stimulation is a generic innate-immune challenge
  used across countless inflammatory-disease transcriptomic screens, not a COPD-specific
  perturbation; no replication cohort is cited; and a transcript-level change in an exploratory
  screen is exactly the kind of finding vulnerable to multiple-testing artifacts and
  reverse-causation/confounding by underlying smoking status of subjects .... This citation
  supports 'SOX6 expression differs in some immune-stimulation context in COPD-diagnosed subjects'
  — it does not support 'SOX6 has COPD-specific immune biology.'"

## Coverage caveat flagged by red-team-skeptic

> Only two structural/immune citations (Pniewska 2015, Cao 2023) appear to underpin the "biology"
> side of this locus's case. Given how strong and one-directional the smoking-genetics evidence
> is, worth stating explicitly in the synthesis that adult lung-tissue/airway-epithelium SOX6
> expression/functional literature (beyond PBMC/embryonic) was searched for and not found, rather
> than leaving the impression that coverage of that angle was exhaustive and simply came up empty
> elsewhere.

Per the researcher's own checkpoint (researcher.md): PubMed searches "SOX6 pulmonary fibrosis
emphysema" (0 hits) and "SOX6 lung epithelium airway development" (0 hits) were run and returned no
hits; this is stated as a genuine negative finding by the researcher, not an unexamined gap — noted
here to close the loop on red-team-skeptic's caveat.

## Other locus-disambiguation note (not excluded, included in main table as VERIFIED, repeated here for clarity)

Ziyatdinov A et al. 2019/2020, Eur J Hum Genet, PMID 31836859 — a smoking-by-ancestry
admixture-mapping study reporting a lung-function locus at cytoband 11p15.2-3 in African-American
COPDGene participants — was checked specifically because of the similar-sounding cytoband name.
Both citation-verifier and red-team-skeptic confirmed this paper's locus harbors PARVA and RAB17,
explicitly NOT SOX6. Retained in the main evidence table as a verified negative control to make
clear this is a distinct, nearby signal and should not be conflated with the SOX6 locus discussed
here.
