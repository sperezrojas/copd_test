## Full red-team-skeptic critique (verbatim)

**Disclosure of search limitation (from red-team-skeptic):** The agent attempted two
independent contradiction-searches (rs34712979/COPD replication; GSTCD/INTS12/NPNT
causal-gene fine-mapping) but the session's web-search budget was already exhausted
(200/200) before either returned results. The critique below is therefore based entirely
on re-analysis of the evidence the researcher already assembled (including the
Sakornsakolpat non-replication the researcher proactively surfaced), not on fresh
independent literature. This is a real gap: NPNT received no independent (reviewer-side)
search pass; everything below is a re-read of researcher-supplied material.

### (a) sQTL-colocalization mechanistic case (Saferali #4/#5) — real but narrower than framed

A 94% colocalization posterior probability is genuine statistical support that the same
causal signal underlies the COPD/spirometry GWAS and the splice-QTL, but colocalization is
not proof of causal mechanism — it's vulnerable to imperfect LD reference panels, residual
allelic heterogeneity, and it says nothing about whether the functional consequence (exon 3
inclusion disrupting an EGF-like domain) is correct. That functional claim rests on
AlphaFold structural modeling, which is a computational prediction, not a wet-lab
validation (no reporter assay, no knock-in mouse carrying the human splice variant, no
biochemical confirmation that the EGF-domain disruption alters NPNT-ITGA8 binding or
downstream signaling). Additionally, #4 (2020 preprint) and #5 (2025 ERJ) are the same
research group's preprint-to-publication pipeline on what appears to be substantially the
same GTEx/LTRC dataset — this is refinement, not independent replication. The convergent
GWAS association across independent cohorts (Hancock 2010 CHARGE, Wain 2015 UK Biobank,
Shrine 2019 multi-ancestry) is real independent replication of a locus effect on
FEV1/COPD; what is NOT independently replicated is specifically that NPNT (vs. a neighbor)
and specifically this splicing mechanism is causal. The researcher's framing conflates
"locus replicates across GWAS" (true) with "mechanism replicates across independent
groups" (not yet true) in places.

### (b) Sakornsakolpat 2018 non-replication (#6) — a real, only partially explained discrepancy

The researcher's methodological-differences explanation (TWAS/total-expression
colocalization vs. splice-junction-usage colocalization; severe COPD/emphysema vs. general
COPD/FEV1) is plausible and probably accounts for much of the discrepancy — a standard
eQTL-based TWAS would likely miss an isoform-usage-only effect with no net expression
change. But this explanation is asserted, not demonstrated (no re-analysis showing NPNT's
overall expression is genuinely flat while splicing shifts). It also leaves open an
alternative reading worth stating plainly: the NPNT splice mechanism may be specific to
lung-function/case-control COPD generally and not detectable in — or not relevant to —
severe/emphysema-predominant COPD, i.e. a phenotypic-subtype specificity limit rather than
a pure technical artifact. This should be carried forward as a genuine qualifier, not fully
dismissed.

### (c) GSTCD/INTS12 alternative-gene history — not fully dismissed for this specific variant

Obeidat 2013 and Kheirallah 2017 concern the original, broader 4q24 lead signal from
Hancock 2010, favoring INTS12 over GSTCD as the target of that eQTL. This is largely
orthogonal to rs34712979, which Wain 2015 explicitly describes as an independent signal
from the INTS12/GSTCD one, and which has a variant-specific, mechanistically legible
splicing effect within the NPNT transcript itself (cryptic splice acceptor, confirmed via
long-read isoform sequencing) — that is a genuinely strong distinguishing argument for
NPNT over its neighbors at this specific variant. However, the researcher's own note that
rs34712979 sits "near the INTS12/NPNT boundary" is a residual concern that was not closed
out: there is no reported check of whether rs34712979 is also an eQTL/sQTL for INTS12 (or
ARHGEF38/TBCK) in the same GTEx/LTRC lung datasets. Absent that negative control,
positional proximity to INTS12 remains a small but real unaddressed possibility of allelic
heterogeneity, even though the balance of evidence favors NPNT specifically for this
variant.

### (d) Pleiotropy — flag, don't over-weight, but one thread is biologically non-trivial

Most of the pleiotropy list (hypertension, depression, glaucoma, AF, information-processing
speed) looks like generic regional/LD-block pleiotropy from GWAS-catalog aggregation across
many independent signals in a gene-dense window, and shouldn't be read as evidence against
a lung-specific mechanism at rs34712979 specifically. The one exception worth flagging
rather than dismissing: NPNT-ITGA8 signaling has a well-established developmental role in
kidney branching morphogenesis (Npnt-null mice have renal agenesis), so the renal-agenesis
association is biologically coherent with NPNT's known developmental biology — but this
raises the question of whether the renal-agenesis-associated variant is the same one
(rs34712979, an adult regulatory/splicing SNP) or a distinct developmental/coding variant
in the same gene (allelic heterogeneity). The record as given doesn't resolve this, so it
should be flagged as an open question rather than either dismissed as noise or treated as
corroborating multi-organ pleiotropy of the single credible-set variant.

### (e) Contested status

NPNT would not be called outright CONTESTED (no paper argues NPNT is wrong and names a
clear alternative causal gene at this specific variant), but the case is weaker than
"definitively established" — Sakornsakolpat 2018 (#6) is a genuine non-replication at the
gene-prioritization level (different colocalization method, same broader disease) that the
researcher appropriately surfaced but only partially explained, and it should be explicitly
carried to synthesis as a live discordant data point, not resolved.

### (f) Other overclaiming / specificity notes

- The "no PubMed hits for NPNT + smoking" (prior #1 assessment) is absence-of-evidence,
  appropriately caveated by the researcher as such — good practice, not overclaiming.
- Wilson 2022 (#7) macrophage/BALF phenotype is in an acute LPS-injury model, not chronic
  COPD; researcher correctly flags this rather than extrapolating to COPD-specific
  neutrophilic/Th17 biology. No CD8+/Th17/COPD-specific single-cell evidence exists for
  NPNT — appropriately reported as a gap rather than papered over with generic
  "inflammation" language (consistent with prior #3's ask for specificity).
- Entries #9-11 (Guo 2025 IPF, Lee 2019 silicosis, Maekawa 2024 SSc-ILD) are
  fibrosis/occupational-lung-disease contexts, not COPD, and the researcher does flag each
  as "not COPD" — good — but synthesis-lead should be warned these cannot be used to
  bolster a COPD-specific structural argument, and note a potential directional tension:
  in IPF, reduced NPNT worsens fibrosis, whereas the COPD risk allele's effect is an
  isoform-usage shift (not simple reduced/increased total NPNT) — these are not obviously
  the same axis of effect, so citing both as generally "structural/developmental lung
  biology" support risks implying more mechanistic coherence than exists across these
  disparate disease models.
- The gnomAD constraint data (LOF-tolerant, oe~0.71) is consistent with — and reinforces —
  the sQTL/isoform-shift mechanism over a haploinsufficiency model; this is a genuine
  point of coherence worth keeping in synthesis, not overclaiming.

### Overall verdict for NPNT: WEAKENED

The locus-level GWAS evidence for 4q24/FEV1/COPD is robust and independently replicated
across cohorts (Hancock 2010, Wain 2015, Shrine 2019), and the splice-QTL mechanism
(Saferali et al.) is a genuinely elegant, variant-specific mechanistic story that
reasonably distinguishes NPNT from GSTCD/INTS12 at this particular credible-set variant.
But the researcher's framing edges toward treating this as a settled causal mechanism
when: (1) the mechanistic/functional evidence is single-group, preprint-to-publication,
and computationally (not experimentally) validated at the protein-structure level; (2)
Sakornsakolpat's non-replication in severe COPD/emphysema is a real, only partly explained
discordant result; (3) the INTS12-boundary proximity was never directly ruled out via a
negative eQTL/sQTL control; and (4) several of the "structural/developmental lung biology"
citations are from non-COPD fibrosis models and shouldn't be treated as directly
corroborating. None of this overturns NPNT as the best-supported gene at this locus, but
synthesis should present it as "well-supported, mechanistically plausible, not yet
definitively proven" rather than "established causal mechanism."

review.
