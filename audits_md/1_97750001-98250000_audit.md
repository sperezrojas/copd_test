## Genes marked CONTESTED by red-team-skeptic (reasoning preserved verbatim)

### MIR137HG — CONTESTED

Two red-team-skeptic passes were run on this locus (the first pass's web-search budget
was exhausted before any independent contradiction search could execute; a second,
follow-up pass attempted the specific unresolved searches and also hit exhausted
WebSearch budget, substituting a weaker Europe PMC full-text proxy search). Both passes
concurred on CONTESTED for MIR137HG. Full verbatim reasoning from both passes:

---

**PASS 1 reasoning (verbatim):**

"1. **Overclaiming.** No overclaiming from the researcher here — if anything the
researcher was appropriately conservative, explicitly labeling the absence of
COPD/FEV1 genetics literature and declining to treat the macrophage-polarization triad
as mechanistic support. That said, the underlying dossier is worth restating plainly:
there is **no COPD or FEV1 genetic evidence for MIR137HG at all** — not weak evidence,
zero. The only statgen hit (Ripke 2011, schizophrenia) is for a different disease
entirely, at the same chromosomal position by coincidence of LD/genomic proximity, not
because MIR137HG has any established respiratory role.

2. **Contradictory immune literature.** The three macrophage-polarization papers (Cao
2025, Liu 2023, Xu 2022) actively work against, not for, MIR137HG as a coherent
immune-mechanism candidate at this locus: they disagree with each other on direction of
effect (pro-M2/anti-inflammatory in acute lung injury vs. pro-M1/pro-inflammatory in
glioma), are drawn from three unrelated disease contexts (none of them COPD or even
chronic airway disease), and one (Cao et al.) is the only one with any lung-relevance
at all (acute lung injury, an acute/reversible model, mechanistically distinct from
COPD's chronic remodeling/obstruction). This is a genuinely 'mixed/contested' evidence
pattern, correctly characterized as such by the researcher, and should not be used to
argue for an immune mechanism.

3. **LD/positional bias.** MIR137HG is the textbook case the priors ask reviewers to
watch for: it physically overlaps the majority (~28/47) of credible-set variants by
sheer virtue of being large and sitting where the LD block is, but every single one of
those variants is low-PIP, non-coding-transcript/intronic, and MODIFIER impact, and
critically, no computational prioritization method (FLAMES/MAGMA/PoPS/ABC) picked
MIR137HG over DPYD despite that variant density. That's a meaningful negative signal
from the tools that do incorporate regulatory/expression evidence, not just an
absence-of-literature problem — it suggests the tools looked at this and didn't find
MIR137HG-specific support even though it's the more variant-dense gene in the region.

4. **Should MIR137HG be CONTESTED?** I'd actually push back slightly on defaulting to
CONTESTED in the strict sense the framework intends (i.e., 'credible contradicting
evidence found') — I did not find a paper that argues FOR MIR137HG and against which a
contradicting paper exists; rather I found an absence of any positive case to begin
with, plus internally-contradictory indirect evidence. This is better characterized as
WEAKENED-to-unsupported than CONTESTED in the adversarial sense, but given the
instructions treat contested/mixed indirect evidence as grounds for CONTESTED, and
given the three macrophage papers directly conflict with each other on
mechanism/direction, I'll mark it CONTESTED on the basis of that internal contradiction,
while noting clearly that this is contradiction within weak indirect evidence, not a
head-to-head rebuttal of a specific MIR137HG-COPD claim (because no such specific claim
exists to rebut).

**Verdict: CONTESTED** (specifically: contested/non-specific indirect immune evidence,
zero direct genetic evidence, unanimous non-prioritization by computational
fine-mapping tools despite physical variant overlap). This is a weaker candidate than
DPYD overall, not a stronger one — being 'not selected by any prioritization method' is
itself a strong signal against it.

**What would resolve it:** any COPD or FEV1 GWAS colocalization or eQTL evidence
showing MIR137HG expression is modulated by credible-set variants in lung or
lung-immune cell types; a within-locus fine-mapping analysis (e.g., functional
fine-mapping using lung eQTL/ATAC-seq) that can adjudicate whether the low-PIP
MIR137HG-overlapping variants have independent regulatory activity from the
DPYD-annotated variants, versus simply being in LD with the DPYD signal (most likely
scenario given PIP distribution — the lead variant and all high-PIP variants are
DPYD-annotated)."

---

**PASS 2 follow-up reasoning (verbatim):**

"**MIR137HG: CONTESTED (verdict unchanged).** My proxy searches found zero evidence
connecting MIR137HG to lung function, COPD, or smoking-behavior GWAS traits — its only
well-replicated GWAS signal remains schizophrenia (consistent with the original
researcher's framing). This is consistent with, and does not overturn, the prior
CONTESTED call; if anything it reinforces the concern that if MIR137HG (rather than
DPYD) is the true effector gene at this locus, the mechanism is far more likely
psychiatric/behavioral (potentially smoking-initiation-adjacent via shared
psychiatric-genetic architecture) than a direct pulmonary one — but I found no direct
GSCAN/Saunders hit to confirm even that pathway, so this remains speculative, not newly
substantiated."

## Search-coverage disclosure (applies to the whole locus)

Both red-team-skeptic dispatches for this locus disclosed that their WebSearch tool
budget was exhausted before or during their assigned contradiction-search tasks (pass 1:
0/2 queries executed; pass 2: 0/4 queries executed, substituted with a weaker Europe PMC
full-text proxy search via WebFetch). This means the following questions remain
genuinely open, not resolved by this review, despite dedicated attempts:
1. Whether this exact chr1:97.75-98.25Mb locus is among the reported genome-wide-
   significant loci in Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019 (COPD), Liu et al. 2019 (GSCAN
   smoking), or Saunders et al. 2022 (tobacco/alcohol) — checked only via abstracts,
   not full supplementary tables.
2. Whether a lung-function (FEV1/FEV1-FVC) GWAS (e.g., Shrine et al.) shows
   colocalization at this locus — no relevant hit found via proxy search, but this is
   weak evidence of absence given the search method's limitations.
3. Whether any published paper proposes an alternative candidate gene at 1p21.3 for
   COPD/lung function/smoking-behavior phenotypes beyond DPYD and MIR137HG — none
   found, but again via a weak proxy-search method.

If additional search budget becomes available for this locus in a future pipeline run,
these three items are the highest-value re-checks and could materially change the
confidence rating (though not necessarily the identity of the prioritized gene) in the
main evidence table.
