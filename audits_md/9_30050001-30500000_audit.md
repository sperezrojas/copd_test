# Audit trail (excluded / contested appendix) — locus 9:30,050,001-30,500,000 (GRCh38)

This file preserves everything excluded from, or flagged as contested within, the main evidence
table at `evidence_tables/9_30050001-30500000.md`, plus verbatim reasoning from citation-verifier
and red-team-skeptic, so a reviewer can audit exactly what was set aside and why.

## Excluded citations (RETRACTED / NOT_FOUND / CLAIM_MISMATCH)

None of the 11 citations produced by the researcher were RETRACTED or NOT_FOUND. One received a
CLAIM_MISMATCH verdict:

- **Hansel NN, et al.** "Genome-Wide Association Study Identification of Novel Loci Associated
  with Airway Responsiveness in Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease." Am J Respir Cell Mol
  Biol. 2015;53(2):226-234. PMID 25514360, PMC4566043, DOI 10.1165/rcmb.2014-0198OC.
  **Verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH.** The paper itself is real (confirmed: GWAS of airway/methacholine
  responsiveness among European-American COPD smokers from the Lung Health Study, with a
  genome-wide-significant chr9p21.2 intergenic signal near LINGO2). However, the researcher's
  cited specific SNP identifiers (rs10491678, rs683471, rs10813121), the exact top p-value
  (4.52×10⁻⁹), and the quoted statement that LINGO2's function is "currently unclear" could NOT
  be confirmed from the abstract or accessible full text — the abstract only supports a
  significance threshold of P<9.57×10⁻⁸. Verified 2026-09-11. **This citation is EXCLUDED from
  being counted as clean supporting evidence in the main table**, though it is still listed
  there (labeled CLAIM_MISMATCH / CONTESTED) for transparency about what the researcher
  originally claimed, per the pipeline's traceability requirement — treat it in the main table
  as "does not support this locus," per the skeptic's explicit recommendation below, not as
  even weak support.

No other citations were excluded. All other 10 citations were VERIFIED by citation-verifier
(see `evidence_tables/_status/9_30050001-30500000/verified_citations.md` for full verbatim
verdicts and reasons), though several of those verified citations are themselves off-target for
this locus (wrong gene paralog, wrong disease category, or purely contextual) — see CONTESTED/
WEAKENED section below for why they carry little or no weight despite being real, unretracted,
accurately-quoted papers.

## Genuine absences of literature (not citation-verifier failures — nothing was submitted to fail)

- **LINC01242**: zero PubMed-indexed literature found for this gene symbol under exact-match
  search. No citation exists to verify or exclude.
- **rs2031830** (the lead variant): zero PubMed-indexed literature found under any combination
  of search terms (smoking, lung function/FEV1, osteoarthritis, alcohol consumption). PubMed's
  search engine does not appear to index this rsID in any full-text/abstract-searchable article.
  The researcher could not corroborate or refute, against primary literature, the osteoarthritis
  and alcohol-consumption GWAS-Catalog associations that Open Targets attributes to this exact
  variant (see Open Targets summary in the main evidence table). This is reported as an
  unresolved evidence gap, not a finding either way.
- **No Hi-C / ABC-model / eQTL literature** was found (PubMed or bioRxiv/medRxiv) connecting this
  intergenic interval to LINGO2, ACO1, or any other more distant gene, consistent with the
  internal pipeline's own ABC-model prioritization returning NA at this locus.

## Genes marked CONTESTED or WEAKENED by red-team-skeptic (verbatim reasoning preserved)

### LINGO2 — CONTESTED

> "The Hansel et al. citation is functionally dead weight, not weak support. The verifier
> already flagged CLAIM_MISMATCH on every specific, checkable detail (rsIDs, p-value, the
> 'function unclear' quote). What's left after stripping the unverifiable specifics is: a paper
> found *some* chr9p21.2 signal near LINGO2 for methacholine airway responsiveness in smokers.
> But the researcher's own spatial check shows that signal (~29.55 Mb) sits 480kb-950kb from
> this locus's actual credible-set interval (30.05-30.5 Mb) — i.e., even taking the paper at
> maximal face value, it is evidence for a *different, non-overlapping* signal near the same
> gene, not evidence for this locus. This is a textbook LD/nearest-gene bias problem: the
> researcher appears to have found 'LINGO2 + lung' and 'LINGO2 is the nearest gene to our locus'
> and connected them, but the genomic coordinates don't actually support connecting the two
> findings. This should be treated as no supporting evidence, not 'weak' evidence."
>
> "The rest of the verified LINGO2 literature is neurobehavioral/movement-disorder, and none of
> it overlaps this locus either. Essential tremor, Parkinson's, neuroticism — none of these
> papers are anchored to rs2031830 or this credible-set interval; they're LINGO2-gene-body
> association studies for unrelated traits. They tell us LINGO2 is a pleiotropic, CNS-relevant
> gene, not that it does anything in lung."
>
> "Zero literature on rs2031830 itself is a genuinely important negative finding, not just an
> absence — given Open Targets' own indexed credible sets tie this exact variant to
> osteoarthritis and alcohol consumption (not COPD/FEV1-FVC), and prior #1 says smoking-mediated
> signals need scrutiny, the total absence of corroborating primary literature for either the OA
> or the alcohol association, combined with the absence of any FEV1/FVC association in Open
> Targets for this variant or LINGO2 generally, is more consistent with this being a
> pleiotropic/noisy signal picked up by a COPD-only (non-smoking-adjusted) GWAS than with a
> lung-specific mechanism."
>
> "No lung structural/developmental or immune/inflammatory literature at all — priors #2 and #3
> both come up empty."
>
> "Verdict: CONTESTED. Not because I found an explicit rival paper naming a different causal
> gene (I couldn't search), but because the one piece of evidence purporting to link LINGO2 to
> pulmonary phenotypes is both citation-verifier-flagged for detail mismatches AND independently
> shown by the researcher to be spatially non-overlapping with this locus. That is close to
> affirmative internal contradiction, not mere weak support — the paper actually undermines
> rather than supports a LINGO2-locus link once its coordinates are examined. Recommend the
> evidence table list Hansel et al. 2015 as 'does not support this locus' rather than 'weak
> support,' and flag rs2031830's OA/alcohol Open-Targets associations as unresolved/unverified
> against primary literature."

### ACO1 — WEAKENED

> "No overclaiming to flag beyond what the researcher already self-flagged — the researcher
> explicitly labeled the Shao et al. 2026 ferritinophagy review as 'weak/non-specific,
> glossary-level,' which is accurate and appropriately hedged, not overclaimed."
>
> "The Fukumoto (IPF) and Mirhadi (ACO2/NSCLC) papers are correctly characterized as off-target:
> IPF is a restrictive fibrotic disease mechanistically and clinically distinct from
> COPD/emphysema, and citing it as COPD-relevant evidence would be a disease-category
> conflation. The researcher did not make this error — it presented Fukumoto only as 'ACO1
> upregulated in IPF,' not as COPD evidence — but I'd flag for synthesis-lead that this citation
> carries essentially zero weight for a COPD locus determination and should not be allowed to
> inflate the evidence count for ACO1 in a summary table."
>
> "No ACO1-COPD or ACO1-lung-function GWAS/TWAS/coloc anywhere, and ACO1 is 1.88 Mb from the
> locus with no eQTL/Hi-C/ABC link — this is about as weak a candidate-gene case as exists in
> this pipeline; distance alone should make anyone skeptical, and the total absence of any
> regulatory-linkage evidence (ABC returned NA) means there isn't even an indirect statistical
> thread connecting the locus to ACO1."
>
> "Verdict: WEAKENED to the point of essentially holds-as-null. I'll call this WEAKENED rather
> than CONTESTED because there's no rival paper actively arguing against ACO1 (there's just
> nothing for it) — but the inference that ACO1 is a plausible candidate at all rests on
> physical proximity alone, with the two 'supporting' citations both about the wrong
> disease/wrong paralog. Synthesis-lead should not treat ACO1 as a meaningfully prioritized
> candidate."

## Skeptic's overall locus-level assessment (verbatim)

> "1. Smoking-mediation concern is well-founded and, if anything, understated. Open Targets'
> target-level scores for both flanking genes favor smoking-behavior traits over COPD/FEV1-FVC,
> and the *only* rsID-level, literature-independent data point available for the lead variant
> itself (its Open Targets GWAS-Catalog credible-set memberships) are osteoarthritis and alcohol
> consumption — not smoking directly, but two more behavior/complex-trait associations that have
> nothing to do with pulmonary mechanism either. This triangulates toward 'pleiotropic,
> behaviorally-linked genomic region' rather than 'lung-specific regulatory region,' even more
> than a pure smoking story would.
> 2. Prior #2 (spirometric concordance) is entirely unmet. No FEV1/FVC literature or database
> association for either flanking gene, and no spirometric paper genuinely overlapping this
> credible-set interval. This is a real absence, not a search-coverage gap, and it should weigh
> heavily against prioritizing any gene here.
> 3. Prior #3 (immune specificity) is simply not invoked by any of the evidence — correctly, the
> researcher didn't force an inflammatory narrative onto a locus with zero immune literature for
> any candidate. That restraint is appropriate.
> 4. Most parsimonious explanation: this is either (a) a statistical false positive from a
> non-smoking-adjusted COPD GWAS at a poorly-resolved locus (max PIP 0.094, ~110-variant credible
> set, no coding/splice variant), or (b) a real signal whose mechanism runs through
> smoking-related behavior/neurobiology (consistent with LINGO2's neurobehavioral literature and
> the variant's OA/alcohol GWAS-Catalog hits) rather than through lung structural or immune
> biology, or (c) a genuinely unresolvable gene-desert signal with no assignable causal gene given
> current data (my view is this is most likely, given fine-mapping failure across
> FLAMES/MAGMA/PoPS/ABC). I do not think the researcher's summary overstates the case — if
> anything 'LINGO2 has the most (still weak/indirect) rationale' slightly undersells how
> thoroughly the one lung-relevant citation for LINGO2 collapses under the verifier's and the
> researcher's own spatial check."
