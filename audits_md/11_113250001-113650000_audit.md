# Audit trail — locus 11:113,250,001-113,650,000 (nearest gene DRD2)

This file contains everything excluded from the final evidence table
(`evidence_tables/11_113250001-113650000.md`) plus the full, verbatim red-team-skeptic
critique, for traceability. It is intentionally kept separate from the evidence table so
the evidence table can be shared without also sharing the full audit trail.

## Excluded citations

**PMID 24004608 / DOI 10.1186/1465-9921-14-89 — Mizuta K, Zhang Y, Xu D, et al. "The
dopamine D1 receptor is expressed and facilitates relaxation in airway smooth muscle."
Respiratory Research, 2013.**

- **Verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH**
- **Researcher's original claim:** the paper states directly (citing prior literature)
  that "the Gi-coupled dopamine D2 receptor is expressed and sensitizes adenylyl cyclase
  activity in airway smooth muscle (ASM) cells" and that inhaled dopamine induces
  bronchodilation in asthmatic patients — offered as independent evidence that DRD2 has a
  genuine local pulmonary physiological role.
- **Citation-verifier's finding:** the paper's title and primary focus is the D1 receptor,
  not D2. "The abstract and background may cite D2 literature, but the paper's main
  finding is about D1, not D2 as claimed." The specific D2-receptor/adenylyl-cyclase
  statement could not be confirmed as accurately representing this paper's content from
  the material reviewed.
- **Disposition:** excluded from the evidence table entirely. Red-team-skeptic further
  flagged that this CLAIM_MISMATCH casts a "cloud" over an adjacent, structurally similar
  background citation used elsewhere in the researcher's findings (PMID 29606146,
  Matsuyama et al. 2018, which also cites "D1 and D2 both expressed on airway smooth
  muscle" as background) — that citation was retained in the evidence table (it was
  independently VERIFIED) but downgraded/flagged as weak/secondhand for this reason. See
  the evidence table's skeptic-verdict column for PMID 29606146.

No citations returned a RETRACTED or NOT_FOUND verdict for this locus. 29 of 30 citations
returned by the researcher were VERIFIED (see
`evidence_tables/_status/11_113250001-113650000/verified_citations.md` for the full
citation-verifier transcript).

## Gene-level verdicts (red-team-skeptic)

Neither gene was given a formal "CONTESTED" label by red-team-skeptic; both received
"WEAKENED" verdicts (TMPRSS5 described as "bordering CONTESTED"). Both are preserved here
in full for transparency since they materially affect how the evidence table's claims
should be weighted.

**DRD2 — WEAKENED.** "The locus-COPD association is real and well-verified, and DRD2 is a
biologically plausible dopaminergic gene sitting in a cluster with genuine smoking/
addiction relevance. But the specific inference 'DRD2 acts on COPD via pulmonary/airway
biology' overreaches the data: the direct pulmonary evidence collapses to one excluded
citation (Mizuta, CLAIM_MISMATCH), one secondhand background claim (Matsuyama), and one
exploratory/uncorrected subgroup finding (Tashkin) — against an explicit absence of
genome-wide FEV1/FEV1-FVC concordance. The immune evidence (gut, CNS) is not lung-relevant
and should not be counted as COPD-specific support. And the addiction/smoking literature
marshaled in DRD2's favor is compromised by good-faith contradicting evidence already in
the researcher's own findings (Huang 2008, McGuire 2011) suggesting ANKK1, not DRD2, is the
functionally causal gene in this cluster — and this credible set isn't even the classic
ANKK1/DRD2 Taq1A signal to begin with. Net: DRD2 remains the better-supported of the two
candidates, but primarily as a smoking-behavior-mediated (not pulmonary-biology)
hypothesis, and even that attribution to DRD2 specifically (vs. a neighboring gene in LD)
is contested, not settled."

**TMPRSS5 — WEAKENED, bordering CONTESTED.** "No COPD, airway, or lung-disease literature
exists for this gene at all. The best available expression data (dated but consistent
across four independent studies, one of which explicitly assayed and found no lung signal)
argues against, rather than for, lung relevance. LoF-tolerance (LOEUF 1.319) and its
position further from/outside the nominal locus further weaken the case. Its only support
is a tied L2G score, which is a statistical artifact of two similarly-distant flanking
genes rather than positive biological evidence. I'd stop short of full CONTESTED only
because no paper has directly tested and refuted a TMPRSS5-COPD hypothesis (as opposed to
just not finding lung expression), but the existing evidence leans toward disconfirming
rather than merely 'absent.'"

## Full red-team-skeptic critique (verbatim)

See `evidence_tables/_status/11_113250001-113650000/critique.md` for the complete,
unabridged critique, including:
- Overclaiming identified (Tashkin FEV1-decline finding, gut/CNS immune findings,
  distance-to-TSS argument, Matsuyama secondhand claim)
- Assessment of whether the ANKK1-not-DRD2 causal-gene caveat was adequately weighted
  (judged: no, under-weighted in the researcher's closing summary)
- Assessment of how the tied L2G score plus absent FEV1/FEV1-FVC/QTL evidence should
  affect prioritization confidence (judged: substantially, favors a smoking-mediated over
  pulmonary-biology interpretation regardless of which gene is causal)
- Assessment of whether TMPRSS5's CNS-restricted-expression argument is overclaimed
  (judged: reasonably solid despite its age; arguably under-stated as affirmative
  contradicting evidence rather than mere absence of evidence)
- Flags for synthesis: (1) unconfirmed whether this 11q23 region is genome-wide
  significant in the primary COPD GWAS (Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019) — a foundational gap;
  (2) suggested but not-yet-performed searches (modern GTEx/Lung Cell Atlas expression
  data, smoking-stratified/MR analyses, cluster-specific COPD fine-mapping papers); (3) no
  new citations submitted for verification from the critique pass.
