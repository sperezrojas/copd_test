### ADGRB2 — WEAKENED

"The fine-mapping case is real (PIP 0.33, highest L2G at most of 45 colocalizing credible sets) and the brain-restricted functional literature (Shiratsuchi 1997, Kee 2002, Meyer 2025, Speca 2017, Purcell 2017, Alsharif & Bhatnagar 2025) is consistently and exclusively neuronal — there is genuinely zero pulmonary biology for this gene. That part holds.

But the 'smoking-behavior/neurobiological pathway' narrative is overclaimed in one specific respect and under-specified in another:

- Overclaim risk: The Liu et al. 2019 GSCAN citation is doing more rhetorical work than the verifier allows. The verifier explicitly could not confirm rs1889571/1p31.1 is among the 406 reported GSCAN loci — it's cited as 'plausible background,' not proof. The researcher does flag this caveat at the end, which is appropriate, but the section headers and OT-context framing ('consistent with prior #1's smoking/neurobiological hypothesis') risk reading, on a skim, as more confirmed than it is. Synthesis-lead should preserve the hedge, not compress it away.
- Under-specified alternative: the OT colocalization list is not pure smoking — it also includes self-reported wheeze (2 UKB cohorts), ADHD/substance abuse, insomnia, educational attainment, adiposity/triglycerides, and externalizing behavior. This looks less like a clean 'smoking-initiation' signal and more like a broad externalizing/impulsivity-behavioral pleiotropy locus (smoking initiation, substance use, ADHD, insomnia, risk-taking traits cluster together in the psychiatric-genetics literature) that happens to also move smoking. That's a materially different story than 'this locus causes COPD via smoking' — it's 'this locus is a behavioral/reward-pathway pleiotropy node, of which smoking is one downstream consequence among several.' The researcher didn't articulate this distinction, and it matters for how confidently synthesis-lead can invoke prior #1.
- The wheeze colocalization is also worth a second look rather than folding into 'no respiratory phenotype': self-reported wheeze in UKB is heavily confounded with smoking, atopy, and asthma, and is not a clean COPD proxy — but its presence means 'zero respiratory signal' is not quite accurate. It's a respiratory symptom signal, of ambiguous specificity, not 'no respiratory signal at all.'

Net: the case for ADGRB2 as the plausible top locus-level candidate is fine-mapping-driven and behaviorally-mediated, but the specific 'smoking' framing is one plausible interpretation among a family of behavioral-pleiotropy interpretations, and the one literature citation invoked to anchor it (GSCAN) does not actually confirm the locus. This is a real, if modest, overreach."

### COL16A1 — WEAKENED (bordering CONTESTED on independence grounds)

"The researcher's own framing — 'weaker/overlapping colocalization consistent with LD-sharing rather than independent causality' — is the right call, and should be pushed further:

- ABC prioritization deserves less independent weight than its inclusion in the candidate list implies, not more. ABC (Activity-by-Contact) is a proximity/enhancer-linking method based on chromatin accessibility and contact frequency in whatever cell type(s) the model was run in — it is not itself disease-association evidence, and critically, the researcher's findings never specify what cell type/tissue the ABC model was trained/run on for this locus. If it's a generic multi-tissue or non-lung (e.g., blood, K562) panel, an ABC call for COL16A1 tells us almost nothing about lung-relevant enhancer wiring. Concrete gap: flag for a check on which cell type the ABC prediction used before giving it any independent weight.
- All COL16A1 functional literature is cross-tissue analogy (gut myofibroblasts/Crohn's fibrosis, glioblastoma invasion, skin photoaging) with explicit researcher caveats that these are indirect. No lung, airway, or protease/antiprotease-balance study exists for this gene specifically, despite ECM/fibrosis being a plausible COPD-relevant class of biology in the abstract.
- The OT context — COL16A1's disease profile 'substantially overlaps with ADGRB2's' and its L2G is lower than ADGRB2's at most (not all) of the 45 colocalizing credible sets — is the strongest argument that COL16A1 is riding the same LD block rather than being an independently implicated gene. This is exactly the 'LD/nearest-gene bias' concern this pipeline is designed to catch, and it applies here to COL16A1's ABC nomination as much as it would to a naive nearest-gene call.

Called WEAKENED rather than CONTESTED because no specific paper argues against COL16A1 or for a different gene over it — the doubt is structural (unresolved LD-sharing, unspecified ABC cell-type context) rather than a positive contradicting finding. Synthesis-lead should not treat ABC-prioritization as adding meaningfully independent support on top of the fine-mapping data without first resolving what tissue it was run in."

### FABP3 — WEAKENED (fairly dismissed)

"The researcher's dismissal is fair and, if anything, could be stated more sharply: FABP3 has literally zero locus-specific genetic evidence (no coloc, no L2G, not even a credible-set variant), and its OT 'COPD association' is explicitly a weak literature-datamining artifact (0.078 overall / 0.644 literature sub-score) with no genetic-datatype backing at all. PoPS is a polygenic-similarity method that nominates genes based on genome-wide functional profile similarity to other trait-associated genes — it is known to occasionally nominate biologically-plausible-sounding but locus-uncorroborated genes, and that appears to be exactly what happened here. The cardiac (Zhuang 2019) and CNS/Alzheimer's (Brosseron 2020) citations are correctly characterized as irrelevant tissue context, not evidence. No overreach here — one of the cleaner, well-calibrated dismissals in the set."

### SPOCD1 — HOLDS (deprioritized, not contested)

"Correctly deprioritized. Not physically absent from the region but carries no fine-mapping, no coloc, no expression, and no lung biology signal. The one immune-relevant citation (Gan et al. 2023, colorectal cancer macrophage polarization) is appropriately flagged by the researcher as an oncology tumor-microenvironment context that should not be over-interpreted as COPD-relevant immunology. The inference ('weak candidate, no meaningful support') matches the evidence presented without overreach."

SPOCD1 is included here for completeness (it was researched as a region-overlapping gene not flagged by any prioritization method) but is not part of the main evidence table since no candidate-gene-level claim about it rose above "weak/unsupported" and it carries no citation whose claim bears meaningfully on COPD risk.

## Cross-cutting issues flagged by red-team-skeptic for whoever reviews this locus next

1. No gene at this locus has any FEV1/FVC or COPD case-control colocalization despite 45 tested credible-set pairs at the lead variant — an important negative finding (pipeline prior #2: "a COPD association without supporting pulmonary-function evidence warrants consideration of alternative pathways"). This locus is a textbook case of that warning and should be stated plainly, not softened.
2. Open question, not resolved within this pipeline's available data: what cell type/tissue underlies the COL16A1 ABC-prioritization call. If resolved to a lung-relevant tissue, this could raise COL16A1's standing relative to ADGRB2.
3. The full Open Targets trait-colocalization pattern (smoking, wheeze, ADHD, substance abuse, insomnia, educational attainment, adiposity, externalizing behavior) is at least as consistent with a broad behavioral/impulsivity-pleiotropy locus as with a clean, smoking-specific mechanism. This nuance should be preserved in any downstream reuse of this locus's conclusions rather than compressed into "smoking pathway."
4. Given the complete absence of independent lung biology for all three protein-coding genes in the region, and the absence of any spirometric colocalization, this locus overall looks like a case where the COPD GWAS signal is plausibly behavior/pleiotropy-mediated (prior #1) rather than reflecting direct pulmonary mechanism — but that conclusion should be attributed to convergent circumstantial evidence (OT trait pattern + neuronal-only functional literature + absence of spirometric coloc), not to any single confirmed literature citation, since none exists.
