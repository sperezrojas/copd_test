# Audit trail — locus 3:168850001-169200000 (lead variant rs879394, nearest gene MECOM)

This file preserves everything excluded from the main evidence table
(`evidence_tables/3_168850001-169200000.md`), plus the full verbatim red-team-skeptic
critique, for traceability. Hand this out only alongside internal review, not to external
collaborators who only need the evidence table itself.

## Excluded citations (CLAIM_MISMATCH)

### 1. Shrine N, Guyatt AL, Erzurumluoglu AM, et al. "New genetic signals for lung function
highlight pathways and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease associations across multiple
ancestries." Nature Genetics, 2019. PMID: 30804560; DOI: 10.1038/s41588-018-0321-7
(Author Correction: PMID 31110354, DOI 10.1038/s41588-019-0438-3).

**Researcher's original claim:** This large lung-function GWAS (400,102 individuals, 279
signals, 139 novel) established a joint lung-function-variant score strongly predictive of
COPD, generalizable across smokers and never-smokers and across ancestries; the 3q26.2/MECOM
region is among the loci contributing to this signal set.

**Citation-verifier verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH.** Verbatim reasoning: "Article exists. Abstract
confirms core findings: '400,102 individuals...279 lung function signals total, with 139 of
which are new' and variants 'strongly predict COPD...generalized across smokers,
never-smokers, and ancestral groups.' However, the specific claim that 'the 3q26.2/MECOM
region is among the loci contributing to this signal set' is NOT mentioned in the abstract.
MECOM is not listed. The broader GWAS findings are verified, but the MECOM-specific claim
cannot be supported from the abstract text."

**Why excluded:** The paper's overall existence and general findings are real, but the
specific attribution to MECOM/3q26.2 was not confirmed (may exist only in a supplementary
table not checked at abstract level). Per pipeline rule, only VERIFIED citations are placed
in the main table; this one is excluded to avoid overclaiming a locus-specific attribution
that hasn't actually been confirmed.

### 2. Higbee DH, Lirio A, Hamilton F, et al. "Genome-wide association study of preserved
ratio impaired spirometry (PRISm)." European Respiratory Journal, 2024. PMID: 38097206;
DOI: 10.1183/13993003.00337-2023.

**Researcher's original claim:** A GWAS of PRISm found strong genetic correlation between
PRISm and spirometric COPD; this paper did NOT specifically flag MECOM/3q26.2 among its
novel PRISm signals.

**Citation-verifier verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH.** Verbatim reasoning: "Article exists. The
researcher claimed this paper 'did NOT specifically flag MECOM/3q26.2/rs879394 among its
novel PRISm signals,' but the abstract explicitly lists 'rs7652391 (nearest gene MECOM)' as
one of four novel signals for PRISm: 'Four of the signals...rs7652391 (MECOM)...have not
been described in association with lung function before.' MECOM IS mentioned. The
researcher's statement that it was not flagged is incorrect."

**Why excluded, and why it is not simply promoted to VERIFIED instead:** The underlying paper
and the verifier's corrected quote are both real and accurate — there genuinely is a novel
MECOM-nearest PRISm GWAS signal (rs7652391) reported in this paper. However, this specific
positive claim ("MECOM is a novel PRISm signal") was never asserted by the researcher and
run through the researcher-claim -> verifier-check pipeline as such; it was only surfaced as
a correction to a different (negative) claim the researcher made. To preserve the discipline
that every evidence-table row traces to a claim that was positively made by the researcher
and positively verified as stated, this is logged here rather than added to the main table.
The orchestrator's synthesis section explicitly flags this as a caveat/consideration (not a
new claim) — see the "Prioritized gene" section of the evidence table.

**Red-team-skeptic's take (verbatim, from critique.md):** "the Higbee et al. 2024 PRISm
signal...is important and under-weighted in the current framing. PRISm is a restrictive/mixed
pattern (reduced FEV1, preserved FEV1/FVC), mechanistically distinct from — and in some
models antagonistic to — classic obstructive COPD pathology. A locus that shows up in both
obstructive (COPD, FEV1/FVC) and restrictive (PRISm) spirometric contexts is more consistent
with a generic 'reduced/altered lung growth or size' mechanism than a COPD-specific
obstructive-pathology mechanism...This should be flagged explicitly to synthesis-lead as a
complicating, not simply supportive, data point."

### 3. Bard-Chapeau EA, Szumska D, Jacob B, et al. "Mice carrying a hypomorphic Evi1 allele
are embryonic viable but exhibit severe congenital heart defects." PLoS ONE, 2014.
PMID: 24586749; DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0089397.

**Researcher's original claim (full, before narrowing):** Evi1 (Mecom) hypomorphic mice show
embryonic hematopoietic and cardiac developmental phenotypes and perinatal lethality from
congenital heart defects; the abstract also notes EVI1 overexpression has been reported in
several epithelial cancers including lung cancer.

**Citation-verifier verdict: CLAIM_MISMATCH** (on the full claim as stated). Verbatim
reasoning: "Abstract confirms hypomorphic Evi1 mice 'die during the perinatal period from
congenital heart defects' and exhibit hematopoietic phenotypes. However, the researcher
claimed 'the abstract also notes EVI1 overexpression has been reported in several epithelial
cancers including lung cancer.' The abstract I retrieved contains no mention of cancer,
epithelial malignancies, or lung cancer. The cardiac/hematopoietic phenotypes are verified,
but the cancer claim cannot be supported from the abstract."

**Disposition:** Rather than excluding this citation entirely, the orchestrator narrowed the
claim to only the verified sub-claim (cardiac/hematopoietic developmental phenotypes in
hypomorphic Evi1 mice) and included that narrowed version in the main evidence table. The
"lung cancer overexpression" sub-claim is dropped and logged here only — it should NOT be
treated as supporting evidence for a lung-specific mechanism for MECOM.

## CONTESTED gene: LINC01997

The red-team-skeptic's full verbatim reasoning (from critique.md) on why LINC01997 is
CONTESTED as a candidate gene at this locus:

"**Call: CONTESTED (evidentiary, not by rebuttal citation).** I want to be precise about what
kind of 'contested' this is: there is no specific paper arguing against LINC01997 (none could
exist, given zero literature), so this doesn't meet the rubric's contradicting-citation bar
in the conventional sense. But the convergent structural evidence — 25/25 independent L2G
calls favoring MECOM, zero functional or disease literature for LINC01997 despite it being
the nearest transcript — constitutes a real, if indirect, case against LINC01997 as the
causal gene at this locus. Recommend synthesis-lead treat LINC01997 as de-prioritized/not
currently supportable, while flagging the residual algorithmic-bias caveat above rather than
treating the L2G non-selection as fully dispositive."

The "residual algorithmic-bias caveat" referenced (verbatim): "L2G models are known to be
trained on features (functional annotation density, known eQTLs, protein-coding gene
databases) that are systematically sparser for lncRNAs, so consistent non-selection could
partly reflect algorithmic/annotation bias against lncRNAs generally, not solely true
non-causality. This is a second-order concern but should be disclosed."

## MECOM: WEAKENED call (not itself excluded, but flagged for caution)

MECOM's citations are all VERIFIED (or verified-in-narrowed-form) and remain in the main
evidence table, but the red-team-skeptic issued an explicit WEAKENED verdict rather than
endorsing MECOM at face value. Full verbatim critique preserved below (also in critique.md):

"**Call: WEAKENED.** The lung-function/COPD statistical genetics case for MECOM is real and
not smoking-mediated (satisfying priors 1-2), and L2G robustly disfavors LINC01997 as an
alternative. But the inference is overreaching if framed as 'MECOM causes COPD via a known
lung-structural or inflammatory mechanism' — most supporting literature is hematopoietic/
leukemia background not specific to this locus, one citation (Paul 2011) is at a wholly
different locus and should not be used as locus-specific mechanism, no eQTL/sQTL evidence
exists linking the credible set to MECOM expression, broad pleiotropy (height, education,
platelet volume, renal, brain imaging) suggests a generic developmental/growth pathway is at
least as plausible as a COPD-specific one, and the PRISm (restrictive-pattern) association is
a genuine complicating factor for 'COPD-specificity' that should be surfaced, not absorbed as
simple confirmatory evidence."

## Search-coverage caveat (applies to the whole locus)

Both the researcher and the red-team-skeptic reported exhausting their WebSearch budget
mid-task during this run:
- Researcher: "WebSearch was unavailable for most of the session (shared budget exhausted),
  which limited my ability to cross-check smoking-GWAS association claims and LINC01997
  characterization beyond PubMed/WebFetch attempts."
- Red-team-skeptic: "Both of my attempted independent contradiction-searches...failed
  outright — the session's web search budget was already exhausted (200/200) before either
  query returned results. Neither MECOM nor LINC01997 received any independent search pass
  from me (not even a shallow one)."

**Recommended follow-up if this locus is revisited:** (1) search for a lung-specific eQTL or
sQTL for MECOM (GTEx lung, Lung eQTL Consortium) — none was found in this run; (2) search for
any published alternative-gene proposals at 3q26.2 for lung-function GWAS (e.g., discussion
of TERC, SKIL, or other neighboring genes); (3) directly confirm or refute a
smoking-initiation/cigarettes-per-day GWAS association for this locus via GWAS Catalog or
GSCAN consortium data, which neither the researcher nor Open Targets could resolve in this
run.
