## Genes marked CONTESTED by red-team-skeptic

### D2HGDH — CONTESTED

Verbatim from red-team-skeptic:

> This is the locus's biggest overclaim risk. D2HGDH's "highest OT COPD score" is being
> used to call it "the strongest statistical-genetics candidate," but that score is
> entirely composed of ~50 independent GWAS credible sets for asthma, allergic rhinitis,
> atopic dermatitis, eosinophil count/fraction, and hayfever — never COPD directly, and no
> FEV1/FEV1-FVC support is reported either. Per prior #2, a COPD case-control signal
> without spirometric support should be viewed skeptically for alternative pathways; here
> it's worse — the actual supporting genetic signal is for a completely different, Th2/
> eosinophilic/atopic disease cluster. The researcher's own Hayden et al. 2018 citation
> (PMID 30373671) is doing more rhetorical work than its content supports: it shows
> childhood-asthma history predicts adult COPD in smokers and that *other, named* asthma
> loci (IL1RL1, IL13, GSDMB, C11orf30-LRRC32) associate with childhood asthma in a COPD
> cohort — it says nothing about D2HGDH and was not designed to test this locus. Using it
> as a bridge to argue D2HGDH's COPD relevance via asthma-COPD overlap is exactly the kind
> of citation-relevance overreach the instructions ask me to flag: the citation is accurate
> (verifier confirmed it), but its deployment here strengthens the D2HGDH argument beyond
> what it can support. The more parsimonious alternative reading: this locus (via
> D2HGDH/GAL3ST2, its consistent OT-favored gene pair) drives an eosinophilic/atopic
> phenotype, and its appearance in the COPD GWAS could reflect case misclassification/
> comorbid asthma contamination in COPD case ascertainment (a known issue in large-scale,
> often self-report or ICD-code-based COPD GWAS) rather than a neutrophilic/pulmonary-
> obstruction mechanism. I'd mark this CONTESTED: not because D2HGDH lacks a real genetic
> signal at this locus, but because the signal, on its own terms, argues for an allergic/
> atopic/eosinophilic-disease gene that has been re-labeled as a COPD candidate largely on
> the strength of an aggregate score and an inapplicable precedent citation. Synthesis-lead
> should treat D2HGDH as "plausible pleiotropic/comorbidity confound with atopic disease"
> rather than "top COPD candidate."

## Genes marked WEAKENED by red-team-skeptic (not full CONTESTED, but flagged as
overclaimed/insufficiently supported relative to how the researcher framed them)

### ATG4B — WEAKENED

Verbatim from red-team-skeptic:

> The "strongest circumstantial case" framing overreaches on three counts. First, the lead
> variant's own PIP is only 0.046 — very low, meaning the fine-mapping itself is highly
> uncertain and most posterior mass sits elsewhere (including on D2HGDH/GAL3ST2/ING5-linked
> variants). Calling ATG4B "strongest" when it isn't even confidently the top variant-level
> candidate within its own credible set overstates confidence. Second, there is no
> GWAS/coloc/TWAS/MR evidence linking ATG4B to COPD or FEV1/FEV1-FVC at all — the entire
> case rests on Chen et al. 2008 (PMID 18830406), which is about cigarette-smoke-extract-
> induced autophagy via Egr-1/E2F-4 and an *Egr-1* (not *Atg4b*) knockout mouse phenotype.
> That's real pulmonary biology, but it is two steps removed from this locus: no ATG4B
> genetic variant, human or mouse, is shown to modulate this pathway or COPD risk. Third,
> and most concerning, OT's own L2G analysis at the exact lead SNP (rs62190299) assigns it
> to ATG4B most confidently for a **BMI** credible set (0.682), not COPD — i.e., the one
> piece of independent gene-assignment evidence for this precise variant points to a
> metabolic/adiposity trait, not lung. That raises an alternative hypothesis (adiposity-
> mediated pleiotropy, or a genuinely non-lung regulatory effect) that the researcher's
> writeup doesn't engage with. Note: the CS-induced-autophagy mechanism is a smoking-
> *exposure*-dependent pulmonary injury pathway, not a smoking-*initiation*/nicotine-
> dependence confound (prior #1) — that distinction is fine and should be preserved — but
> it does not rescue the missing genetic link. Net: plausible pathway, zero gene-specific
> genetic evidence, and the one relevant L2G data point argues against a lung-specific
> mechanism at this exact variant.

### GAL3ST2 — WEAKENED

Verbatim from red-team-skeptic:

> The Zhu et al. 2026 PNAS finding (PMID 41774787) is genuinely the most airway-tissue-
> specific, mechanistically concrete paper at this locus (primary human airway epithelial
> cells, AAV delivery, antiviral glycan-sulfation effect relevant to virally-triggered
> exacerbations). But "worth flagging prominently" overstates what's actually known: (1)
> GAL3ST2 has never been tested in a COPD cohort, COPD tissue, or COPD model; (2) direction
> of effect is unestablished — it's not shown whether the COPD-associated allele increases
> or decreases GAL3ST2 function/expression, so even granting the mechanism, whether it
> would be risk-conferring or protective for COPD/exacerbation is unknown; (3) GAL3ST2's
> own OT COPD score (0.0257) is lower than D2HGDH's, and it is the *secondary* gene in most
> of the same allergic/eosinophil credible sets that dominate this locus, again raising the
> eosinophilic/atopic-pleiotropy alternative rather than a COPD-specific one. This is a good
> mechanistic hypothesis to retain for follow-up, not a leading candidate to elevate above
> better-genetically-supported (even if trait-mismatched) genes.
