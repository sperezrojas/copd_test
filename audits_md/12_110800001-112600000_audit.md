## Excluded citations

**PMID 20301303** (cited under PTPN11, background reference for Noonan syndrome /
PTPN11 gain-of-function / pulmonary valve stenosis). Citation-verifier's verbatim verdict:
"No PubMed record found for PMID 20301303. GeneReviews chapters are indexed in NCBI Bookshelf
but may not have traditional PMIDs. Unable to verify this citation via standard PubMed search."

**Consequence:** the "PTPN11 gain-of-function causes Noonan syndrome with pulmonary valve

this review. Per red-team-skeptic: "the Noonan-syndrome rationale that likely motivated
PTPN11's inclusion has no surviving citation post-verification — this should be surfaced to
synthesis-lead explicitly, since the researcher's ranking... may be resting partly on a claim
that no longer has literature support."

### CLAIM_MISMATCH — excluded as direct evidence for the gene claimed

**PMID 34230638** (Morrison J et al., Genet Med 2021;23(11):2213-2218 — cited under NAA25).
Citation-verifier's verbatim verdict: "Article identifies pathogenic missense variants in
**NAA20**, not NAA25. NAA20 is the catalytic subunit and NAA25 the auxiliary subunit of the
same NatB complex, so the researcher's framing... is directionally accurate about the complex
biology, but the paper's disease-causing variants and patient phenotypes are in NAA20, NOT
NAA25 itself. Flag as CLAIM_MISMATCH: do not cite this paper as direct evidence of NAA25
pathogenicity/phenotype; it is only indirect (complex-partner) evidence." Retained in the main
evidence table ONLY as indirect complex-biology context, explicitly labeled as not direct
NAA25-specific evidence, per citation-verifier's guidance.

### Not peer-reviewed — preprint only, excluded from main evidence table

**PMID 37662190** (Berry SB et al., "Disruption of Aldehyde Dehydrogenase 2 protects against
bacterial infection," bioRxiv preprint, cited under ALDH2 immunology). Citation-verifier's
verbatim verdict: "bioRxiv metadata shows published_doi: 'NA' (not formally published in a
peer-reviewed journal as of verification). Posted 2023-08-26, still preprint status. Preprint
abstract does support the claim about Aldh2-deficient macrophages/enhanced TB control, but this
is unvetted (non-peer-reviewed) research and MUST be labeled as a preprint, not treated as
equivalent to a peer-reviewed finding." The paper exists, is not retracted, and its claim
(Aldh2-deficient macrophages/mice show enhanced control of intracellular bacterial infection,
including increased resistance to pulmonary M. tuberculosis in a hypersusceptible model) is
accurately represented by the researcher — but because it has not cleared peer review and the
pipeline's evidence table is restricted to VERIFIED (peer-reviewed-equivalent) citations, it is
excluded from the main table pending formal publication. Synthesis-lead/study team may wish to
track this preprint for future publication, as it is the only immune/host-defense-specific
ALDH2-lung mechanistic finding identified in this search.

## CONTESTED gene designations (red-team-skeptic, verbatim reasoning preserved)

### HECTD4 — CONTESTED with PTPN11 and RPL6
"HECTD4 — Top L2G in its own set (0.803) but PTPN11 and RPL6 are unresolved co-candidates in
the *same* credible set, and the only mechanistic paper (Lee et al.) is a hepatic (liver
CYP2E1/lipogenesis/ethanol) mechanism, explicitly not lung. Zero lung/immune literature
otherwise (both explicit negative findings). The researcher's mid-tier ranking is reasonable,
but 'HECTD4' as a named causal gene is not distinguishable from PTPN11/RPL6 by the evidence
presented — this is a straightforward LD/nearest-plausible-gene ambiguity that should be marked
CONTESTED with PTPN11 and RPL6, not silently resolved by picking the highest raw L2G number.
RPL6's extreme constraint (LOEUF 0.176, core ribosomal protein) is itself evidence against RPL6
being the true regulatory target of a common GWAS variant, which by elimination modestly favors
HECTD4 or PTPN11 over RPL6 specifically — but that argument is not made explicit by the
researcher and should be."

### PTPN11 — CONTESTED with HECTD4 and RPL6
"PTPN11 — This is the second unresolved LD case. PTPN11 is a runner-up in the HECTD4-top set
(never top gene in its own right), and the citation-verifier already rejected the sole
disease-mechanism citation (GeneReviews Noonan/PMID 20301303, not found in PubMed) — meaning
the 'Noonan syndrome pulmonary valve stenosis' plausibility argument the researcher likely
wanted to use has *no verified citation at all*. The three verified PTPN11 papers are all
hematology/oncology (HSC self-renewal, CD8+ T-cell/Runx1, JMML) with zero lung relevance. Given
PTPN11 is extremely LoF-constrained (LOEUF 0.156) — a profile more consistent with a highly
deleterious rare-variant disease gene (Noonan syndrome) than a common GWAS regulatory hit —
there's a real risk that PTPN11's high L2G is a statistical fine-mapping artifact from
proximity to a constrained, well-annotated gene rather than genuine common-variant causal
biology. CONTESTED with HECTD4/RPL6 for the LD ambiguity, and separately flagged: the
Noonan-syndrome rationale that likely motivated PTPN11's inclusion has no surviving citation
post-verification."

### ATXN2 — verging on CONTESTED with SH2B3 and PHETA1
"ATXN2 — Same pattern as SH2B3/CUX2: zero COPD/lung statgen and zero immune literature; the two
verified papers... describe metabolic (BCAA/fatty acid/liver), obesity/T1D/BP/neurodegenerative
(polyQ) biology. ATXN2 shares its own top-L2G credible set with PHETA1/SH2B3 as runners-up —
another unresolved LD ambiguity. There is no lung-specific thread at all here; the case rests
entirely on the L2G score. WEAKENED, verging on CONTESTED for LD ambiguity with SH2B3/PHETA1 in
its own credible set."

### MAPKAPK5 — bordering on CONTESTED (paralog-rationale undermined by its own best evidence)
"MAPKAPK5 — Zero lung/COPD literature (explicit negative). The most useful verified citation
here (Ronkina et al.) actually argues *against* assuming MK5 shares MK2/MK3's TNF-alpha-driving
inflammatory role — 'no known common function for all three MKs' — and a second paper (Yego &
Dillman) shows MK2, not MK5, drives the cytokine response in a lung-relevant (sulfur-mustard
keratinocyte) model, with MK5 appearing only as a keyword. This is exactly the
paralog-assumption problem [the pipeline's immune-complexity prior] warns about... This should
be read as evidence *against* an inflammatory-program rationale for MAPKAPK5, not neutral.
WEAKENED, bordering on CONTESTED — the strongest MAPKAPK5-adjacent literature actively argues
against the paralog-based inflammatory mechanism that would be the natural reason to prioritize
this gene."

## Additional red-team-skeptic critiques carried forward (not full CONTESTED designations, but material qualifications)

- **ALDH2 (WEAKENED, not simply "top gene")**: "ALDH2 has the strongest statistical... and the
  most abundant literature at the locus, but the literature itself is mixed-to-contradictory on
  mechanism and repeatedly undercuts a clean COPD-diagnosis story — it supports a
  spirometric/smoking-gene-environment-interaction case more than a direct-disease-causation
  case."
- **SH2B3 (WEAKENED)**: "biologically plausible immune candidate but no lung/COPD evidence
  exists; ranking it at the same tier as PTPN11... implies a level of confidence the underlying
  data don't support."
- **TRAFD1 (WEAKENED — overclaim identified)**: "Ranking TRAFD1 #2 overall, above
  SH2B3/PTPN11/HECTD4 which all have higher L2G scores, is an overreach: the synthesis is
  privileging mechanistic specificity/method rigor from an unrelated disease [celiac disease]
  over the actual statistical genetic evidence for COPD at this locus... the immune mechanism
  is real and rigorously established, but 'directly relevant' to COPD is not demonstrated."
- **CUX2 (HOLDS, but flagged as an important unresolved divergence)**: "This is a case where
  strong, repeated statistical fine-mapping/colocalization signal is not matched by any
  biological story, which should raise — not lower — suspicion that CUX2's apparent primacy is
  a fine-mapping/LD artifact... or alternatively that CUX2 has an as-yet-uncharacterized lung
  role... Constraint asymmetry note: CUX2 LOEUF 0.383 [constrained] vs. SH2B3 LOEUF 1.35
  [tolerant] — this constraint asymmetry is actually a point in CUX2's favor as the more
  plausible dosage-sensitive causal gene, which the researcher did not use as an argument."
