Prioritized gene

TNRC6A — Confidence: LOW.

TNRC6A is named as the single best-supported candidate at this locus, but the confidence level must be LOW, not higher, and this is a case where the pipeline's own critique stage substantively weakened the initial read rather than confirming it.

In favor of TNRC6A: it is the unanimous candidate across every prioritization method (nearest gene, FLAMES, MAGMA, PoPS, ABC), it is Open Targets' own L2G-favored gene both for COPD specifically (~0.79-0.81) and for ~20 other unrelated traits at this locus over the only plausible competing neighbor gene (SLC5A11), and it is strongly loss-of-function constrained (LOEUF ≈ 0.26, pLI = 1), consistent with dosage sensitivity. Most importantly, one VERIFIED, TNRC6A-specific (not paralog) mechanistic paper (PMID 37127654) shows TNRC6A has a non-redundant role in airway multiciliated cell differentiation via a distinct AGO2-associated local-translation mechanism — a plausible, lung-specific structural pathway (mucociliary dysfunction) that is not merely generic housekeeping biology.

Against a confident call: the red-team-skeptic's critique (preserved in full in the audit file) identified that this apparent strength is substantially offset by three points that should not be read as mere "gaps": (1) TNRC6A/16q13 is absent from the 82 genome-wide-significant loci in the largest prior COPD case-control GWAS referenced (Sakornsakolpat 2019, PMID 30804561 — CLAIM_MISMATCH, excluded from the table above) and absent from a dedicated DLCO GWAS (PMID 30694715), meaning the only quantitative COPD-genetic evidence for TNRC6A at all is the Open Targets L2G score, which is a model output rather than independent literature replication and carries some risk of circularity with the GWAS underlying this very locus file; (2) the locus's extreme cross-trait pleiotropy — including a strong educational-attainment/cognitive-function association, a trait genetically and phenotypically correlated with smoking behavior — leaves the smoking-mediated-effects prior genuinely unresolved rather than cleared, since the one directly relevant smoking-behavior GWAS check (Liu 2019, PMID 30643251) could not be confirmed either way (CLAIM_MISMATCH, excluded from the table above); and (3) no COPD-relevant inflammatory/immune mechanism (neutrophilic, Th1/Th17, macrophage, CD8+ T-cell) was identified for TNRC6A at all — the only immune literature places it in unrelated systemic autoimmune autoantigen biology. The skeptic explicitly declined to mark TNRC6A CONTESTED (no alternative causal gene was identified or supported at this locus — SLC5A11 and ARHGAP17 both lack any credible-set overlap or prioritization support and were not elevated as competitors), but assigned an explicit WEAKENED verdict to the overall inference.

There is no real runner-up gene to name: SLC5A11 and ARHGAP17 are documented in the researcher checkpoint only for regional completeness, have zero prioritization-method support and zero credible-set variant overlap, and neither the researcher nor Open Targets provides any evidence elevating either over TNRC6A. This is therefore not a tied-candidate-gene problem in the usual LD sense; it is a single-candidate-gene locus where the strength of mechanism for that one candidate is itself ambiguous.

What would resolve this: (a) direct confirmation of whether this locus/rs8049667 reaches genome-wide significance in a more recent or larger COPD case-control or spirometry (FEV1, FEV1/FVC) GWAS than Sakornsakolpat 2019 — the absence in a 2019 GWAS is not dispositive given subsequent, larger meta-analyses may have picked it up; (b) a GWAS Catalog / PhenoScanner lookup of rs8049667, rs6497759, and rs3803716 directly (never completed in this run due to WebSearch budget exhaustion in both the researcher and red-team-skeptic sessions — see the note above); (c) a locus-level (not just genome-wide abstract-level) check of whether this 16q13 region reaches significance in the large tobacco/alcohol-use GWAS (Liu 2019) to properly adjudicate the smoking-mediation question; and (d) additional functional evidence tying TNRC6A's multiciliogenesis role specifically to COPD-relevant airway pathology (e.g., in COPD patient airway epithelium or smoke-exposure models), rather than the developmental/homeostatic airway biology shown so far.
