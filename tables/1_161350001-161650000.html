Prioritized gene

FCGR3A — confidence: LOW.

Neither candidate gene has direct COPD-specific genetic or functional evidence: PubMed searches for "HSPA6 COPD" and "FCGR3A COPD" each returned essentially no on-topic hits, and neither gene has a published lung-function (FEV1/FEV1-FVC) association. The choice between them therefore rests on indirect evidence, and that evidence is genuinely mixed rather than decisive.

FCGR3A is favored over HSPA6 for three reasons, each individually modest: (1) the locus's own lead variant, rs2099684 (PIP=0.85), has a specific, verified functional annotation — a cell-type-resolved eQTL for FCGR3A in NK cells (Yazar et al. 2026, PMID 42102734) — whereas the same variant has no analogous functional link to HSPA6; if anything this finding is a competing mechanism that argues against HSPA6, which is why red-team-skeptic marked HSPA6 CONTESTED rather than merely under-supported. (2) FCGR3A's established immune-cell-intrinsic biology (NK cells, CD16+ monocytes, ADCC/immune-complex handling) is at least mechanistically compatible with the heterogeneous immune programs implicated in COPD (standing prior #3), even though no COPD- specific study tests this directly. (3) Open Targets' COPD target-disease association score is higher for FCGR3A (0.104) than HSPA6 (0.041) on both the genetic_association and literature datatype components.

However, this preference for FCGR3A should not be read as strong. Red-team-skeptic identified substantial reasons for caution that are preserved here rather than smoothed over: the NK-cell eQTL is a blood/PBMC finding, not lung-tissue evidence; the Takayasu-arteritis and malaria-antibody associations cited in support of FCGR3A are both non-COPD, non-lung phenotypes used as mechanistic analogy rather than direct corroboration, and the Takayasu signal itself is explicitly reported as a joint "FCGR2A/FCGR3A" association, not FCGR3A-specific; and in one of the two malaria studies cited, rs2099684 itself was not even the variant that reached significance. Most importantly, FCGR3A cannot be confidently distinguished from its immediate, highly homologous paralogs FCGR3B and FCGR2A at this locus — no cited study isolates FCGR3A's effect from FCGR3B/FCGR2A using allele-specific or CNV-aware data, and MAGMA (the only internal method prioritizing FCGR3A) is known to have limited resolution among tightly linked paralogous genes. This is compounded by Open Targets' own structured data: FCGR3B, a gene not even on this locus's internal candidate list, has both the highest COPD genetic_association score (0.433) and the most frequent top-L2G ranking of any gene in this cluster across the non-COPD traits surveyed. Red-team-skeptic explicitly flagged that the true causal gene at this locus may not be HSPA6 or FCGR3A at all, but rather FCGR3B — a possibility this review cannot resolve because FCGR3B was outside the candidate-gene scope defined by the internal pipeline for this locus.

Runner-up: HSPA6 (nearest gene, PoPS- and ABC-prioritized) — marked CONTESTED by red-team- skeptic on the strength of the specific competing eQTL finding above, not merely absent evidence. Out-of-scope but flagged: FCGR3B — not a candidate gene per the internal pipeline definition for this locus, but surfaced by both Open Targets structured data and red-team-skeptic critique as plausibly the strongest gene-level candidate in this cluster; it involves neutrophil biology directly relevant to COPD's neutrophilic-inflammation axis (standing prior #3).

What would resolve this: (1) A COPD- or lung-function-specific eQTL/colocalization study in lung tissue or relevant lung-infiltrating immune-cell types (not blood/PBMC) for FCGR3A vs. FCGR3B vs. FCGR2A vs. HSPA6 at this exact locus. (2) CNV-aware or allele-specific expression analysis capable of separating FCGR3A from FCGR3B, given FCGR3B's common copy-number variation. (3) Direct FEV1/FEV1-FVC association testing for this credible set. (4) Extension of the candidate-gene list at this locus to formally include FCGR3B, given the structured database signal pointing toward it. Given the number of open questions, this locus's gene assignment should be treated as unresolved/low-confidence by the study team pending further evidence, not as a settled call between HSPA6 and FCGR3A alone.
