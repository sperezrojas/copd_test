Evidence Table - Locus 5:132150001-132750000 (nearest gene IRF1)

Region: 5:132,150,001-132,750,000 (GRCh38). Internal pipeline candidate genes: nearest gene IRF1; MAGMA-prioritized AFF4; PoPS-prioritized HSPA4; ABC-prioritized IRF1; credible-set nearest gene CARINH (lncRNA, formerly IRF1-AS1). All 8 credible-set variants (rs2057656, rs3857440, rs3846730, rs1023518, rs2548993, rs7713065, rs10077785, rs11748326) are intronic/non-coding-transcript variants annotated to CARINH; none is missense/splice, so no additional per-variant literature search beyond the lead variant was performed, per pipeline scope rules.

Two additional genes — SLC22A5 (OCTN2) and SLC22A4 (OCTN1) — were added as full candidates after the Open Targets structured L2G lookup (see summary below) showed them outscoring the internal pipeline's nominated genes for the COPD phenotype specifically. Seven further physically nearby Th2-cluster/region genes (IL3, IL4, IL5, IL13, CSF2, RAD50, KIF3A) were screened at a shallower level and are documented in the audit appendix rather than the main table, since none showed a locus-specific signal (see audit file).

Prioritized gene

Prioritized gene: IRF1 — Confidence: LOW

This call is close, and the evidence genuinely does not cleanly distinguish IRF1 from SLC22A5; this section names a single best pick as required, but with substantial caveats made explicit.

IRF1 is favored primarily because it has the only tissue-specific, smoking-context, directly COPD/emphysema-relevant mechanistic paper at this locus (Xia et al. 2024, PMID 38582189 — IRF1-triggered PANoptosis of alveolar epithelial cells linked to smoking-related emphysema via a microbiome mechanism), is competitive with SLC22A5 on Open Targets L2G score across most COPD/lung-function GWAS (and ahead of it for several asthma GWAS), and is the nearest gene, the ABC-prioritized gene, and physically overlapped by every credible-set variant (via CARINH, its antisense transcript). However, the red-team-skeptic's critique substantially weakens this pick, and that critique is preserved here rather than smoothed over:

  1. No genetic anchor. No GWAS, fine-mapping, or colocalization paper in the verified literature names IRF1 as the causal gene at this locus — the entire IRF1 evidence base above is mechanistic/biological-plausibility literature, much of it in non-COPD models (CF, pediatric RSV) or asthma-oriented, and IRF1 is a heavily-studied interferon-response transcription factor in general, which inflates its apparent literature support independent of locus-specificity.
  2. Directional inconsistency, unresolved (CONTESTED). Hey et al. 2021 (PMID 34764283) shows enhanced Irf1 activity driving excessive macrophage inflammation in a muco-obstructive mouse model, while Veerati et al. 2020 (PMID 32499788) shows IRF1-dependent antiviral induction is delayed/blunted in COPD/asthma donor epithelial cells — opposite directions of dysregulation, not reconciled by any source reviewed.
  3. SLC22A5 runner-up, and closer than a casual read suggests. SLC22A5 has the single highest locus-specific L2G score of any gene for the FinnGen COPD GWAS (0.316, ~4-6x IRF1's 0.053), and remains roughly tied with IRF1 in a never-smoker-stratified FEV1/FVC GWAS — one of the very few pieces of evidence at this locus bearing directly on prior #1 (distinguishing smoking-mediated from lung-biology-mediated signal) and prior #2 (COPD/lung-function concordance). Its case is weakened mainly by the poor mechanistic fit of its known Mendelian disease (autosomal recessive carnitine deficiency, complete biallelic LoF) to a common heterozygous regulatory variant, and by no dedicated peer-reviewed COPD paper naming it either — a gap symmetric with IRF1's own gap, not a point of differentiation.
  4. Unresolved alternative hypothesis (per red-team-skeptic, not independently confirmed further here): asthma-COPD overlap / phenotype misclassification. This locus sits in the classical chr5q31.1 Th2-cytokine-cluster asthma/atopy region. IRF1's L2G edges ahead of SLC22A5 specifically for asthma GWAS and for "Asthma-COPD overlap syndrome" as a queried trait; KIF3A has documented (if locus-distinct) asthma/atopic-dermatitis genetics nearby; and IL5's high Open Targets clinical score reflects eosinophilic-COPD/asthma biologic trials. It remains a live, unresolved possibility that part of this locus's "COPD" GWAS signal (particularly in registry/ICD-code-based cohorts such as FinnGen) reflects asthma/atopy biology or asthma-COPD-overlap-syndrome case misclassification rather than COPD/emphysema-specific biology — for either IRF1 or SLC22A5. This was not resolved by any evidence gathered in this review.
  5. CARINH is not an independent causal candidate (transcript-overlap artifact of IRF1's antisense partner), and AFF4 and HSPA4 are both weakly supported (AFF4: zero Open Targets COPD disease-association evidence despite MAGMA significance and high constraint, with its only lung phenotype coming from a mechanistically distinct rare monogenic syndrome; HSPA4: no COPD/lung/ immune literature found at all).

Runner-up: SLC22A5. What would resolve the tie: a dedicated statistical-fine-mapping or colocalization paper (or new analysis) explicitly naming either IRF1 or SLC22A5 as the eQTL/pQTL target of the credible-set variants in lung tissue or airway epithelium; cell-type-resolved single-cell data distinguishing an IRF1-macrophage/epithelial mechanism from an SLC22A5-epithelial- transporter mechanism; and spirometry-confirmed (not ICD-code-only) COPD case definition in the GWAS used, to rule out asthma-COPD-overlap confounding. Given the closeness of this call and the unresolved directional and phenotype-misclassification issues above, this recommendation should be treated as provisional by the study team, not a settled result.
