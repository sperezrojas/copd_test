This locus does not support a confident single-gene call. Reasoning, referencing the table and Open Targets summary above:

  • No computational gene-prioritization tool (FLAMES/MAGMA/PoPS/ABC) converged on any gene at this locus (locus input file).
  • No gene at this locus has any COPD- or FEV1-specific evidence from Open Targets (L2G, colocalization, or curated evidence rows are all null/zero for SOX11 and LINC01249, the only two named genes considered), and the internal pipeline's literal nearest-gene call, LOC105373397, is a functionally uncharacterized transcript whose true coordinates in fact sit ~604 kb outside the defined locus window entirely (see LOC105373397 row above) — effectively ruling it out as a serious candidate.
  • SOX11, the nearest annotated protein-coding gene, sits ~855 kb from the lead variant, also outside the defined window. Its literature record (Sock et al. 2004 and related rows above) is real but embryonic/developmental or acute-injury in nature, not adult-COPD- or spirometry-specific, and its immune biology is confined to an unrelated oncologic (B-cell lymphoma / SCLC) context. Per the red-team critique, SOX11 risks being over-weighted simply because it is the only gene here with a literature trail — a "streetlight effect" that should not be mistaken for genuine locus-specific support.
  • LINC01249's only literature hit is an unrelated thyroid-hormone GWAS in a different (though neighboring) LOC-identifier gene, and Open Targets shows essentially no disease evidence for it.
  • The most locus-specific structured-data signal available — Open Targets' fine-mapping of the lead variant and several other top-PIP credible-set variants to "chronotype measurement" GWAS studies — is intriguing because, unlike the SOX11/LINC01249 literature, it actually concerns the credible-set variants themselves rather than a distant gene. It is corroborated only insofar as chronotype/sleep traits are independently known to be genetically correlated with smoking behavior (Gibson et al. 2019, PMID 30365022, VERIFIED — a real, general finding, not a locus-specific one). But no verified or independently-searched literature names this exact locus, rs7585899, SOX11, or LINC01249 in a chronotype/circadian context, so this remains an unconfirmed alternative hypothesis, not a demonstrated mechanism.
  • A further, at-least-equally-plausible alternative (raised by red-team-skeptic) is that this is a statistical/winner's-curse false-positive or an unresolved long-range regulatory signal in a gene desert, given that even Open Targets' own L2G pipeline (a much larger, better-powered dataset) produced no gene assignment for the credible sets it did register at this exact locus.

Given (a) no converging computational prioritization signal, (b) zero COPD/FEV1-specific evidence for any candidate gene from Open Targets, (c) the nearest-gene and nearest-protein-coding-gene calls both sitting hundreds of kb outside the defined locus window, and (d) a competing chronotype/smoking-behavior hypothesis that is itself unconfirmed by locus-specific literature — the study team should treat this locus as unresolved at the gene level. If forced to rank, SOX11 is the least-implausible named candidate by virtue of having any lung-relevant biology at all (even if developmental/ non-COPD-specific) and being the nearest protein-coding gene in the desert, with LINC01249 and LOC105373397 as clear runners-down given their near-total absence of relevant biology. Confidence: INSUFFICIENT.
