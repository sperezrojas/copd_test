Smoking-behavior context (applies across genes)

A targeted (PubMed/bioRxiv only, not a formal full-summary-statistics lookup) search found no evidence that this locus (rs7181543/SMAD3/AAGAB region) is a named signal in the two largest smoking-behavior GWAS reviewed: Quach et al. 2020 nicotine-dependence GWAS (PMID 33144568 / DOI 10.1038/s41467-020-19265-z, VERIFIED) and the GSCAN/MVP smoking-initiation/ cessation meta-analysis (Xu et al. 2020, PMID 33082346 / DOI 10.1038/s41467-020-18489-3, VERIFIED; 99 initiation loci, 13 cessation loci, neither naming this region). This is provisional, not conclusive — a formal locus lookup in full GSCAN/nicotine-dependence summary statistics was not performed. The red-team-skeptic additionally notes that the Zhang 2024 pleiotropy paper (PMID 38817868) places SMAD3 alongside CHRNA4, a canonical smoking-behavior/nicotinic-receptor gene, in a shared pleiotropic-gene list for a different trait pair (lung disease/esophageal cancer) — raising an unconfirmed but plausible smoking-pleiotropy alternative that should not be dismissed solely on the basis of the provisional negative GSCAN/nicotine-dependence lookup above.

Interpretation flag (orchestrator, carried through researcher and red-team-skeptic): across a wide range of biologically unrelated traits queried at this identical variant (adiposity, renal function, asthma, neuroticism), the same three genes reshuffle by similar small margins, and none crosses ~0.5 for any COPD-relevant trait. This pattern is more consistent with Open Targets' L2G model being driven substantially by non-trait-specific features (e.g., genomic distance) at this locus than by trait-specific functional genomic evidence. These L2G scores should be read as weak/non-discriminating between SMAD3, AAGAB, and IQCH, not as strong gene-prioritization evidence for any one of them — in particular, AAGAB's numerically highest score should not be read as biological support for AAGAB, which has zero literature-supported connection to lung/airway biology.

Prioritized gene

SMAD3 — confidence: LOW.

This is a close call and the pipeline's own statistical evidence (Open Targets L2G and colocalization) does not favor SMAD3 at this specific credible set — SMAD3 has the lowest locus-specific L2G score of the three candidate genes (0.366 for COPD, 0.345 for chronic airway obstruction, 0.304 for FEV1/FVC ratio, versus AAGAB's 0.431/0.438/0.485), and no eQTL/molecular-QTL colocalization was found for SMAD3 expression at this locus. Two major hypothesis-free lung-function GWAS (Hancock 2010, Soler Artigas 2011) do not name SMAD3 among their genome-wide-significant loci, and its inclusion in a third, larger lung-function GWAS (Shrine 2019) could not be confirmed. The red-team-skeptic explicitly flagged this locus as having an "unresolved/close-call gene assignment" rather than a confidently confirmed SMAD3 call.

Despite this, SMAD3 is the best-supported single pick for three reasons that together outweigh AAGAB and IQCH, though not decisively: (1) Convergent, mechanistically detailed, multi-decade mouse-knockout evidence (Chen 2004 PMID 15591413, Bonniaud 2004 PMID 15265946, Farkas 2011 PMID 21743024) directly demonstrates that Smad3 loss impairs alveolarization, reduces tropoelastin, increases MMP-9-driven matrix destruction, and causes emphysema-like pathology that is aggravated by cigarette smoke exposure — this is gene-general biological plausibility, not locus-specific confirmation, but it is substantially stronger and more specific to COPD-relevant lung pathophysiology than anything found for AAGAB or IQCH, both of which have zero literature connecting them to lung/airway biology of any kind. (2) SMAD3 is strongly LoF- and missense-constrained in gnomAD, consistent with it being a dosage-sensitive gene where regulatory dysregulation (as opposed to coding loss-of-function, which causes the distinct Mendelian Loeys-Dietz syndrome) is a biologically coherent, if unconfirmed, mechanism for a common noncoding GWAS variant. (3) AAGAB's numerically superior L2G score is very likely an artifact: it recurs as the top-ranked gene across a wide range of biologically unrelated traits queried at this same variant (adiposity, renal function, asthma, neuroticism), which is the signature of a non-trait-specific (likely distance-based) L2G feature rather than genuine AAGAB-specific regulatory evidence — and AAGAB's only established human disease mechanism (keratinocyte AP-1/AP-2 clathrin-adaptor trafficking, causing palmoplantar keratoderma) has no plausible route to pulmonary biology. IQCH is weaker still: not independently prioritized by any of FLAMES/MAGMA/PoPS/ABC, appearing only via LD-tagged low-PIP variants, with an established testis-specific biological role (spermatogenesis) that has no plausible lung connection.

Runner-up: AAGAB (by L2G score alone, but with no supporting biological literature — see caveats above). What would resolve the tie: a direct lung-tissue eQTL/sQTL colocalization analysis (e.g., GTEx lung, or a dedicated lung eQTL catalog such as Lamontagne et al.'s lung eQTL resource) at this exact credible set, since Open Targets found no molecular-QTL colocalization for any of the three genes and its L2G scores appear to be non-discriminating at this locus; and/or a formal locus-level lookup in full GSCAN/nicotine-dependence summary statistics to rule out (or confirm) the smoking-behavior- mediated alternative raised by the CHRNA4-adjacent pleiotropy finding (Zhang 2024, PMID 38817868). Confidence is marked LOW rather than MODERATE or HIGH because the strongest evidence for SMAD3 (mouse knockouts) answers a different question than the one this locus-specific analysis needs answered (does this variant regulate SMAD3 in human lung), and the pipeline's own locus-anchored statistical evidence does not currently support that link.
