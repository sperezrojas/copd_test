Prioritized gene

Prioritized gene: RBMS3 Confidence: LOW

RBMS3 is named as the best-supported candidate gene at this locus for the simple reason that it is the only candidate gene under consideration — nearest gene, FLAMES-prioritized, and PoPS-prioritized all agree (MAGMA and ABC did not weigh in), and all 15 credible-set variants (lead rs17666332, PIP=0.198) fall entirely within RBMS3 introns with no competing overlapping gene identified. There is no gene-identity ambiguity to resolve here (see red-team-skeptic: "HOLDS (clean single-gene assignment, FLAMES+PoPS concordant)").

However, confidence in RBMS3 as a genuine, mechanistically-understood COPD gene (as opposed to simply "the gene at this locus") is LOW, for reasons the evidence table and Open Targets summary above lay out explicitly:

  1. The statistical-genetics case is weaker than the pipeline's own database score suggests. The Open Targets gwas_credible_sets score (0.83) is circular — it derives from the same GWAS meta-analysis that produced this locus's own credible set, not an independent source. When the researcher searched for independent replication in three major, well-powered, systematic lung-function/COPD GWAS papers (Wain 2017, Shrine 2019, Sakornsakolpat 2019 — together enumerating 279 lung-function signals and 82 genome-wide-significant COPD loci), citation-verifier's full-text checks found zero mentions of RBMS3, 3p24, or rs17666332 in any of them. This is informative negative evidence, not merely an evidence gap, and the corresponding Open Targets literature sub-score for COPD (0.085) is consistent with this — independent literature support for RBMS3-COPD is nearly absent.
  2. The mechanistic/functional literature is real but entirely non-lung/non-COPD in its direct evidence base. RBMS3's most robust literature (VERIFIED, per table above) documents it as a Wnt/β-catenin/c-Myc-restraining tumor suppressor across multiple tissues (including lung epithelium in cancer contexts — Vaishnavi 2022, Liang 2015, Lv 2023), a TGF-β/Smad2-dependent regulator of neural-crest chondrogenesis (Jayasena & Bronner 2012), a stabilizer of profibrotic Prx1/collagen I transcripts in liver fibrosis (Fritz & Stefanovic 2007), and a developmental regulator in pancreas (Lu 2012). None of this literature directly tests RBMS3 in airway/alveolar structural biology, emphysema models, or lung inflammation.
  3. The only independent, verified non-cancer GWAS hit for RBMS3/3p24 is exfoliation syndrome (Aung et al. 2017, PMID 28553957), a fibrotic ocular disease — and RBMS3's single strongest disease association by Open Targets score across its entire phenome is myopia. This raises a live, unresolved alternative hypothesis that RBMS3 (or this locus) may act through an ocular/connective-tissue-fibrosis mechanism that happens to also register a signal in COPD GWAS, rather than through lung-specific biology per se — though this is explicitly a reviewer-constructed hypothesis, not a claim any cited paper makes.
  4. The smoking-mediation question (Prior #1) is flagged but unresolved. Open Targets independently scores RBMS3 for "smoking initiation" (0.264), which is exactly the alternative pathway Prior #1 asks reviewers to consider — but no literature (PubMed) was found linking RBMS3 to smoking behavior, so this cannot be confirmed or ruled out.
  5. A pattern of overclaiming/citation error was caught in this locus's research pass: the researcher's initial claim of "repeated replication" in three major GWAS papers was contradicted by full-text verification, and a claimed RBMS3-height GWAS citation (PMID 41897845) turned out to be an unrelated sheep-genomics paper. Per red-team-skeptic, this pattern (not a single isolated slip) warrants extra caution about this gene's evidence base specifically, beyond the normal level of scrutiny.

There is no runner-up candidate gene to name at this locus (RBMS3 is the sole candidate across all four prioritization methods that returned a call), so this is not a "tied paralogs" situation — the uncertainty here is not about which gene, but about whether the locus reflects genuine, mechanistically-specific COPD/lung biology at all, or a not-yet-replicated statistical signal, or a pleiotropic/ocular-fibrosis/smoking-behavior mechanism instead. What would resolve this: (a) replication of the rs17666332/RBMS3 credible-set signal in a more recent, independently-analyzed COPD or lung-function GWAS meta-analysis than the three checked here; (b) a direct test of RBMS3 in airway epithelium, alveolar structural cells, or an emphysema/lung-fibrosis model; (c) a dedicated look at whether this locus or RBMS3 shows a genome-wide-significant signal in a smoking-behavior GWAS (e.g., GSCAN/Liu et al. 2019) to adjudicate the smoking-mediation hypothesis.
