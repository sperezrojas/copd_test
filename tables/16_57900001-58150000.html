Prioritized gene

MMP15 — Confidence: LOW-to-MODERATE (reported as LOW given the unresolved L2G contradiction; see below)

MMP15 is my best-supported single recommendation at this locus, but the case is closer than a first read suggests, and I am deliberately not overstating confidence.

In favor of MMP15: It carries the lead variant (rs11076213, PIP=0.533) and the great majority of the credible-set PIP mass, and it is the nearest, MAGMA-, and ABC-prioritized gene from the internal pipeline. Three independent verified sources support pulmonary-biology relevance: an older, independent lung-function GWAS naming MMP15 among 16 genome-wide-significant loci for FEV1/FEV1-FVC (PMID 21946350); an independent MR/colocalization druggable-genes study linking MMP15 expression to COPD/lung-function risk (PMID 38660492, though its MMP15-specific signal used whole-blood, not lung, eQTL); a lung-tissue MMP15 eQTL with suggestive pleiotropy to OSA/FEV1-FVC (PMID 36574455); and a human AAT-deficiency cohort study showing alveolar MMP15 protein tracks with worse lung function and emphysema in PI*MZ carriers (PMID 41253406, n=39, shared finding with MMP3). MMP15 (MT2-MMP) also fits the well-established protease-antiprotease-imbalance paradigm central to COPD/emphysema pathophysiology (biological prior #2), and no smoking-behavior-mediation literature was found (though this was only checked via PubMed/bioRxiv, not a direct smoking-GWAS lookup, so should not be read as ruling it out).

Against treating this as a settled call: The red-team-skeptic identified a genuine, unresolved tension that I am carrying forward rather than resolving by fiat: Open Targets' own independent L2G calculation (on a different, older, 2019 COPD GWAS) ranks MMP15 lowest of the six genes at this locus (0.054), while ZNF319 (0.518, also the most LoF-constrained gene here) and SPMIP8 (0.346) score substantially higher. No colocalization evidence anywhere in Open Targets links any gene's molecular QTLs to a COPD/FEV1 signal at this locus, meaning none of the "supporting" MMP15 literature above constitutes a resolved statistical fine-mapping/colocalization proof for this specific GWAS signal — the 2011 GWAS citation does not establish LD/positional identity with rs11076213, the MR study's MMP15 result rests on blood (not lung) eQTL, and the AAT-deficiency cohort finding is a small, subgroup-specific, protein-correlational result shared with an unrelated gene (MMP3). ZNF319's dismissal in the literature review rested mainly on the absence of a lung-specific PubMed hit, which the skeptic correctly notes is not itself strong evidence against a real gene — zinc-finger transcription factors are routinely understudied outside the tissue where someone happened to look, and ZNF319's status as a known disease gene (leukodystrophy, craniofacial development) demonstrates it is not a null/pseudogene locus.

Runner-up: ZNF319 (highest independent L2G, most constrained gene at the locus, but only described human phenotypes — leukodystrophy, cleft lip/palate — are extrapulmonary, plus one exploratory, non-mechanistic neutrophil single-cell finding).

What would resolve the tie: (1) Direct colocalization analysis of this project's own COPD/FEV1 GWAS credible set against MMP15 and ZNF319 lung-tissue and immune-cell eQTL/sQTL/pQTL data (not available in Open Targets for this specific study); (2) confirmation of whether the 2011 Soler Artigas GWAS "MMP15" signal (PMID 21946350) is in LD with rs11076213 or is a distinct, more distal signal; (3) a lung-tissue (not blood) eQTL/MR result for ZNF319, which was not found in either the researcher's or skeptic's searches; (4) any functional lung or airway-immune phenotyping for ZNF319, which is currently absent from the literature.

Given this locus carries a strong statistical fine-mapping signal (PIP=0.53) squarely in MMP15 and a biologically coherent, if not definitively locus-matched, mechanistic story, MMP15 remains the recommendation — but confidence is marked LOW because the single most directly comparable structured-genetics data source available (Open Targets' independent L2G) actively points away from it, and this has not been explained away, only contextualized as coming from a different/older GWAS. The study team should weigh this explicitly rather than treat MMP15 as a closed case.
