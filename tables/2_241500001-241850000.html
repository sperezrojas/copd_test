Prioritized gene

Prioritized gene: ATG4B. Confidence: LOW.

This call is close, and the pipeline's own evidence does not cleanly separate ATG4B from D2HGDH — naming a single gene here is a judgment call under genuine uncertainty, not a settled conclusion.

Reasoning: ATG4B is the nearest gene to the lead variant rs62190299 and carries the bulk of the credible-set PIP mass (including the lead variant itself, a 3'UTR variant rs62190321, and a synonymous variant rs11538896), and it is the only gene at this locus with a documented, COPD/smoking-specific functional mechanism directly tying its molecular pathway to cigarette-smoke-induced pulmonary pathology: cigarette smoke extract induces autophagy in human pulmonary epithelial cells via an Egr-1/E2F-4-mediated pathway that transcriptionally upregulates Atg4B, and Egr-1-knockout mice are resistant to cigarette-smoke-induced emphysema (PMID 18830406, VERIFIED). ATG4B is also Open Targets' own top L2G-predicted gene at the exact lead variant position, albeit for a BMI GWAS credible set rather than COPD.

However, red-team-skeptic correctly identifies this case as WEAKENED, not strong: no GWAS, colocalization, TWAS, or Mendelian-randomization study links ATG4B genetic variation itself to COPD or FEV1/FEV1-FVC; the Chen et al. 2008 mechanism manipulates Egr-1, not Atg4b, in vivo; the lead variant's own PIP is low (0.046); and the one relevant cross-trait L2G data point at this exact SNP favors BMI over COPD, raising an unaddressed alternative hypothesis of adiposity-mediated pleiotropy rather than direct lung biology.

The runner-up, D2HGDH, actually carries the strongest quantitative Open Targets genetic-association score for COPD specifically (0.314, driven by real gwas_credible_sets-datatype evidence, versus ATG4B's literature-only 0.0114) and is Open Targets' dominant L2G gene across dozens of asthma/allergic-rhinitis/atopic- dermatitis/eosinophil-trait GWAS credible sets at this locus. Red-team-skeptic marked D2HGDH's case CONTESTED, however: that supporting genetic signal is for atopic/Th2/ eosinophilic-inflammation traits, not COPD directly, no FEV1/FEV1-FVC evidence exists for D2HGDH, and the one COPD-adjacent citation offered in its support (Hayden et al. 2018, PMID 30373671, on childhood-asthma/COPD overlap) does not examine D2HGDH itself and was judged to be used past its evidentiary scope. GAL3ST2, D2HGDH's consistent runner-up gene in the same allergic/eosinophil credible sets, adds a mechanistically striking but COPD-untested airway-epithelial antiviral function (PMID 41774787) that could plausibly matter for exacerbation risk, but this too was marked WEAKENED by the skeptic for lack of any COPD-specific genetic or directional evidence.

Because ATG4B, D2HGDH, GAL3ST2, and (to a lesser extent) ING5 all share meaningful credible-set PIP mass in overlapping LD at this locus, and none has gene-specific COPD colocalization/fine-mapping evidence that would break the tie, this pick should be read as "best available single nomination under real ambiguity" rather than a confident call. What would resolve the tie: (1) a COPD- or FEV1/FEV1-FVC-specific colocalization/fine-mapping analysis (not just aggregate L2G/association scores) at this exact locus; (2) eQTL/sQTL data in COPD-relevant tissue (airway epithelium, lung macrophages) showing which gene's expression the risk allele actually perturbs; (3) direct confirmation of whether the ~50 asthma/eosinophil GWAS credible sets attributed to D2HGDH by Open Targets reflect the same causal variant as the COPD credible set, or a distinct, non-overlapping signal; (4) functional validation of GAL3ST2's antiviral mechanism in a COPD-specific model. KIF1A (MAGMA/PoPS-prioritized) is excluded from serious consideration: it lies ~675kb-1.1Mb outside the physical locus boundaries, never appears as an Open Targets L2G gene for any trait at this locus, and its established biology (axonal transport, KIF1A-associated neurological disorder) has no plausible COPD mechanism — its prioritization is best explained as a gene-level/pathway statistical artifact rather than a mechanistically or genetically grounded candidate. DTYMK (ABC-prioritized) and ING5 are similarly deprioritized: both have zero Open Targets COPD association data and no COPD/lung-function literature support, with only non-COPD (cancer-cell-biology and hematologic/cardiovascular, respectively) evidence identified. PDCD1, found at the extreme upper edge of the locus (~100bp overlap), has compelling general COPD CD8+ T-cell immunology support (PMID 26517304) but carries zero locus-specific evidentiary weight (no credible-set representation, no prioritization- method support, arbitrary physical overlap) and should not be weighed in this locus's gene call.
