Prioritized gene

MTMR2 — confidence: LOW.

MTMR2 is the best-supported single gene call at this locus on structural grounds: it carries the lead variant (rs10765776, PIP=0.108) and the great majority of the credible set's posterior probability, including the locus's one missense variant (rs3824874, low PIP=0.038), is the ABC-prioritized gene, and has the most consistent top Open Targets L2G score among the three physically-overlapping genes specifically for the phenotype class (smoking initiation, nicotine/tobacco dependence) that this locus's fine-mapped signal is most robustly tied to. This is corroborated by independent literature (Sanna et al. 2020, VERIFIED, PMID 32920024) confirming the FAM76B/MTMR2/CEP57 block as a known pleiotropic addiction/behavioral-trait locus outside of Open Targets.

However, confidence is explicitly LOW, not higher, for several reasons that the evidence table and red-team critique above make unavoidable:

  1. No gene-specific evidence ties MTMR2, individually, to smoking behavior at this exact locus in primary literature — the supporting tobacco/nicotine GWAS papers (Liu 2019, Toikumo 2024) are cited only as plausible sources of the Open-Targets-reported signal; neither confirms genome-wide significance at this locus at the gene level from what was checked. The strongest direct evidence for MTMR2 specifically is a database L2G score (unverified literature claim, by pipeline design).
  2. The credible set is diffuse (max PIP 0.108), and CEP57 is a real runner-up — CEP57 has comparable or higher L2G for cognitive/educational-attainment phenotypes and cannot be cleanly excluded as contributing to, or even driving, part of this same pleiotropic signal; the researcher and skeptic both describe CEP57 as more likely a passenger, but that conclusion rests on the same imperfect L2G/credible-set data that supports MTMR2.
  3. MTMR2's own established biology (Schwann-cell myelin phosphoinositide phosphatase, causing CMT4B1) has no documented connection to airway, alveolar, or immune biology, and no lung eQTL/sQTL or COPD/spirometric GWAS association exists for MTMR2 in Open Targets or in any citation the researcher or verifier found.
  4. Most importantly, if MTMR2 (or this locus generally) is truly driving the COPD GWAS signal, the best-evidenced mechanism is smoking/nicotine-dependence-behavior mediation (per biological prior #1), not direct pulmonary structural or immune biology. No positive evidence of a lung-intrinsic mechanism exists for any of the four genes evaluated at this locus.

Runner-up: CEP57 (behavioral/cognitive-trait L2G signal, LD/credible-set overlap, no prioritization by any internal method, no pulmonary biology) and MAML2 (the internal pipeline's FLAMES/MAGMA/PoPS consensus pick, with a biologically plausible but gene-unconfirmed Notch/goblet-cell-metaplasia hypothesis — its sole supporting citation, Tsao et al. 2011, does not test MAML2 or any MAML paralog, only upstream Notch pathway components, so this should be read as a hypothesis worth testing directly, not as evidenced). FAM76B is confidently the weakest candidate of the four (lowest L2G, minimal characterization, only tangential lncRNA/locus-adjacent evidence) and is not a serious contender.

What would resolve this call: (a) direct locus-level lookup of whether this exact region reaches genome-wide significance for smoking initiation/nicotine dependence in the GSCAN (Liu 2019) or TUD (Toikumo 2024) GWAS supplementary tables, confirming the gene-level association rather than relying on Open Targets' L2G; (b) a lung-tissue or brain-tissue eQTL/sQTL colocalization analysis specifically for MTMR2 vs. CEP57 vs. FAM76B vs. MAML2 at this variant, since none currently exists in Open Targets; (c) any COPD case-control or spirometric (FEV1/FEV1-FVC) GWAS/TWAS/MR result for this locus, which is entirely absent from both the Open Targets lookup and the literature search performed here; (d) functional testing of MAML2 itself (not just upstream Notch pathway components) in airway goblet-cell/mucous-metaplasia models, to determine whether its statistical-prioritization consensus (FLAMES/MAGMA/PoPS) reflects real biology or shared method bias, as the red-team-skeptic raised.
