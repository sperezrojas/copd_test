Prioritized gene

FOXP1 — Confidence: MODERATE

FOXP1 is the only candidate gene at this locus by every measure available: nearest gene, FLAMES, MAGMA, and PoPS all independently converge on it, the entire 21-variant credible set (including the lead variant rs6796042, PIP=0.36) is intronic within FOXP1 itself, and Open Targets L2G returns FOXP1 as the sole candidate gene across every disease/trait tested at this variant. There is no positional competitor gene to weigh against it, so the "which gene" question is essentially answered — this is a one-gene locus.

What is NOT well-resolved, and keeps confidence at MODERATE rather than HIGH, is the causal mechanism and its COPD-specificity. The structured Open Targets colocalization data at this exact variant shows only weak-to-moderate concordance with FEV1 (h4=0.39) and FVC (h4=0.27) — below the conventional h4>0.8 "confident colocalization" bar — while showing strong colocalization (h4 0.74-0.98) with several traits that are not COPD (asthma/COPD-overlap, Crohn's disease, heart failure, multiple sclerosis, ADHD, multiple skin cancers). The red-team-skeptic explicitly marked "this locus's phenotypic effect is lung/COPD-specific" as CONTESTED on this basis, and I agree that conclusion is not resolved by the current evidence. The strongest gene-specific mechanistic literature (Andreas et al. 2022, PMID 36221131, tying FOXP1 lung-function variants to protease/MMP and IL-6/IL-8 dysregulation) uses different sentinel variants (rs1499894, rs35480566) than this locus's lead rs6796042, and no LD or conditional analysis links them — so this evidence supports "FOXP1-the-gene is lung-function-relevant somewhere in its body" more than it supports "this specific credible set acts through pulmonary biology." The developmental biology (Li et al. 2012, 2016) showing FOXP1/FOXP4 control airway secretory-cell fate and a non-Th2 airway-hyperresponsiveness phenotype is compelling as a plausible mechanism class (epithelial/goblet-cell/smooth-muscle), and is consistent with the locus's strong asthma/COPD-overlap colocalization, but does not by itself establish that this is what's happening at this GWAS signal specifically. Immune evidence (CD8+ T-cell quiescence, B-cell germinal-center/class-switch regulation) is real and well-replicated but broad and not COPD-specific. Direction of effect (which allele of rs6796042 raises vs. lowers FOXP1 expression, and whether that direction matches the "more FoxP1 -> better lung function" pattern in Andreas et al.) was not established in this review and is a concrete, resolvable gap.

No runner-up gene exists at this locus (this is not a "tied paralogs" situation) — the uncertainty here is entirely about mechanism/specificity, not gene identity. What would resolve it: (1) an LD check confirming or ruling out whether rs6796042 is in strong LD with Andreas et al.'s sentinel FEV1/FVC variants; (2) a direction-of-effect analysis linking the COPD/chronic-airway-obstruction risk allele at rs6796042 to FOXP1 expression direction in lung tissue/epithelium specifically; (3) colocalization of this same COPD credible set against smoking-behavior GWAS (not available in the current Open Targets pull) to more rigorously rule out smoking-mediation rather than relying on absence-of-a-hit; (4) confirmation of whether FOXP1 is explicitly named in Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019's COPD-loci gene list (currently CLAIM_MISMATCH/unresolved) via its supplementary tables.
