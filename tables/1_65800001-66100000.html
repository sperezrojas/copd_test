Prioritized gene: PDE4B — Confidence: LOW

PDE4B is the best-supported single candidate at this locus, but only narrowly, and the call is close. In favor of PDE4B: (1) it is the only gene physically overlapping the locus window, and all 60+ credible-set variants (including the lead variant rs12068439, PIP=0.208) are intronic to PDE4B — LEPR and LEPROT lie ~150-190 kb outside the window entirely and are candidates only via statistical gene-level prioritization (FLAMES/ MAGMA/PoPS), not physical overlap or fine-mapping; (2) the only locus-specific colocalization/L2G evidence available anywhere in Open Targets for this lead variant (from an unrelated GERD/peptic-ulcer-disease GWAS, GCST011500) strongly favors PDE4B over LEPR (L2G 0.833 vs 0.058) — i.e., whenever a locus-specific fine-mapping computation exists at this exact position, it nominates PDE4B, even though that computation is for a different disease; (3) PDE4B has substantial, verified, direct mechanistic evidence for macrophage and neutrophil cAMP-dependent inflammatory signaling in the lung (see table rows PMID 15585880, 34318714, 39956855, 34771000), and PDE4 inhibition (roflumilast) is an approved COPD therapy.

Confidence is capped at LOW, not MODERATE, for reasons the red-team-skeptic identified explicitly and that this synthesis does not override: (a) no COPD or FEV1/FEV1-FVC genetic-association, GWAS, TWAS, colocalization, or Mendelian-randomization evidence anywhere in the literature searched, or in Open Targets, independently implicates PDE4B — Open Targets registers a zero genetic_association score for PDE4B on both COPD and FEV1, meaning PDE4B's elevated overall COPD score is a clinical/drug-class artifact (the whole PDE4A-D family is targeted non-selectively by roflumilast), not gene-specific genetic evidence; (b) much of the verified mechanistic literature is from acute LPS-induced lung injury or idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis models — pathologically distinct paradigms from chronic cigarette-smoke-driven COPD — with only one study (Lim 2021, PMID 34771000) directly using a cigarette-smoke COPD model; (c) PDE4B also has strong, verified CNS/behavioral pharmacology and a real Open Targets genetic-association signal for smoking initiation and nicotine dependence, raising a genuine, unresolved alternative hypothesis (per pipeline prior #1) that this locus's COPD signal is smoking-behavior-mediated rather than pulmonary — though the specific literature attribution underlying that hypothesis (that PDE4B is a named locus in the GSCAN 2019 smoking GWAS) could NOT be confirmed by either the researcher or citation-verifier, so this alternative is itself unproven, not just PDE4B's case; (d) red-team-skeptic explicitly marked PDE4B CONTESTED on these grounds.

Runner-up: LEPR. LEPR has real strengths PDE4B lacks — a genuine, gene-level genetic_association signal with FEV1 in Open Targets (0.532) and direct human airway tissue evidence (Bruno 2005, PMID 16135719) that leptin/LEPR co-localizes with activated CD8+ T cells in COPD submucosa and tracks with disease severity, fitting this pipeline's priors on COPD's CD8+/T-cell inflammatory programs. However, LEPR is disfavored as the top pick primarily because it lies entirely outside the locus's physical window and credible set, and its supporting genetic literature is internally contradicted (Hansel 2009, PMID 19196818, found LEPR SNPs associated with FEV1 decline in smokers, but van den Borst 2011, PMID 22056234, found the same LEPR variants NOT associated with lung function) and includes one instance where the researcher's original claim overstated LEPR's role and was corrected by citation-verifier to CLAIM_MISMATCH (Lombard 2012 — excluded from this table; see audit). LEPR is also a very well-replicated BMI/obesity locus, and general BMI-to-lung-function Mendelian-randomization evidence (Wielscher 2021, Probst-Hensch 2021) raises a parallel, equally unresolved alternative hypothesis that LEPR's FEV1 signal is BMI-mediated pleiotropy rather than a direct pulmonary/immune mechanism.

This is a genuinely close call between two contested genes, not a confident pick. What would resolve it: (1) lung-tissue eQTL/sQTL/pQTL colocalization for PDE4B and LEPR/LEPROT specifically at rs12068439 and its LD partners (the single most decisive missing piece, per red-team-skeptic — not available in Open Targets or the literature searched); (2) direct confirmation (or refutation) of whether PDE4B is a named locus in GSCAN 2019 (PMID 30643251); (3) identification of the actual source COPD GWAS underlying this specific credible set and that paper's own stated gene assignment for this locus (neither Repapi 2010, Hancock 2010, nor Sakornsakolpat 2019 could be confirmed to discuss this locus at all); (4) a dedicated LEPROT evidence pass, since it was under-searched and sits in the same statistically-prioritized neighborhood as LEPR; (5) a modern-cohort replication of Hansel 2009's LEPR-FEV1-decline finding. Until then, both PDE4B and LEPR should be treated by the study team as CONTESTED candidates rather than a resolved prioritization.
