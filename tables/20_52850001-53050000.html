Prioritized gene

TSHZ2 — Confidence: LOW.

TSHZ2 is named as the prioritized gene by default, not by strength of pulmonary-biology evidence: it is the sole protein-coding gene in the 20:52,850,001-53,050,000 interval (confirmed via Ensembl lookup), so there is no competing candidate to weigh it against, and the FLAMES/ABC prioritization methods both point to it as well. There is no runner-up gene at this locus.

However, the totality of verified evidence argues that this locus's COPD-GWAS signal is most parsimoniously explained by mechanisms other than direct pulmonary biology, and the red-team-skeptic explicitly marked the mechanistic inference of pulmonary causality CONTESTED (see audit file) even though TSHZ2's gene-identity is not in dispute. Specifically:

  • Open Targets shows zero COPD evidence and only a very weak FEV1 association (0.035) for TSHZ2 (Open Targets summary above) — a direct violation of biological prior #2 (concordance with spirometric traits should strengthen prioritization; its near-total absence here, alongside a COPD signal, is itself informative).
  • Every one of the 16 GWAS/eQTL credible sets Open Targets has indexed at this locus's two highest-PIP variants is for a non-pulmonary trait — overwhelmingly smoking initiation, externalizing behavior/substance use, and adiposity/lipid traits (L2G ~0.87-0.89 for TSHZ2 throughout) — consistent with biological prior #1 (this COPD GWAS is not adjusted for smoking, so a smoking-behavior locus can surface as a COPD hit without acting through lung biology).
  • No verified literature (table above) links TSHZ2 to COPD or spirometric lung function at all; the closest lung-relevant findings are a modest sarcoidosis candidate-gene association (Zhou 2012 — mechanistically distant Th1/Th17 granulomatous disease, not COPD) and a lung-adenocarcinoma tumor-suppressor role (Zhao 2021 — general epithelial cell-cycle biology, not airway remodeling).
  • The Teashirt gene family's characterized mouse developmental phenotypes belong to paralogs Tshz1 (skeletal/craniofacial) and Tshz3 (ureteric smooth muscle), not Tshz2, and TSHZ2 was directly excluded (by sequencing) as a cause of a paralog-associated human developmental phenotype (PUJO) — undercutting any extrapolation from paralog biology to a TSHZ2-specific structural/developmental lung role.
  • TSHZ2 is LoF-constrained in gnomAD, indicating it is functionally important somewhere, but no source identifies that functional importance as lung-specific.

What would resolve this: a direct TSHZ2 or 20q13.33 association with FEV1/FEV1-FVC in a spirometry GWAS (smoking-adjusted or unadjusted), a COPD association that persists after conditioning on smoking behavior, or a mechanistic study of TSHZ2 in airway/alveolar epithelium or COPD-relevant immune populations (e.g., CD8+ T cells, given the eQTL colocalization) — none of which currently exist in the verified literature or in Open Targets. Until such evidence emerges, TSHZ2 should be reported to the study team as the only candidate gene at this locus but flagged as a locus whose COPD signal may be substantially or wholly smoking-behavior-mediated (with adiposity/metabolic mediation as a secondary alternative), rather than as a novel pulmonary-biology COPD gene.
