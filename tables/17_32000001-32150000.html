Confidence: LOW / INSUFFICIENT.

This locus presents a genuine, unresolved tie that should not be papered over. On statistical-genetics/colocalization grounds, LRRC37B and SUZ12 are the most frequent top-2 L2G genes across 9 independent spirometry (FEV1/FVC/PEF) credible sets sharing the lead variant (7/9 and 6/9 respectively), consistent with prior #2 (spirometric concordance is strong disease-relevant evidence). However, neither has any COPD-, spirometry-, or smoking-specific published literature beyond the Open Targets lookup itself: LRRC37B's only substantive mechanistic literature is a hominid-specific neurodevelopmental (cortical neuron excitability) mechanism (PMID 38134874) with no lung relevance, and SUZ12's is broad, essential PRC2/chromatin biology (T-cell differentiation, hematopoiesis, tumor immunology) that is emphatically nonspecific to airway or COPD biology, plus one lung-cancer carcinogenesis paper (PMID 40211119) that does not transfer cleanly to COPD. COPRS and UTP6 occasionally outscore both in individual credible sets (COPRS reaching the single highest raw L2G score at the locus, 0.555) but are even less consistent, and COPRS in particular has no verifiable literature at all in this review (a genuine search-access gap, not a finding of no biology — flagged for follow-up in the audit appendix). This four-way instability across near-identical spirometry traits is, per the red-team-skeptic, "more consistent with unresolved LD [in a segmentally-duplicated region] than with a biologically distinguishable winner" — none of these four genes should be called "prioritized" on the current evidence.

Separately, ASIC2 — the locus file's own MAGMA/PoPS statistical prioritization pick — should be treated as effectively ruled out for this specific credible set: it does not physically overlap the stated locus window (sitting ~0.9-1 Mb away), has zero colocalization support across all 31 Open Targets credible sets at the lead variant, and its only relevant functional literature describes pulmonary vascular resistance/hypertension biology (PMID 31169471) — a different lung compartment/mechanism than the airway/parenchymal-destruction biology that defines COPD/emphysema. This divergence between a locus-level statistical test (which can pick up LD/window-proximity signal) and both fine-mapped colocalization and independent coordinate verification is exactly the kind of case prior #2 anticipates: a COPD-adjacent statistical association without supporting pulmonary-function colocalization warrants skepticism of the implicated gene.

The one candidate with unambiguous, disease-relevant, causal (not merely correlative) mechanistic evidence is RHOT1 (Miro1): cigarette-smoke-extract exposure reduces Miro1 protein in three independent primary/immortalized human lung epithelial models with accompanying mitochondrial dysfunction (PMID 31593750), and lung-epithelium-specific Rhot1 knockout mice show augmented neutrophilic/macrophage lung inflammation after both acute and chronic cigarette-smoke exposure relative to wild-type (PMID 35366248) — an in vivo, cell-type-specific, loss-of-function experiment whose own authors frame it explicitly around COPD. Per prior #1, this is correctly understood as smoking-exposure-conditional pulmonary toxicity (cigarette smoke damaging lung epithelial mitochondrial quality control), not a smoking-behavior/ nicotine-neurobiology confound, so it survives that scrutiny cleanly. The catch, which both the researcher and red-team-skeptic flag prominently and which this synthesis will not paper over: RHOT1's statistical/positional support is the weakest of any gene discussed here (a single, low-ranking L2G appearance at 0.053 in only 1 of 9 credible sets; a near-zero, literature-only Open Targets COPD association score; and a gene body sitting mostly outside the stated locus window at its centromeric edge). Genetics and mechanism disagree at this locus, and both halves of that disagreement need to remain visible to the study team rather than being resolved by fiat here.

What would resolve this: (1) a repeat, unblocked literature search on COPRS (the researcher's search was blocked by access failures, not a confirmed absence of biology, and COPRS holds the single highest raw L2G score at the locus); (2) fine-mapped colocalization/eQTL evidence specifically distinguishing LRRC37B from its segmental-duplication paralogs (LRRC37A/LRRC37A2), since current L2G assignment to "LRRC37B" may not cleanly resolve paralog identity; (3) any COPD-specific (not spirometry-only) fine-mapped credible set at this locus, which does not currently exist in Open Targets; (4) direct RHOT1 eQTL/pQTL colocalization with lung function or COPD case-control GWAS at this locus, which was not found here, to connect its strong mechanistic case to the actual genetic signal. Given all of the above, this synthesis explicitly declines to manufacture higher confidence than the evidence supports: rate this locus LOW/INSUFFICIENT for prioritization purposes, with RHOT1 flagged as the mechanistically most compelling candidate worth follow-up despite its weak genetic colocalization, and LRRC37B/SUZ12 flagged as the statistically most consistent but mechanistically least lung-specific candidates.
