Prioritized gene

TGFB2 — Confidence: MODERATE

TGFB2 is the best-supported candidate gene at this locus. The credible set places ~94% of its PIP mass on three TGFB2 intronic/upstream variants (rs2799098 PIP=0.511, rs10495098 PIP=0.229, rs2799097 PIP=0.201), versus only ~3.7% on RRP15-annotated variants. Open Targets' independent L2G-style scoring corroborates this by a wide margin (5-15x higher scores for TGFB2 than RRP15 across COPD, FEV1/FVC, and FEV1, drawn from the same underlying GWAS studies — see Open Targets summary above). The verified literature adds genuine, multi-domain support: concordant genome-wide-significant associations with COPD case-control status, FEV1/FEV1-FVC in both general-population and smoker-restricted cohorts, CT-quantified emphysema patterns, and DLCO (rows 1-6 in the table above); a lung-fibroblast-specific regulatory/enhancer signal at this general locus (Castaldi 2014, Parker 2019); a mouse model in which reduced mesenchymal TGFbeta2 causes emphysema with reduced elastin (Goodwin 2023); and human Mendelian evidence that TGFB2 haploinsufficiency is highly dosage-sensitive (Loeys-Dietz syndrome type 4), consistent with the strong gnomAD pLoF constraint.

Confidence is capped at MODERATE, not HIGH, for reasons the red-team-skeptic identified and that are preserved in the audit file: (1) two of the researcher's cited large COPD GWAS papers (Hobbs 2017, Sakornsakolpat 2019 Nat Genet) received a CLAIM_MISMATCH verdict — their abstracts do not explicitly name TGFB2, so those two specific citations are excluded from the verified table above, even though the papers themselves are real and their general subject matter (large COPD GWAS reconfirming known loci) is consistent with TGFB2's inclusion; (2) the LDS4 (human loss-of-function) phenotype is reported as aortic/skeletal/ craniofacial/ocular, not pulmonary, across a ~3,896-case systematic review — a genuine gap between the Mendelian dosage-sensitivity evidence and the hypothesized pulmonary mechanism, only partly explained by ascertainment bias in cardiovascular-focused LDS surveillance; (3) the strongest CRISPR-validated functional variant (rs1690789, Parker 2019) is ~200kb downstream of TGFB2 and is not one of this locus's own credible-set variants, so it establishes gene-level plausibility (TGFB2 expression is fibroblast-regulated by nearby elements) rather than functional validation of rs2799098 itself; and (4) the smoking-behavior alternative hypothesis (prior #1) was not adequately excluded — the researcher's PubMed search for TGFB2/this locus in smoking-initiation/cessation GWAS was negative but explicitly flagged as incomplete due to search-tool quota exhaustion, and a direct GSCAN/GWAS Catalog lookup for rs2799098 or proxies was never performed. This remains an open item for follow-up, not a resolved question, and is why this section does not claim HIGH confidence.

No serious runner-up gene exists at this locus — RRP15 is not a close competitor (low PIP, low Open Targets scores, no lung/immune-relevant literature identified) — so this is not a close call between two genes in LD. It is, however, a call that should be reported with the explicit caveats above rather than as fully resolved. Evidence that would raise confidence to HIGH: (a) a direct GWAS Catalog/GSCAN lookup confirming rs2799098 (or proxies) is not a genome-wide-significant smoking-behavior hit; (b) lung-tissue eQTL colocalization specifically for rs2799098/rs10495098/rs2799097 (not just the distal rs1690789 variant); and (c) confirmation from the full text (not just abstracts) of Hobbs 2017 and Sakornsakolpat 2019 Nat Genet that TGFB2/this locus is indeed among their reported loci.
