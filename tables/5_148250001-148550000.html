Prioritized gene

HTR4 -- confidence: LOW-MODERATE.

HTR4 has the deepest and most consistent statistical-genetics support of any gene at this locus: three independent GWAS/candidate-gene cohorts (Repapi 2010, Wilk 2012, Soler Artigas 2011) show concordant association across FEV1, FEV1/FVC, and COPD case-control status -- satisfying the pulmonary-function concordance prior well -- with corroboration from targeted resequencing (London 2014) and a modern multi-ancestry WGS paper explicitly naming HTR4 as a locus whose fine-mapping resolution improved with multi-ancestry data (Kim et al. 2026). Uniquely for a GWAS-nominated gene, HTR4 also has genuine causal support from Htr4-null mouse studies (House 2014, House 2017) showing altered baseline lung mechanics and airway hyperresponsiveness via a vagal/neural mechanism, plus developmental lung expression data (Hodge et al. 2013). Evidence against smoking-behavior mediation is present but incomplete (Wilk 2012's independence from the CHRNA5/3 smoking signal in the same paper; Yang 2013's never-smoker replication; Obeidat 2011 showing HTR4 was not the top smoker-stratified hit) -- no direct smoking-trait GWAS cross-trait lookup was completed in this search (search budget was exhausted before it could be done), so this should be treated as suggestive, not settled.

However, red-team-skeptic raised two findings that meaningfully cap confidence and must be weighed honestly rather than smoothed over:

  1. Mechanistic mismatch risk. The causal mouse phenotype is a reversible, vagally-mediated airway-hyperresponsiveness phenotype with explicitly NO structural, fibrotic, or inflammatory phenotype in COPD-relevant injury models (LPS, bleomycin, house-dust-mite), and Hodge et al. found no functional 5-HT4 receptor in adult human airway smooth muscle or bronchial epithelium. AHR is the canonical asthma trait, not classic fixed-obstruction COPD pathology -- this evidence supports HTR4 acting through a neuro-respiratory/reflex pathway that may be more asthma/ACO-adjacent than COPD-specific, and should not be reframed in synthesis as "HTR4 causes COPD" without this caveat.
  2. Discordant quantitative signal favoring SH3TC2. SH3TC2 -- the MAGMA/PoPS-prioritized gene, with zero literature connection to lung biology -- carries the single highest Open Targets FEV1 L2G-style scores at this entire locus (0.79-0.83, vs. HTR4's ~0.05 with one 0.593 outlier). This signal could not be traced to a specific study or variant by either the researcher or citation-verifier, and red-team-skeptic explicitly marked SH3TC2's overall evidence CONTESTED rather than dismissible -- an unresolved, unexplained, high-magnitude quantitative signal, not refuted, just unexplained.

Runner-up: SH3TC2 (essentially unsupported by literature, but not fully ruled out). Its candidacy rests entirely on MAGMA/PoPS statistical prioritization and the untraced Open Targets FEV1 score; its only characterized biology (Schwann-cell myelination, CMT4C) has no documented lung/airway connection in any source found. If the untraced OT FEV1 signal were resolved to a real, gene-specific fine-mapping/colocalization result, this call could flip.

What would resolve the tie: (a) tracing the Open Targets SH3TC2 FEV1 credible-set rows to their underlying GWAS study and variant IDs (via a direct Open Targets API query for variant/study fields, or by searching the Shrine et al. 2023 / large FEV1 GWAS supplementary gene-to-locus tables directly) to determine whether this is a genuine gene-specific association or a proximity/LD artifact; (b) a direct smoking-initiation/cigarettes-per-day GWAS (GSCAN/Liu et al. 2019 or more recent) cross-trait lookup at rs3995091/rs7733410 to settle whether the HTR4 signal is smoking-behavior-mediated; (c) lung-specific eQTL/sQTL colocalization data for both HTR4 and SH3TC2 (GTEx Lung, Lung eQTL Consortium) at this exact credible-set interval, which neither this literature search nor the Open Targets lookup could supply with variant-level resolution.

Given the genuinely strong and repeated (if mechanistically nuanced) literature base for HTR4, weighed against the unresolved discordant quantitative signal for SH3TC2 and the asthma-adjacent character of HTR4's best causal evidence, this is called for HTR4 at LOW-MODERATE confidence, not HIGH -- the study team should treat SH3TC2 as a genuine open question at this locus rather than a closed one.
