Open Targets summary (structured database lookup — not a verified literature citation; not reviewed by citation-verifier or red-team-skeptic)

  • CYP2A6 (ENSG00000255974): Across 30 independent GWAS credible sets anchored on the lead variant rs7251570, CYP2A6 is the ONLY gene with any non-null L2G score in every one of them (scores ~0.065-0.41, generally modest but completely one-sided vs. other genes), including 0.133 for the COPD PheCode-496 "chronic airway obstruction" study (GCST90480254) and 0.130-0.133 for chronic bronchitis, "chronic lung diseases including COPD, emphysema and bronchitis," cigarettes-per-day, smoking status, nicotine dependence, and nicotine-metabolite-ratio studies. High-confidence colocalization (H4 = 0.984-0.996) ties the COPD/chronic-bronchitis/chronic-airway-obstruction and nicotine-metabolite-ratio GWAS signals to a shared GTEx LIVER transcript-usage QTL for a CYP2A6 transcript (ENST00000599960) — no lung-tissue colocalization was tested in Open Targets' catalog for this locus. Aggregate target-disease association score (COPD) = 0.168 (literature datatype 0.321, genetic_association datatype 0.261). Genetic constraint: gnomAD LoF-tolerant (oe ~0.64, moderate constraint bin) — consistent with CYP2A6's known common pharmacogenomic null alleles.
  • C19orf47 (ENSG00000160392): No non-null L2G score in any of the 30 credible sets; zero Open Targets disease-association evidence of any datatype linking C19orf47 to COPD. LoF-tolerant (oe ~0.59, moderate constraint bin).
  • AKT2 (ENSG00000105221): No non-null L2G score in any of the 30 credible sets; near-zero (0.0044) aggregate COPD association score (literature datatype only, no genetic_association contribution) — roughly 40-fold lower than CYP2A6's. Highly gnomAD LoF-CONSTRAINED (oe ~0.26, most constrained bin) — a dosage-sensitive gene in general (known monogenic severe-insulin-resistance/lipodystrophy gene via coding mutation), but with no genetic or colocalization evidence tying this locus's regulatory signal to it specifically.
  • EGLN2 (ENSG00000129521; not queried directly in the Open Targets step, since it was added as a candidate afterward by the researcher): by implication from the CYP2A6 scan above, EGLN2 did not appear with any non-null L2G score in any of the 30 credible sets tested (only CYP2A6 did), and no colocalization result for EGLN2 was surfaced. This is an absence of evidence in the structured data, not a formal null query result for EGLN2 specifically — see audit file for full discussion of why EGLN2 is nonetheless a literature-nominated competing candidate at this locus.

Prioritized gene

CYP2A6 — Confidence: LOW.

CYP2A6 has the largest, most consistent, and most rigorously tested body of evidence at this locus: it is the only gene with any L2G signal across all 30 Open-Targets credible sets anchored on the lead variant, it colocalizes at high confidence with a liver transcript-usage QTL shared by the COPD/chronic-bronchitis and nicotine-metabolite-ratio GWAS signals, and — most importantly — two independent, well-designed Mendelian randomization studies (PMID 42219484, mediated MR with a negative-control reverse-MR arm; PMID 38335242, multivariable MR) both support a model in which CYP2A6's effect on COPD risk is substantially mediated through smoking quantity/nicotine intake rather than direct lung-intrinsic biology — exactly the mechanism Prior 1 anticipates for this locus.

However, this call is genuinely close, for two reasons the evidence table and audit make explicit rather than paper over. First, red-team-skeptic marked the whole CYP2A6 body of evidence WEAKENED (not simply accepted): the liver-based colocalization and the pediatric PM2.5/Th2 airway-epithelial finding (PMID 38986136) argue that CYP2A6's role here is best understood as smoking/xenobiotic-metabolism-mediated and disease-nonspecific, not as direct, COPD-specific lung-intrinsic biology — meaning the mechanism is well supported, but it is a mechanism of shared risk (smoking behavior), not gene-specific structural or immune pathology unique to COPD. Second, and more importantly for the "which gene" question: red-team-skeptic marked the runner-up candidate, EGLN2, CONTESTED but explicitly on the grounds that EGLN2's evidence (multiple independent lung- and blood-eQTL/methylation studies — see audit — nominating EGLN2 over CYP2A6 as the functionally causal COPD gene at this exact 19q13.2 locus) is "same-tier eQTL/statistical evidence as CYP2A6... not more solidly established than CYP2A6," i.e. the skeptic's critique is that the tie has not been resolved, not that EGLN2 is clearly wrong. CYP2A6 wins on structured-data corroboration (L2G presence across all 30 credible sets; C19orf47 and AKT2 score essentially zero by comparison) and on having direct causal-mediation MR evidence that EGLN2 lacks, but no lung-tissue colocalization or EGLN2-specific functional/mechanistic study exists to formally adjudicate CYP2A6 vs. EGLN2 at the molecular level.

Runner-up: EGLN2 (excluded from the main evidence table above because red-team-skeptic marked it CONTESTED; full evidence and reasoning in the audit file). What would resolve the tie: (1) a lung-tissue (not just liver) colocalization analysis of this locus's COPD/chronic-bronchitis credible sets against modern lung eQTL/sQTL datasets (e.g., current GTEx or a large lung-eQTL consortium) capable of distinguishing a CYP2A6 vs. an EGLN2 lung-expression signal; (2) an EGLN2-specific functional/mechanistic study in airway or alveolar tissue (none currently exists — the EGLN2 case rests entirely on eQTL/methylation association, not functional validation); (3) formal statistical fine-mapping/coloc directly comparing CYP2A6- and EGLN2-transcript causal probability at this locus, ideally in an Open-Targets-indexed lung dataset. AKT2 and C19orf47 are not viable competing candidates on current evidence (near-zero-to-zero Open Targets association scores, no locus-specific genetic evidence, and a suspected genome-build mismatch placing both physically outside the stated GRCh38 locus window — see audit) and should not be prioritized without first resolving that coordinate issue.

Regardless of the CYP2A6-vs-EGLN2 resolution, the broader, better-supported conclusion for this locus is that its COPD association is substantially smoking/nicotine-behavior-mediated (Prior 1), which should inform how any follow-up functional work at this locus is framed.
