Prioritized gene

Gene: HTRA1 Confidence: LOW

This call is close, and confidence is capped at LOW because the verified evidence does not cleanly distinguish HTRA1 from its immediate neighbors ARMS2 and PLEKHA1 in this tightly-linked 10q26 block, and because no verified source anywhere argues for a specific gene's causal role in COPD at this locus — every mechanistic and causal-gene argument in the evidence table above is drawn from the AMD literature, not COPD.

HTRA1 is named as the best-supported single pick, on balance, for three reasons: (1) it carries the four highest-PIP credible-set variants (rs10887154 PIP=0.219, rs2268351 PIP=0.193, rs1009326 PIP=0.193, rs17103659 PIP=0.162), an order of magnitude above the PLEKHA1-annotated variants (highest PIP=0.0021); (2) it has the most extensively characterized, mechanistically plausible general biology of any gene in the region — a secreted serine protease regulating TGF-β bioavailability and ECM/elastic-lamina turnover (Kato 2021, PMID 34779414; Monte 2025, PMID 41142321; Chuanfen 2022, PMID 35841197) and driving monocyte/macrophage persistence and pro-inflammatory polarization (Beguier 2020, PMID 32814029; Ahamed 2022, PMID 36142120) — biology that is directly analogous to processes implicated in emphysema pathogenesis (protease- driven ECM remodeling, macrophage activation), even though it has never been demonstrated in lung tissue specifically; and (3) it is moderately constrained against loss-of-function in gnomAD (oe=0.595, upperBin6=2), more so than either PLEKHA1 or PPAPDC1A/PLPP4.

However, red-team-skeptic's critique (preserved in full in the audit file) substantially tempers this call and is the reason confidence is LOW rather than MODERATE: the credible-set variants are all non-coding, MODIFIER-impact, intronic/downstream variants whose annotation to HTRA1 reflects nearest-gene/VEP convention in a locus with three physically overlapping genes (HTRA1, ARMS2, PLEKHA1), not functional attribution. Critically, the one place this exact three-gene ambiguity has been substantively adjudicated in the literature — AMD, the disease this locus is actually famous for — the resolution has trended AWAY from HTRA1: the original Jakobsdottir et al. 2005 (PMID 16080115) linkage paper favored PLEKHA1/ARMS2 over HTRA1 coding variants, and later mechanistic work (Fritsche 2008 PMID 18511946; Kanda 2007 PMID 17884985; summarized explicitly in Kortvely & Ueffing 2016, PMID 26427389: AMD risk haplotypes affect ARMS2 expression specifically, "making ARMS2 the best candidate...within this locus," NOT HTRA1) argues the regulatory effect belongs to ARMS2. ARMS2 itself has no credible-set variant annotated to it in this COPD locus, but per the skeptic this may reflect a known VEP/annotation bias toward the larger overlapping HTRA1 transcript rather than genuine absence of an ARMS2 regulatory relationship — an under-explored possibility, not a refuted one.

Compounding this, Open Targets' own COPD GWAS study has no fine-mapped credible set anywhere in this region at all (raising the unresolved question, flagged by red-team-skeptic, of whether this locus is genuinely genome-wide-significant in the published COPD/lung-function GWAS or only in the internal pipeline's meta-analysis), HTRA1's only eQTL-GWAS colocalization in Open Targets is with body height (not COPD/FEV1-FVC), two earlier/smaller lung-function GWAS explicitly exclude this locus (Soler Artigas 2015 PMID 26635082; Obeidat 2011 PMID 21625484), and no lung-specific mechanistic literature exists for HTRA1 at all — the ECM/TGF-β evidence is entirely cerebral small-vessel disease (CARASIL), and the macrophage/monocyte evidence is entirely retina/choroid. Whether smoking mediates this locus's COPD association also remains genuinely unresolved (not confirmed or denied from GSCAN/MVP smoking-GWAS abstract-level search).

Runner-up: PLEKHA1 — real causal precedent at this exact LD block, but for AMD (Jakobsdottir 2005), itself since contested by ARMS2-favoring work; PIP ~100-fold lower than HTRA1's top variants within this COPD credible set; is the only one of the three named genes with a GTEx lung-tissue molecular QTL, but that credible set's own colocalization test against 107 GWAS partner traits found none reaching COPD or FEV1/FVC — an empirical, if database-level rather than literature, argument against a PLEKHA1 lung mechanism specifically.

Second runner-up (flagged, not formally scored above): ARMS2 — no credible-set variant annotated to it and no COPD-specific evidence, but per red-team-skeptic's critique, its absence from the credible set may be an artifact of nearest-gene/VEP annotation convention rather than genuine biological irrelevance, given its well-documented role as the more likely functionally relevant gene for the AMD signal at this identical locus.

PPAPDC1A/PLPP4 (the PoPS-prioritized gene) is not competitive: no credible-set variant annotated to it, zero Open Targets evidence for COPD, and its only characterized biology (oncogenic proliferation signaling in lung/gastric cancer cell lines) has no plausible connection to COPD/emphysema pathophysiology.

What would resolve the tie: (1) direct confirmation of whether this locus (10q26, chr10:122.3-122.6Mb) is actually among the genome-wide-significant loci in Sakornsakolpat 2019 (82 COPD loci) or Shrine 2019 (279 lung-function signals) — this is currently unconfirmed and should be checked against the papers' supplementary locus tables before treating this as an established COPD hit at all; (2) an LD lookup (e.g., LDlink) between this locus's lead variant (rs10887154) and the classic AMD-associated variants (rs11200638, the HTRA1 promoter SNP; and rs10490924, the ARMS2 A69S variant) to determine whether the COPD signal is in strong LD with the AMD signal (favoring an LD/pleiotropy explanation) or statistically distinct from it (favoring an independent pulmonary mechanism); (3) a COPD/lung-tissue-specific eQTL or single-cell dataset covering HTRA1, ARMS2, and PLEKHA1 expression in airway/alveolar cell types, which does not currently exist in the literature searched; (4) direct confirmation from GSCAN/MVP smoking-GWAS supplementary tables of whether this locus is a smoking-behavior locus, which would support a smoking-mediated (rather than direct pulmonary) mechanism regardless of which gene is causal.

The study team should treat this as an open call between HTRA1 and its immediate 10q26 neighbors (ARMS2, PLEKHA1) rather than a settled HTRA1 assignment, pending the LD and locus-membership checks above.
