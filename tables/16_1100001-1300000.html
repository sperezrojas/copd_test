Prioritized gene

Gene: CACNA1H Confidence: LOW

This is a close call between CACNA1H and TPSG1, and the evidence does not cleanly resolve it — the skeptic's critique should be read alongside this recommendation, not discounted.

CACNA1H is favored on strength of statistical/positional evidence alone: it is the nearest gene, and 13 of 15 credible-set variants (cumulative PIP ~0.98, including both missense variants) are annotated to it, versus TPSG1 having zero credible-set variants and being implicated only via the orthogonal PoPS method. However, every other line of evidence for CACNA1H points away from a pulmonary mechanism: its only well-established human disease biology is neuro-endocrine (familial hyperaldosteronism type IV via a gain-of-function mutation unrelated to this locus's variants; epilepsy pharmacogenomics; migraine), its one variant-level phenotype claim at this exact credible-set variant (rs61734410/ethosuximide response) failed to replicate, no PubMed literature of any kind connects CACNA1H to COPD, spirometry, or airway/immune biology (a search explicitly performed and returning zero hits), and Open Targets' own genetics-evidence pipeline registers no COPD signal for this gene despite readily registering genetics evidence for its actual known diseases. The Open Targets colocalization data at this exact variant point toward smoking-cessation behavior and blood-cell-count traits, not lung function — a plausible but currently uncorroborated alternative mechanism (per this project's smoking-mediation prior) that neither the researcher nor the skeptic could confirm or refute in the literature.

The runner-up, TPSG1 (the PoPS-prioritized gene), has the single most mechanistically direct COPD-relevant finding at this locus — a mouse knockout of its ortholog (Prss31) that reduces cigarette-smoke-induced experimental COPD pathology — but this is one mouse loss-of-function study with a double-edged phenotype (protective for smoke-induced pathology, harmful for baseline airway hyperreactivity), no supporting human genetics, and no credible-set variant support at all.

What would resolve this tie: (1) a human eQTL/sQTL or TWAS study directly testing CACNA1H or TPSG1 expression against COPD case-control status or FEV1/FEV1-FVC in lung tissue (none was found by either the researcher or Open Targets); (2) literature or GWAS catalog evidence directly testing whether this locus's signal (or CACNA1H specifically) associates with smoking initiation/cessation/ heaviness, to test the smoking-mediation alternative the Open Targets colocalization data raises; (3) an independent lung-relevant functional (not skin/uterus QTL) dataset at this exact variant. Given the near-total absence of pulmonary-specific evidence for the top fine-mapped gene and the thin, double-edged evidence for the PoPS runner-up, this locus's gene assignment should be treated as low-confidence by the study team, not as a settled call.
