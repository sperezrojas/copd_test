Prioritized gene

CWF19L2 — Confidence: LOW

CWF19L2 is the best-supported candidate at this locus, but the support is thin and the confidence level should not be overstated. The case rests almost entirely on the Open Targets structured database evidence above, not on the verified literature table: CWF19L2 is simultaneously the nearest gene, the FLAMES-prioritized gene, and the MAGMA-prioritized gene, and its exon-usage QTLs in two independent datasets (GTEx adipose, GEUVADIS LCL) colocalize with a COPD GWAS signal at high posterior probability (H4 up to 0.995) — while Open Targets found precisely zero colocalizing molecular QTLs for the only other protein-coding gene physically in the region, ALKBH8, whose L2G score is ~10x weaker and which is only PoPS-prioritized (a genome-wide, non-locus-specific method). The red-team critique concurred that this differential (coloc present vs. absent; L2G 0.190 vs. 0.020; three-method convergence vs. one) is genuinely gene-discriminating and not simply LD/ nearest-gene bias, since ALKBH8 sits even farther from the credible set and still shows no molecular QTL signal of its own.

However, every verified literature citation for CWF19L2 is non-pulmonary (spliceosome lariat-RNA debranching, mouse spermatogenesis, an incidental retinal-splicing finding), and no PubMed/bioRxiv/WebSearch query by either the researcher or the red-team-skeptic found any COPD-, spirometry-, or smoking-specific publication naming CWF19L2. The colocalizing QTLs themselves are in adipose and lymphoblastoid tissue, not lung, so tissue relevance to COPD biology is inferred, not demonstrated. The red-team-skeptic's verdict for CWF19L2 was WEAKENED specifically on these grounds: "statistically prioritized, mechanistically unconfirmed in lung." Smoking-mediation could not be ruled in or out — GSCAN smoking-GWAS summary statistics were never directly queried by researcher or skeptic (both flagged this as an unresolved gap), and GWAS Catalog lookups for the lead variant returned no trait associations at all (though this specific negative result could not be independently confirmed by the skeptic due to an API error).

The runner-up, ALKBH8, is weaker on every statistical-genetics axis (no colocalization, ~10x lower L2G, not nearest/FLAMES/MAGMA-prioritized, farther from the credible set) but contributes the single most directly lung-relevant mechanistic finding of either candidate: Alkbh8-deficient mouse lungs show oxidative stress, reduced selenoprotein levels, and heightened susceptibility to naphthalene (a tobacco-smoke-relevant PAH)-induced lung injury. This is a mouse toxicology study with no human genetic or COPD-specific corroboration, and the red-team-skeptic rated ALKBH8 WEAKENED bordering CONTESTED — not because a rival gene was found to independently implicate ALKBH8 by name, but because the statistical-genetics evidence actively argues against it relative to its neighbor.

What would resolve this call: a lung-tissue-specific eQTL/sQTL colocalization for CWF19L2 (the existing colocalizations are adipose/LCL, not airway or lung parenchyma); any direct COPD case-control, spirometry (FEV1/FEV1-FVC), or smoking-behavior GWAS/TWAS/MR publication naming either gene; and a direct query of GSCAN smoking-initiation/cessation/ heaviness summary statistics at this locus to assess smoking-mediation, none of which were available within this review's tool access. Given the total absence of COPD-specific human literature for the top candidate and the reliance on cross-tissue statistical colocalization alone, confidence is marked LOW rather than MODERATE or HIGH despite the clear statistical edge over the only other gene physically in the region.
