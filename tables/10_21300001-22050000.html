Prioritized-gene synthesis

Prioritized gene: MLLT10 — Confidence: LOW

This locus does not resolve cleanly, and I want to be explicit that this call is close rather than manufacture false certainty. Four internal prioritization methods point in four different directions (nearest=SKIDA1, FLAMES/PoPS=MLLT10, ABC=CASC10/MIR1915HG, MAGMA=NA), the single highest-PIP credible-set variant (rs12770228, PIP=0.34) sits in a non-coding lncRNA host gene with essentially zero disease-genetics literature of its own, and none of the four candidate genes has a COPD or FEV1/FVC-specific genetic-association hit in Open Targets — only DNAJC1 has any positive quantitative pulmonary-trait signal (FEV1, score 0.811), and the red-team-skeptic's critique (see audits/ appendix) persuasively argues that signal is currently too provisional (unverified primary GWAS source, ~300-400kb physical/statistical disconnection from the credible-set mass, and — per the newly verified Meyer et al. 2017 ARDS abstract — a plausible non-replication precedent from a related acute-lung-injury phenotype where a follow-up full study prioritized a different gene, FLT1) to be weighted as a serious competing candidate rather than a flagged open question.

I am naming MLLT10 as the single best-supported pick for the following reasons, while explicitly flagging why this is not a high-confidence call:

  • It is the only candidate gene supported by two independent internal gene-prioritization methods (FLAMES and PoPS) that integrate GWAS signal genome-wide, rather than by proximity or a single regulatory-linkage method.
  • It physically spans (in intron/regulatory sequence) the largest number of credible-set variants of any gene at this locus, even though — per the skeptic's valid caveat — this partly reflects MLLT10's large genomic footprint rather than concentrated causal signal, and no single MLLT10-annotated variant individually exceeds PIP=0.041.
  • It is strongly LoF-constrained (gnomAD oe=0.20, 90% CI 0.14-0.29), consistent with PoPS/FLAMES tending to favor broadly important, dosage-sensitive genes — this is a double-edged observation (it could reflect either true biological importance at this locus, or simply that constrained/essential genes are systematically favored by these network/expression-based prioritization methods regardless of tissue specificity, as the skeptic notes).
  • Its only disease-association signal of note (asthma, in Open Targets' broader profile) is, per both researcher and skeptic, most plausibly a comorbidity/genetic-correlation artifact inherited from an unrelated endometriosis GWAS (Rahmioglu et al. 2023) rather than a direct lung-function hit — this is a genuine weakness of the MLLT10 case, not a strength, and is reported here rather than concealed.

Explicit runner-up and what would resolve the tie: CASC10/MIR1915HG is a real competitor and arguably has the strongest raw statistical claim to primacy — it carries the dominant share of fine-mapped PIP (~0.40, driven by the lead variant itself) and is the ABC-prioritized (enhancer/promoter regulatory-linkage) gene. It was not selected as the top pick because it has literally zero genetic-association-datatype evidence for any disease in Open Targets and no COPD/lung-relevant functional literature — a lncRNA host gene this thinly characterized cannot currently be distinguished from the alternative, self-flagged-by-the-researcher hypothesis that its ABC-assigned regulatory element may actually act on a neighboring gene (SKIDA1 or MLLT10) rather than on MIR1915HG itself. What would resolve this tie: a chromatin-conformation (Hi-C/ABC-validating) or CRISPRi-based enhancer-target study at this specific locus showing which gene's promoter the lead-variant-containing regulatory element actually contacts/activates, or a direct COPD/FEV1 colocalization or TWAS hit for either MLLT10 or MIR1915HG specifically (neither currently exists in Open Targets or the literature reviewed here). DNAJC1 remains a third, weaker possibility pending independent replication of its FEV1/ARDS signals in a peer-reviewed, COPD- or chronic-airflow-obstruction-specific study — see audit appendix.

Given the absence of any gene-specific COPD/FEV1/FEV1-FVC colocalization or fine-mapping evidence distinguishing MLLT10 from CASC10/MIR1915HG (or, less plausibly, DNAJC1) at this locus, confidence is LOW, not MODERATE or HIGH, and this recommendation should be weighed by the study team alongside the runner-up case above, not treated as settled.
