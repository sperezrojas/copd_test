Prioritized-gene synthesis

Prioritized gene: FLRT1 — Confidence: LOW

FLRT1 carries by far the largest share of credible-set posterior probability at this locus (~0.65 of total PIP mass across its intronic/5'UTR variants, including the lead variant rs592271 itself, PIP=0.112) and is the nearest gene — the strongest purely statistical- genetic signal in the evidence table. However, this pick is genuinely weak and should not be read as a confident call. The verified literature (rows 1-5 above) describes FLRT1 almost exclusively in neurodevelopmental contexts (axon guidance, cortical neuron migration, FGFR1 signaling potentiation) with reported expression in kidney and brain, not lung, and no COPD/spirometry/smoking-trait literature or Open Targets genetic evidence exists for this gene. The red-team-skeptic explicitly flagged (critique verbatim in audits/11_64050001-64300000_audit.md) that this "no biology found" framing is being used to implicitly deprioritize the top-PIP gene without ruling out the alternative that these intronic/regulatory credible-set variants are actually a distal eQTL for a neighboring gene (RPS6KA4, PLCB3, FERMT3, or STIP1 are all within the same ~250kb window) — no GTEx lung-eQTL or colocalization data was retrieved for FLRT1 or any other gene at this locus in this review, which is the single largest unresolved gap.

Runner-up 1 — PLCB3 (mechanistically the best-supported gene, but statistically weak and carrying a negative COPD genetic result): PLCB3 has the most diverse mechanistic evidence at this locus — airway epithelial IL-8/TLR signaling in cystic fibrosis, MUC5AC mucin regulation, and FcɛRI/mast-cell-driven airway inflammation — and is one of the two most LoF-constrained genes at the locus (o/e=0.37). However, a rare PLCB3 missense variant tested directly in a COPD case-control meta-analysis (9,888 cases/27,060 controls) was not significantly associated with COPD (OR=1.05, P=0.247), and the skeptic flagged that this negative result deserves more weight than the researcher's framing gave it, and that the mechanistic evidence spans distinct disease contexts (CF, allergic/Th2 mast-cell biology) not cleanly reconciled with COPD's typically non-allergic, neutrophilic phenotype. PLCB3's credible-set PIP mass (~0.04) is far smaller than FLRT1's.

Runner-up 2 — RPS6KA4 (independently prioritized by both MAGMA and PoPS, but its case is explicitly CONTESTED by the skeptic): RPS6KA4 has a genuine, verified mechanistic role in macrophage innate-immune cytokine/interferon regulation (MSK2 via p38/DUSP1), directly relevant to prior #3 (inflammatory complexity), and an Open Targets-flagged (but NOT literature-verified) genetic association with smoking initiation/cessation traits relevant to prior #1. The skeptic explicitly contested the researcher's "strong dual-pathway candidate" characterization: RPS6KA4 has zero credible-set variant support of its own (a gene-level MAGMA/PoPS signal only), the smoking-trait claim remains unconfirmed after attempted independent verification, and the macrophage mechanism, while real, is not lung/COPD-specific.

What would resolve this tie: (1) GTEx or other lung-tissue eQTL/colocalization data for the credible-set variants against FLRT1, MACROD1, RPS6KA4, PLCB3, FERMT3, and STIP1 — the single most important missing piece, since it would directly test the distal-eQTL alternative hypothesis the skeptic raised for FLRT1; (2) independent confirmation (via GWAS Catalog or PhenoScanner, not PubMed) of the RPS6KA4-smoking-initiation/cessation association; (3) an FEV1/FEV1-FVC spirometry-trait GWAS lookup for all candidate genes, which is currently absent for every gene at this locus and, per the skeptic, should push overall locus confidence down regardless of which single gene is favored. Given all of the above, this locus is best characterized as weakly resolved overall, and the FLRT1 pick here should be weighed by the study team as a tentative, PIP-driven default rather than a biologically confirmed causal call.
