Prioritized gene

Prioritized gene: XRN2. Confidence: LOW.

This locus is one of the least clear-cut in the pipeline so far, and the call is close. The strongest quantitative signal — convergence of FLAMES, MAGMA, and PoPS on XRN2, plus the only nonzero (if weak) Open Targets COPD genetic-association scores among the protein-coding genes here (XRN2, KIZ, NKX2-4, in that rough order of statistical/PIP support) — favors XRN2 over the nearest gene, NKX2-2. Critically, none of the locus's own high-PIP credible-set variants (rs2249048, rs68173807, rs804389, rs11697152) are assigned to XRN2 by Open Targets' own L2G; they are all assigned to NKX2-2, but via colocalization with brain white-matter DTI and refractive-error traits, not COPD or lung function — i.e. the gene with the best fine-mapping-based regulatory support (NKX2-2) has no COPD-specific signal, while the gene with the best COPD-specific (if weak) signal (XRN2) has no fine-mapping/colocalization support at the locus's actual GWAS peak. The red-team-skeptic explicitly cautioned against "crowning XRN2 as the likely causal gene merely because it has the best statistical-tool convergence," since none of XRN2's cited mechanistic literature (lung-cancer EMT, PARP1 synthetic lethality, gut-mucosal HIV/XRN2) is COPD-specific, airway-immune-specific, or even lung-tissue-specific in one case, and since the three "background" lung-function GWAS papers cited for it (Shrine 2019/2023, Sakornsakolpat 2019) have not been confirmed to actually list this locus/gene in their own tables.

NKX2-2 is the runner-up and cannot be ruled out: it is the nearest gene, has by far the clearest regulatory/colocalization evidence at the locus's own lead variants (L2G ~0.75-0.76, H4 up to 0.996), and its verified literature shows it is expressed and functionally relevant in at least one lung neuroendocrine cell context (SCLC, per Lawson et al.) even though it is absent from another (typical carcinoid, per Wang/Yang et al.) — a genuine, unresolved internal contradiction in the record (flagged CONTESTED above) that argues for caution rather than dismissal. Its principal weakness is that all of its colocalization support at this locus is with non-pulmonary traits (brain DTI, refractive error) and it has zero Open Targets COPD genetic-association evidence at all, plus an independent, well-documented pleiotropic association of this same sub-region with Ewing sarcoma — none of which excludes a lung role, but none of which supports one either.

KIZ and NKX2-4 are both weaker than either lead candidate (KIZ: general mitotic gene, extremely low-PIP missense variant, no lung/immune literature; NKX2-4: negligible PIP, essentially no literature at all) and are not competitive picks.

What would resolve the tie: direct evidence that this exact locus/lead variant reaches genome-wide significance in the major lung-function GWAS (Shrine 2019/2023, Sakornsakolpat 2019) with a locus-specific gene assignment in their own fine-mapping; a COPD- or lung-function-specific eQTL/colocalization study for XRN2 or NKX2-2 in airway/lung tissue (as opposed to brain, gut, or cancer cell lines); and/or direct evidence on NKX2-2 expression in non-neoplastic pulmonary neuroendocrine cells or airway epithelium (as opposed to the neoplastic-marker literature currently available). Absent that, this locus should be reported to the study team as a low-confidence, essentially tied call between XRN2 (statistical/nominal-genetic-association favorite, mechanistically unsupported for COPD) and NKX2-2 (regulatory/fine-mapping favorite at the locus, but for non-pulmonary traits, with a real internal contradiction in its lung-neuroendocrine literature).
