Prioritized gene

ME3 — confidence LOW.

This call is close and should be read as such. Of the three candidates, ME3 has (a) the strongest and most internally consistent Open Targets L2G support for both COPD (0.636, highest of the three) and FEV1 (six scores clustered 0.562-0.713, the tightest and highest cluster of the three genes), and (b) partial physical overlap with the fine-mapped credible- set window (unlike PRSS23, which sits 300-470 kb outside it). Against that, the red-team critique is correct that ME3 has no direct GWAS/TWAS paper naming it for COPD or lung function, and its only mechanistic literature (Huang 2024, Lee 2021, evidence-table rows above) is generic NADPH/redox biology from gastric and liver cancer — a mechanism (oxidative-stress buffering) that is COPD-plausible in principle per the smoking/oxidative- stress literature broadly, but not demonstrated in lung tissue and not specific enough to distinguish ME3 from many other redox-pathway genes genome-wide.

The runner-up, CCDC81, is a genuinely close alternative and cannot be dismissed: it is the nearest gene, carries nearly the entire credible set (including the lead variant, PIP=0.865), and has the single most lung/trachea-tissue-specific piece of functional evidence at this locus (Liu et al. 2025, showing CCDC81 is required for ciliogenesis and is markedly expressed in mouse lung/trachea — directly relevant to the mucociliary-clearance axis of COPD structural biology). However, its Open Targets L2G score is exactly zero for both COPD and FEV1, which — per the red-team critique — is a meaningful negative data point (L2G models incorporate positional/distance features, so a zero score for the nearest gene is evidence of active deprioritization by an integrative model, not simply "no data yet"), and its remaining functional literature is non-lung basic cell biology (sperm centrioles, Tetrahymena, HU/IHF superfamily structural biology) bridged to COPD relevance largely via a primary-ciliary-dyskinesia background citation that the skeptic judged does more rhetorical than evidentiary work, since PCD is a distinct Mendelian ciliopathy from common-variant COPD.

PRSS23 is the weakest-supported of the three and was marked CONTESTED by the red-team skeptic: one of its citations was rejected as CLAIM_MISMATCH, it does not physically overlap the credible set, its EMT/fibrosis mechanistic literature is entirely non-lung (cardiac valve, skin/scleroderma, cancer), and its most COPD-adjacent evidence (smoking/biomass-smoke methylation studies) cannot be distinguished from reverse causation — smoke exposure altering methylation independent of the germline credible-set variants — rather than reflecting the genetic mechanism actually being tested at this locus.

What would resolve this tie: gene-specific colocalization/fine-mapping evidence linking the rs11234628 credible-set haplotype to expression of ME3, CCDC81, or PRSS23 specifically in lung/airway epithelial tissue (e.g., lung eQTL colocalization, not currently retrievable from the Open Targets fields queried); an explicit methylation-QTL analysis testing whether the PRSS23 smoking-associated CpGs are under control of the credible-set variants (to distinguish germline mechanism from reverse causation); and the contradicting/alternative- gene literature search flagged as incomplete by the red-team skeptic (searches for rs11234628 or "11p13 lung function" naming a gene other than these three). Until any of that is done, this recommendation should be treated as provisional and low-confidence, not a settled call.
