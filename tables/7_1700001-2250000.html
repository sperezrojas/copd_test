Prioritized gene

MAD1L1 — Confidence: MODERATE

MAD1L1 is, with high confidence, the correct gene assignment at this locus: nearest gene, FLAMES, MAGMA, and PoPS unanimously agree, there is no ABC-implicated alternative, the 33-variant credible set is 100% intronic to MAD1L1, and Open Targets' L2G scoring places MAD1L1 far ahead of the flanking genes NUDT1 and MRM2 across all 85 credible sets overlapping the lead variant — including at a credible set specifically mapped to "chronic airway obstruction" / COPD (L2G 0.627 vs. 0.204 and 0.107). No literature or database evidence anywhere makes a case for NUDT1 or MRM2 as the causal gene here (see Open Targets summary and researcher's runner-up-gene notes). On gene identity alone, this would merit HIGH confidence.

However, this pipeline evaluates genes as candidates for the COPD mechanism, and the mechanistic story at this locus is genuinely unresolved — which is why overall confidence is downgraded to MODERATE rather than HIGH. The verified evidence base is heavily weighted toward a smoking-behavior/neuropsychiatric-mediated interpretation rather than a pulmonary-structural or immune-inflammatory one, consistent with this pipeline's prior #1: MAD1L1's strongest disease associations in Open Targets are schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, MDD, and PTSD, its smoking-initiation genetic-association sub-score (0.526) is among its highest of any trait, and — most tellingly — variant rs56226325, which is itself a member of this locus's own COPD credible set (PIP=0.0072), is independently reported (Levey et al. 2020, PMID 31906708) as a cross-psychiatric-disorder risk signal. This is not merely a neighboring pleiotropic locus; it is literal shared-variant overlap between this locus's COPD signal and psychiatric-disease risk. Meanwhile, no FEV1/FEV1-FVC association for MAD1L1 was found anywhere (neither in Open Targets nor in a direct literature search), the gene shows no genetic constraint against loss-of-function variation (gnomAD oe≈1.0-1.2, arguing against a haploinsufficiency-driven mechanism), the one substantive "structural lung disease" finding (Allen et al. 2020, PMID 31710517) is for IPF — a fibrotic disease pathophysiologically distinct from COPD/emphysema — and the one COPD-specific host-expression finding (Gao et al. 2024, PMID 38622589) is a cross-sectional microbiome-transcriptome correlation that red-team-skeptic judges confounding-prone and non-mechanistic. No dedicated COPD colocalization, functional knockout, or mouse-model study of MAD1L1 exists in the literature reviewed.

In short: MAD1L1 is very likely the correct gene at this locus, but whether its COPD association reflects genuine pulmonary/immune biology or is substantially a downstream readout of a smoking-initiation/neurobehavioral pathway (per this pipeline's prior #1) remains unresolved with current evidence, and the balance of what evidence exists leans toward the latter. The single highest-value follow-up identified by both researcher and red-team-skeptic is resolving whether MAD1L1/this specific 7:1.7-2.25Mb region is among the 13 FEV1/FEV1-FVC-replicated novel loci in Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019 (PMID 30804561) — this could not be confirmed from the retrieved full text by either the researcher or the citation-verifier, and would meaningfully shift this call toward pulmonary-structural (if confirmed) or further toward smoking-mediated/uncertain (if it is not one of the replicating loci). The study team should weigh this distinction — gene identity vs. causal pathway — explicitly when using this locus for downstream prioritization (e.g., functional follow-up should probably test both a pulmonary-epithelial and a CNS/behavioral hypothesis rather than assuming the former).
