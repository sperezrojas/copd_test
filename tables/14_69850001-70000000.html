Prioritized gene

SMOC1 — Confidence: LOW.

SMOC1 is the best-supported single candidate at this locus, but the support is almost entirely positional/computational rather than biological, and the confidence should not be read as higher than that. In favor: SMOC1 is the nearest gene to and physically contains the sole credible-set variant (PIP=0.9957, intronic), is independently flagged by FLAMES, and is the clear top-ranked gene by Open Targets' locus-specific Locus2Gene model (0.763 vs. 0.067 for the only other gene the model scored, SLC10A1). Against: as the red-team-skeptic emphasized, this L2G "win" reflects an uncontested field of only two scored genes, not strong discrimination against a broad candidate set; there is zero colocalization evidence for this credible set; zero literature-indexed statistical-genetics support (no PubMed hit linking SMOC1 to lung function, COPD, or spirometry); and zero GWAS Catalog association between SMOC1 and any spirometric trait (its other GWAS Catalog hits are height, bone density, lean mass, and erythrocyte volume — anthropometric/hematologic, not pulmonary). The six VERIFIED citations establish SMOC1 as a genuinely well-characterized BMP-pathway antagonist causal for Ophthalmo-acromelic/Waardenburg Anophthalmia syndrome in humans and mice, but none of them mention a lung, airway, or alveolar phenotype — this is a real evidentiary gap, not an artifact of incomplete search, per both the researcher and the skeptic's independent corroborating search. Per Prior #2 (spirometry concordance), a COPD-only association with no supporting FEV1/FEV1-FVC signal "warrants consideration of alternative pathways" — and here no alternative pathway (including smoking-mediation, which neither the researcher nor the skeptic could confirm or exclude) was identified either, leaving the mechanism at this locus genuinely unresolved.

No credible runner-up exists in the evidence gathered: SLC39A9 (the PoPS-flagged alternative) has zero L2G support, zero Open Targets disease association, and — per the researcher's own coordinate lookup — lies entirely outside the analyzed region (~390kb from the locus start), a materially weaker positional case than SMOC1. SLC10A1 (not pipeline-flagged) is the only other gene the L2G model scored, but at 0.067 versus SMOC1's 0.763, with no lung-relevant literature and biology (hepatic bile-acid transport, HBV/HDV receptor) that has no plausible pulmonary connection.

This is therefore not a case of two tied candidates needing a tiebreaker — SMOC1 is clearly the best-supported candidate among those evaluated — but the LOW confidence rating reflects that "best-supported" here means best-supported positionally, not biologically validated for a pulmonary mechanism. What would resolve this: a lung-tissue eQTL/pQTL colocalization for SMOC1 at this locus, a spirometric-trait (FEV1/FEV1-FVC) GWAS association concordant with the COPD signal, or any functional lung/airway phenotype data for SMOC1 (currently entirely absent from the literature reviewed). Absent that, this locus should be treated as an unresolved/positional candidate-gene call for the study team's judgment, not a biologically confirmed COPD gene.
