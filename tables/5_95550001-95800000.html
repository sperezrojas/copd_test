Evidence Table - Locus 5:95550001-95800000 (nearest gene SPATA9)

Lead variant: rs466748 (PIP=0.334, 5:95,674,324, intronic to SPATA9). All credible-set variants are intronic/downstream/intergenic/non-coding (no missense/splice variants). FLAMES/MAGMA/PoPS/ABC gene-prioritization methods were all NA at this locus in the original pipeline run.

Targeted re-opening (2026-09-11): the study team discovered a genome-build error in the original MAGMA/PoPS gene-location merge file. A corrected re-merge surfaced a new candidate gene at this locus, RFESD, which was independently confirmed (via Ensembl/Open Targets coordinates) to genuinely physically overlap this locus and the credible-set span — the lead variant rs466748 itself sits inside RFESD's gene body (RFESD and SPATA9 overlap/abut at this exact position). RFESD was researched, verified, and critiqued with the same rigor as the original candidates; see its dedicated section below. The original SPATA9/RHOBTB3 evidence base is unchanged and was not re-verified in this re-opening.

Only citations with verifier verdict VERIFIED, and verified as gene-specific (not CLAIM_MISMATCH), are included below. See audits/5_95550001-95800000_audit.md for excluded citations and contested/weakened gene verdicts with reasoning preserved verbatim.

Open Targets summary

Structured database lookup only — NOT verified literature citations, did not go through citation-verifier or red-team-skeptic. Retrieved directly via Open Targets Platform GraphQL API, 2026-09-11 (SPATA9/RHOBTB3 portion), addended 2026-09-11 (RFESD portion, targeted re-opening).

SPATA9 (ENSG00000145757, nearest gene / primary candidate):

  • Genetic constraint: LOEUF (oeUpper, pLoF) = 1.402, constraint score ≈ 0.0000026 — tolerant of loss-of-function, i.e. no evidence of haploinsufficiency-type constraint.
  • Open Targets aggregate COPD association score: 0.225 (genetic_association datatype: 0.367; literature datatype: 0.061). No direct FEV1 disease-entity association recorded.
  • Locus-to-gene (L2G): Open Targets' own fine-mapping of this exact locus (COPD GWAS study GCST007692, n=257,811; credible set lead variant rs153916, matching a variant in our own credible set) gives L2G = 0.604 for SPATA9 (top-ranked at this credible set).
  • Colocalization (COLOC_PIP_ECAVIAR) at this same credible set: H4 ≈ 0.97 with FEV1/FVC ratio across six independent GWAS, including "FEV1/FVC ratio in never smokers" (GCST90308603, H4=0.970, 30 colocalizing variants) — locus-level (gene-agnostic) evidence that this region acts on pulmonary function detectably in never-smokers.
  • SPATA9's broader Open Targets disease-association profile is dominated by spermatogenesis/testis phenotypes (Sertoli cell-only syndrome, male infertility, globozoospermia, abnormal sperm morphology), consistent with its canonical annotated function.

RHOBTB3 (ENSG00000164292, secondary/context gene — not in locus file's candidate list):

  • Genetic constraint: LOEUF (oeUpper, pLoF) = 0.771, constraint score ≈ 0.0026 — more constrained against LoF than SPATA9, though not in the conventionally "highly constrained" range (LOEUF < 0.35).
  • Open Targets aggregate COPD association score: 0.0845 (genetic_association datatype: 0.139) — substantially lower than SPATA9's.
  • L2G at the same credible set (rs153916) as above: 0.229, second to SPATA9's 0.604.
  • Broader disease-association profile dominated by literature-datatype hits for breast cancer/myeloid leukemia and a long tail of animal-model-datatype skeletal-dysplasia phenotypes (likely nonspecific mouse-knockout phenotype matches).

RFESD (ENSG00000175449, new candidate added 2026-09-11 targeted re-opening; surfaced by corrected MAGMA/PoPS genome-build re-merge, not by the original pipeline):

  • Physical overlap (orchestrator-confirmed via target.genomicLocation, GRCh38): RFESD spans chr5:95,646,718-95,684,773 — entirely inside the 5:95,550,001-95,800,000 locus window, and the locus's single highest-PIP credible-set variant, rs466748 (PIP=0.334, 5:95,674,324), falls physically INSIDE RFESD's gene body (this position is intronic to both SPATA9 and RFESD, which overlap/abut here). This is a genuine physical-overlap finding, not a build-error artifact — confirmed independently and separately by the researcher via Ensembl REST lookup.
  • Genetic constraint: LOEUF (oeUpper, pLoF) = 0.915 — moderately constrained (more than SPATA9's 1.402, less than RHOBTB3's 0.771; rank order RHOBTB3 < RFESD < SPATA9 for LoF constraint at this locus).
  • Open Targets aggregate COPD association score: no COPD association record exists for RFESD at all (vs. SPATA9's 0.225 and RHOBTB3's 0.0845). No FEV1/lung-function association record either. Of 12 total diseases associated with RFESD in Open Targets, none are lung/COPD-related (top hits: vitiligo, alcohol drinking, seasonal allergic rhinitis, placental abruption, osteosarcoma, infectious disease, hepatocellular carcinoma, myelodysplastic syndrome, AML, ovarian serous cystadenocarcinoma, gastric cancer, lymphoma).
  • Locus-to-gene (L2G): queried directly at the same credible-set variant (rs153916) across all 9 credible sets Open Targets has there (including the exact COPD GWAS GCST007692 and all 5 FEV1/FVC-ratio GWAS at this locus), plus 6 more credible sets at the lead variant rs466748 itself (15 queries total) — RFESD never appears as a scored L2G candidate at any of them. Only SPATA9 and RHOBTB3 ever appear in the L2G output at this locus. This is a structural absence (never scored, not merely low-ranked), corroborated by two further independent negative findings from the researcher: an EBI GWAS Catalog search returning "no results" for RFESD across any trait, and zero PubMed GWAS/eQTL hits for RFESD. Caveat (per red-team-skeptic): L2G's feature set draws on functional annotation and eQTL/pQTL data that RFESD — whose UniProt function field is empty — may simply lack, making this partly a "sparse-annotation blind spot" rather than a fully considered rejection; it should not be read as definitive proof against RFESD, only as one of several converging negative signals.
  • No colocalization evidence found for RFESD at this locus in any query performed.

Prioritized gene

SPATA9 — Confidence: LOW. (Reaffirmed 2026-09-11 after targeted evaluation of RFESD; call unchanged from the original review.)

SPATA9 is named as the sole candidate gene at this locus by the internal pipeline (nearest-gene annotation; FLAMES/MAGMA/PoPS/ABC all NA even after the genome-build correction, meaning no orthogonal gene-prioritization method independently corroborates it), and it carries the highest Open Targets L2G score (0.604 vs. RHOBTB3's 0.229, and vs. RFESD's — never scored at all) at the one credible-set variant Open Targets independently fine-mapped for this exact COPD GWAS locus. That locus also shows very strong, consistent colocalization (H4 ≈ 0.97) with FEV1/FVC ratio across six GWAS, including a never-smokers-only cohort — evidence that this region acts on pulmonary function through a mechanism not purely mediated by smoking behavior (prior #1). SPATA9 itself was also explicitly named as a genome-wide-significant lung-function locus in the 2011 Soler Artigas discovery GWAS (PMID 21946350, VERIFIED).

However, per the red-team critique (both the original pass and the fresh RFESD-focused pass), this case is genuinely weak on closer inspection:

  • The Soler Artigas 2011 citation is the ONLY SPATA9-specific literature support that exists; there has been no gene-specific mechanistic, TWAS, eQTL, or MR follow-up on SPATA9 in 15 years, and zero published work on the lead variant rs466748 itself.
  • SPATA9's only well-characterized molecular function is spermatogenesis (Sertoli-cell biology, male infertility, globozoospermia) — there is no mechanistic literature connecting it to airway, alveolar, or any other lung-relevant biology.
  • gnomAD shows SPATA9 to be tolerant of loss-of-function variation (LOEUF ~1.40), which does not support a highly penetrant, dosage-sensitive causal role.
  • The Open Targets L2G margin (0.604 vs 0.229 vs. RFESD's absence) is modest relative to RHOBTB3 and not fully independent of the nearest-gene prior that nominated SPATA9 in the first place, since L2G partly incorporates distance-to-gene features.
  • The FEV1/FVC never-smoker colocalization is gene-agnostic — it does not distinguish SPATA9 from RHOBTB3 (or, now, RFESD) and should not be read as gene-specific confirmation for SPATA9.
  • Both red-team passes' own independent contradiction-searches failed entirely (exhausted search budget both times) — so the absence of contested/contradicting literature reported here reflects unperformed searches, not a clean literature record, for all three genes.

On RFESD specifically (the subject of this targeted re-opening): RFESD genuinely physically overlaps this locus and the lead credible-set variant (rs466748 sits inside its gene body), so it was a legitimate candidate to evaluate rather than a build-error false positive — but on every other axis it is the weakest of the three genes considered here. It has zero COPD or FEV1/lung-function association in Open Targets, is never scored at all by Open Targets' own L2G model across 15 independent queries at this locus (a structural absence, not a low rank — though partly confounded by RFESD's sparse functional annotation, per the red-team-skeptic's caveat), has no GWAS Catalog entry for any trait whatsoever, and its only functional literature (a hepatocellular-carcinoma knockdown study and a maternal peripheral-blood autism transcriptome study) is non-pulmonary and does not establish any lung-relevant biology. Its own protein function is not even characterized in UniProt. Per the red-team-skeptic's verdict, RFESD is "WEAKENED, bordering on excludable" — it does not create a three-way tie and does not unseat either SPATA9 or RHOBTB3 as the closer contenders.

Given this, SPATA9 remains the best-supported single candidate — it has the higher L2G score among genes L2G actually scores, a direct (if old and unreplicated) gene-named GWAS hit, and is the pipeline's actual designated candidate (both before and after the genome-build correction, which did not change SPATA9's own candidacy) — but the call remains close between SPATA9 and RHOBTB3, RFESD is a documented but substantially weaker third candidate whose relevance rests almost entirely on raw physical gene-body overlap with the lead variant rather than any association or functional evidence, and confidence should remain LOW rather than MODERATE or HIGH. What would resolve the SPATA9-vs-RHOBTB3 tie: (1) lung/airway single-cell or bulk-tissue expression data (e.g., GTEx, Human Lung Cell Atlas) for SPATA9 vs. RHOBTB3; (2) an eQTL/TWAS/colocalization analysis specifically distinguishing which gene's expression the credible-set variants affect in lung tissue; (3) any functional study of SPATA9 or RHOBTB3 in airway epithelium, alveolar cells, or a COPD/emphysema model; (4) a completed (not exhausted-budget) independent contradiction search. What would resolve RFESD's status specifically: (5) direct lung eQTL/sQTL catalog lookups (e.g., GTEx lung, lung-specific eQTL studies) for RFESD that Open Targets' L2G model may be missing due to its sparse annotation; (6) confirmation of whether RFESD and SPATA9 share a bidirectional promoter or overlapping regulatory element that would make them mechanistically inseparable at this locus. Until then, this prioritization should be treated as a tentative, statistically-motivated best guess, not a mechanistically grounded conclusion.
