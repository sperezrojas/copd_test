Prioritized-gene synthesis

Prioritized gene: EMSY, but with LOW confidence and a genuinely unresolved runner-up (LRRC32).

This locus does not cleanly resolve to a single causal gene on the evidence assembled here, and that ambiguity should be stated plainly rather than smoothed over. Both EMSY and LRRC32 are plausible, and the literature itself — nearly every discovery paper for this GWAS interval (Ferreira 2011, Chen 2019, Li 2015 review) — names the locus jointly as "C11orf30(EMSY)/LRRC32," which reflects a genuine, long-standing unresolved gene assignment in the field, not a gap specific to this review.

Case for EMSY: it is the nearest gene and the only one of the three candidates whose gene body falls inside the literal stated locus window (76,444,887-76,553,625, versus LRRC32 starting ~7.5 kb past the window's stated end and ACER3 starting ~210 kb past it). EMSY has functional, mechanistic support as a transcriptional repressor that directly affects an epithelial barrier program (Elias et al. 2019, VERIFIED) and a separately documented role repressing type I interferon responses in lung tissue, albeit in a cancer rather than COPD context (Marzio et al. 2021, VERIFIED). EMSY is also strongly LoF-constrained in gnomAD (constraint score 1.00), consistent with a gene under purifying selection whose dosage matters.

Case for LRRC32 (the runner-up, and arguably the stronger candidate on genomics grounds): Open Targets' own locus-to-gene (L2G) algorithm — which integrates regulatory/functional-genomic features, not just distance — nominates LRRC32 over EMSY as the top gene at this exact credible-set interval across roughly 15 independent GWAS studies of other traits sharing this interval. The original 2011 discovery paper for this locus (Ferreira et al., asthma) names LRRC32/GARP, not EMSY, as the gene nearest the lead signal. LRRC32 encodes GARP, a well-characterized presenter of latent TGF-beta on regulatory T cells and platelets — directly relevant to Biological Prior #3 (inflammatory/immune heterogeneity beyond neutrophils, including Treg/TGF-beta axes) — and a VERIFIED functional study (Meyer-Martin et al. 2016) shows GARP inhibition reduces allergic airway inflammation in a humanized mouse model, which is more directly airway/lung-relevant than any evidence found for EMSY specifically (EMSY's strongest mechanistic evidence, Elias 2019, is skin-only). One Open Targets gwas_credible_sets evidence row links LRRC32 to COPD specifically (L2G = 0.201), though the underlying study/variant could not be resolved and should be treated as suggestive only.

What neither gene has: no FEV1 or FEV1/FVC association evidence was found for EMSY, LRRC32, or ACER3 in Open Targets, and no direct, COPD-specific (as opposed to asthma/atopic-disease or asthma-COPD-overlap) functional or genetic study was found in the literature for either EMSY or LRRC32. Essentially the entire GWAS evidence base at this interval is for atopic/Th2 allergic diseases (asthma, atopic dermatitis, food allergy, allergic rhinitis) and one autoimmune disease (alopecia areata via LRRC32), not COPD. Per Biological Prior #2, the absence of spirometric (FEV1/FEV1-FVC) support for either gene is a genuine weakness, and the most parsimonious reading of the evidence is that this locus's association with the COPD phenotype (in a GWAS not adjusted for asthma/atopy history) may reflect shared genetic liability to allergic sensitization or asthma-COPD overlap rather than intrinsic airflow-obstruction biology — an alternative hypothesis that should be weighed seriously, not dismissed.

What would resolve the tie: gene-specific colocalization or fine-mapping evidence using a COPD or lung-function (FEV1/FEV1-FVC) GWAS credible set at this exact interval (not available in Open Targets for either gene at the time of this review); an airway- or lung-epithelium extension of the Elias et al. 2019 EMSY skin-barrier experiments; or a lung/airway-specific functional study of LRRC32/GARP analogous to Meyer-Martin et al. 2016 but using a COPD-relevant (smoke-exposure, emphysema, or neutrophilic-inflammation) model rather than allergic airway inflammation.

EMSY is named as the top pick here only because of its stronger positional claim (literally inside the stated window) and its two independent, disease-relevant mechanistic threads (epithelial barrier regulation; interferon repression, albeit cross-disease-context) — but this is a close call, LRRC32 is a legitimate runner-up that Open Targets' own regulatory-evidence-based L2G scoring would rank above EMSY at this interval, and confidence is marked LOW accordingly. ACER3 is not treated as a serious candidate: it sits far outside both the stated window and the credible-set interval, has zero fine-mapping/L2G/colocalization support, and its sole basis (MAGMA/PoPS statistical prioritization) is exactly the kind of signal susceptible to LD/gene-density contamination from its physically closer neighbors (EMSY, LRRC32) that this review process is designed to catch — see audit file for the red-team-skeptic's CONTESTED verdict on ACER3.
