Prioritized gene

Prioritized gene: SPATS2L Confidence: MODERATE

SPATS2L is the clear pick for which gene is implicated at this locus: it is the nearest gene, the FLAMES- and ABC-prioritized gene, the only protein-coding gene physically within the stated locus boundaries, the only gene returned by Open Targets' L2G model at the credible-set variants (L2G=0.783 for a lung-function/FEV1-FVC GWAS colocalization), and the eGene at multiple independent GTEx eQTL credible sets at the same variants. SATB2 (MAGMA/PoPS pick) sits ~679 kb outside the locus, was not returned by L2G at any queried variant, has no COPD genetic-association signal in Open Targets, and has no COPD-relevant structural or immune literature — every SATB2 lung-related citation in the evidence table above is either a diagnostic marker for excluding lung origin of a tumor or a cancer-biology mechanism, none an airway-remodeling or inflammatory mechanism. The red-team-skeptic's CONTESTED verdict on SATB2 is adopted here in full; SATB2 is not a serious competing candidate at this locus.

The confidence is capped at MODERATE, not HIGH, because of the unresolved question of what disease process SPATS2L's association reflects. The gene's dominant, best-replicated literature identity is an asthma/inhaled-beta-agonist bronchodilator-response and pediatric-asthma-exacerbation-severity gene (Himes 2012 and multiple replications, all via the correlated variant rs295137), not a COPD-specific or classical airway-remodeling/inflammatory gene. The red-team-skeptic raised a specific, unresolved alternative that this evidence table has not been able to rule out: because COPD case definitions typically require post-bronchodilator spirometry, a variant that genuinely alters bronchodilator pharmacodynamics (as SPATS2L appears to, per the ADRB2-knockdown mechanism in Himes 2012) could shift COPD case/control status at the diagnostic threshold as a measurement/ascertainment effect, independent of any true difference in underlying COPD disease biology — this is distinct from, and arguably more parsimonious than, the more commonly considered "asthma-COPD-overlap (ACO) misclassification" explanation, and neither this review nor the one COPDGene ACO-specific study found (Hayden et al. 2018, which did not test SPATS2L directly) resolves it. In addition: (a) no LD data confirms that the pharmacogenetic variant rs295137 is actually correlated with this locus's lead credible-set variant rs985256, so applying the asthma-pharmacogenetic literature to characterize this specific COPD signal involves an unverified assumption of shared mechanism by gene-proximity; (b) the immune evidence offered (ARDS interferon signature, psoriasis T-cell activation) is real but drawn from diseases mechanistically distant from COPD and should not be read as COPD-specific immune support; (c) smoking-mediation (this pipeline's prior #1) is genuinely untested here — no smoking-initiation/cessation/heaviness GWAS literature or GWAS Catalog/GSCAN data could be retrieved in this session, so this is an open gap, not a ruled-out mechanism.

What would resolve this: (1) an LD check between rs985256 (this locus's lead/credible-set variant) and rs295137 (the asthma-pharmacogenetic variant) to establish whether they tag the same signal; (2) a direct query of smoking-initiation/cessation/heaviness GWAS summary statistics (e.g., GSCAN) at this locus; (3) COPD-tissue-specific (sputum, BAL, explanted lung) expression or functional data for SPATS2L, which is currently absent from the literature reviewed; (4) a study directly partitioning the COPD case-control signal at this locus by asthma-history/ACO status. Until then, SPATS2L should be reported to the study team as the best-supported gene at this locus by a wide margin over SATB2, but with an explicitly flagged, unresolved question of whether its COPD association reflects direct pulmonary/inflammatory disease biology, asthma-COPD-overlap misclassification, or a bronchodilator-response measurement artifact intrinsic to how COPD is diagnosed.
