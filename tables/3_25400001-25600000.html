Prioritized gene

RARB — Confidence: MODERATE

RARB is the only candidate gene at this locus, and the case for it is unusually convergent at the statistical level: it is simultaneously the nearest gene, the FLAMES-prioritized gene, and the PoPS-prioritized gene; the lead credible-set variant (PIP=0.893) sits within its intron; and Open Targets' L2G model assigns essentially all its prediction mass to RARB (no competing gene) across 16 independent credible sets spanning three COPD GWAS and multiple lung-function GWAS. Verified literature independently corroborates that RARB (by gene name, from the abstract) has been identified as a genome-wide-significant locus for spirometric lung function (FEV1/FEV1-FVC) as far back as 2011 (Soler Artigas et al., PMID 21946350) and nominally replicated for airflow obstruction in 2012 (Wilk et al., PMID 22837378) — independent of and predating the modern fine-mapped COPD signal, which satisfies prior #2 (COPD–spirometry concordance) unusually robustly for a single-candidate locus. This is reinforced by database-level evidence (not literature) that the COPD credible set colocalizes strongly (h4>0.99) with a never-smoker-only lung-function GWAS and a smoking-interaction-modeled study, arguing against a purely smoking-behavior-mediated mechanism (prior #1) for the pulmonary-function association specifically, even though no literature directly resolves germline smoking-initiation-GWAS overlap at this locus either way.

Confidence is capped at MODERATE, not HIGH, for reasons the red-team-skeptic identified as WEAKENED at the gene level: (1) two of the four statistical-genetics citations relied on (Sakornsakolpat 2019, PMID 30804561; Shrine 2019, PMID 30804560) received CLAIM_MISMATCH verdicts — their abstracts confirm the studies are real and on-topic but do not confirm RARB is specifically named among their loci, so they are excluded from the evidence table above and should not be read as literature confirmation of gene-level attribution (that inference currently rests on the Open Targets L2G/coloc database evidence, not the literature); (2) the single most gene-specific mechanistic anchor (Massaro et al. 2000, RARB-knockout mice, PMID 11074013) establishes RARB as a developmental, perinatal-period brake on alveolar septation, but the direction of effect this implies for a common regulatory variant's influence on adult COPD risk is not established, and the human translational literature actively complicates a naive extrapolation: the FORTE randomized trial of ATRA in human emphysema was negative on its primary endpoints (Roth et al. 2006, PMID 17099008), and adult human/mouse lung organoid data show RA signaling has an inverted (growth-restraining) effect in the adult lung versus the developing lung (Ng-Blichfeldt et al. 2018, PMID 30236449); (3) no eQTL/pQTL/TWAS/MR literature or Open Targets molecular-QTL colocalization was found anywhere linking the GWAS signal to RARB expression specifically — the entire causal chain from variant to RARB to lung phenotype currently rests on positional/fine-mapping/PoPS/L2G convergence, not direct molecular evidence; and (4) one of the five methylation/smoking-context citations (Scesnaite et al. 2012, PMID 22217548) received a CLAIM_MISMATCH verdict (its reported findings concern p16, not RARB) and is excluded from the evidence table above.

No competing gene was identified at this locus by any source consulted (researcher, Open Targets, or red-team-skeptic, though the latter's dedicated alternative-gene search was not completed this session due to exhausted search budget — see audit for this disclosed gap), so there is no "runner-up" gene to name. What would most resolve the remaining uncertainty and support upgrading to HIGH confidence: (a) direct eQTL/sQTL colocalization evidence (currently entirely absent) establishing which direction and in which tissue/cell type the credible-set variant shifts RARB expression or splicing; (b) a full-text/supplementary-table check of the two CLAIM_MISMATCH-flagged large GWAS papers to confirm RARB's presence and effect direction among their reported loci; and (c) a direct check of whether this locus/variant appears in the GSCAN or equivalent smoking-behavior GWAS loci list, which remains genuinely unresolved by literature search to date.
