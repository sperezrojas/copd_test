Prioritized gene

KCNH3 — confidence: LOW. (Unchanged by the TUBA1A addendum.)

KCNH3 is the strongest statistical-genetics candidate at this locus by a wide margin: it is the nearest gene, and >85% of the credible-set posterior inclusion probability mass (lead variant rs933738, PIP=0.358, plus rs7132792, PIP=0.020) sits in its introns — an order of magnitude more concentrated than any other gene at this locus (DNAJC22's single best variant reaches only PIP=0.055; SPATS2's dozens of variants each stay below PIP=0.028; TUBA1A carries zero credible-set PIP mass at all). On fine-mapping alone, this is a clear statistical winner, and red-team critique explicitly affirms that PIP concentration of this kind is exactly the sort of signal fine-mapping is designed to surface.

However, the literature evidence is genuinely two-sided on mechanism, not confirmatory of a pulmonary role. All nine verified KCNH3 citations describe an essentially exclusively neuronal/CNS biology (hippocampal excitability and epilepsy, suprachiasmatic-nucleus circadian firing, human neurodevelopmental disease explicitly described as "primarily expressed in the nervous system," and psychoactive/behavioral effects of pharmacological channel inhibition) — no lung, airway, or COPD-specific literature exists for KCNH3 anywhere in the search performed. Open Targets' own aggregate association scores put KCNH3 highest of all candidate genes for both FEV1 (0.30) and smoking initiation (0.55), with smoking modestly higher — an ambiguous signal, not a resolving one. Red-team critique specifically flagged that "absence of pulmonary literature is not evidence for a smoking mechanism" and that the smoking-mediation narrative and an unstudied peripheral/airway role for this ion channel are two live, currently indistinguishable hypotheses.

TARGETED ADDENDUM FINDING: TUBA1A does not change this call. TUBA1A (an alpha-tubulin gene surfaced by a corrected MAGMA/PoPS genome-build re-merge) was researched, verified, and critiqued with the same rigor as the original candidates. Red-team-skeptic's explicit verdict: TUBA1A carries zero credible-set PIP mass and zero Open Targets association data across all five queried traits — a null profile even more complete than KMT2D's. Its dominant biology (dominant-negative, highly constrained, rare monogenic lissencephaly/tubulinopathy) shares KMT2D's "Mendelian gene ≠ GWAS gene" architecture mismatch, but sharper: TUBA1A lacks even KMT2D's positional/credible-set-adjacent anchor or its repeated engagement by an independent human disease literature. TUBA1A has no smoking-behavior literature at all (unlike KCNH3), so it cannot serve as a tie-breaker on the one axis that actually drives KCNH3's LOW confidence rating. Its most COPD-adjacent evidence — four papers describing Th17-mediated autoimmunity against a lung self-antigen called "K-alpha1-tubulin" in post-transplant bronchiolitis obliterans, including a direct association with FEV1 decline — was investigated at length by two independent citation-verifier passes, both of which concluded that the mapping of this antigen name to the TUBA1A gene specifically (as opposed to a different alpha-tubulin paralog) could not be confirmed from the literature searched, and is marked CONTESTED in the evidence table above pending that resolution. Net: TUBA1A is added as a third, weaker positional runner-up (ranked below both KMT2D and DNAJC22 in evidentiary strength per red-team critique) and does not shift the locus-level prioritization or confidence level.

Runner-up: KMT2D (MAGMA-prioritized). KMT2D has genuinely interesting developmental lung-biology support — a mouse knock-in model (catalytically-dead Kmt2d) with impaired alveolar/airway sacculation, plus human case reports of pulmonary hypertension and interstitial lung disease in Kabuki syndrome (KMT2D loss-of-function) patients — and is the single most LoF-constrained gene among the original 12 candidates queried in Open Targets (though TUBA1A, added in this addendum, is now more constrained still). But red-team critique identifies this as the packet's most important overreach risk: KMT2D lies entirely OUTSIDE the ABF credible set (its candidacy rests solely on MAGMA's gene-based test); its strongest mechanistic evidence is an unreviewed 2026 bioRxiv preprint in a near-null homozygous mouse perturbation; and its human evidence is case reports in a rare, highly penetrant monogenic syndrome — a genetic architecture qualitatively different from a common noncoding GWAS variant of modest effect (the "Mendelian gene ≠ GWAS gene" fallacy).

Second runner-up: DNAJC22. Carries the second-highest-PIP credible-set variant locus-wide (rs11168988, PIP=0.055) but is explicitly described in its own primary literature as "so far unstudied." Red-team critique explicitly cautions against treating this literature vacuum as evidence against DNAJC22 — it is a "known unknown," and on a like-for-like basis this gene arguably deserves comparable or greater weight than SPATS2 or MCRS1 (or now TUBA1A), which have more literature but literature that is either irrelevant, non-replicated, or of unconfirmed gene-specificity.

Third runner-up (new, this addendum): TUBA1A. A MAGMA/PoPS-positional-only candidate surfaced by the study team's genome-build correction. Weakest statistical-genetics case at this locus (zero credible-set PIP, zero Open Targets association data across all traits queried); dominant biology is a rare, highly constrained, dominant-negative Mendelian neurodevelopmental disease (lissencephaly/tubulinopathy) poorly suited to explain a common-variant signal; its single most COPD-adjacent evidentiary thread (Th17-mediated anti-"K-alpha1-tubulin" autoimmunity in lung-transplant rejection, including an FEV1-decline association) carries an unresolved and potentially disqualifying gene-identity risk (see CONTESTED rows above). Per red-team-skeptic's explicit ranking, this is the weakest candidate gene reviewed at this locus, weaker than KMT2D.

What would resolve this call: (1) direct confirmation of whether rs933738 (or an LD proxy) is genome-wide significant in the GSCAN or a later smoking-initiation GWAS, independent of Open Targets' aggregate score; (2) any airway-tissue, lung single-cell/scRNA-seq, or pulmonary-eQTL expression data for KCNH3 and DNAJC22 specifically, which no source consulted here provided; (3) confirmation of whether the COPD GWAS association at this locus attenuates after adjustment for smoking behavior (pack-years/smoking status), which would directly test the smoking-mediation hypothesis; (4) a functional or genetic study connecting KMT2D common variation (not rare LoF) to lung phenotypes, and ideally a locus-specific colocalization analysis between this COPD signal and a KMT2D-region eQTL, given KMT2D's exclusion from the credible set; (5) new, from this addendum: definitive resolution — via NCBI Gene/HGNC alias records, UniProt cross-references, or direct confirmation from the original research group — of whether the "K-alpha1-tubulin" self-antigen used throughout the lung-transplant Th17-autoimmunity literature is in fact encoded by TUBA1A, or by a different alpha-tubulin paralog (e.g. TUBA1B, hypothesized but not confirmed by red-team-skeptic); this single fact materially determines whether TUBA1A's immune-domain evidence carries any weight at all; (6) a systematic check of whether the genome-build correction that surfaced TUBA1A also shifted positional scores for other candidate genes at this locus, to confirm TUBA1A was not reviewed in isolation while other build-affected genes went unchecked.

Given the unresolved mechanistic ambiguity (pulmonary vs. smoking-behavior) for the statistically-favored gene, the credible-set/MAGMA discordance for the mechanistically most compelling gene, and the addition of a third, even weaker positional candidate whose strongest evidence carries an unresolved gene-identity risk, this call remains marked LOW confidence — it should not be read as a settled causal-gene assignment.
