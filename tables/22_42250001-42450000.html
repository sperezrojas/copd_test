Prioritized gene

TCF20 — Confidence: LOW.

TCF20 is the only gene at this locus with meaningful statistical fine-mapping support: it is the nearest gene to the lead variant and carries roughly 86%+ of the credible-set PIP mass across seven annotated variants, all intronic or upstream (no coding consequence), and it is extremely LoF-constrained (gnomAD oe=0.019). By physical/ statistical overlap alone it is the clear best-supported candidate over SHISA8 (zero credible-set variant overlap; PoPS-only, genome-wide-similarity-based), NFAM1 (a single synonymous variant at PIP=0.003, essentially noise-level), and LINC01315 (~1.3% cumulative PIP, entirely oncology-focused literature with no lung relevance).

However, confidence cannot be rated higher than LOW because the mechanistic case is thin and partly contradicted on closer inspection. No PubMed literature ties TCF20 directly to COPD, lung function, or the lead variant rs8142378 (zero hits across all three targeted searches) — there is no spirometric or pulmonary-function corroboration at all, which per prior #2 should raise consideration of an alternative pathway. TCF20's best-established disease biology (Vetrini et al. 2019, PMID 30819258, VERIFIED) is a monogenic neurodevelopmental syndrome driven by severe loss-of-function haploinsufficiency — a different phenotype and mechanism than a common noncoding COPD-associated variant, so this should be read as evidence of general dosage sensitivity/biological importance, not direct COPD mechanism (per red-team-skeptic). The one lead toward a plausible alternative mechanism consistent with prior #1 — smoking-behavior mediation via TCF20's neurobehavioral biology, suggested by an Open Targets aggregate "smoking initiation" trait score (0.267) — could NOT be substantiated in the primary literature: the GSCAN smoking-GWAS citation intended to support it returned a CLAIM_MISMATCH verdict from citation-verifier (no mention of chromosome 22 or TCF20 found in the accessible text), so this hypothesis remains open and unconfirmed rather than a finding.

The runner-up, NFAM1, offers real immune biology (ITAM-signaling adaptor with a key-regulatory-gene role in asthma-exacerbation transcriptomics, PMID 39917297, VERIFIED) that is more consistent with prior #3's inflammatory-complexity framing than anything found for TCF20 — but its case is undermined by near-zero statistical support at this locus (one synonymous variant, PIP=0.003) and the complete absence of COPD-specific (as opposed to asthma- or non-pulmonary-immune) evidence. SHISA8 and LINC01315 are both effectively ruled out: SHISA8 has zero physical linkage to the credible set and exclusively neuronal biology; LINC01315 has minimal PIP and an entirely oncology-focused literature footprint (including one retracted paper, excluded).

What would resolve this call: (1) direct confirmation (e.g., via GSCAN/GWAS Catalog supplementary tables or a newer/larger smoking-initiation GWAS) of whether a genome-wide-significant smoking-initiation or related behavioral-trait signal actually localizes to chr22:42.25-42.45Mb — this is the single check that would most change this locus's prioritization, per red-team-skeptic; (2) any FEV1/FEV1-FVC spirometry GWAS or lung-function TWAS/colocalization evidence for TCF20, currently entirely absent; (3) any COPD-specific (not asthma-only) transcriptomic or functional evidence for NFAM1 in airway epithelium or COPD-relevant immune cell types. Until then, TCF20 is the best-supported pick by statistical fine-mapping and constraint, but the causal mechanism at this locus — pulmonary-structural, smoking-behavior-mediated, or otherwise — remains unresolved, and this recommendation should be weighed accordingly by the study team.
