Prioritized gene

SIX3 — confidence: LOW.

SIX3 is the best-supported gene at this locus by every quantitative/statistical criterion available: it is the nearest gene to both high-PIP credible-set variants, it carries the Open Targets L2G score for COPD (0.7145, highest-confidence tier), and — critically — it is the only gene ever returned as the L2G prediction across every other-trait GWAS credible set overlapping this same lead variant pair (see Open Targets summary above). By contrast, CAMKMT — unanimously prioritized by the internal FLAMES/MAGMA/PoPS pipeline — has zero L2G evidence for COPD, zero disease-relevant published literature, a human deletion-syndrome model with no pulmonary phenotype (Bartholdi et al. 2013, table above), and is now confirmed (independently, via two data sources) to lie physically outside the defined locus window entirely. Per the red-team-skeptic's critique, this pattern is best explained as a proximity/gene-size artifact of the MAGMA/PoPS SNP-to-gene mapping methodology (CAMKMT spans ~415 kb) rather than genuine convergent support, and CAMKMT is marked CONTESTED in the audit appendix.

However, confidence in SIX3 is capped at LOW, not HIGH or MODERATE, for two reasons that the skeptic raised and that are not resolved by the evidence in hand:

  1. Mechanism is unresolved. No published literature or Open Targets association evidence links SIX3 to FEV1, FEV1/FVC, spirometry, or any lung-intrinsic structural/immune biology — SIX3's only established biology is forebrain/eye/hypothalamus/pituitary developmental patterning (holoprosencephaly), which has no plausible route to adult airflow obstruction. The alternative hypothesis — that this locus's COPD association is smoking-behavior- mediated (per biological prior #1) — is plausible and consistent with extensive Open Targets colocalization with smoking-status GWAS at the same variants, but is supported in the peer-reviewed literature by exactly one MTAG paper (Cheng et al. 2023) that names SIX3 alongside NCAM1 and a DRD2-proximal locus. This is real, verified evidence, but it is thin, and the Open Targets colocalization pattern itself is a database output, not an independently verified/verifiable literature claim.
  2. The causal gene vs. regulatory-element question is unresolved. Both high-PIP credible-set variants (rs501964, rs11692742) are intergenic, ABC prioritization is NA for this locus, and no eQTL/Hi-C evidence was found linking either variant to SIX3 expression specifically (as opposed to, e.g., a distal enhancer whose target gene assignment by nearest-gene/L2G proximity could still be wrong, or LINC01833, a non-coding transcript physically between the credible-set variants and SIX3, acting as a host/regulatory element). This alternative was explicitly flagged as underweighted by the red-team-skeptic and was not resolved by this review.

Runner-up: none genuinely competitive — CAMKMT is CONTESTED (see audit appendix) and SIX2 has no supporting evidence of any kind (renal-only developmental biology, not flagged by any prioritization method). SIX3 is the clear best pick among the four genes considered, but the call on mechanism (lung-intrinsic vs. smoking-behavior-mediated vs. unresolved distal regulatory element) remains open. What would resolve this: an eQTL/Hi-C/ABC dataset directly linking rs501964/rs11692742 to SIX3 (or another gene) expression in lung or neural/brain-relevant tissue, and/or a dedicated COPD/spirometry GWAS analysis stratified or adjusted for smoking status at this specific locus.
