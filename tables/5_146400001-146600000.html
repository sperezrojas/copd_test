Never scored by Open Targets' L2G model at this locus at all — zero colocalization support for any of the 7 credible sets anchored near the lead variant, despite being the MAGMA- and PoPS-prioritized gene.   • GPR151: No direct Open Targets modeled association with COPD, FEV1, or FEV1/FVC. Prioritized gene

TCERG1 — Confidence: LOW

TCERG1 is the best-supported single candidate at this locus, but the case is weak in absolute terms and the call is close. Reasoning:

  • Fine-mapping/physical evidence favors TCERG1. The bulk of the 55-variant credible set, including the lead variant rs73793188 (PIP=0.114), falls within TCERG1 introns. TCERG1 is also the dominant Open Targets L2G-nominated gene (L2G 0.34-0.75) for the credible set's actual colocalizing traits (white blood cell count, neutrophil count, height), which show strong mutual colocalization (h4>0.92) with the lead-variant signal — i.e., whatever this locus is doing, TCERG1 is the gene statistical fine-mapping assigns it to, more so than its neighbors GPR151 or PPP2R2B.
  • But there is no COPD-specific or spirometry-specific evidence for TCERG1 at all, from Open Targets (count=0 for COPD/FEV1/FEV1-FVC) or from the verified literature. The one lung-phenotype paper found (BPD extracellular-vesicle biomarker, PMID 41552916) is a biomarker association in a developmentally and etiologically distinct neonatal disease, not adult COPD, and red-team-skeptic explicitly flags this as an overclaiming risk if leaned on too hard. TCERG1's best-replicated independent genetic disease role (Huntington's disease age-of-onset modification via a coding hexamer repeat, PMID 36064847) is neurodegenerative, unrelated to lung biology. Despite strong statistical colocalization with neutrophil/WBC-count traits (directly relevant to prior #3, inflammatory heterogeneity), no mechanistic literature was found connecting TCERG1 to neutrophil, macrophage, or T-cell inflammatory programs relevant to COPD — this is a genuine, explicitly-flagged evidentiary gap, not an oversight.
  • Runner-up: PPP2R2B, the MAGMA/PoPS-prioritized gene. Per red-team-skeptic, this represents a genuine unresolved method-vs-method conflict, not a case that should be quietly resolved in TCERG1's favor: MAGMA and PoPS (genome-wide burden/proximity-style methods) prioritize PPP2R2B, while fine-mapping/colocalization gives it zero support at all (its gene body sits >200kb from the credible set). PPP2R2B does have one mechanistically detailed, genuine T-cell immune function (PP2A-Bβ regulating IL-2-deprivation-induced T-cell apoptosis, PMID 21746932) but only in the unrelated context of systemic lupus erythematosus, never tested in COPD or airway immunity — marked CONTESTED as COPD evidence by red-team-skeptic.
  • Alternative hypothesis: GPR151, tagged by only 2 variants of very low PIP (<0.001) at the edge of the credible set, has by far the strongest literature-supported mechanistic story of the three genes (habenula-restricted nicotine/smoking-reward biology, PMID 30429765, 40295925, 34210852) — but this case is architecturally weak at this specific locus given its minimal credible-set representation, and would support prior #1 (smoking-mediated effect) rather than direct pulmonary or COPD-specific immune biology. Notably, the one immune-relevant GPR151 finding directly bearing on a peripheral-immune mechanism (Rios et al. 2026 preprint, PMID 42620318) found no change in peripheral immune activation, cutting against rather than for an airway-immune role.
  • What would resolve this: (1) a COPD or spirometry (FEV1/FEV1-FVC) GWAS/colocalization signal directly at this locus (currently absent from Open Targets for all three genes); (2) a functional study connecting TCERG1 to neutrophil, macrophage, or airway epithelial biology to explain its blood-cell-count colocalization; (3) direct confirmation (not found by citation-verifier) of whether this locus is an established hit in primary smoking-behavior GWAS (e.g., the GSCAN consortium tables), which would tip the balance toward prior #1 for either TCERG1 or GPR151; (4) fine-mapping/eQTL evidence specific to PPP2R2B to explain why MAGMA/PoPS prioritize it despite its physical distance from the credible set.

Given the absence of direct COPD/lung evidence for any candidate, the unresolved method-vs-method conflict between fine-mapping and burden-based prioritization, and the lack of mechanistic literature explaining TCERG1's own colocalization signal, this recommendation is offered at LOW confidence for the study team's own weighing, not as a settled call.
