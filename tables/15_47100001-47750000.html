Prioritized-gene synthesis

Prioritized gene: SEMA6D Confidence: LOW

SEMA6D is named by every internal prioritization method (nearest gene, FLAMES, MAGMA, PoPS) and is the only protein-coding gene in this ~650kb interval, so there is no competing candidate to weigh it against — this is a "gene desert" locus in the sense that gene identity is not in question, but the mechanism and even the strength of the causal case are. That distinction matters for the confidence call.

The verified literature converges strongly and repeatedly on SEMA6D as a smoking-behavior/neurobehavioral pleiotropy gene: independent, cross-ancestry replication in smoking initiation/cessation GWAS (Piga et al. 2022), a direct MR link from smoking to SEMA6D-implicated striatal neurobiology (Trofimova et al. 2026), and further convergent signal across externalizing/substance-use traits (Konzok et al. 2024), restless legs syndrome (Schormair et al. 2017, the single highest-scoring trait for SEMA6D in Open Targets), schizophrenia/cannabis-smoking shared architecture (Song et al. 2022), and cognitive decline (Contreras et al. 2026). This pattern is corroborated independently by Open Targets, where SEMA6D's COPD association score (0.268) ranks well below nearly every smoking- and behavior-related trait score (0.34-0.44), and where SEMA6D shows zero detectable association with FEV1 or FEV1/FVC at all — a direct application of biological prior #2 (absence of spirometric concordance for a COPD-only signal warrants considering alternative pathways).

However, per the red-team critique, this smoking-mediation story should not be overstated as proven: the smoking-trait GWAS and the (smoking-unadjusted) COPD GWAS draw on overlapping biobank cohorts, so the co-occurrence of signals is not fully independent evidence, and an equally parsimonious alternative is that SEMA6D is a broadly pleiotropic neurodevelopmental/behavioral gene (it is strongly LoF-constrained, LOEUF ~0.29, and shows genome-wide signal across ADHD, RLS, insomnia, MDD, schizophrenia, and cognitive decline) that MAGMA/PoPS gene-set methods enrich for regardless of any specific causal role in lung or smoking biology. Neither the researcher nor the red-team was able to confirm (due to search-budget limits) whether SEMA6D/15q21 is even among the 82 loci reported in the primary COPD GWAS (Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019) — a foundational open gap for this locus.

The direct lung/immune literature is real but does not resolve the mechanism question: Naito et al. 2022 establishes that lung-mesenchymal-derived SEMA6D regulates ILC2 biology and susceptibility to allergic lung inflammation via Plexin A1, but this is Th2/allergic-endotype biology, distinct from COPD's typically neutrophilic/CD8+ Tc1-Tc17/macrophage-driven pathology (per prior #3, and as the red-team explicitly flagged) — it should be read as general biological plausibility for lung-expressed SEMA6D function, not COPD-mechanism evidence. The broader immune literature (T-cell regulation, cancer-context CD8+ T-cell effects) is genuine but directly contradictory in direction of effect across tissue contexts (immunosuppressive in HNSCC vs. immune-activating in colorectal cancer via the same Plexin-A4 axis) and entirely non-COPD, so it cannot be used to build a coherent COPD-specific immune hypothesis in either direction. A cited Behcet's-disease GWAS association (Jung et al. 2023) uses a different lead variant (rs4143322) than this locus's lead (rs59321834), and LD between the two was not established — this should not be treated as corroborating evidence for this locus.

Bottom line: SEMA6D is the only plausible gene at this locus, so there is little value in naming a "runner-up" — but the causal mechanism (smoking-behavior-mediated versus direct pulmonary/immune biology versus generic neurobehavioral pleiotropy unrelated to COPD at all) remains genuinely unresolved, and the case for a direct pulmonary mechanism is specifically weak (no FEV1/FEV1FVC signal, no emphysema/airway-remodeling model data, contradictory immune-direction evidence, wrong immune endotype in the one strong lung mechanistic paper). What would resolve this: (1) confirmation of whether this locus/SEMA6D is among the discovery loci in Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019 or a more recent COPD GWAS meta-analysis; (2) a smoking-adjusted or MR-based sensitivity analysis of the COPD association at this specific locus to test whether it attenuates after accounting for smoking behavior; (3) an LD check between rs4143322 and rs59321834; (4) any COPD/emphysema-specific animal or human-tissue functional data for SEMA6D, which does not currently exist in the literature found. Given all of this, confidence is rated LOW rather than MODERATE or HIGH — the gene identity is essentially undisputed, but the causal-pathway story is not, and the pipeline's own priors point toward this being a plausible case of a smoking/behavior-mediated signal rather than direct COPD lung pathology.
