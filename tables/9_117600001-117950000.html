Prioritized gene

Prioritized gene: TLR4 Confidence: LOW

TLR4 is the best-supported single candidate at this locus, but the case is far weaker than TLR4's general reputation would suggest, and confidence should be marked LOW rather than MODERATE or HIGH. In favor of TLR4: it is the sole protein-coding gene physically located within the GRCh38-defined interval (Ensembl REST confirms no other named gene overlaps the region), and it is the sole gene nominated by Open Targets' own L2G model applied directly to this locus's credible sets (L2G ~0.53-0.59, corroborated by very high colocalization H4 0.92-0.99 between the COPD signal and several other traits at this locus). Against strong confidence: no eQTL, sQTL, pQTL, or expression-colocalization evidence was found anywhere in the literature tying the credible-set variants themselves (rs1887781 or its 13 LD partners) to TLR4 expression or function; a systematic, purpose-built lung-eQTL/COPD-GWAS integration study (Lamontagne et al. 2018) that mapped causal genes for 60/129 known COPD loci did NOT recover TLR4; the extensive TLR4-COPD candidate-gene literature (21 citations) concerns entirely different SNPs (Asp299Gly, Thr399Ile, rs11536889, rs10759932, etc.) not in this credible set, and is itself directionally inconsistent across populations and across mouse models (Zhang 2006 vs. Haw 2017 report opposite effects of TLR4 loss-of-function on emphysema; Rohde 2005 vs. Gutiérrez-Romero 2024 report opposite risk/protective alleles for the same SNP); and Open Targets' own literature-vs-genetics score breakdown (0.96 literature vs. 0.44 genetic_association) suggests TLR4's COPD association in the database is substantially inflated by general reputation. Critically, the colocalizing trait set at this exact locus (subjective well-being, hip osteoarthritis, frailty, shortness of breath, mood swings) includes no spirometric lung-function trait and no smoking-behavior trait, raising a genuine, unresolved alternative hypothesis that this locus acts through a general health/frailty/behavioral-symptom pathway rather than TLR4-specific pulmonary or innate-immune biology — an alternative the pipeline's own biological priors flag as worth taking seriously rather than defaulting to "TLR4 = inflammation = COPD" by reputation.

TNC is not a viable competing candidate: independent confirmation via both Ensembl REST and Open Targets shows TNC's actual GRCh38 coordinates lie ~2.6-2.7 Mb outside this locus, most likely reflecting a genome-build coordinate mismatch in the upstream MAGMA/PoPS pipeline (its GRCh37 coordinates coincidentally fall within this locus's numeric range). TNC is absent from Open Targets L2G at this locus, has a near-zero genetic_association score, and has no GWAS/eQTL/colocalization literature connecting it to COPD at all; its general airway-remodeling tissue-expression literature is itself mixed (Löfdahl 2011 positive vs. Muñoz-Esquerre 2015 null). TNC should be treated as CONTESTED/likely spurious at this locus, and this build-mismatch finding should be escalated to whoever maintains the MAGMA/PoPS input pipeline, since it may affect other loci.

What would resolve the tie/raise confidence in TLR4: (1) a direct eQTL/sQTL colocalization analysis of rs1887781 (or LD partners) against TLR4 expression in lung tissue, whole blood, or relevant immune-cell types (e.g. GTEx, eQTLGen, or a lung-specific eQTL dataset not captured in Lamontagne 2018); (2) confirmation (via supplementary-table lookup, not accessible to the researcher via PubMed/bioRxiv alone) of whether this exact chr9 region appears among the loci reported in Shrine et al. 2019 (279 lung-function signals) or Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019 (82 COPD loci); (3) a colocalization or Mendelian-randomization analysis of this locus against FEV1/FEV1-FVC specifically, which is currently absent from Open Targets' data at this locus. Until then, TLR4 should be reported as the best-supported positional/statistical candidate at this locus, not as a mechanistically confirmed COPD effector gene.
