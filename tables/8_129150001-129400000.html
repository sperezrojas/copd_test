Prioritized gene

Prioritized gene: GSDMC — Confidence: INSUFFICIENT

This call is close, and the honest answer is that neither nominated gene has evidence that survives scrutiny as a clear pulmonary-biology explanation for this signal — both were independently marked CONTESTED by red-team-skeptic (see rows above and audits/8_129150001-129400000_audit.md). I am naming GSDMC as the marginally better-supported pick for the study team's consideration, but this should be read as "least-bad, most-testable candidate," not a confident causal call, for the following reasons:

  • For GSDMC: it is the nearest protein-coding gene and the FLAMES-prioritized gene, and it retains the only verified, COPD-tissue-specific correlative evidence in this dossier — Liu, Huang, Xu 2023 (PMID 37194062), which places GSDMC among 8 up-regulated "pyroptosis-related genes" in a WGCNA-derived module in COPD small airway epithelium. Gasdermin-family pore-forming pyroptosis biology is mechanistically plausible for inflammatory/cell-death programs relevant to COPD in principle. However, three independent attempts to substantiate a more specific GSDMC-COPD mechanistic pathway (Liu et al. 2023 Cell Biol Toxicol — NOT_FOUND/claim-unverifiable; Feng et al. 2022 — CLAIM_MISMATCH, the review actually ties COPD pyroptosis to NLRP3/caspase-1/GSDMD, not caspase-8/GSDMC; and the researcher's own uncitable recalled GSDMC/caspase-8/PD-L1 mechanism) all failed verification — a pattern red-team-skeptic explicitly flags as evidence of a hypothesis being reverse-fitted to citations. GSDMC also sits ~350 kb outside this locus interval and the entire credible set (i.e., it is not a fine-mapped or colocalized gene here — classic nearest-gene assignment), and Open Targets shows zero genetic-association-datatype support for COPD, with its strongest actual genetic signals pointing to musculoskeletal and oncological traits.
  • Runner-up, CCDC26/LINC00977: this lncRNA gene body physically contains the entire credible set and the lead variant itself (PIP=0.706, intronic) and is independently ABC-prioritized — a real positional/regulatory argument in its favor that GSDMC lacks. However, it has no lung eQTL/sQTL, zero COPD disease association in Open Targets, and its only characterized regional mechanism (Oktay et al. 2016, PMID 27282637 — the single most mechanistically important paper in this dossier) demonstrates that the region's flagship functional variant (rs55705857, a different variant from rs10095076) acts as a long-range enhancer on the distal MYC promoter, with CCDC26 RNA itself rarely expressed and unaffected by genotype — direct precedent that elements within this gene body may not act through the CCDC26 transcript at all.
  • Why the call is close / what would resolve it: the decisive missing evidence is functional: (1) chromatin-conformation (Hi-C/promoter-capture) or reporter-assay data testing whether rs10095076 and its LD partners specifically contact the GSDMC promoter, the CCDC26 locus, or the more distal MYC/PVT1 promoters (~1.0-1.5 Mb away); (2) a COPD-relevant or lung-tissue eQTL/sQTL for GSDMC or CCDC26 at this exact variant (none found in either GTEx-derived Open Targets data or the literature search); and (3) a direct supplementary-table check of the two major reference GWAS (Shrine et al. 2019, PMID 30804560; Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019, PMID 30804561) and of major smoking-behavior GWAS (e.g., GSCAN) at this exact position, none of which was completed to the supplementary-table level in this review (abstract-level only) — red-team-skeptic explicitly flags this as an open gap, not a settled negative. Until that functional and supplementary-table evidence is in hand, MYC and PVT1 should also be considered as candidate distal-effector genes at this locus rather than assuming the causal gene is either GSDMC or CCDC26/LINC00977 — this was flagged by red-team-skeptic as an unexplored alternative hypothesis not pursued by the researcher in this pass.
