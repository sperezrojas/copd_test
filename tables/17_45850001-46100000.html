Evidence Table — Locus 17:45850001-46100000 (nearest gene MAPT)

Region: chr17:45,850,001-46,100,000 (GRCh38). Lead variant: rs242559 (17:45,948,522, PIP=0.533, intron_variant in MAPT). Locus context: this locus sits within the well-known 17q21.31 MAPT/CRHR1/KANSL1/STH/SPPL2C inversion polymorphism region (H1/H2 haplotype), an ancient (~3 million year divergence), near-complete-LD structural block spanning ~900kb-1Mb. This is why the credible set here contains 956 variants — the largest in the dataset — spanning five genes (MAPT, MAPT-AS1, KANSL1, STH, SPPL2C). This is expected biology given the locus definition, not a fine-mapping failure, and gene-level statistical/nearest-gene assignment in this region is intrinsically difficult to resolve (see Prioritized-gene section below).

Candidate genes evaluated: MAPT (nearest gene, ABC-prioritized), MAPT-AS1, KANSL1, STH, SPPL2C (all assigned credible-set variants within this window), PRR15L (MAGMA-prioritized), PNPO (PoPS-prioritized), and CRHR1 (not assigned a credible-set variant in this window but overlapping the window's start edge and repeatedly co-implicated across the primary literature and Open Targets L2G lookups — see below).

Prioritized gene

MAPT — confidence: LOW.

This locus is genuinely contested among three genes (MAPT, KANSL1, and CRHR1), and the verified evidence does not cleanly resolve which is causal — this is stated explicitly rather than manufactured away. MAPT is named here as the single best-supported pick for four reasons: (1) it hosts the lead credible-set variant (rs242559, PIP=0.533) and the large majority of the 956-variant credible set — while this is expected under near-complete LD regardless of causality (and is explicitly non-diagnostic per the red-team critique), it is still the strongest physical/statistical anchor available; (2) an independent, much larger lung-function GWAS (Shrine et al. 2019, n=400,102) makes its own gene-call at essentially this same signal and lands on MAPT (Table 1, PMID 30804560), not KANSL1 or CRHR1; (3) a dedicated lung-tissue eQTL/network-integration study of COPD (Morrow et al. 2018, PMID 29335020) independently found MAPT as the top integrative-network gene, explicitly linking MAPT expression regulation to COPD genetic risk in lung tissue; and (4) MAPT carries the locus's missense credible-set variants, even though these are established as common H1/H2-tagging variants rather than damaging alleles.

This call is close, and confidence is capped at LOW for concrete reasons: KANSL1 has arguably better trait-specific evidence — the UK BiLEVE study (Wain et al. 2015, PMID 26423011) found a genome-wide-significant, smoking-independent (present in never-smokers) FEV1-extremes/COPD signal with CNV-dosage support specifically at KANSL1, and Koolen-de Vries syndrome (KANSL1 haploinsufficiency) clinical literature independently documents a real (if not COPD-like) developmental airway phenotype. Meanwhile Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019 (PMID 30804561), the dedicated COPD case-control GWAS from the same consortium/era, uses its own nearest-gene annotation to call this region's pulmonary-fibrosis-overlap signal CRHR1 — a third candidate with no assigned credible-set variant in this window at all. Compounding the ambiguity, Open Targets' contemporary aggregate scoring gives KANSL1 the LOWEST COPD score and the HIGHEST smoking-initiation score of any gene in this window — the opposite pattern predicted by the "KANSL1 = smoking-independent pulmonary gene" reading of Wain et al. 2015 — while MAPT's own aggregate scores are also substantially weighted toward smoking-initiation and risk-taking behaviour rather than COPD specifically. In short: three major papers touching this exact locus give three different "implicated gene" answers (Wain 2015 -> KANSL1, Shrine 2019 -> MAPT, Sakornsakolpat 2019 -> CRHR1), which is the classic signature of nearest-gene/LD-block annotation instability in a near-complete-LD inversion block, not convergent evidence for any one gene.

Runner-up: KANSL1 (close second; would be the pick if trait-specificity/smoking-independence were weighted above statistical/physical centrality). Third candidate: CRHR1 (weakest overall evidence — no credible-set variant in this window, lowest Open Targets COPD score, and no direct mechanistic literature — but should not be dismissed given it is the dedicated COPD GWAS's own nearest-gene call for this region).

What would resolve this tie: (a) a formal statistical or functional fine-mapping / gene prioritization analysis specific to COPD/lung function at this exact locus (e.g. colocalization with a lung-tissue eQTL/sQTL/pQTL dataset restricted to this signal, or an independent COPD/spirometry GWAS's supplementary locus table explicitly naming a single implicated gene with a stated method); (b) a knockout or conditional lung-epithelial/airway-specific functional study of MAPT, KANSL1, or CRHR1 individually (rather than relying on Koolen-de Vries syndrome's whole- body haploinsufficiency phenotype or an NSL-complex-partner study in skin); and (c) confirmation of whether the GSCAN smoking-initiation GWAS (PMID 30643251) itself reaches genome-wide significance at 17q21.31, which would help adjudicate how much of the locus's aggregate genetic signal is smoking-mediated versus directly pulmonary. The study team should weigh this three-way ambiguity explicitly rather than treat MAPT as a settled causal-gene call.
