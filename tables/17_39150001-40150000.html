Prioritized gene

ORMDL3 — confidence: LOW (reaffirmed after THRA addendum)

This call is close, and the pipeline instructions require naming a single best pick even when the evidence does not cleanly distinguish among genes in tight LD — that is exactly the situation here. The runner-up is GSDMB, and neither can be confidently excluded with the evidence in hand. THRA (added in this 2026-09-11 addendum after a genome-build-error correction surfaced it as a candidate) was investigated with the same rigor as the original 13 genes and does not change this tension or unseat ORMDL3 — see the dedicated THRA discussion below for why.

Both ORMDL3 and GSDMB carry the two highest Open Targets COPD-association scores among the original 13 genes at this locus (ORMDL3 0.131, GSDMB 0.192) and the two highest asthma scores (0.460 and 0.483 respectively) — but per the red-team-skeptic's critique (see audit), this Open Targets COPD score and the human genetic literature at this locus (Sakornsakolpat 2019, Shrine 2019) are locus-class evidence that does not, by itself, distinguish ORMDL3 from GSDMB, ZPBP2, or even PGAP3, because all sit in the same CTCF-bounded, domain-wide cis-regulatory block (Verlaan et al. 2009, PMID 19732864) whose disease-associated haplotypes jointly alter GSDMB and ORMDL3 expression. Neither gene has a credible-set coding/splice variant whose functional effect has been tied to a COPD (rather than asthma) outcome: GSDMB's two directly characterized coding/splice variants (rs2305480, rs11078928; Panganiban et al. 2018, PMID 29330013) were characterized entirely against asthma risk and in vitro pyroptosis, not COPD; ORMDL3 carries no coding/splice credible-set variant at all.

The tie-breaker favoring ORMDL3 over GSDMB is the existence of two experimental papers showing cigarette-smoke-induced ORMDL3 up-regulation directly in COPD-relevant cell types — bronchial epithelium (Chen et al. 2025, PMID 40317248) and airway smooth muscle from smokers with/without COPD (Chen et al. 2021, PMID 34624393) — with ORMDL3 knockdown blunting smoke-induced ER stress, mucus hypersecretion, and inflammation. This is the only mechanistic narrative at this locus that is both (a) smoking-triggered, satisfying prior #1's call to evaluate smoking-mediated pathways, and (b) occurring in adult, disease-relevant airway cell types rather than exclusively in childhood-asthma or allergic mouse models. By contrast, GSDMB's deepest mechanistic evidence (Panganiban 2018; Das/Miller/Broide 2017 mouse model) is asthma-outcome literature, and per the skeptic's critique, framing it as COPD-supportive risks conflating asthma mechanism with COPD relevance, given this locus's extensive documented asthma-COPD overlap (Sakornsakolpat 2019 explicitly lists this as one of 14 shared loci) and the fact that the COPD GWAS is neither smoking- nor asthma-history-adjusted.

However, this tie-breaker is itself weak: the skeptic correctly notes that the ORMDL3 smoke-induction studies are correlative expression-level observations in exposed/diseased cells, not genotype-stratified data showing the GWAS risk allele itself modulates the smoke response, and the one human COPD genetic-association paper for ORMDL3 (Balantic et al. 2013, PMID 23964555; P=0.045, small unadjusted Slovenian cohort) is too weak to be treated as confirmatory. Confidence is therefore LOW, not MODERATE.

Why THRA does not change this call: THRA (thyroid hormone receptor alpha) was added to this review in a 2026-09-11 addendum after a genome-build-error correction to the study team's MAGMA/PoPS gene-location merge file surfaced it as a candidate never previously evaluated. Independent coordinate-checking confirmed THRA (chr17:40,058,290-40,093,867) is a genuine physical resident of this locus's 1 Mb window, so the correction itself is legitimate. However: (1) THRA carries no credible-set variant — the nearest fine-mapped variant is ~139 kb away (rs7224129, PIP=0.0006, annotated to ORMDL3) — so THRA is not competing with ORMDL3/GSDMB on the same fine-mapping evidentiary axis at all; it is evidentiarily closer to CSF3/PSMD3 (window-resident, no credible-set variant) than to the credible-set-anchored candidates. (2) Despite Open Targets assigning THRA the single highest raw COPD-association score at this locus (0.252, versus GSDMB 0.192 and ORMDL3 0.131), exhaustive literature search found zero THRA-specific GWAS, colocalization, TWAS, or MR evidence for COPD or spirometric traits. (3) The strongest and most decision-relevant evidence found is actually negative: the human THRA loss-of-function disease (RTHα, OMIM 614450), characterized across four independent case series (Vlaeminck-Guillem et al. 2015, PMID 26585273; Moran et al. 2017, PMID 28471274; Korkmaz et al. 2019, PMID 30747412; Jorge et al. 2024, PMID 38744258), has a well-documented multi-system clinical phenotype (growth, dysmorphism, neurodevelopment, GI, cardiac) that includes no reported pulmonary/respiratory phenotype, and three RCT/systematic-review-level studies found no benefit of thyroid-hormone supplementation for bronchopulmonary dysplasia (Smith et al. 2000, PMID 11076326; Ng et al. 2008, PMID 18590560; Ibrahim et al. 2006, PMID 16625632). Per the red-team-skeptic, this combination — no credible-set variant, no statistical-genetics support, and negative-leaning mechanistic evidence from the direct human LOF disease model — means THRA's Open Targets COPD score should be treated as an unresolved, uncorroborated database anomaly (possibly reflecting text-mining/pleiotropy-scoring artifacts from THRA's broad multi-system disease-association profile) rather than evidence that should reweight or unseat the ORMDL3-vs-GSDMB tie-break, which rests on genuine (if imperfect) fine-mapping-adjacent and mechanistic literature that THRA entirely lacks. THRA is therefore recorded here as an evaluated-but-not-prioritized candidate with a documented open gap (whether THRA was among the ~17 candidate genes Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019 implicated at this locus was not resolvable from the accessible text — PMID 30804561 — and remains an open item for anyone with access to that paper's supplementary tables), not dismissed outright.

What would resolve the ORMDL3-vs-GSDMB tie: (1) A locus-specific colocalization/fine-mapping analysis (e.g., using lung-tissue and airway-epithelium eQTL panels) that assigns the COPD credible-set signal preferentially to ORMDL3 or GSDMB expression, rather than jointly to both as the current domain-wide cis-regulatory evidence suggests. (2) An asthma-history-adjusted or Mendelian randomization analysis of the COPD GWAS signal at this locus, to test whether the COPD association is fully mediated by asthma liability (as the skeptic recommends as a priority follow-up) or represents an independent adult/smoking-driven signal. (3) Genotype-stratified (risk-allele-dependent) functional data showing the credible-set variants themselves — not just disease-state or smoke-exposure status — modulate ORMDL3 or GSDMB expression/function in COPD-relevant airway cells. What would resolve THRA's status specifically: direct access to the Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019 supplementary gene tables to confirm/refute THRA's membership among the 17 candidate genes named at 17q21.1; a THRA-specific lung-tissue eQTL/colocalization query; and tracing the Open Targets "bronchopulmonary dysplasia" and COPD association scores back to their underlying data sources to determine whether they reflect genuine genetic-association evidence or database aggregation artifacts.

PGAP3, despite topping the positional/ABC-prioritization ranking and harboring the highest-PIP credible-set variants, is not the recommended pick: it has the lowest Open Targets COPD score of the core candidates (0.0067), no COPD-specific literature beyond one hedged, multi-gene SMR/TWAS pleiotropy signal (Wen et al. 2025, PMID 40917930) that explicitly requires functional validation, and is best interpreted — per both the researcher and red-team-skeptic — as a "hitchhiker" gene tagging the true regulatory signal rather than an independent COPD driver. KRT15 has genuinely COPD- and smoking-specific structural biology (Zhu et al. 2023, PMID 38007424; Ievlev et al. 2023, PMID 36512409) but only MAGMA-level (not fine-mapping/coloc) genetic support and no demonstrated link to this locus's specific credible-set variants, so it is not preferred over ORMDL3/GSDMB despite its compelling biology. CSF3 has the most distinctively COPD- and smoking-specific combination of evidence of any gene reviewed (a locus-adjacent neutrophil-count GWAS hit, Okada et al. 2010, PMID 20172861, plus a direct smoking-cohort lung-function-decline association, He et al. 2008, PMID 18353856) but is excluded from primary consideration because it was added via independent gene-window enumeration, carries no credible-set variant, and its LD relationship to this locus's actual fine-mapped signal (which clusters ~300-450 kb away, mostly in PGAP3/GSDMB) has not been established — per the red-team-skeptic, it should be treated as a flagged hypothesis-generating side-finding requiring an LD check, not folded into the ranked candidate list for this specific credible set. THRA (see discussion above) sits in a similar or weaker evidentiary category to CSF3/PSMD3 and is likewise excluded from primary consideration.
