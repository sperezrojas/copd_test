Prioritized-gene synthesis

Prioritized gene: SORCS3. Confidence: MODERATE (on gene identity), but the mechanism by which this locus associates with COPD is best characterized as LOW-confidence / smoking-neurobehaviorally-mediated rather than a direct pulmonary mechanism — these are two separate questions and should not be conflated into a single confidence label.

Why SORCS3 over CNNM2 (runner-up) on gene identity: All 19 credible-set variants at this locus are intronic within SORCS3 under GRCh38 — the same build used for fine-mapping — so SORCS3 is the gene the statistical fine-mapping signal physically sits inside, not an LD/nearest-gene inference (red-team-skeptic explicitly confirmed this is not an artifact). CNNM2, by contrast, was flagged as MAGMA/PoPS-prioritized based on apparent physical overlap, but the researcher's independent Ensembl check found CNNM2 only overlaps this window under GRCh37 — under GRCh38 it sits ~1.5 Mb away. The red-team-skeptic agreed this is a "legitimate, likely-disqualifying flaw in CNNM2's positional evidence specifically for this locus." Open Targets' own aggregate COPD-association score independently corroborates this asymmetry: SORCS3 scores 0.359 (with a substantial genetic_association sub-score of 0.590), while CNNM2 scores a near-null 0.00275 with no genetic_association contribution at all. Combined, the positional (build-correct fine-mapping) and structured-database evidence both point to SORCS3, not CNNM2, as the better-supported candidate gene at this specific locus.

Why confidence on mechanism is LOW, not HIGH, despite this: No spirometry, FEV1, FEV1/FVC, or any lung-function GWAS/TWAS/colocalization evidence exists for SORCS3 anywhere in the verified literature — a targeted search returned zero hits, and a comprehensive GWAS-catalog review of 25 SORCS3-associated phenotypes found none pulmonary (Kamran et al., though its exact "72/25" figures are unconfirmed by the verifier — see audit). SORCS3 shows near-exclusive neuronal expression and a well-established synaptic/neurodevelopmental mechanism (Hermey 2004, Breiderhoff 2013, Christiansen 2017) with no lung expression reported anywhere. What SORCS3 does have is a very strong, repeatedly replicated psychiatric-genetics signal (depression, the only gene shared across 5 psychiatric-disorder GWAS, and — per Open Targets' structured variant-level lookup, not yet independently gene-confirmed in any smoking-GWAS paper's own text — this exact locus is tagged by multiple large smoking-behavior GWAS). Per this pipeline's smoking-mediation prior, this pattern is consistent with a COPD association driven by a neurobehavioral/smoking-propensity pathway rather than direct pulmonary biology. However, the red-team-skeptic explicitly cautioned that this is an argument from absence on both sides — nobody has run a SORCS3-specific lung-function GWAS/TWAS to test a pulmonary mechanism directly, and no smoking-GWAS paper's own abstract independently names SORCS3 (the locus-tagging claim rests on the Open Targets structured lookup alone) — so "smoking-mediated" should be read as the best-supported current hypothesis, not an established mechanism.

CNNM2 as runner-up: Its case, if the build-coordinate concern is set aside, rests on well-established but pulmonary-unrelated biology (Mg2+ transport/hypomagnesemia, a likely causal role in a 10q24.32 blood-pressure locus, and a robust, functionally validated schizophrenia risk-gene role), plus one independent, gene-specific smoking-behavior GWAS hit (rs12780116) that does not depend on the build question. A very recent (2026) cross-trait schizophrenia-pulmonary TWAS flags CNNM2 as a candidate bridging psychiatric and pulmonary comorbidity, but its own authors state direct pulmonary evidence "remains limited" — this should be read as suggestive shared-genetic-architecture evidence, not a CNNM2-lung-function hit.

What would resolve this / raise or lower confidence: (1) confirming which genome build the internal MAGMA/PoPS pipeline used for CNNM2's "physical overlap" call, to settle whether its candidacy at this exact locus is a build artifact or genuine; (2) a smoking-adjusted COPD or lung-function GWAS at this locus, to test directly whether the association attenuates after conditioning on smoking exposure (the most direct test of prior #1 here); (3) a SORCS3- or CNNM2-specific lung-tissue eQTL/pQTL colocalization or lung-function (FEV1/FEV1-FVC) GWAS hit, which does not currently exist for either gene; (4) independent confirmation, in a smoking-GWAS paper's own text (not just Open Targets' structured tagging), that SORCS3 specifically (not just the surrounding LD block) is the smoking-associated gene. Until then, this should be treated as SORCS3 being the best-supported gene at this locus with LOW confidence in a specific disease mechanism (most likely smoking-mediated, pulmonary mechanism not excluded), CNNM2 as a weaker runner-up whose positional case is now in question, and WBP1L/AS3MT/CYP17A1/NT5C2/BORCS7 as low-priority candidates with no meaningful evidence found.
