Prioritized gene

PBX3 — Confidence: MODERATE

PBX3 is the best-supported candidate gene at this locus, but the case rests almost entirely on statistical/fine-mapping evidence rather than on a confirmed COPD-relevant literature mechanism, which caps confidence below HIGH.

Supporting the call: 41 of 45 credible-set variants (including PIP-ranked variants rs7044841, rs10986983, rs10760401, and most others) fall within PBX3 introns, and Open Targets' locus-to-gene-style scoring independently and consistently favors PBX3 over the nearest gene MAPKAP1 at every one of 7 shared credible-set variants across 7 independent GWAS studies (PBX3 scores 0.47–0.78 vs. MAPKAP1's 0.12–0.28 at the same variants; see Open Targets summary above), and PBX3 has the highest overall COPD association score (0.431) and highest genetic_association datatype score (0.709) of all five candidates. This is a case where fine-mapping and an independent statistical prioritization method agree, and — notably — correctly override "nearest gene" status (MAPKAP1), rather than a case of nearest-gene bias.

Weighing against higher confidence: the single literature finding that most directly ties PBX3 to lung tissue (Williams et al., PMID 39220670 — a PBX3-intronic variant colocalizing with a lung eQTL for a respiratory-infection-susceptibility GWAS) failed to replicate in a Stage-2 meta-analysis. PBX3's colocalization with COPD itself is internally inconsistent across independent GWAS Catalog studies (h4 ranging 0.02–0.95), and its colocalization with FEV1 is likewise inconsistent (0.36–0.81). The locus colocalizes broadly with many unrelated traits, consistent with a wide pleiotropic LD block rather than one clean causal signal. Critically, the specific mechanism by which this locus might act — smoking-mediated (prior #1; suggested by strong Open-Targets-level colocalization with "Smoking status – Current," asthma, and chronic lower respiratory disease) versus direct pulmonary-function/structural biology (prior #2; suggested inconsistently by the FEV1 coloc) versus something else — remains genuinely unresolved: the one literature citation offered to substantiate a PBX3-smoking link (GSCAN, PMID 30643251) does not itself mention PBX3 or 9q33 and was flagged CLAIM_MISMATCH by citation-verifier. PBX3's own literature footprint is dominated by hematopoiesis/leukemia biology and oncology (including lung cancer cell lines), not lung development or COPD-relevant airway immunology — none of which was found to extend to a documented lung-developmental or airway-epithelial PBX3 role specifically.

Runner-up: MAPKAP1 (nearest gene) is clearly disfavored — it is systematically outscored by PBX3 at every shared credible-set variant across 7 independent studies, and 41/45 credible-set variants sit in PBX3, not MAPKAP1, introns. STRBP and PTGS1 are both independently confirmed (via direct Ensembl coordinate lookups by both the researcher and red-team-skeptic) to lie ~1.5 Mb and ~2.4 Mb outside the stated locus boundaries respectively, have zero Open Targets genetic evidence, and their supporting literature is drawn entirely from unrelated biological contexts (spermatogenesis/cancer-cell splicing for STRBP; NSAID drug-adverse-event pharmacology for PTGS1) — both should be treated as very unlikely candidates at this locus pending a coordinate/build sanity check upstream. PBX3-DT (LOC51145) has zero literature and zero Open Targets data, and its ABC prioritization is plausibly non-independent of PBX3 given the two genes' divergent, likely-shared bidirectional promoter.

What would resolve this to HIGH confidence: independent replication of the PBX3 lung-eQTL finding in a larger respiratory-disease or COPD-specific cohort; a literature-confirmed (not just database-coloc) link between the PBX3/9q33 region and smoking-behavior GWAS signals, to establish or rule out prior #1 as the operative mechanism; or a COPD/FEV1-specific fine-mapping/colocalization result that is consistent (rather than 0.02–0.95 across studies) and would settle whether prior #2 (direct pulmonary-function biology) is the better-supported mechanism. The study team should weigh this recommendation accordingly — this is our best-supported pick given available evidence, not a settled causal-gene call.
