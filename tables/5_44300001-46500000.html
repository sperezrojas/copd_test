Prioritized gene

FGF10 — MODERATE confidence.

FGF10 is the best-supported single candidate gene at this locus, but the call is closer than the biology alone would suggest, and the reasoning has to be stated carefully because of a genuine tension in the statistical data.

Why FGF10: Of the three candidates, FGF10 is the only one with (a) a clear, convergent, largely COPD/emphysema-specific mechanistic literature — direct mouse loss-of-function and gain-of-function evidence that FGF10 insufficiency causes emphysema-like structural phenotypes and that FGF10 replacement reverses established cigarette-smoke/elastase emphysema and pulmonary hypertension (PMID 37884305, 19115389, 36183124), reinforced by human rare-variant evidence that FGF10 dosage is critical for normal lung structure (PMID 37560881); (b) very strong genetic constraint (gnomAD LoF oe = 0.056, essentially no tolerated LoF variants), which is exactly the pattern expected for a dosage-sensitive developmental gene; and (c) the single cleanest, most internally consistent statistical signal in the interval — Open Targets L2G assigns FGF10 (not MRPS30) the causal role by a >10-fold margin, reproducibly, across two independent COPD GWAS and asthma, with strong COPD-COPD and COPD-asthma colocalization.

Why the confidence is MODERATE and not HIGH: The FGF10-region variants that carry this strong L2G/colocalization signal (e.g. rs2330553, PIP≈0.002) are low-PIP passengers in this specific locus's own credible set — the actual lead, highest-PIP variant of this locus (rs116806673, PIP=0.17) sits ~600 kb away, in the HCN1/MRPS30 region, where gene attribution is genuinely unresolved: Open Targets L2G disagrees between HCN1 and MRPS30 across the two COPD GWAS studies queried, and even those two COPD studies only weakly colocalize with each other at that variant (h4=0.44). In other words, the strongest fine-mapped signal in this locus's credible set is not the same signal for which the literature and cross-study statistical evidence most clearly point to a single causal gene. FGF10-specific evidence also has two real gaps: no direct FEV1/FEV1-FVC spirometric-trait literature was found for FGF10 despite an extensive search, and its verified immune evidence is confined to asthma (Th2/eosinophilic) and ARDS models rather than COPD-typical neutrophilic/CD8+ T-cell programs (both noted, not suppressed, in the table above). One candidate citation asserting a direct FGF10-airway-branching-COPD human association (Smith et al., PMID 29339516) could not be confirmed by the citation-verifier to actually name FGF10 and has been excluded (see audit).

Runner-up: HCN1 (and, more weakly, MRPS30), at the high-PIP sub-locus. At the lead variant, HCN1 is the dominant L2G gene for smoking initiation/cessation/status and related behavioral traits (colocalizing with the COPD signal at h4 0.85-0.93), consistent with this being a smoking-behavior-mediated signal per prior 1 rather than direct pulmonary biology — a hypothesis the researcher could not independently corroborate via PubMed (a search-coverage gap, not a refutation) but which the red-team-skeptic judged plausible enough, combined with the CLAIM_MISMATCH on the one HCN1-COPD citation found (PMID 42026014, whose actual novel loci are DRAIC/RFX3), to mark HCN1 CONTESTED rather than supported. MRPS30 is judged the weakest of the three: it is LoF-tolerant (oe≈0.96, no dosage-sensitivity signal), has no lung/COPD-relevant literature of any kind, and its only well-established genetics is as the effector gene at an entirely separate, independent 5p12 breast-cancer GWAS locus — consistent with the classic nearest-gene/LD-proximity false-lead scenario the pipeline's biological priors ask reviewers to watch for.

What would resolve the tie: (1) a colocalization/fine-mapping analysis run on the exact same COPD GWAS summary statistics used to build this locus's own credible set (rather than the Open-Targets-indexed studies, which may differ in cohort/ancestry/phenotype definition from the internal pipeline's COPD meta-analysis), explicitly testing whether the FGF10 and HCN1/MRPS30 regions represent one shared haplotype or two independent signals; (2) a smoking-adjusted COPD GWAS or multivariable MR analysis at the HCN1/MRPS30 sub-locus to directly test whether the signal survives conditioning on smoking behavior; and (3) direct FEV1/FEV1-FVC spirometric association data for FGF10, which is currently entirely absent from the literature reviewed.
