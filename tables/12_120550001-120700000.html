Locus-level note on smoking mediation (not a citation-table row; synthesized from structured data + critique)

The researcher's two literature citations intended to directly link this locus's top credible-set variants (rs2859263, rs1168067) to smoking-initiation/lifetime-smoking GWAS were found CLAIM_MISMATCH by the citation-verifier (see audit appendix) — the specific variant/p-value claims were not confirmed in the cited papers' abstracts. Independent of those two disputed citations, the Open Targets structured lookup (see below; not a literature citation, not subject to verification) shows Open Targets' own GWAS-catalog credible-set holdings at these exact variant positions are overwhelmingly for smoking-behavior traits (not COPD or lung function), with CABP1 consistently the L2G-prioritized gene. The red-team-skeptic noted this OT evidence is not fully independent in provenance from the disputed citations (likely same underlying summary statistics, viewed via database rather than abstract), and recommended the smoking-mediation hypothesis be treated as a leading but weakly triangulated hypothesis at low-to-moderate confidence, not an established finding.

Prioritized gene

CABP1 — Confidence: LOW.

CABP1 is the single best-supported candidate at this locus, but the support is almost entirely positional/statistical rather than functional. It is the nearest gene to the lead variant, hosts the second-highest-PIP credible-set variant (rs1168067, PIP=0.284, intronic), and is the consistent Open Targets locus-to-gene (L2G)-prioritized target (scores ~0.43-0.69) at both of the two highest-PIP credible-set variants, specifically for smoking-behavior GWAS credible sets (Open Targets summary above). However: (1) the two literature citations meant to directly confirm this locus's variant-level association with smoking-initiation/lifetime-smoking traits were CLAIM_MISMATCH on verification (audit appendix) and cannot be relied on as stated; (2) CABP1's own established molecular biology (neuronal/retinal/cochlear Ca2+-channel and IP3-receptor modulation; evidence table rows above, all WEAKENED by the skeptic) has no genuine COPD- or airway-specific functional literature — the single bronchial-epithelial-cell-line connection (Kasri et al., PMID 12746431) is a generic mechanism paper, not airway-pathology-specific evidence; and (3) CABP1 shows no COPD association record at all in Open Targets' curated evidence (score 0).

The runner-up is CABP1-DT, the lncRNA that physically hosts the single highest-PIP credible-set variant (rs2859263, PIP=0.615) — a fact the red-team-skeptic flagged as creating real tension with CABP1's L2G-based prioritization (highest-PIP variant sits in one gene, but statistical/L2G prioritization points to the neighboring gene) that should not be resolved silently in CABP1's favor. CABP1-DT has no independent functional literature at all, so this tension cannot currently be broken with available evidence.

RPLP0 and GCN1/GCN1L1 are not competitive: both lie 350-500kb outside the locus/credible-set window, have no genetic (GWAS/TWAS/coloc) evidence tying them to this specific signal, and their Open Targets COPD association is driven only by text-mining (RPLP0) or animal-model matching (GCN1), not human genetics. GCN1's mouse perinatal-respiratory-failure phenotype (Yamazaki et al., PMID 32324833, VERIFIED) is a genuine and striking developmental-lung finding, but it arises from near-complete loss-of-function alleles and a phenotype (perinatal respiratory failure) that is mechanistically distant from adult, chronic, smoking-associated airflow obstruction — the skeptic explicitly cautioned against letting this finding move GCN1 up the prioritization list.

What would resolve this call: (a) direct confirmation, against the Saunders et al. and Pasman et al. papers' supplementary loci tables (not abstracts), of whether rs2859263/rs1168067 are genuinely genome-wide significant for smoking initiation/lifetime smoking as claimed; (b) lung-tissue eQTL/colocalization data for CABP1 and CABP1-DT specifically (not reported by the researcher and flagged by the skeptic as a notable gap); and (c) any direct COPD or FEV1/FEV1-FVC GWAS evidence at this locus, none of which was found in this review round. Given the citation problems, the functional-biology vacuum, and the skeptic's WEAKENED verdict on every candidate gene at this locus, this call should be treated as the best available lead, not an established finding — the study team should weigh it accordingly.
