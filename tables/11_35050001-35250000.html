Overall skeptic assessment (gene-level, from red-team-skeptic critique)

Verdict: WEAKENED. The gene-elimination logic (CD44 is the sole protein-coding gene physically present at this locus; the 12-variant credible set is tight and entirely intronic to CD44; nearest-gene/FLAMES/MAGMA/PoPS/ABC unanimously agree) is sound, and CD44 should remain the prioritized candidate at this locus. However:

  • There is zero gene-specific statistical-genetics support — no TWAS, colocalization, MR, or eQTL finding names CD44 as causal at this locus.
  • CD44 is confirmed absent from both major consortium papers covering this trait space (Shrine et al. 2019 Nat Genet, 279 lung-function signals; Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019 Nat Genet, 82 COPD loci) — see excluded/audit appendix.
  • Open Targets' own COPD association score for CD44 is driven entirely by text-mined literature, with no curated genetic-association, L2G, or colocalization data resolvable for this variant.
  • The structural and immune mechanistic literature is real and substantial but establishes CD44 as a biologically plausible COPD-pathway gene in general, not that this specific locus acts through those mechanisms — no eQTL or other data ties the risk-allele direction of effect at rs353642 to any of the observed CD44 expression/functional changes.
  • A genuine, unreconciled directional inconsistency exists across cell types (CD44 decreased in COPD airway smooth muscle and alveolar macrophages; increased on COPD submucosal neutrophils). Plausible as cell-type-specific biology, but not resolved by current evidence.
  • Not marked CONTESTED in the strong sense: no competing positive claim for an alternative causal gene exists at this locus (there is no other protein-coding gene present), and the "contesting" evidence is an absence/negative finding, not a rival hypothesis.

Prioritized gene

CD44 — Confidence: LOW.

CD44 is the only reasonable pick at this locus: it is the unanimous choice of all four internal prioritization methods (nearest gene, FLAMES, MAGMA, PoPS) plus ABC, and — per the researcher's independent Ensembl region query — it is the only protein-coding gene physically present in the 11:35.05-35.25 Mb region (the only other overlapping features are lncRNAs CD44-AS1/CD44-DT and several unnamed lncRNA IDs, which are out of scope for this gene-level review). There is no competing protein-coding gene to weigh against it, so this is not a close call between two candidates in LD — it is a single-candidate locus.

Despite that structural clarity, confidence is capped at LOW rather than MODERATE or HIGH because, per the red-team-skeptic's WEAKENED verdict above, the case for CD44 rests entirely on (a) elimination (no other protein-coding gene to consider) and (b) general mechanistic plausibility from the hyaluronan/CD44 axis in airway biology and inflammation — and not on any gene-specific statistical-genetics finding. Open Targets shows no curated genetic-association or colocalization evidence for this variant, and this locus/CD44 is confirmed absent from both major published consortium GWAS papers for lung function (Shrine et al. 2019) and COPD case-control status (Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019) — a nontrivial negative signal from two large, well-powered studies of essentially the same trait space, not merely an absence of evidence. No eQTL, TWAS, colocalization, or Mendelian-randomization evidence ties the direction of effect of the COPD risk allele at rs353642 to any specific direction of CD44 expression or function, which matters because the verified mechanistic literature itself shows an unreconciled directional split (CD44 decreased in COPD airway smooth muscle [PMID 19282346] and alveolar macrophages [PMID 23148928], but increased on COPD submucosal neutrophils [PMID 19703829]) — plausible as cell-type-specific biology, but not resolved by current evidence, and not anchored to the GWAS signal itself.

What would resolve this and raise confidence: (1) an eQTL or colocalization analysis tying rs353642 (or its credible-set proxies) to CD44 expression in a COPD-relevant tissue/cell type, with a defined direction of effect; (2) direct confirmation (e.g., via GWAS Catalog / supplementary tables) of whether this exact locus/lead variant appears in Shrine et al. 2019 or Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019 under a different lead SNP in LD with rs353642, since the citation-verifier confirmed CD44 is absent from the named loci/signal lists of both papers; (3) a PheWAS/GWAS-Catalog pleiotropy lookup for rs353642 to rule out that this signal is primarily a blood-cell-count or osteoarthritis-related pleiotropic effect rather than a lung-specific one (flagged by red-team as an unresolved follow-up). There is no runner-up gene to name at this locus given the clean single-protein-coding-gene structure — the open question is mechanism and causal confirmation, not gene identity.
