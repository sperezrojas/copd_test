Prioritized gene

Prioritized gene: SCN1A-AS1. Confidence: LOW.

This call is close, and is made with substantial reservations that the study team should weigh carefully.

Why SCN1A-AS1 over the alternatives: Of the four candidates, SCN1A-AS1 is the only one with a genuine gene-specific statistical case at this locus: the lead credible-set variant (rs5027759, PIP=0.713) and 44 of 47 credible-set variants (essentially all of the fine-mapped PIP mass) sit inside its gene body, versus only 3 very-low-PIP variants (each ≤0.0016) in SCN1A and zero in SCN2A or SCN9A. SCN2A — the MAGMA/PoPS-prioritized gene — is CONTESTED outright: its genomic coordinates (chr2:165,194,993-165,392,310, confirmed independently by both the researcher's Ensembl lookup and the orchestrator's Open Targets lookup, see summary above) place it ~600-850 kb outside the stated locus window, meaning it fails the input pipeline's own physical-overlap criterion for PoPS genes. This should be treated as a likely artifact of PoPS's expression/pathway-similarity-based scoring rather than genuine locus-level support, pending upstream review of a possible build mismatch. SCN9A has no gene-specific evidence of any kind and was not flagged by any prioritization method; it is included only for positional completeness at the locus boundary.

Why confidence is only LOW, not MODERATE or HIGH: The case for SCN1A-AS1 rests on positional/statistical concentration of the credible set plus a biologically plausible but explicitly unconfirmed mechanistic story: a body of literature (Hsiao 2016, PMID 27333023; Schneider 2024, PMID 38283997; Diaz 2026, PMID 42181696 — all VERIFIED) establishes that antisense transcripts overlapping the SCN1A locus (named "SCN1A-NAT," "SCN1A-dsAS," "SCN1A-usAS" in the primary literature) act as cis-repressors of SCN1A expression in brain tissue, and are validated therapeutic targets for raising SCN1A expression in Dravet syndrome. However, the researcher explicitly flagged, and red-team-skeptic concurred, that transcript-identity between these literature-named transcripts and the Ensembl-curated gene model "SCN1A-AS1" (ENSG00000236107) is plausible but not independently confirmed — antisense-RNA nomenclature is notoriously inconsistent across RefSeq/Ensembl/primary literature. Even granting that identity, every single piece of mechanistic evidence is from brain/neuronal tissue in a pediatric-epilepsy context; there is zero published lung, airway, or immune-cell expression or functional data for SCN1A-AS1 (or the related transcripts) anywhere in the literature found, and Open Targets independently shows zero association data of any kind for this gene and COPD. The regulated gene, if the mechanism holds, is SCN1A itself — whose own established biology (central brainstem CO2/H+ chemoreception control, implicated in Dravet-associated apnea/SUDEP; six VERIFIED citations, all WEAKENED by red-team-skeptic as tissue/mechanism-mismatched to adult airflow obstruction) offers no direct pulmonary-structural or airway-inflammatory mechanism for COPD either.

Runner-up: SCN1A (nearest gene). If the credible set's regulatory target is in fact SCN1A itself (via the antisense mechanism above, or via some other regulatory path independent of SCN1A-AS1), SCN1A would be the effector gene, not SCN1A-AS1. The evidence currently cannot distinguish between "SCN1A-AS1 acts autonomously" and "SCN1A-AS1's real role here is purely as a cis-regulator whose effect is entirely attributable to SCN1A" — both readings are consistent with everything found, and neither is supported by any lung-specific evidence.

What would resolve the tie / raise confidence: (1) independent confirmation that the Ensembl "SCN1A-AS1" gene model is transcript-identical to the literature-described SCN1A-NAT/dsAS/usAS antisense RNAs; (2) any GTEx or lung-tissue-specific eQTL/expression data showing rs5027759 or its proxies affect SCN1A, SCN1A-AS1, or SCN9A expression specifically in lung, airway epithelium, or immune cells (none was found by the researcher); (3) direct confirmation (via full-text/supplementary-table access, which the researcher and citation-verifier could not obtain) of whether this locus is or is not a genome-wide-significant hit in the GSCAN smoking-initiation GWAS or in the Sakornsakolpat/Shrine COPD and lung-function GWAS — the one claim offered as supporting a smoking-behavior-mediated alternative explanation (GSCAN pathway enrichment of voltage-gated ion channel genes) was flagged CLAIM_MISMATCH by citation-verifier and should be treated as currently unsupported, not simply unresolved; (4) a GWAS Catalog/PhenoScanner check for rs5027759, which the researcher reports as null but which citation-verifier could not independently confirm before exhausting its search budget.

Alternative hypothesis explicitly noted: given the complete absence of any direct lung-structural, spirometric, or COPD-specific mechanistic evidence for any of the four candidate genes, and given this gene family's overwhelming established biology is CNS/PNS neuronal excitability, a genuinely live alternative explanation is that this locus's COPD association (if real) is mediated through a neurobiological/smoking-behavior pathway rather than direct pulmonary biology — but this alternative currently has no confirmed direct genetic evidence either (no locus-specific smoking-GWAS hit was found, and the one candidate supporting citation failed verification). A third possibility — that this is a false-positive or weak/borderline locus — cannot be excluded given the near-total absence of corroborating evidence across every domain searched (statistical genetics, pulmonary structural biology, and immune/inflammatory biology alike).
