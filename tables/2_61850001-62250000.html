Prioritized gene

COMMD1 — Confidence: LOW.

COMMD1 is the best-supported single pick at this locus, but the confidence ceiling is set by the weakest link identified by the red-team-skeptic, not by the volume of functional literature. The case for COMMD1 rests on three independent lines, each with material limitations: (1) Positional/fine-mapping: COMMD1 is the nearest gene and carries 4 of 5 credible-set variants (~92% of summed PIP), including the lead missense variant rs55677935 (PIP=0.579) — but the skeptic notes this variant's actual functional consequence (domain location, predicted deleteriousness) has not been characterized in any cited source; "missense + high PIP" is a reasonable prior, not direct functional evidence. (2) Open Targets L2G: COMMD1 is the consistent top (often sole) L2G gene across three independent CRP-level GWAS credible sets registered at this exact locus, clearly outscoring CCT4 and B3GNT2 in the one study where they appear at all — this is real evidence for "COMMD1 over its neighbors" as the physically/regulatorily implicated gene. But CRP is a nonspecific systemic acute-phase marker, and this pipeline's own COPD/FEV1 signal is not itself indexed in Open Targets at this locus — so the L2G evidence resolves which gene better than it resolves whether this is lung biology. (3) Functional literature: one paper (Besiktepe et al 2017) directly measures COMMD1 in human COPD/emphysema lung tissue (lower in emphysematous regions), which is genuinely the most disease-relevant citation at this locus, but it is correlational, cross-sectional, and does not test the genetic variant — it cannot separate COMMD1 loss as cause vs. consequence of tissue destruction. The remaining six COMMD1 citations (NF-κB, CFTR/ENaC, HIF-1α, DNA repair) are from cystic fibrosis or oncology models, not COPD, and one of them (Suraweera et al 2021, NSCLC) reports COMMD1 upregulated with worse prognosis — directionally inconsistent with the emphysema paper's finding of COMMD1 being lower in more-damaged tissue, undercutting any simple unified "COMMD1 dose" narrative across contexts.

Runner-up: B3GNT2 has genuinely strong, replicated, mechanistically-validated genetics — but for ankylosing spondylitis (AS), not COPD, via an NK-cell-specific immune axis mechanistically distinct from COPD's classically implicated neutrophil/CD8+T/Th17/ macrophage programs, and it sits ~200kb from the lead variant with zero assigned credible-set PIP, relying entirely on ABC (enhancer-promoter) linkage — a method with a non-trivial false-positive rate at this range. No LD check between the AS-associated B3GNT2 variants and the COMMD1 credible-set variants was performed or is available from current sources, so whether this is the same causal signal or an independent, coincidentally-adjacent AS locus in a gene-dense immune-active region of chr2p13-15 remains genuinely unresolved. CCT4 is the weakest candidate: no credible-set variant, no COPD/lung literature at all, high gnomAD constraint more consistent with general essentiality (core TRiC/CCT chaperonin subunit) than COPD-specific dosage sensitivity, and its sole functional citation is lung-cancer tumor immunology with no established bridge to COPD.

What would resolve this: (i) direct functional/computational characterization of rs55677935 (AlphaMissense/CADD, protein domain/structure context); (ii) an LD check between the B3GNT2/AS-associated variants and this locus's COPD credible-set variants, to determine whether B3GNT2's AS signal and this COPD signal are the same or independent; (iii) a COPD- or spirometry-specific GWAS/TWAS/MR/colocalization study directly naming any of these three genes (none currently exists in PubMed or Open Targets for any of the three); (iv) confirmation of whether smoking-initiation/heaviness GWAS overlap was checked for CCT4 and B3GNT2 specifically (only explicitly confirmed negative for COMMD1). Given the near-total absence of COPD-specific genetic or functional evidence for any gene at this locus, and the reliance on cross-disease inference throughout, LOW confidence is the appropriate ceiling — this is the best-supported call among three candidates with genuinely thin, non-COPD-specific support, not a well-evidenced COPD gene assignment.
