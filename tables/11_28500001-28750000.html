Prioritized gene

METTL15 — Confidence: LOW.

METTL15 is the only candidate gene identified at this locus by every prioritization method the internal pipeline ran (nearest gene, FLAMES); MAGMA, PoPS, and ABC did not prioritize any gene here, and no rival candidate gene or competing paper was identified in the 11:28.58-28.62 Mb credible-set interval (though red-team-skeptic notes an active search for alternative candidate genes at this interval was not completed this session due to search-budget exhaustion — this remains a flagged thoroughness gap, not a closed question). By default, then, METTL15 is the best-supported single gene to carry forward — but the supporting evidence is weak-to-moderate and does not resolve which biological pathway, if any, explains the locus's COPD association.

The molecular-function literature (10 VERIFIED papers) firmly establishes METTL15 as the mitochondrial 12S rRNA m4C methyltransferase required for mitoribosome small-subunit biogenesis and OXPHOS capacity — solid biology, but generic mitochondrial housekeeping function with no lung-specific expression emphasis, developmental role, or airway phenotype reported anywhere in the literature reviewed. The only in vivo knockout study (Averina et al. 2022, VERIFIED) found a comparatively mild, viable phenotype (muscle/metabolic/learning effects) with lung tissue apparently not assessed at all — an absence of evidence, not evidence of absence, per the red-team critique. gnomAD constraint data (Open Targets summary above) show METTL15 is LOF-tolerant, arguing against a classic haploinsufficiency mechanism.

The statistical-genetics case rests on: (1) an Open Targets L2G score of 0.52 for METTL15, derived from a different credible set (MVP PheCode-496 study, lead variant rs11030399) than this pipeline's own locus (lead variant rs10835362, ~40kb away) — plausibly, but not demonstrably, the same causal signal (red-team point b); and (2) three independently VERIFIED GWAS/TWAS papers (Kimbrel 2023 suicidality, Deng 2024 ADHD TWAS, Zhao 2025 ADHD imaging genetics) converging on a general neurobehavioral/psychiatric association for this locus, plus Open Targets colocalization data (not independently verified as a literature claim) showing comparably strong H4 for smoking-initiation/substance-use traits (H4 0.75-0.91) and for asthma/wheeze (H4 0.77-0.87). Per the red-team critique, this means the colocalization evidence does not clearly favor a smoking-mediated-non-pulmonary mechanism (Prior #1) over an airway-inflammatory/atopic mechanism (Prior #2/#3) — both are similarly well-supported by colocalization strength, and neither is confirmed by verified literature specific to this locus. No direct spirometric (FEV1/FEV1-FVC) colocalization or GWAS-locus confirmation was found, but per the red-team's critique this reflects an untested trait pair in the available data (and an inability to confirm locus membership in the foundational Shrine 2019 lung-function GWAS or Sakornsakolpat 2019 COPD GWAS from abstract-level search — both CLAIM_MISMATCH, see audit), not a confirmed negative finding.

Runner-up: none identified — no other gene was prioritized by the internal pipeline or proposed by any reviewed paper at this locus.

What would resolve this call: (1) a formal pairwise LD/colocalization check between this pipeline's own COPD credible set (lead rs10835362) and the MVP PheCode-496 credible set (lead rs11030399) to establish whether they represent the same causal signal, rather than assuming "same broad LD block" implies shared causality; (2) direct spirometric (FEV1, FEV1/FVC) GWAS lookup or colocalization at this locus, which is currently simply absent from the data reviewed; (3) lung/airway-tissue expression or phenotyping data for METTL15 (e.g., GTEx lung eQTL, airway-epithelial single-cell data, or a lung-specific conditional knockout), none of which was found in the literature reviewed; and (4) a targeted search for alternative candidate genes or regulatory targets in the 11:28.58-28.62 Mb interval, which was not completed this session. Given all of the above, METTL15 is named as the best-supported candidate at this locus by default (sole candidate, weak-to-moderate L2G, well-characterized but non-lung-specific molecular function), but confidence is marked LOW because the evidence does not establish whether — or through which pathway (neurobehavioral/smoking, airway-inflammatory, or mitochondrial-oxidative-stress) — METTL15 itself is mechanistically responsible for this locus's COPD association, as opposed to the locus's GWAS signal reflecting pleiotropic neurobehavioral/smoking-propensity biology with no specific pulmonary role for METTL15 at all.
