Lead variant: rs10962142 (9:15,756,096, intron_variant, PIP=0.038 — a low-PIP lead in a very flat, diffuse 384-variant credible set) Nearest gene: CCDC171 · MAGMA-prioritized: CCDC171 · PoPS-prioritized: CCDC171 · FLAMES-prioritized: PSIP1 · ABC-prioritized: PSIP1

There is no missense, splice-donor, or splice-acceptor variant anywhere in the credible set, so per this pipeline's scope rules, variant- level literature search was performed only for the lead variant rs10962142; all other evidence below is gene-level.

SNAPC3 (general RNA Pol II/III snRNA-transcription housekeeping complex subunit) has no prioritization support from any of the five methods and no disease-specific literature; it is not carried into the table below beyond this note. BNC2, mentioned only as a minor Open Targets L2G contributor (0.066) at one FEV1/FVC credible set, lies outside this exact locus window and was not treated as a primary candidate.

Open Targets summary (structured database lookup — not a verified literature citation)

This section did not go through citation-verifier or red-team-skeptic; it is a structured database summary, queried directly from the Open Targets Platform GraphQL API on 2026-09-11.

CCDC171 (ENSG00000164989):

  • L2G scores at every fine-mapped pulmonary-function GWAS credible set overlapping this locus consistently and strongly favor CCDC171: FEV1 (GCST90310160, GCST90565844) = 0.672 each; FEV1 (GCST90691840, GCST90692680, SuSiE fine-mapped) = 0.628 each; FEV1/FVC (GCST007080) = 0.540 (vs. BNC2 = 0.066, PSIP1 = 0.063 at the same credible set).
  • No Open Targets evidence record is directly tagged to COPD (MONDO_0005002) itself at this locus — all locus-specific genetic-association evidence found is tagged to FEV1/FEV1-FVC.
  • GTEx lung eQTL/exon-usage colocalization present (h4 ≈ 0.945), plus broad eQTL/sQTL colocalization across many other tissues (not lung-specific in breadth).
  • gnomAD constraint: NOT LoF-constrained (o/e ≈ 1.06, 90% CI 0.90–1.25) — tolerant of loss-of-function variation.
  • Overall (genome-wide, not locus-specific) Open Targets disease-association profile (76 total diseases): top hits are atrial fibrillation, "respiratory system disorder," risk-taking behaviour, shortness of breath/dyspnea, alcohol drinking, morbid obesity, dyslexia, several cancers, stroke, heart failure, and further down smoking initiation (score 0.183). COPD itself is absent from all 76 rows.

PSIP1 (ENSG00000164985):

  • L2G scores at the same pulmonary-function credible sets are minor/secondary throughout: 0.053 (FEV1 studies), 0.063 (FEV1/FVC study) — roughly 10x lower than CCDC171 at every one.
  • PSIP1's own cis-pQTL credible sets are NOT at this locus (its plasma-protein QTL hits are trans, on other chromosomes).
  • gnomAD constraint: STRONGLY LoF-constrained (o/e ≈ 0.23, 90% CI 0.14–0.40, pLI-like ≈ 1.0) — a general essentiality signal, not a disease-specificity signal.
  • Overall (genome-wide) Open Targets disease-association profile (3,262 total diseases —a much broader/more pleiotropic gene than CCDC171): dominated by HIV infectious disease and a long tail of somatic-mutation-driven cancer associations. No COPD/FEV1/FEV1-FVC signal appears among its broad top-40 rows.

SNAPC3: no Open Targets query performed (no prioritization support from any method; general housekeeping transcription-complex subunit with no plausible COPD mechanism).

Prioritized gene

CCDC171 — confidence: LOW.

CCDC171 is the best-supported single candidate at this locus, but the case is narrower than it first appears and should not be overstated. The strongest evidence is genuinely locus-specific and convergent: Open Targets' L2G scoring favors CCDC171 over PSIP1 by roughly 10-fold at every one of four independently fine-mapped FEV1/FEV1-FVC GWAS credible sets overlapping this region (a structured-database signal, not itself a verified literature citation, but consistent across independent datasets), and CCDC171 shows GTEx lung eQTL/exon-usage colocalization (h4 ≈ 0.945). Under this pipeline's priors, concordant pulmonary-function-trait evidence is treated as strong, disease-relevant support, and here it consistently and specifically favors CCDC171 over PSIP1 (whose own credible sets at this exact locus are minor/secondary, and whose only mouse-knockout structural data explicitly reports no pulmonary/airway phenotype — see the PSIP1 rows above).

However, per the red-team critique, the case for CCDC171 stops well short of an established mechanism. CCDC171 has zero dedicated primary functional/mechanistic literature — its entire evidentiary basis in the table above is either (a) statistical/regulatory prioritization (Open Targets L2G, eQTL colocalization — not literature), (b) incidental cross-trait GWAS/proteomics hits in unrelated traits (ADHD/risk-taking, BMI, CKD proteomics, chicken egg weight), or (c) a pathway-level (not gene-specific) thematic enrichment for ciliogenesis/centriole genes reported in the Shrine et al. 2019 lung-function GWAS — and citation-verifier could not confirm that CCDC171 (or PSIP1) is actually named as the candidate gene for this specific chr9:15.5–16.1 Mb signal in that paper's full signal list (Supplementary Table 3 was inaccessible). Describing CCDC171 as "a ciliary gene" would be overclaiming; the honest characterization is "statistically and regulatorily prioritized for a pulmonary-function mechanism at this locus, with the specific mechanism unknown."

A second complication, also raised by the red team, is that CCDC171's own broad (genome-wide, not locus-specific) Open Targets disease profile is topped by a behavioral/metabolic-impulsivity cluster (atrial fibrillation, risk-taking behaviour, alcohol drinking, morbid obesity, dyslexia) with only a weak smoking-initiation signal (0.183) — and COPD itself does not appear at all among CCDC171's 76 genome-wide disease associations. This is independently corroborated by real (verified) literature: CCDC171 shows TWAS-level association with ADHD and risk-taking behavior, and independent GWAS associations with BMI (both via gene-diet interaction and a functionally validated 3'UTR miRNA-binding variant). Per Prior #1 (smoking-mediated effects), this raises a genuine, unresolved alternative hypothesis: some or all of this locus's COPD association could be mediated through smoking-related behavior/impulsivity biology rather than (or in addition to) direct pulmonary-structural biology. The evidence assembled here cannot distinguish between "CCDC171 acts on lung function directly" and "CCDC171/this locus is a behavioral-pleiotropy locus that elevates COPD risk via smoking" — both remain live.

Runner-up: PSIP1, favored by the FLAMES and ABC prioritization methods, but with converging evidence against a pulmonary role at this locus specifically: minor/secondary L2G scores at every FEV1/FEV1-FVC credible set here, no COPD/lung-function literature naming it as causal anywhere, and a knockout-mouse study that explicitly found no pulmonary phenotype despite extensive phenotyping. Its one directly-relevant piece of primary literature (French et al. 2016) does show this locus's regulatory architecture is genuinely capable of driving PSIP1 expression via a shared enhancer — but the demonstrated functional context is ovarian cancer cell lines, not lung, and the implicated variant differs from this locus's COPD lead variant.

What would resolve the tie: (1) direct confirmation (e.g., by checking Shrine et al. 2019's Supplementary Table 3, or a follow-up lung-function/COPD fine-mapping paper) of which gene, if either, is explicitly named at this exact signal; (2) lung/airway-specific single-cell or bulk-tissue eQTL/functional data for CCDC171 (currently only broad, multi-tissue, non-lung-specific colocalization is available, aside from one GTEx lung exon-usage credible set); (3) a direct test of whether this locus reaches genome-wide significance in a primary smoking-initiation/cigarettes- per-day GWAS (GSCAN), which was not established here — only unrelated downstream PRS-application papers were found; (4) any primary functional study of CCDC171 in airway epithelium, cilia, or lung development, which does not currently exist in PubMed. Given the combination of a functionally uncharacterized top candidate, an unresolved dual-mechanism (behavioral vs. pulmonary) possibility, and an unconfirmed gene-naming link to the one relevant landmark GWAS paper, confidence is marked LOW rather than MODERATE or HIGH despite CCDC171's clear numerical edge over PSIP1 in the locus-specific statistical evidence.
