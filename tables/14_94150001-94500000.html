Prioritized gene

SERPINA1 — confidence: HIGH.

SERPINA1 is the clear, well-supported causal gene at this locus. The evidence base is unusually strong for a GWAS locus: (1) the sole credible-set variant, rs28929474 (PIP=0.961), is a missense variant sitting directly in SERPINA1's own coding sequence and is independently and unambiguously identified across multiple verified sources as the PiZ (Glu342Lys) allele, the classic cause of alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency; (2) the causal mechanism (loop-sheet polymerization, ER retention in hepatocytes, reduced circulating AAT, unopposed neutrophil elastase activity in the lung) is established by decades of biochemistry (Lomas 1992) and an animal model using the identical amino-acid substitution (Zhang 2019); (3) uniquely for this pipeline, there is interventional/causal evidence — the RAPID RCT (Chapman 2015) showed that restoring circulating AAT via augmentation therapy slows emphysema progression, directly supporting the protease-antiprotease mechanism rather than relying on observational association alone; (4) fine-mapping literature (Thun et al. 2013) independently supports the rare PiS/Pi*Z variant class — not a broader common-SNP LD block — as the causal driver at this locus, addressing the concern that credible-set/GWAS signals can reflect synthetic associations; (5) Open Targets' structured L2G evidence is near-maximal (~0.98-0.99) across 9 independent COPD evidence rows, corroborating the literature-based case.

The alternative candidate, PRIMA1 (PoPS-prioritized), is confidently excluded: it lies ~590 kb outside the stated locus window (confirmed via two independent structured lookups), has an exclusively CNS/neuromuscular-junction cholinergic gene function with no pulmonary or immune literature identified across an actively adversarial search, and has zero Open Targets COPD/GWAS evidence. The red-team-skeptic concurred this is a legitimate absence-of-evidence finding (not merely an unsearched gap) and treated PRIMA1's PoPS prioritization itself as the anomaly requiring explanation (a likely PoPS pathway-similarity false-positive) rather than as competing evidence against SERPINA1.

One important caveat that tempers claims about the underlying GWAS association's population-level mechanism (though it does not change the gene assignment): the case-control COPD/emphysema literature is dominated by studies framed around rare ZZ homozygotes, whereas the GWAS credible-set signal is a population allele-dosage association almost certainly driven predominantly by the much more common Z-allele heterozygotes (PiMZ). At the heterozygote level, spirometric-trait concordance is genuinely contested: some studies support increased COPD/spirometric risk in PiMZ, especially smokers (Molloy 2014), but a large population-based cohort found no main effect (Thun 2012 SAPALDIA) and a very large multi-cohort meta-analysis found the opposite direction of effect on FEV1/FVC (North 2016). This heterogeneity does not undermine SERPINA1 as the causal gene — the molecular mechanism at the ZZ/rare-variant level is undisputed and interventionally validated — but the synthesis should not overstate that the GWAS association itself has been shown to act uniformly through reduced spirometric function at the population (heterozygote) level. Confidence in the gene call remains HIGH; confidence in a single unified mechanistic narrative for the population-level GWAS beta specifically is more moderate, and this nuance is preserved in the audit appendix.
