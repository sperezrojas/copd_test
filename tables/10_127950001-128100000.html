Prioritized gene

PTPRE — Confidence: LOW.

PTPRE is the only candidate gene at this locus (sole gene overlapping the credible-set window; the single credible-set variant, rs10829316, PIP=0.947, is intronic to PTPRE, and red-team-skeptic independently confirmed no other annotated gene falls within the region). In that narrow sense, gene assignment is unambiguous — there is no competing paralog or neighboring gene in tight LD to weigh against it, which is a genuine point in favor of treating PTPRE as the locus's physically-implicated gene.

However, the evidence that PTPRE acts on COPD through any specific, demonstrated biological mechanism is weak, and the strongest, most reproducible signal in the entire evidence base actively points away from a pulmonary mechanism:

  • There is no direct PTPRE-COPD genetic association in primary literature, and Open Targets' own database confirms this (COPD absent from PTPRE's 406-disease association list; the sole COPD-linked record is a score-0.02 text-mining co-mention, not genetic evidence).
  • There is no FEV1/FEV1-FVC association for PTPRE in literature or in Open Targets (zero evidence records), which per this pipeline's own prior on spirometry concordance warrants real scrutiny of alternative pathways.
  • No lung-tissue eQTL/sQTL colocalization for rs10829316-PTPRE was found or reported — the intronic, high-PIP variant's regulatory mechanism (if any) in lung tissue is undemonstrated.
  • No structural/developmental lung biology exists for PTPRE at all.
  • The only COPD-adjacent phenotype associations (allergic/atopic asthma, Tremblay 2008 and Murk 2011) are both contested — one non-replicated, one nullified by covariate adjustment — and should not be counted as positive support.
  • Genuine immune/myeloid-lineage mechanistic biology exists (JAK-STAT regulation in myeloid leukemia cell lines; osteoclast/bone-resorption biology; neutrophil-enriched expression per Human Protein Atlas, a database source, not independently verified) — plausibly consistent with prior #3's emphasis on innate-immune/neutrophilic COPD programs, but none of it has been tested in lung, airway, or COPD models, so it remains suggestive background biology rather than demonstrated lung-relevant mechanism.
  • The single most consistent, most reproducible independent genetic signal for PTPRE across every trait searched is not pulmonary or even confirmed smoking-behavioral — it is neurodevelopmental: TWAS association with autism spectrum disorder at brain tissue (Rodriguez-Fontenla & Carracedo 2021), brain-restricted eQTL colocalization with the ASD GWAS signal at r=0.97 (Dominguez-Alonso et al. 2023), and differential PTPRE expression across schizophrenia progression stages in prefrontal cortex (Narayan et al. 2008). Three independent CNS-focused findings converging on brain-specific PTPRE regulation constitute real contesting evidence against confidently assigning this locus a lung-causal mechanism, even though none of them directly tests or refutes COPD.
  • Open Targets' strongest genetic-association signal for PTPRE (smoking initiation, score ~0.10) is directionally consistent with this pipeline's prior #1 (smoking-mediated effects) as an alternative to direct pulmonary biology — but this specific link could not be confirmed by name in the accessible text of the three major smoking-behavior GWAS papers checked by both the researcher and red-team-skeptic, so it remains an unconfirmed lead rather than established evidence.

Red-team-skeptic's overall verdict for PTPRE at this locus is CONTESTED: the case for PTPRE as causal rests almost entirely on positional exclusivity (sole overlapping gene, high credible-set PIP) rather than on any demonstrated COPD-relevant mechanism, and the strongest competing/alternative-hypothesis evidence (CNS/neurodevelopmental regulation, and a plausible but unconfirmed smoking-behavior link) is at least as strong as anything pointing toward direct pulmonary or COPD-specific immune biology.

There is no credible competing gene to name as a runner-up at this locus — PTPRE is positionally unambiguous. The uncertainty here is not "which gene" but "what mechanism, if any, links this gene to COPD." What would resolve this: (1) lung-tissue eQTL/sQTL colocalization data for rs10829316 (e.g., GTEx lung, or airway-epithelium/immune-cell single-cell eQTL resources) to establish whether this variant actually regulates PTPRE expression in a COPD-relevant tissue; (2) direct confirmation (or refutation) of whether PTPRE is named as an implicated gene in the supplementary tables of the major smoking-behavior GWAS papers (Saunders et al. 2022 in particular, given its fine-mapping); (3) any COPD- or lung-specific functional study of PTPRE (knockout/knockdown in airway epithelial or lung immune cells), which does not currently exist in the literature searched. Given all of this, confidence is marked LOW rather than MODERATE or HIGH — this reflects genuine uncertainty about mechanism, not a data-collection failure; the researcher's and skeptic's searches were thorough and the absence of pulmonary evidence is a real, reported finding rather than a gap in search effort.
