Prioritized gene

ADGRB2 — confidence: LOW

ADGRB2 is the best-supported single candidate at this locus on fine-mapping grounds alone: it carries the highest-PIP credible-set variants (rs1889571 PIP=0.33 plus several more, all ADGRB2 intronic), is the nearest gene, and has the higher locus-to-gene (L2G) score at the large majority of the 45 GWAS/QTL credible sets that colocalize at rs1889571 in Open Targets (Open Targets summary table above). Its established literature biology (Shiratsuchi 1997, Kee 2002, Meyer 2025, Speca 2017, Purcell 2017, Alsharif & Bhatnagar 2025 — all HOLDS in the evidence table) is exclusively and consistently brain-restricted: synapse/dendritic-spine development, brain angiogenesis modulation, and a single reported human disease phenotype (progressive spastic paraparesis) that is neurological, not pulmonary. There is a complete absence of lung, airway, or COPD-relevant biology for ADGRB2 in PubMed, and Open Targets records zero association evidence (of any kind) between ADGRB2 and COPD specifically.

The case for prioritizing ADGRB2 over COL16A1 and FABP3 despite this absence of direct pulmonary biology rests on pipeline prior #1 (smoking-mediated pathway): the same lead variant colocalizes at high confidence (H4 0.81-0.98) with smoking initiation, smoking status, age of smoking initiation, and self-reported wheeze across many cohorts — but NOT with COPD case-control status or any spirometric trait (FEV1/FVC) in any of 45 tested credible sets. This is consistent with the locus acting on COPD risk indirectly, through a neurobehavioral pathway affecting smoking, rather than through direct lung structural or immune biology.

However, per red-team-skeptic's critique (see audit), this conclusion should be held with real caution and is capped at LOW confidence for two specific reasons: (1) the one literature citation that could have anchored the "smoking" interpretation in primary literature — the GSCAN tobacco/alcohol GWAS (Liu et al. 2019) — was verified as a real, non-retracted paper but citation-verifier could NOT confirm that this exact 1p31.1/ADGRB2 locus is actually among its 406 reported genome-wide-significant loci; the "smoking pathway" narrative for this locus currently rests entirely on the Open Targets colocalization data, which is a structured database lookup, not a verified literature claim. (2) The full pattern of colocalizing traits (smoking, wheeze, ADHD, substance abuse, insomnia, educational attainment, adiposity, externalizing behavior) is at least as consistent with a broad behavioral/impulsivity-pleiotropy locus as with a clean, smoking-specific mechanism — smoking-initiation may be only one of several correlated downstream manifestations of a shared reward/impulse-control pathway, not necessarily the operative link to COPD risk at all.

Runner-up: COL16A1 has a smaller, coherent secondary body of L2G support at the same locus (higher L2G than ADGRB2 at several individual credible sets, e.g., insomnia, one smoking-status cohort) and a plausible-in-principle ECM/fibrosis biology class (from non-pulmonary tissues: gut myofibroblasts/Crohn's, glioblastoma, skin), but no gene-specific lung/airway literature and a disease-association profile that substantially overlaps with ADGRB2's — consistent with COL16A1 largely riding the same LD/credible-set signal rather than being an independently causal gene. Its ABC-prioritization status could not be weighted more heavily here because the cell type/tissue underlying that ABC call was not available in this lookup — an open question that, if resolved to a lung-relevant tissue, could raise COL16A1's standing. FABP3, despite its PoPS nomination, has no locus-specific genetic corroboration (no credible-set variant, no L2G/coloc support at all) and no lung-relevant functional literature — it is not a serious contender at this locus based on current evidence.

What would resolve the tie/uncertainty: (a) direct confirmation (e.g., from GWAS Catalog or GSCAN supplementary tables) of whether this exact locus is a reported smoking-initiation/tobacco-use hit; (b) an FEV1/FVC or COPD-specific GWAS colocalization at this variant, which does not currently exist in Open Targets; (c) lung-tissue-specific eQTL/sQTL colocalization for ADGRB2, COL16A1, or FABP3 (the only eQTL credible sets found at this variant were thyroid, not lung); (d) clarification of which cell type/tissue the ABC model was run on for the COL16A1 call.
