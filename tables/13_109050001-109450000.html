Prioritized gene

MYO16 — confidence: LOW.

This call is close, and the evidence explicitly does not cleanly resolve between MYO16 and its overlapping antisense lncRNA MYO16-AS1 — red-team- skeptic marked MYO16 CONTESTED and MYO16-AS1 WEAKENED (i.e., a real but thin and non-COPD-specific alternative), and that verdict is carried forward here rather than overridden.

Why MYO16 is still the lead pick: it is the nearest gene to the lead variant, the sole protein-coding gene overlapping/flanking this locus (an independent Ensembl gene-region check turned up only lncRNAs otherwise), and is independently flagged by FLAMES, MAGMA, and PoPS (per the locus input file) as well as by a moderate Open Targets L2G score (0.381) tied to strongly replicated lung-function (FEV1/FVC, FVC) colocalization across six GWAS/biobanks (Open Targets summary above) — concordance across COPD-adjacent spirometric traits that prior #2 treats as meaningful support for genuine pulmonary biology. No smoking-behavior GWAS colocalization was found at this locus in the structured lookup, and no PubMed-indexed literature links MYO16 to smoking-initiation/nicotine traits, which argues against prior #1 explaining this signal (though red-team-skeptic correctly notes this is an absence-of-evidence argument, not a completed search of smoking-GWAS summary statistics, and flags that MYO16's own neuronal/presynaptic biology is exactly the gene class that recurrently appears in smoking-behavior GWAS — this alternative hypothesis has not been ruled out and is a specific, concrete gap for follow-up).

Why confidence is capped at LOW, not MODERATE or HIGH: (1) the FLAMES/MAGMA/PoPS/L2G convergence on MYO16 is not fully independent evidence — per red-team-skeptic, these tools share a structural prior favoring proximity and protein-coding status over lncRNAs, and the L2G algorithm's ~0 weighting of MYO16-AS1 despite MYO16-AS1's own strong, real eQTL colocalization reflects that same bias rather than a settled adjudication. (2) The Shrine et al. 2019 lung-function GWAS citation (PMID 30804560) is VERIFIED only as locus-level evidence — it does not itself name MYO16 in the accessible text, so citing it as "evidence implicating MYO16" would overstate what the paper shows (see audit). (3) MYO16's own established literature (rows above: PMID 32824179, 32451869, 31474830, 28485726, 23596177, 11588169) is essentially all neuronal/cytoskeletal/neuropsychiatric, with zero structural-lung-biology, airway-epithelium, or alveolar-remodeling literature despite this locus being genome-wide significant for lung function since at least 2019 — a gap red-team-skeptic argues is informative in its own right, not merely "under-studied." (4) The only COPD-specific literature for MYO16 (PMID 41137279, PMID 38933922) is weak, non-validated, exploratory bioinformatic/ML gene-list evidence that both red-team-skeptic and the researcher flag as hypothesis-generating only, with a real risk of pseudo-replication (same low-specificity study design in both papers). (5) MYO16-AS1, the runner-up, has its own real (if COPD-non-specific) lung-tissue functional literature — a demonstrated molecular mechanism in lung adenocarcinoma (PMID 38041756, which explicitly states MYO16-AS1 "is expressed mainly in lung tissue") and dysregulation in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis co-expressed with inflammatory-pathway genes (PMID 40782499) — that MYO16 itself entirely lacks, which is genuine contradicting/competing evidence, not just a caveat.

Runner-up: MYO16-AS1 (antisense lncRNA overlapping MYO16). Favored by: real cis-eQTL colocalization with the same lung-function GWAS signal in blood/neutrophils, and independent (non-COPD) lung-tissue functional literature that MYO16 itself lacks. Disfavored by: ~0 Open Targets L2G weight, no COPD-specific literature at all, and its supporting literature being oncology/IPF context rather than COPD or airway obstruction directly.

What would resolve the tie: (a) COPD case-control (not just lung-function trait) colocalization or fine-mapping directly implicating one gene over the other; (b) cell-type-resolved expression/functional data for MYO16 or MYO16-AS1 specifically in airway epithelium, ciliated cells, or airway/alveolar smooth muscle — the tissue contexts most relevant to prior #2 and #3 that neither gene currently has; (c) a direct check of GSCAN/smoking- initiation and nicotine-dependence GWAS summary statistics at this credible set, given MYO16's neuronal/presynaptic biology plausibly fits a smoking-behavior-mediation model (prior #1) that has not been formally ruled out, only found absent in the searches performed to date; and (d) a mechanistic explanation (or dismissal as an assay artifact) for the unexplained trans-pQTL colocalization with AGER and CEACAM5. This is the study team's call to make with that additional evidence in hand — this section is a best-supported recommendation, not a final determination.
