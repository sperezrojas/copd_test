Prioritized gene

Prioritized gene: RXRA. Confidence: LOW.

RXRA is the best-supported single candidate at this locus, but the call is close and rests on a genuine tension that the evidence does not resolve. On one side: RXRA has strong, independent, decades-old mechanistic literature establishing that RAR/RXR heterodimer signaling — and RXRA allele dosage specifically (McGowan et al. 2000, PMID 10919981, a compound RARγ-null; RXRA+/- mouse) — is required for normal postnatal alveolar septation and elastin-dependent alveolar architecture, a textbook structural mechanism directly relevant to COPD/emphysema pathogenesis. RXRA is also the only gene at this locus with any positive biological narrative at all (PRRC2B and POMT1 both "HOLD" as essentially artifactual per the red-team-skeptic — see Open Targets summary and audit). RXRA is also strongly pLoF-constrained, consistent with being a dosage-sensitive gene of the kind common variant regulatory signals can plausibly perturb.

On the other side, and why confidence is capped at LOW rather than higher: the red-team-skeptic explicitly marked RXRA CONTESTED as the causal gene for this specific COPD GWAS signal (see audits/9_134250001-134450000_audit.md for full reasoning). The Open Targets structured lookup shows a complete absence of any COPD or FEV1/FEV1-FVC credible-set or L2G evidence at this exact locus, despite the same variants being confidently (L2G 0.65-0.83) linked to RXRA for 19 other, non-pulmonary traits (hematologic, endocrine, anthropometric) — and despite large, well-powered lung-function/COPD colocalization studies (Shrine et al. 2019; Sakornsakolpat et al. 2018; Boueiz et al. 2019) that successfully find colocalized causal genes at other loci not finding one here. Most directly, Jolliffe et al. 2017 (PMID 28161533) genotyped a credible-set variant (rs10881578) in 278 COPD patients and found a null result for FEV1, FEV1/FVC, COPD severity, and sputum inflammatory-cell counts — a direct, targeted contradiction of a lung-specific effect for this exact signal. The only mechanistic papers proposing a direct RXRA-COPD lung axis (Zhang et al. 2025/2024, RXRA-PLA2G2A) come from a single group in journals flagged for quality-control concerns and are unreplicated; one of the two is not even a lung phenotype. The skeptic also raised a live, arguably more parsimonious alternative hypothesis: this locus's genetic signal may act on hematologic, endocrine, or body-composition biology, with the COPD case-control association arising as a pleiotropic/confounded echo of that biology rather than a direct pulmonary mechanism — a hypothesis this evidence base cannot rule out. Smoking-mediation could also not be excluded (GSCAN smoking-GWAS loci were not confirmed to overlap or not overlap this locus).

Runner-up: No other candidate gene at this locus has any positive evidence — PRRC2B and POMT1 both hold as very likely statistical/positional artifacts of MAGMA/PoPS respectively, with no genetic, structural, or immune literature connecting either to lung or COPD biology. The nearest thing to a genuine alternative is VAV2, which appeared as a minor secondary L2G hit (score ~0.05) in two Open Targets credible sets at this locus but was never independently searched or evaluated by either the researcher or the red-team-skeptic (WebSearch budget was exhausted before the skeptic could attempt this) — this is a real gap, not a ruled-out alternative.

What would resolve the tie/raise confidence: (1) a direct COPD-specific or FEV1/FEV1-FVC-specific colocalization or fine-mapping analysis at this exact locus (not currently available in Open Targets or in the literature reviewed); (2) independent replication of the RXRA-PLA2G2A axis in cigarette smoke-exposed lung tissue outside the single reporting group; (3) a targeted search of VAV2's biology and any GWAS/colocalization support at this locus; (4) direct confirmation of whether this locus overlaps a genome-wide significant smoking-behavior (GSCAN) signal. Absent that additional evidence, this is the pipeline's best-supported recommendation, not a high-confidence causal-gene call, and the study team should weigh the CONTESTED status accordingly.
