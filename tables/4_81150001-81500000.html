Prioritized gene

Gene: PRKG2 — Confidence: LOW

This is a close, unresolved call and should be read as such. None of the three candidates (PRKG2, RASGEF1B, C4orf22/CFAP299) has any genetic evidence (GWAS, colocalization, Mendelian randomization, or TWAS) directly linking it to COPD, lung function, or smoking-behavior traits — every targeted PubMed search for these connections returned zero hits for all three genes, the GWAS Catalog has no catalogued record for the lead variant rs6831114, and Open Targets shows a hard zero for curated COPD evidence for both PRKG2 and CFAP299. The credible set itself is diffuse (lead variant PIP=0.186, entirely intergenic/MODIFIER, no missense/splice variant anywhere), and the internal prioritization methods disagree with each other (FLAMES favors PRKG2, PoPS favors CFAP299, MAGMA/ABC report nothing) — itself a sign of genuine locus-level ambiguity rather than convergent support.

I still name PRKG2 as the best-supported pick, with LOW confidence, for three reasons that fall short of a strong case but exceed the alternatives: (1) it is the nearest gene to the lead variant and the only candidate with a (very-low-PIP, PIP=0.0014) credible-set variant physically inside it (rs3114135); (2) it has genuine, verified, mechanistically specific alveolar/airway epithelial biology — AT2 cell fate and alveolar regeneration (PMID 35313961) and airway epithelial ENaC/fluid-clearance regulation (PMID 19359370) — that, while not COPD-disease-modeled and correctly labeled WEAKENED rather than strong evidence by the red-team-skeptic, is at least in the correct organ and cell-type domain (alveolar/ airway epithelium) rather than an unrelated tissue; (3) its constraint profile shows moderate missense and LoF depletion (oe 0.335–0.685), consistent with (though not proof of) functional importance. Against this: the red-team-skeptic correctly flagged that this epithelial-biology evidence is being asked to substitute for entirely absent genetic evidence, and that PRKG2's abundant immune-cell-eQTL landscape in Open Targets is QTL-breadth only, with zero mechanistic literature connecting it to immune function or disease — an open question, not supporting evidence.

Runner-up: C4orf22/CFAP299 (PoPS-prioritized) is explicitly weaker: the red-team-skeptic marked it CONTESTED because its only two verified citations are, on inspection, unfavorable — one shows this exact gene was considered and rejected as the causal gene in an unrelated GWAS gene-cluster context (PMID 24739539), and the other shows its actual characterized function (testis/germ-cell apoptosis regulation) directly contradicts the motile-cilia/PCD-family hypothesis its gene name would suggest for lung relevance (PMID 30820741). RASGEF1B is not flagged by any internal prioritization method and rests on weak positional overlap with a wide locus window plus a real but disease-nonspecific TLR/macrophage inflammatory mechanism (also marked WEAKENED, bordering on "should not be treated as a peer candidate").

What would resolve this tie: direct COPD or FEV1/FEV1-FVC GWAS/coloc evidence for any of the three genes (e.g., a published lung-function GWAS colocalization at this exact locus, or an eQTL/pQTL colocalization in lung tissue specifically — notably absent from Open Targets for both genes queried); a cigarette-smoke or elastase disease model showing a PRKG2-dependent emphysema-relevant phenotype (rather than the current baseline-organoid and non-disease ENaC data); or fine-mapping/functional evidence narrowing which gene(s) the lead variant rs6831114 and its credible set actually regulate (e.g., a lung-tissue promoter-capture Hi-C or CRISPRi enhancer screen at this locus). Absent any of that, this call should be treated as provisional and low-confidence, not a settled gene assignment, and the study team may reasonably weigh PRKG2 and CFAP299 as roughly comparable long-shots rather than accept PRKG2 as clearly favored.
