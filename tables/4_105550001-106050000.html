  • Disease association score vs. COPD (MONDO_0005002): 0.558 overall (genetic_association subscore 0.904; literature subscore 0.269).
  • 13 gwas_credible_sets (L2G-based) evidence records link NPNT to COPD: scores 0.947 (x11), 0.942, 0.814 — consistently high-confidence L2G gene assignment across apparently multiple underlying COPD GWAS credible sets. Variant/study/effect-size detail fields were null in the API response for this datasource (a data-completeness gap in what Open Targets exposes via this query path, not an omission by this review).
  • Also strongly associated with asthma (score 0.544, genetic_association 0.893) and "Chronic Obstructive Asthma" (score 0.360), and more broadly with "lower respiratory tract disorder" (0.442) and "respiratory system disorder" (0.440) — a broad obstructive/respiratory signal, not COPD-specific.
  • Also shows substantial genetic_association-driven scores for several apparently unrelated traits: hypertension/essential hypertension, major depressive disorder, glaucoma, atrial fibrillation, information processing speed, and renal agenesis — indicating regional/gene-level pleiotropy in GWAS-catalog-derived data. Per red-team critique, most of this is plausibly generic LD-block pleiotropy, but the renal-agenesis association is biologically coherent with NPNT-ITGA8's established role in kidney branching morphogenesis and should be flagged as an open question about allelic heterogeneity (same variant vs. a different NPNT variant) rather than dismissed.
  • Pulmonary fibrosis (MONDO_0002771) association score is low (0.078) and driven ENTIRELY by literature/text-mining (0.638), with NO genetic_association contribution — i.e., a literature-only link, not a genetic one; consistent with the IPF/fibrosis citations in the table above being mechanistic-context papers, not genetic evidence for a COPD (or any) genetic association.
  • Genetic constraint (gnomAD, target-level, not disease-specific): NPNT is NOT constrained against loss-of-function variation (LOF constraint score ~4.94e-9, oe = 0.712, 90% CI 0.543-0.944) and is roughly neutral for missense constraint (oe = 0.936). This argues against a classic haploinsufficiency mechanism and is more consistent with a regulatory/splicing-modulation mechanism, as reported in the Saferali et al. sQTL studies above.

Prioritized gene

Prioritized gene: NPNT Confidence: MODERATE

NPNT is the best-supported candidate gene at this locus, but the evidence stops short of definitive proof, so confidence is capped at MODERATE rather than HIGH.

Supporting the call: NPNT is the sole pipeline-nominated candidate (nearest gene, sole credible-set gene) and the credible-set variant rs34712979 (PIP=0.9988) sits within its annotated transcript with a splice-region consequence. This is independently corroborated by (1) robust, multi-cohort GWAS replication for lung function/COPD at this general locus (Hancock 2010; Wain 2015; Shrine 2019), (2) an unusually direct, variant-specific mechanistic story — rs34712979 creates a cryptic splice acceptor altering NPNT exon 3 usage, and this sQTL colocalizes with COPD/spirometry GWAS signals at 94% posterior probability, with AlphaFold modeling suggesting disruption of an EGF-like protein domain (Saferali et al., preprint PMID 33173926 and published PMID 39978861), and (3) very high, consistent Open Targets L2G scores (0.81-0.95 across 13 credible-set records) for NPNT-COPD specifically. The gnomAD constraint profile (LOF-tolerant, not haploinsufficient) is coherent with a splicing/regulatory rather than complete-loss-of-function mechanism, matching the Saferali mechanism exactly. Prior #1 (smoking-mediation) evidence argues against a smoking-behavior explanation: Wain et al. 2015 found this signal generalizes to never-smokers and is genetically distinct from smoking-behavior loci in the same dataset.

Weighing against higher confidence, per the red-team critique (WEAKENED verdict): the mechanistic case rests on one research group's own preprint-to-publication work using substantially the same dataset (not independent replication of the mechanism, even though the underlying locus-COPD GWAS association is independently replicated across cohorts), and the structural/functional claim is computationally (AlphaFold) rather than experimentally validated. Sakornsakolpat et al. 2018 (PMID 29566699) is a genuine, only partially explained non-replication — a severe-COPD/emphysema TWAS/colocalization study that did not flag NPNT among its confirmed genes. The neighboring genes GSTCD and INTS12 have a documented history as alternative candidates for the broader 4q24 lung-function locus (Obeidat 2013; Kheirallah 2017), and while rs34712979's NPNT-specific splicing evidence is a strong distinguishing argument, no negative eQTL/sQTL control ruling out INTS12 at this exact variant was reported.

Runner-up: INTS12 (favored over GSTCD by lung eQTL evidence for the original, broader 4q24 signal — Obeidat 2013, Kheirallah 2017 — though this evidence concerns the original lead signal, not rs34712979 specifically).

What would resolve the remaining uncertainty: (1) independent replication of the rs34712979-NPNT splicing/colocalization result by a group other than Saferali/Castaldi and colleagues, ideally with orthogonal wet-lab validation (minigene splicing reporter assay, or a knock-in model) rather than AlphaFold modeling alone; (2) a direct eQTL/sQTL test of rs34712979 against INTS12/GSTCD/ARHGEF38/TBCK expression in the same lung datasets, to formally rule out allelic heterogeneity with the neighboring genes; (3) reconciliation of why Sakornsakolpat et al.'s severe-COPD/emphysema TWAS did not detect NPNT — ideally a reanalysis testing isoform-usage (not just total-expression) colocalization in an independent severe-COPD cohort.
