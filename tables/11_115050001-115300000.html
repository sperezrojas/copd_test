Prioritized gene

CADM1 — Confidence: LOW

CADM1 is named here as the best-supported candidate gene at this locus, but with a confidence level of LOW, for these reasons:

  1. Uniqueness in the interval, not competitive strength, drives the pick. CADM1 is the only gene physically present across nearly the entire ~1.1 Mb locus window, and all four prioritization methods (nearest-gene, FLAMES, MAGMA, PoPS) converge on it, with no competing gene body to weigh against. The Open Targets L2G analysis independently and consistently nominates CADM1 as the dominant gene at the lead variant across 22 unrelated GWAS traits (L2G 0.50-0.72). There is no serious alternative-gene hypothesis to consider here — this is a genuinely different situation from typical LD-ambiguous loci with two or more plausible genes.
  2. But the evidence tying CADM1 specifically to COPD or lung function is weak. Open Targets shows zero genetic-association evidence for COPD and zero evidence of any kind for FEV1 or FEV1/FVC. No PubMed-indexed COPD or spirometry GWAS paper names CADM1. The mechanistic literature (rows 1-5 in the evidence table above) is real and internally consistent but, per red-team-skeptic, rests on an unreplicated single-research-group hypothesis (human tissue n=4 in one key experiment) studying post-translational ectodomain shedding in already-diseased tissue — a mechanism that has never been connected back to the germline noncoding credible-set variants themselves, to CADM1 expression/eQTL direction, or to any population-scale genetic association. This is the central, unresolved gap: "CADM1 protein biology plausibly contributes to emphysema pathology once smoke-exposed" is not the same claim as "the common noncoding GWAS variants in this credible set act through CADM1 to cause COPD," and no evidence in this dossier bridges that gap.
  3. The smoking-mediation hypothesis (prior #1) is also not well supported on close inspection. Open Targets shows a smoking-initiation genetic-association signal for CADM1, but citation-verifier's direct check of the largest smoking-initiation GWAS (Xu et al. 2020, N=842,717) found CADM1/rs3793957 is NOT among its 99 confirmed smoking-initiation loci or 13 cessation loci — a substantive negative finding, not just an open question. So this locus does not have a clean story under either the "intrinsic pulmonary biology" framework (prior #2) or the "smoking-behavior-mediated" framework (prior #1) at the level of human statistical genetics; the strongest positive evidence anywhere in this dossier is cell/mouse mechanistic biology, not human genetics of any kind.
  4. Immune evidence is present but should be weighted as tangential, per red-team-skeptic: general lymphocyte-adhesion biology (not lung-specific), a skin-autoimmunity mouse model offered only as an analogy, and a fatal-asthma (not COPD) tissue study where the specific coculture-upregulated gene was the paralog CADM4, not CADM1 itself.

What would resolve this and raise confidence: (a) independent replication of the CADM1 ectodomain-shedding/emphysema mechanism by a group other than Ito/Hagiyama/Mimae/Ri et al., ideally in a larger, non-Japan-only cohort; (b) a CADM1 lung-tissue eQTL or splice-QTL colocalizing with the credible-set variants (none was identified in this search, and Open Targets shows no COPD/lung-function credible set overlapping this variant at all); (c) direct confirmation (or refutation) of whether CADM1/rs3793957 is a genome-wide-significant hit in the largest smoking-initiation/cessation GWAS supplementary tables (spot-checked negative for the one paper directly reviewed; not exhaustively checked against all consortium papers' full loci lists). Because there is no competing candidate gene at this locus, "runner-up" in the usual LD-ambiguity sense does not apply — the open question is not "CADM1 vs. gene X" but "is CADM1's role here established, or is it simply the only gene available to blame."
