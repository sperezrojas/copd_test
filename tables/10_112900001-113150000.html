Prioritized gene

TCF7L2 — Confidence: LOW.

TCF7L2 is named as the prioritized gene for this locus by default, since it is the only protein-coding gene overlapping the region (all 12 credible-set variants are intronic to TCF7L2; the nearest neighboring protein-coding gene, VTI1A, lies ~80 kb outside the locus with no credible-set variant nearby). However, this naming should not be read as an endorsement of a genuine pulmonary or COPD-specific mechanism for TCF7L2 at this locus. The verified evidence base points strongly toward this locus being the well-known, extremely well-replicated TCF7L2 type 2 diabetes locus (rs7903146, the single most replicated T2D GWAS variant in human genetics) rather than a locus with independent COPD-relevant biology:

  • No literature (PubMed or GWAS-Catalog-style search) links TCF7L2, rs7903146, or any LD partner in this credible set to COPD, lung function (FEV1/FEV1-FVC), or smoking-behavior traits — a negative finding independently corroborated by red-team-skeptic.
  • Open Targets' own aggregate score for TCF7L2-COPD is low (0.134, rank ~586/6,323) and dominated by a modest genetic_association component (0.217), versus an overwhelming T2D association score (0.665, genetic_association 0.773) — TCF7L2 is unambiguously better characterized as a T2D gene than a COPD gene in aggregated evidence.
  • No colocalization was found between the COPD GWAS credible set and any TCF7L2 molecular QTL (lung eQTL/tuQTL or neutrophil eQTL), despite TCF7L2 expression being under genetic control in both tissues — the COPD-associated variants and the QTL-associated variants at this locus are simply different SNPs.
  • The tangential lung/immune literature offered (asthma-remission methylation, AT2-aging bioinformatics, hypoxic pulmonary-hypertension splicing, and an alveolar-macrophage preprint) spans different diseases, tissues, species, and mechanisms with no connection to this locus's actual noncoding credible-set variants; red-team-skeptic judged this pattern-matching rather than converging support, and one piece of evidence (Zemke et al., mouse beta-catenin knockout) directly argues against an essential Wnt/beta-catenin role in airway epithelial maintenance/repair.
  • Per red-team-skeptic, the researcher's "shared-risk-factor confounding" hypothesis (that the apparent COPD signal reflects T2D/obesity/smoking comorbidity in an unadjusted COPD GWAS rather than direct pulmonary biology) is a reasonable, biologically plausible inference given the totality of evidence, but is not itself literature-established — none of the T2D-COPD MR/epidemiology papers cited examine this locus specifically, and this hypothesis should be treated as the researcher's own interpretive synthesis, not a verified finding.

Runner-up: None — TCF7L2 is the only candidate gene physically at this locus, so there is no gene-level alternative to weigh it against. The real alternative hypothesis is not a different gene but a different interpretation: that this is a T2D-pleiotropy/case-ascertainment artifact in the COPD GWAS (via comorbidity-driven collider bias or shared risk factors) rather than a locus requiring gene-level biological interpretation at all. What would resolve this: (1) a direct colocalization analysis between this specific COPD credible set and TCF7L2 lung/neutrophil eQTLs (not available through the Open Targets query path used here); (2) conditional/sensitivity analysis of the COPD GWAS adjusting for T2D/BMI/metabolic syndrome to see if the signal attenuates; (3) a locus-specific (not genome-wide) MR analysis of this variant's effect on COPD independent of T2D. Confidence is marked LOW rather than INSUFFICIENT because the evidence, while largely negative/indirect, is reasonably thorough and consistently points in one direction (T2D-locus, not lung-biology locus) rather than being genuinely balanced or absent.
