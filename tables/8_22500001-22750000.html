Prioritized gene

BIN3 — confidence: LOW.

BIN3 is named as the single best-supported candidate, but this call is close and should not be overstated. The case for BIN3 rests entirely on statistical/genetic evidence: it is the top locus-to-gene (L2G) prediction for both the actual COPD GWAS credible set (0.642) and an independent FEV/FVC-ratio GWAS (0.368) at the lead variant, it carries the single highest-PIP credible-set variant (the lead variant itself, PIP=0.220), and the lead variant's credible set contains lung-tissue-specific molecular QTLs (exon-usage, transcript-usage, splicing) tagging BIN3 specifically — concordance across COPD and spirometric traits, per the biological priors, is meaningful evidence of pulmonary biology. However, per the red-team-skeptic's critique, this ranking is not settled: CCAR2 (the runner-up) is actually the top L2G gene for two independent vital-capacity (FVC) GWAS at this same variant (0.569 and 0.444, vs. BIN3's 0.313 and 0.545) — the lung-function L2G ranking flips depending on which spirometric trait/study is used, so the credible set cannot cleanly separate BIN3 from CCAR2 on fine-mapping evidence alone. Critically, BIN3 has zero verified mechanistic literature connecting it to lung structural or immune biology — every mechanistic paper found (myogenesis, cataracts/ lymphoma in knockout mice, general amphiphysin-family cell biology) is in a non-lung tissue context, and one candidate supporting citation (a yeast-homolog paper) had to be struck entirely as CLAIM_MISMATCH (wrong paralog). CCAR2, the runner-up, has a biologically attractive senescence/inflammation mechanism (DBC1-SIRT1-NF-κB axis) that is conceptually relevant to COPD's known inflammaging biology, but again with zero lung-tissue-specific literature (all mechanistic data is liver, adipose, or cancer cell lines) and is strongly LoF-constrained (pLoF o/e=0.20), suggesting an essential, pleiotropic role rather than a lung-specific one.

Runner-up: CCAR2. What would resolve this tie: (1) a lung-tissue-specific eQTL/sQTL or single-cell expression dataset directly comparing BIN3 vs. CCAR2 regulatory activity in airway/parenchymal cells under COPD-relevant conditions (e.g., cigarette-smoke exposure, hypoxia); (2) an independent, non-overlapping-authorship replication of any lung/COPD-specific BIN3 or CCAR2 phenotype in mouse or human tissue; (3) fine-mapping with additional COPD/spirometry cohorts to determine whether the BIN3-favoring or CCAR2-favoring L2G pattern is consistent once sample sizes/methods are harmonized across studies. PDLIM2 deserves explicit mention as a contested third possibility: it has the single most COPD-specific, cell-type-resolved mechanistic literature at this locus (myeloid PDLIM2 repression in human COPD/ILD lungs, causal in a mouse acute-lung-injury model) but has no locus-level genetic-association evidence at all — its COPD link in Open Targets is literature/text-mining only, and its credible-set variant assignments are negligible-PIP (<0.003). PDLIM2's mechanistic case also currently rests on one overlapping research group's publications (2025-2026) and should be treated as awaiting independent replication, not as established biology. This locus does not have a single gene with both strong statistical fine-mapping support and strong lung-specific mechanism — that combination does not exist here for any of the six genes evaluated. The study team should weigh BIN3 as the best current statistical bet while recognizing the real possibility that CCAR2 or PDLIM2 could be more directly causal, pending the additional evidence described above.
