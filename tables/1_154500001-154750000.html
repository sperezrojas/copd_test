Prioritized gene

CHRNB2 — Confidence: LOW

CHRNB2 is the best-supported single candidate at this locus, but the case is an inference stack rather than a directly demonstrated mechanism, and red-team-skeptic's critique (preserved in full in the audit appendix) substantially tempers what would otherwise read as a confident call. The evidence favoring CHRNB2 over ADAR consists of: (1) it carries the lead variant and the higher-PIP half of the credible set (PIP 0.290 + 0.206 vs. ADAR's 0.237 + 0.225 + 0.026 — the credible set itself splits roughly evenly and cannot statistically separate the two genes); (2) Open Targets L2G at every colocalizing GWAS-Catalog signal found for this exact locus favors CHRNB2 over ADAR by a factor of 2-7x, but every one of those colocalizing signals is a smoking-behavior or blood-cell-count trait, not COPD or lung function — Open Targets has no COPD-labeled colocalization at this locus for either gene, and CHRNB2's own COPD association score in Open Targets carries no genetic_association datatype contribution at all; (3) eight independent verified literature citations tie CHRNB2 (or its immediate nAChR gene-family/interacting partners) to smoking initiation, heaviness, dependence, cessation, and age of onset — a pattern directly analogous (by gene-family paralogy, not by shared statistical signal) to the well-established CHRNA5/CHRNA3/CHRNB4 15q25.1 locus — but none of these eight citations use this locus's own credible-set variants, and in several of them (Pintarelli 2017, Liu 2018) CHRNB2 is the non-significant partner within its own gene family; (4) no CHRNB2 lung-structural or immune literature exists at all, which is consistent with (but does not prove) a smoking-mediated rather than direct-pulmonary mechanism per Prior 1.

ADAR is the credible runner-up: it is LoF-intolerant and missense-constrained (more dosage-sensitive than CHRNB2 by Open Targets' constraint metrics), has a mechanistically plausible connection to COPD's interferon/innate-immune inflammatory heterogeneity (Prior 3), and one directly airway-disease-relevant finding (reduced ADAR editing associated with asthma severity and eosinophilic/cytokine dysregulation) — but no ADAR-specific COPD statistical-genetics evidence exists, its L2G at every shared smoking-trait signal is consistently secondary to CHRNB2's, and its credible-set variants are regulatory (downstream/intronic) rather than coding, which for this degree of LoF-intolerance is at least as consistent with LD-tagging of the CHRNB2 signal as with an independent ADAR-driven effect.

What would resolve the tie: (a) a direct GTEx/lung-tissue eQTL lookup for ADAR at this locus's exact credible-set variants (flagged by red-team-skeptic as not yet done in this pipeline; would move ADAR from "plausible" to "supported" or rule it out); (b) a formal statistical colocalization (e.g., coloc PP4) between this COPD credible set and the GSCAN smoking-initiation/nicotine-dependence summary statistics, rather than relying on Open Targets' L2G-on-a-different-trait as a proxy; (c) confirmation of whether the GSCAN GWAS itself reaches genome-wide significance at this precise sub-locus (not independently verified in this pipeline run).

KCNN3 is excluded from serious consideration: it is not in the fine-mapped credible set (flagged only by the polygenic-similarity method PoPS), has zero Open Targets L2G colocalization at this locus, and its literature is dominated by an unrelated atrial-fibrillation GWAS signal elsewhere in the genome. Per red-team-skeptic, it should be regarded as "no locus-specific evidence identified; retained only because a polygenic-similarity method flagged it" — a materially weaker epistemic status than either CHRNB2 or ADAR, not a co-tier "weak candidate."

Given that the credible set itself cannot statistically distinguish CHRNB2 from ADAR, and that no COPD-specific (as opposed to smoking-specific) colocalization exists for either gene, this call is marked LOW confidence rather than MODERATE or HIGH. The study team should weigh whether the follow-up analyses above are feasible before treating CHRNB2 as settled.
