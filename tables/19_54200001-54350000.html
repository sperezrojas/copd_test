Prioritized gene

LILRB2 — Confidence: MODERATE (for gene identity at this locus; LOW confidence in a COPD-specific mechanism)

LILRB2 is the best-supported candidate gene at this locus, but the case is a statistical/fine-mapping one rather than a mechanistically proven COPD-biology one, and red-team-skeptic's verdict on it was explicitly WEAKENED, not fully verified-as-characterized. The positive case: two of the five credible-set variants are missense changes within the LILRB2 coding sequence (rs383369, PIP=0.378; rs386056, PIP=0.047) and the top-PIP variant (rs383925, PIP=0.433) is intronic to LILRB2 — this is gene-specific variant annotation, not just nearest-gene proximity inference. Open Targets' own L2G algorithm assigns LILRB2 a high, disease-matched score (0.861) at the lead variant for an actual COPD GWAS study, and this is reinforced by dense multi-tissue eQTL/sQTL colocalization to LILRB2 transcripts, including a GTEx lung splicing QTL (see Open Targets summary above).

Against this: (1) the L2G signal is pleiotropic — the identical lead variant gets equally high L2G to LILRB2 for hematocrit and HDL-cholesterol, suggesting a generic blood/myeloid regulatory hub rather than COPD-specific action; (2) the largest available multi-ancestry lung-function GWAS (Shrine et al. 2019, PMID 30804560) found no signal at this locus for FEV1/FEV1-FVC, directly discordant with pipeline prior #2's expectation of spirometric concordance; (3) the largest COPD coding-variant exome-array meta-analysis (Moll et al. 2021, PMID 33909500) also did not flag LILRB2; (4) no COPD-specific cellular/functional literature exists for LILRB2 — the closest mechanistic precedent is an acute lung injury (not chronic COPD) alveolar-macrophage pyroptosis study (PMID 38758159); (5) the missense-variant-specific literature is largely unusable — two citations were NOT_FOUND, one (endometriosis) was a corrected non-significant finding, and one (Zika transmission) showed a protective rather than risk association, none in a COPD/pulmonary context; (6) LILRB2's own #1 platform-wide disease association (Alzheimer's) has no established literature mechanism, leaving the shared-vs-coincidental-pleiotropy question at this variant genuinely open; (7) two neighboring LILR-family paralogs (LILRA1, LILRB5) show comparable molecular-QTL colocalization at the same credible-set variants and are not cleanly excluded — the paralog-specificity of the signal rests substantially on L2G's disease-matched scoring rather than independent gene-specific functional literature.

Runner-up: NLRP12 (PoPS-prioritized) — confidence LOW/INSUFFICIENT as an alternative. NLRP12 has no L2G or colocalization support at this locus, sits ~375-560 kb outside the credible-set window, and its dominant human phenotype (monogenic periodic fever syndrome) is mechanistically distinct from common-variant COPD susceptibility. However, red-team-skeptic notes this should not be fully dropped: PoPS is an independent, similarity-based method not restricted by physical distance or colocalization, NLRP12 is a documented myeloid/T-cell negative regulator of NF-κB signaling (a mechanistically coherent, untested hypothesis for excess innate/neutrophilic inflammation if lost), and the one direct pulmonary functional test (PMID 22291998) specifically probed a Th2/asthma-type model, not the neutrophilic/innate-immune inflammatory program most relevant to COPD per pipeline prior #3 — so absence of evidence in that one negative mouse study is not the same as evidence of absence for a COPD-relevant mechanism.

What would resolve this call: (a) an independent lung-function (FEV1/FEV1-FVC) or COPD subtype (e.g., exacerbation-frequency, emphysema-predominant) GWAS/fine-mapping analysis that specifically discriminates LILRB2 from LILRA1/LILRB5/LILRB3 at this locus, ideally with tissue-specific (lung, alveolar macrophage) colocalization rather than blood/whole-tissue eQTL; (b) a direct smoking-initiation/nicotine-dependence GWAS lookup at rs383925/rs383369 (flagged by red-team-skeptic as not completed in this review due to exhausted search budget) to rule in/out a smoking-behavior-mediated mechanism per pipeline prior #1; (c) COPD-specific (not acute-injury or oncology) functional data on LILRB2 in airway/alveolar myeloid cells or CD8+ T-cell programs; (d) confirmation of whether NLRP12's PoPS signal reflects a genuine trans-acting or long-range regulatory mechanism, versus a false positive from genome-wide similarity scoring. Given the weight of the evidence, LILRB2 remains the recommended candidate for the study team to carry forward, but this should be treated as a moderate-confidence statistical/positional call awaiting COPD-specific mechanistic confirmation, not a settled result.
