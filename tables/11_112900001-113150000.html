Prioritized gene

NCAM1 — Confidence: MODERATE

NCAM1 is the only candidate gene named by any prioritization method (nearest gene, FLAMES, MAGMA, PoPS) for this locus, and Open Targets' own L2G algorithm independently and consistently favors NCAM1 by roughly a 10-fold margin over its nearest neighbors DRD2 and ANKK1 across all 185 GWAS credible sets registered at the lead variant rs7943198 — including the credible sets explicitly labeled COPD ("chronic airway obstruction," L2G=0.857) and FEV1/FVC ratio (L2G=0.77-0.79). This is corroborated by verified literature specific to NCAM1 itself (not merely the surrounding gene cluster): a GWAS-significant NCAM1 variant tied to smoking behavior with a brain trans-eQTL effect on NCAM1 (Wain et al. 2015, PMID 26423011); NCAM1 (not TTC12/ANKK1/DRD2) independently named as a candidate causal gene via multi-omic fine-mapping in a large PTSD GWAS (Nievergelt et al. 2024, PMID 38637617); and NCAM1 specifically implicated (via a variant, rs7945073, that is itself a member of this locus's own credible set) in fine-mapping of a genetic signal shared between smoking, cannabis use, and schizophrenia (Song et al. 2022, PMID 35235886). A newly identified mechanistic paper also shows NCAM1 has a direct functional role in pulmonary arterial smooth muscle cell proliferation/migration relevant to pulmonary hypertension (Chen et al. 2024, PMID 39696429) — genuine, non-marker-level lung-vascular biology for this gene, albeit in a pulmonary-hypertension (WHO Group 3-adjacent) rather than classic airway-obstruction/emphysema context.

Confidence is capped at MODERATE, not HIGH, for reasons the red-team-skeptic raised that are not fully resolved by the verified evidence: (1) the mechanism by which this locus drives the COPD association remains genuinely unsettled — the credible set's flat PIP distribution (lead PIP only 0.05 across ~63 variants) is equally consistent with a single pleiotropic regulatory variant or with multiple partially-LD'd signals, and no molecular-QTL (eQTL/pQTL) colocalization tying the variant to NCAM1 expression in lung tissue was found in either the Open Targets lookup or the literature search — only GWAS-GWAS colocalization, which cannot itself distinguish "one causal variant" from "two nearby causal variants in strong LD." (2) Older candidate-gene-era literature (Gelernter et al. 2006, PMID 17085484; Yang et al. 2008, PMID 18828801) localizes the historically strongest nicotine-dependence and alcohol/drug-dependence signal in this exact gene cluster to TTC12/ANKK1 rather than NCAM1, an unresolved tension with the modern fine-mapping literature that favors NCAM1 — plausibly reconciled by NCAM1 occupying far more physical/LD space in this locus than the smaller TTC12/ANKK1 genes (a large-gene absorption effect), but this was not definitively resolved. (3) Per the skeptic's critique, the FEV1/FVC ratio concordance should NOT be read as independent evidence for a direct-pulmonary-biology hypothesis distinct from smoking-mediation — a variant that increases smoking initiation/heaviness would be expected to produce FEV1/FVC association simply through smoking-induced lung damage, and no cited study adjusts for smoking exposure to test whether the FEV1/FVC signal survives independently. (4) The CD56+ cytotoxic-lymphocyte immune evidence (Freeman et al. 2014, PMID 25078269; Marquardt et al. 2017, PMID 27670241) is real but marker-level only — CD56 is used purely as a flow-cytometry gating marker in both papers, with no measurement of NCAM1 genetic variation or expression; this should be treated as unlinked biological plausibility rather than a competing mechanistic pathway for this locus.

Net call: given four independent prioritization methods, an order-of-magnitude L2G margin over both plausible neighboring genes, and multiple independently verified literature sources naming NCAM1 itself (not just the gene cluster) in modern GWAS fine-mapping, NCAM1 is confidently the correct physically/statistically implicated gene at this locus. What remains genuinely uncertain — and is the reason this is MODERATE rather than HIGH confidence — is whether NCAM1's contribution to COPD risk here is substantially mediated through smoking-related neurobiology (the best-supported hypothesis, consistent with biological prior #1 and this locus's extensive, repeated footprint in addiction/psychiatric GWAS) versus a direct pulmonary mechanism (for which the strongest candidate evidence, the PASMC/pulmonary-hypertension paper, does not address classic airway-obstruction biology). No specific alternative gene is nominated by any source as a stronger candidate than NCAM1; TTC12 and ANKK1 are the only plausible alternatives raised (via the historical addiction-genetics literature), but they lie outside the credible set and outside the locus window as defined in the input file, so this is not treated as a true tied contest requiring a runner-up call — it is a mechanism-of-action uncertainty, not a gene-identity uncertainty.
