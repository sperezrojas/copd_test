Prioritized gene

Prioritized gene: NFIB Confidence: LOW

NFIB is named here as the best-supported single candidate at this locus, but with substantially lower confidence than the raw statistical-genetics/Open Targets picture alone would suggest, and the red-team critique (run twice, including an independent follow-up search pass) materially weakens the direct-pulmonary-mechanism narrative.

Reasoning:

  • Physical/statistical case for NFIB over ATP5PDP3 is essentially uncontested. NFIB is the sole protein-coding gene in the 9:14.0-14.3Mb interval (independently confirmed via Ensembl regional gene enumeration by the researcher); the credible set's overwhelming variant mass sits on NFIB-annotated intronic/3'UTR/downstream variants; ATP5PDP3 is a processed pseudogene tagged only by a handful of PIP<0.005 variants, has zero Open Targets evidence of any kind, and the skeptic's critique explicitly HOLDS its exclusion. This part of the call is not close.
  • However, the mechanistic case for why NFIB drives COPD risk specifically is weak and contested. Open Targets shows comparable-magnitude L2G-style evidence for NFIB with both COPD (0.783, 0.702) and smoking initiation (0.571, 0.571), and zero credible-set evidence for FEV1 or pulmonary-function-measurement traits — the opposite of the concordance pattern biological prior #2 says should strengthen a pulmonary-biology call. Two independent red-team-skeptic passes (the second specifically chasing this question via Europe PMC/GWAS Catalog/Semantic Scholar after the first pass's web searches failed on budget exhaustion) could not confirm NFIB's presence in either the Sakornsakolpat 2019 COPD-loci table or the Shrine 2019 lung-function-signal table, and found zero full-text mentions of "NFIB" in either smoking-initiation GWAS paper (GSCAN 2019, Saunders 2022) — so it remains unconfirmed which trait's discovery literature the Open Targets credible-set evidence actually derives from.
  • All of the strong, citation-dense mechanistic literature for NFIB (Hsu 2011, Lajoie 2014, Pais 2013, Steele-Perkins 2005 via Campbell 2008) is fetal/perinatal lung-maturation knockout biology — a fundamentally different regime (complete loss of function in organogenesis) from a common noncoding variant's partial dosage effect on adult airflow obstruction. No study bridges this gap. The one piece of evidence touching adult, smoke-exposed pulmonary biology (Gao et al. 2007, NFI-B and cigarette-smoke- modulated lysyl oxidase promoter activity in rat fibroblasts) is suggestive but concerns promoter occupancy, not a disease-outcome link.
  • A credible, evidence-supported competing hypothesis exists: NFIB's single most robustly established human dosage-sensitivity phenotype is a neurodevelopmental/ macrocephaly syndrome (Malan syndrome; Schanze 2018, Zenker 2019) — a CNS/behavioral phenotype that sits naturally alongside the locus's smoking-initiation signal. The red-team critique explicitly raises the hypothesis that this locus could act on smoking behavior via CNS/neurodevelopmental NFIB biology, with the COPD signal downstream of smoking exposure rather than reflecting independent pulmonary pathology — and notes nothing in the assembled evidence rules this out.
  • Per pipeline guidance, this is exactly the situation where naming a single best pick while being explicit about why the call is close is required, rather than manufacturing unwarranted certainty. There is no realistic runner-up gene at this locus (ATP5PDP3 is not a serious contender), so the "close call" here is not between two genes but between two mechanisms for the same gene: direct pulmonary/developmental biology vs. smoking-behavior-mediated (possibly via the same CNS/neurodevelopmental pathway responsible for Malan syndrome). What would resolve this: (1) direct access to the Sakornsakolpat 2019 and Shrine 2019 supplementary locus tables to check whether NFIB/ chr9:13.9-14.3Mb appears among the lung-function-replicated signals (blocked by CAPTCHA/paywall in this run); (2) a colocalization or conditional (smoking-adjusted) analysis of the COPD signal at this locus against the smoking-initiation signal, which no available literature currently provides; (3) any lung-tissue eQTL or single-cell expression data placing NFIB expression changes in adult COPD-relevant airway/alveolar cell types (not found in this search).
  • Confidence is therefore LOW, not MODERATE or HIGH: the gene-identity call (NFIB over ATP5PDP3) is solid, but the disease-mechanism call (direct pulmonary vs. smoking-mediated) that would be needed to confidently characterize why NFIB matters for COPD is unresolved and, on the current evidence, arguably tilts toward smoking-mediation rather than the developmental-lung-biology narrative that dominates the literature search by citation count alone.
