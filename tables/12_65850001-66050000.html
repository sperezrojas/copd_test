Important caveat carried over from red-team-skeptic critique: the L2G score of 0.672 should NOT be read as independent corroboration of a COPD-specific pulmonary mechanism. L2G-style models are driven largely by distance-to-gene and regulatory/QTL features that answer "which gene," not "via which trait or pathway." In a single-gene desert locus like this one, HMGA2 will score as the top gene assignment for essentially any genome-wide- significant signal here — height, aortic diameter, type 2 diabetes, or COPD — because those architecture features are trait-agnostic. The high L2G score confirms only that HMGA2 is the correct gene assignment for whatever is driving the statistical signal; it does not arbitrate between a COPD-specific mechanism and height/anthropometric pleiotropy bleeding into a case-control COPD phenotype. Open Targets' own literature-datatype score (0.012) is consistent with the researcher's finding of essentially no independent biological/functional literature converging on HMGA2-COPD specifically.

Prioritized gene

Prioritized gene: HMGA2. Confidence: LOW.

HMGA2 is named because it is the only physically plausible candidate gene at this locus — this is a genuine gene desert (HMGA2 plus the non-coding antisense HMGA2-AS1 are the only genes overlapping the 200 kb window), so there is no competing gene-assignment ambiguity of the classic "LD block, several genes" kind. On pure gene identity, confidence would be HIGH. But the prioritization question this pipeline asks is broader than gene assignment — it is whether the evidence supports HMGA2 acting through COPD-relevant pulmonary or immune biology, and here the verified evidence base is weak and substantially contested:

  • The Open Targets L2G score (0.672) and overall association score (0.249) look moderately supportive on their face, but per the Open Targets summary above and the red-team-skeptic's critique, L2G is gene-assignment evidence, not mechanism evidence, and is trait-agnostic in a gene desert — it does not distinguish a COPD mechanism from a height-pleiotropy artifact.
  • The flagship COPD case-control GWAS citation for this class of locus (Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019, PMID 30804561) could not be confirmed, even after a dedicated follow-up literature pass, to actually name HMGA2/12q14.3 among its 82 genome-wide-significant COPD loci (CLAIM_MISMATCH on this specific point; excluded from the table above, full reasoning in the audit file).
  • A dedicated follow-up check found, with high confidence, that HMGA2/12q14.3 does NOT appear among the 279 genome-wide-significant signals of the major quantitative lung-function GWAS (Shrine et al. 2019, PMID 30804560), despite that study's very large sample size (n=400,102) and height-adjusted design — this is verified negative evidence against a direct, independently-replicated pulmonary-function mechanism at this locus.
  • Neither credible-set lead variant (rs113744258, rs4338565) has any dedicated PubMed-indexed functional or association literature, and every credible-set variant's GWAS Catalog profile is dominated by anthropometric/cardiometabolic traits, not lung function or COPD.
  • HMGA2/rs1042725 (in this locus's own credible set) is one of the most robustly replicated common-variant height loci in the human genome (Weedon et al. 2007, PMID 17767157, VERIFIED), and height is an established, if only partially explanatory, confounder of FEV1/FVC-based case definitions (North et al. 2016, PMID 26831755, VERIFIED, though only an indirect comparator for this specific locus).
  • The structural/developmental and immune/inflammatory biology found for HMGA2 is real and verified, but entirely off-tissue (liver, skin, peripheral nerve) or off-disease (IPF, lung adenocarcinoma) relative to COPD/emphysema specifically — none of it demonstrates a COPD- or airway-specific mechanism.

The red-team-skeptic explicitly marked HMGA2 CONTESTED at this locus on exactly this basis: not because a competing gene exists, but because a coherent, evidence-backed competing causal hypothesis (height/anthropometric pleiotropy inflating a COPD case-control signal, rather than direct pulmonary biology) has not been ruled out, and the one study that could most directly resolve it (a locus-specific height-mediation MR or multi-trait fine-mapping analysis) does not appear to exist in the published literature. The skeptic also raised a genuine, unresolved alternative in the other direction — that HMGA2's growth-regulatory chromatin biology could plausibly act on lung developmental growth (e.g., airway-to-parenchyma dysanapsis) via the same pathway that produces its height association, rather than being a pure statistical artifact — but this remains speculative and untested.

What would resolve this: a locus-specific Mendelian randomization or multi-trait fine-mapping analysis directly testing whether the HMGA2/12q14.3 COPD association survives adjustment for height or genetically-predicted height; direct inspection of Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019's and Shrine et al. 2019's supplementary locus tables to confirm definitively whether/how this locus appears in each; and any COPD- or emphysema-specific (not IPF- or lung-adenocarcinoma-specific) functional study of HMGA2 in airway or alveolar tissue. Until that evidence exists, this pipeline's recommendation is LOW confidence: HMGA2 is the correct gene call by elimination, but whether its association with the COPD phenotype at this locus reflects COPD-relevant pulmonary/immune biology, or is substantially an anthropometric (height) pleiotropy artifact of the case-control definition, is unresolved and should be weighed accordingly by the study team.
