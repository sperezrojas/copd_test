Prioritized gene

ZBTB20 — Confidence: LOW.

ZBTB20 is the best-supported gene at this locus on structural/statistical grounds: it is the nearest gene, is FLAMES- and PoPS-prioritized, physically contains all 19 credible-set variants, and has by far the highest Open Targets locus-to-gene score (0.61-0.65) of any nearby gene (TIGIT/ZNF80/QTRT2/CCDC191 all ≤0.16) at the lead variant rs1606494 across all three GWAS credible sets recorded there. This is corroborated by an independent, verified literature citation (Cheng et al., PMID 37147289) explicitly naming ZBTB20 as a biologically important region in a smoking-behavior multi-trait GWAS.

However, confidence in ZBTB20 as a COPD-relevant gene, as opposed to simply "the correct gene at this LD block," is LOW, and the pipeline's own red-team-skeptic marked this gene CONTESTED at the COPD-relevance level (not at the gene-identity level) for good reason: every trait directly observed at the exact lead variant in Open Targets is smoking cessation, smoking behavior, mean corpuscular volume, or (via colocalization) height/blood pressure/aortic size — never COPD or a spirometric trait. Open Targets' curated disease-association database records zero association between ZBTB20 and COPD, FEV1, FEV1/FVC, emphysema, or "obstructive pulmonary" by any query attempted. Independent PubMed searches for ZBTB20 + FEV1/lung function/spirometry likewise returned zero hits. The gene's well-verified literature biology — B-cell/plasma-cell humoral immunity, lung-cancer epithelial proliferation, a developmental lung-capillary marker-gene role, and Primrose-syndrome neurodevelopmental haploinsufficiency — is substantial but not COPD- or airway-inflammation-specific in any of the programs (neutrophilic, CD8+ T-cell, Th1, Th17/Tc17, macrophage) most classically implicated in COPD.

Applying prior #1 directly: the convergent picture (highest L2G specifically for smoking-cessation/smoking-behavior GWAS at this variant, an independently replicated smoking-behavior MTAG hit, zero COPD/spirometry signal anywhere) makes it more likely that this locus entered the COPD GWAS credible-set list because smoking behavior is a strong causal/confounding driver of COPD case status in summary statistics not adjusted for smoking, rather than because ZBTB20 acts on lung pathophysiology directly. No formal mediation or Mendelian-randomization analysis of ZBTB20/rs1606494 on COPD via smoking was identified in this search — this is the single largest fillable gap, not a settled question. The disclosed search-budget exhaustion (both the researcher and the red-team-skeptic were unable to run a direct "ZBTB20 rs1606494 COPD GWAS" web search or any bioRxiv/medRxiv preprint search) means the possibility of an undiscovered direct COPD/spirometry association or preprint cannot be fully ruled out, which is an additional reason confidence is capped at LOW rather than MODERATE.

Runner-up: none of the other nearby genes (TIGIT, ZNF80, QTRT2, CCDC191) has any competitive evidence — their L2G scores are far lower and none was independently researched in depth (TIGIT in particular was flagged by the red-team-skeptic as a genuinely unresearched candidate, given search-budget exhaustion, though its low L2G argues against it being the driver gene). ZBTB20-AS5 has essentially no independent support beyond ABC-based physical proximity.

What would resolve this: (1) a direct, budget-unconstrained literature/GWAS-catalog search for ZBTB20 or rs1606494 against COPD/FEV1/FEV1-FVC specifically (not run in this pipeline due to search-budget exhaustion at two separate stages); (2) a formal smoking-mediation or Mendelian-randomization analysis of this locus's effect on COPD, decomposing direct pulmonary effect from smoking-behavior-mediated effect; (3) any independent evidence for or against TIGIT at this locus, given its immunological plausibility for COPD despite its lower L2G score.

The study team should weigh this recommendation as: "ZBTB20 is very likely the correct gene at this locus, but this locus's contribution to COPD risk is plausibly smoking-behavior-mediated rather than pulmonary-mechanistic, and that specific question was not resolved by the evidence gathered here."
