Evidence Table — Locus 20:25100001-33050000

Region: chr20:25,100,001-33,050,000 (GRCh38), ~7.95 Mb. Lead variant: rs17123518 (20:32,660,463, PIP=0.1248, intronic in C20orf203). Candidate genes from internal pipeline: Nearest = C20orf203; FLAMES = NA; MAGMA = NECAB3; PoPS = E2F1; ABC = NINL. Ancestry/cohort: not provided.

Locus-architecture note (applies to the whole table below): This window straddles the chr20 centromere (~28.1-30.4 Mb GRCh38). The 121 credible-set variants split into two clusters ~7 Mb apart — a proximal cluster (~32.19-32.88 Mb, containing the lead variant: C20orf203, ASXL1, EFCAB8, MAPRE1, DNMT3B, KIF3B, POFUT1, PLAGL2, NOL4L) and a distal cluster (~25.10-25.78 Mb: NINL, PYGB, NANP, ABHD12, GINS1, ZNF337, ZNF337-AS1, ENTPD6, FAM182B, VN1R108P) — with no credible-set variants at all fine-mapped through the intervening gene-poor pericentromeric gap. Per red-team-skeptic critique (preserved in full in the audit file), this architecture means recombination between the clusters is effectively suppressed, so the two clusters should be read as two largely independent candidate-gene questions rather than one locus with one causal gene. The maximum PIP anywhere is 0.125 (C20orf203), with the next three genes nearly tied (ASXL1 0.068, EFCAB8 0.066, MAPRE1 0.047) — a diffuse, not clean, signal even before biology is weighed. NECAB3 (MAGMA) and E2F1 (PoPS) are gene-based statistical prioritizations with no anchoring credible-set variant.

No FEV1/FEV1-FVC spirometry-GWAS concordance was identified for any gene at this locus in the literature search conducted — this is a locus-wide gap, not a per-gene one, and the red-team critique concluded it should meaningfully lower confidence that this locus reflects direct pulmonary biology as opposed to smoking-behavior-mediated or indeterminate mechanism.

Prioritized-gene synthesis

Prioritized gene: ASXL1. Confidence: LOW.

This locus does not support a high-confidence single-gene call, for reasons documented throughout the table and audit above: fine-mapping is diffuse (max PIP 0.125, with three other proximal-cluster genes nearly tied), the ~8 Mb window straddles the chr20 centromere and effectively contains two independent candidate-gene questions (proximal vs. distal cluster) rather than one clean locus, and no gene at this locus has any spirometry (FEV1/FEV1-FVC) evidence to corroborate a direct pulmonary mechanism.

Within that constrained picture, ASXL1 is the best-supported single candidate: it has the highest Open Targets genetic_association-datatype score of any gene at this locus (0.441, the only gene here with any such score) and the highest overall Open Targets COPD association score (0.269); it carries the second-highest PIP in the whole credible set (0.068, rs2424880); and it has real, verified clinical literature directly linking ASXL1 clonal hematopoiesis to COPD exacerbation history (PMID 38320547) and to COPD generally via myeloid inflammatory mechanisms (PMID 36941354, 36938794). However, this call is explicitly weakened, not strong: every piece of that clinical/mechanistic evidence concerns somatic clonal-hematopoiesis mutations acquired with age in blood cells, not the germline noncoding intronic credible-set variant actually present at this locus — no evidence currently shows the germline locus affects ASXL1 expression, dosage, or somatic CH acquisition rate, so the connection between "GWAS hit near ASXL1" and "ASXL1-driven CH promotes COPD" is presently a name-match, not a demonstrated regulatory link (flagged explicitly by red-team-skeptic). It is also possible the Open Targets genetic_association score for ASXL1 reflects this same GWAS's own locus-to-gene assignment rather than independent corroborating evidence — this was not resolvable within this session and should be checked before treating the Open Targets score as independent support.

Runner-up: C20orf203 (the nearest gene and highest-PIP gene overall, 0.125). Its only verified literature ties it to a nicotine-addiction gene-discovery screen and brain expression, not pulmonary biology — this is consistent with (and arguably supports) the locus's smoking-mediation prior rather than a direct-pulmonary-biology story, but that reframes the causal hypothesis rather than strengthening a COPD-specific mechanism for this gene. Second runner-up (distal cluster only): NINL (ABC-prioritized, highest cumulative PIP in the distal cluster), which has genuinely well-characterized innate-antiviral-immune biology plausibly relevant to COPD exacerbations, but has never been tested in lung tissue.

What would resolve the tie / raise confidence: (1) an eQTL or eQTL-colocalization check of whether the credible-set variants at this locus (rs2424880 and neighbors) actually regulate ASXL1 expression, ideally in lung/blood/myeloid tissue, and whether they influence CH-variant-allele-fraction/acquisition rate; (2) confirmation of whether Open Targets' ASXL1 genetic_association score for COPD is independent of this study's own locus-to-gene pipeline or circular with it; (3) a dedicated spirometry-GWAS (FEV1/FEV1-FVC, e.g. UK Biobank/SpiroMeta) lookup at both the proximal and distal clusters, since none was found in this pass; (4) a dedicated smoking-behavior GWAS (GSCAN-type) lookup at both clusters, to test whether this signal is better explained as smoking-initiation/heaviness genetics than as COPD-specific pulmonary biology — plausible given C20orf203's nicotine-addiction discovery history; (5) re-running the PYGB missense-variant (rs2228976, rs2227891) literature/ClinVar search that could not be completed in this session due to tool-budget exhaustion, before ruling PYGB out. Given all of the above, the study team should treat ASXL1 as the best-available lead, not a settled causal-gene call, and should weigh seriously whether this locus is better characterized as two independent, individually weak signals than as one gene.
