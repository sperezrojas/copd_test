Prioritized gene

KSR2 — confidence: LOW.

KSR2 is my best-supported single pick at this locus, but the confidence is capped at LOW, not higher, for reasons the red-team-skeptic's WEAKENED verdict lays out and that I am not overriding.

The strongest evidence for KSR2 is the structured Open Targets L2G/colocalization signal: KSR2 is the consistent top-scoring gene (~0.69-0.70) at the lead variant rs12424627 across three independent FEV1/FVC-ratio GWAS, with near-perfect (H4=1.0) cross-study colocalization, and a distant runner-up (RFC5, ~0.10) — directly supporting pipeline prior #2 (spirometric concordance strengthens prioritization). This is a real, reproducible statistical-genetic signal. However, per the skeptic's critique, this signal (a) is a structured-database output that was never itself run through citation-verifier and has zero independent peer-reviewed literature corroboration (no PubMed publication ties KSR2 to COPD, FEV1, or lung function directly — see researcher's checkpoint), and (b) is in tension with Open Targets' own separate disease-association layer, which shows zero evidence rows for KSR2 against COPD/FEV1/emphysema despite the strong QTL-level signal — an unresolved inconsistency, not one I am resolving in KSR2's favor.

KSR2's only well-verified literature (all 11 KSR2 citations above, VERIFIED) documents its established biology as a RAS/MAPK/ERK scaffold protein controlling hypothalamic energy balance and monogenic obesity (Pearce et al. 2013, PMID 24209692; Revelli et al. 2011, PMID 21127480; Guo et al. 2016, PMID 28180061; Abu-Rub et al. 2024, PMID 39202327) — with no direct structural, developmental, or immune lung mechanism identified anywhere in the literature search. An obesity-mediated indirect pathway to lung function is a plausible hypothesis but is untested by any cited paper, and the skeptic further notes it may be mechanistically backwards (obesity-driven restriction typically raises, not lowers, FEV1/FVC ratio — the opposite of an obstructive signature) unless the actual direction of effect at this locus is confirmed, which this review was not able to do from the locus input file alone. Separately, KSR2's Open Targets disease-association profile shows a non-trivial smoking-initiation score (0.288) that was never followed up with a dedicated smoking-behavior GWAS literature search — per pipeline prior #1, this leaves open whether the FEV1/FVC signal at this locus is genuinely pulmonary-biology-mediated or reflects smoking-behavior confounding via KSR2's appetite/reward-circuitry biology. This is an unresolved evidence gap, not a resolved negative.

Runner-up: NOS1 (FLAMES-prioritized). The red-team-skeptic marked NOS1 CONTESTED: it has zero L2G signal at the lead variant, and four independent, well-powered candidate-gene/ exhaled-NO genetic studies (Aminuddin et al. 2013, PMID 24192154; Bouzigon et al. 2012, PMID 22590587; Dahgam et al. 2012, PMID 22368302; Demeo et al. 2007, PMID 17690329) all found NO significant NOS1 association with COPD, lung function, or FeNO — active contradicting evidence, not merely absence of evidence. NOS1's genuine, well-verified mechanistic biology (neuronal NOS/iNANC bronchodilation; De Sanctis et al. 1997, PMID 9357865; De Sanctis et al. 1999, PMID 10330441) is real but is asthma/allergic-airway physiology in animal models, not COPD-specific structural biology, and cannot substitute for the repeated negative human genetic findings. NOS1 is not a competitive alternative to KSR2 at this locus on current evidence.

What would resolve the tie/raise confidence: (1) direct literature or GWAS Catalog confirmation of the FEV1/FVC-ratio association's effect direction (obstructive vs. restrictive) at rs12424627, to test whether the obesity-mediated hypothesis is even directionally consistent; (2) a targeted smoking-initiation/nicotine-dependence GWAS lookup (e.g., GSCAN, Liu et al. 2019) at the 12q24.23 locus, to rule in/out smoking-behavior mediation given KSR2's non-trivial OT smoking-initiation score; (3) resolution of why Open Targets' disease-association layer shows no COPD/FEV1/emphysema evidence for KSR2 despite the strong L2G/coloc signal (ingestion gap vs. genuine absence); (4) any dedicated KSR2-lung-function publication, which does not currently exist in PubMed. Until these are resolved, KSR2 remains the best-supported single candidate but confidence should stay LOW, and the study team should treat this locus as needing targeted follow-up before being presented with higher confidence.
