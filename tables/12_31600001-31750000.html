Prioritized gene

Gene: AMN1 Confidence: LOW

This call is close, and the case for AMN1 is weaker than a simple "highest PIP" read would suggest — the red-team-skeptic's critique should be weighed heavily here. AMN1 carries the highest combined fine-mapping PIP (~0.78 across its two intronic variants, rows above), but that is positional annotation only: no eQTL, colocalization, chromatin/regulatory annotation, or any human functional/genetics literature supports AMN1 as the causal effector gene (all five verified AMN1 citations are yeast/fungal cell-biology studies of a homologous mitotic-exit-network gene, with no demonstrated relevance to human tissue of any kind, let alone lung). Critically, the skeptic points out that AMN1's higher PIP over ETFBKMT may simply reflect where intron boundaries happen to fall relative to each SNP within a single shared LD block/credible set (all four credible-set variants are one statistical unit), not independent evidence that the regulatory mechanism belongs to AMN1 rather than ETFBKMT. Runner-up: ETFBKMT (combined PIP ~0.18, same credible set) — it has a real, biochemically confirmed human mitochondrial function (ETFB lysine methylation regulating fatty-acid-oxidation electron transfer, PMID 25416781) that is mechanistically compatible with an oxidative-stress framework but has zero direct lung, smoking, or COPD literature support, and its strongest Open Targets disease link is type 2 diabetes, not COPD. A second, structurally distinct alternative is DENND5B (PoPS-prioritized, strongly LoF-constrained/dosage-sensitive per Open Targets), but its gene body sits outside the locus boundary (only the antisense lncRNA DENND5B-AS1 overlaps), and its only documented human phenotypes — BMI (common variant) and a severe neurodevelopmental disorder (rare de novo variants) — are both non-pulmonary, so this pick requires accepting a long-range regulatory mechanism that is not evidenced here.

None of the three genes shows lung-function (FEV1/FEV1-FVC) association evidence in the literature searched, and none shows a meaningfully elevated smoking-initiation signal in Open Targets (AMN1 not recorded; DENND5B 0.051; ETFBKMT 0.071, both weak) — so this locus's COPD signal is not well explained by either of the two main mechanistic hypotheses in this pipeline's priors (direct pulmonary biology or smoking-mediated neurobiology). What would resolve the tie: a lung-tissue eQTL/colocalization analysis for AMN1 vs. ETFBKMT vs. DENND5B(-AS1) at this locus (e.g., GTEx lung, or a COPD-relevant airway epithelium/immune-cell eQTL resource), chromatin-contact or ABC-style regulatory-element mapping to determine which gene's promoter the credible-set variants actually contact, and an independent FEV1/FEV1-FVC GWAS lookup for all three genes/variants. Given the near-total absence of gene-specific, lung-relevant, non-positional evidence for any of the three candidates, the study team should treat this locus as unresolved and prioritize functional follow-up (eQTL/coloc, reporter assays) over literature-based prioritization alone.
