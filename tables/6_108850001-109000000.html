Prioritized gene

ARMC2 — confidence: MODERATE

ARMC2 is the best-supported candidate gene at this locus, but on statistical/positional grounds rather than a confirmed lung mechanism. All three credible-set variants (rs2798641 PIP=0.625, rs2806356 PIP=0.290, rs2768551 PIP=0.058) sit in ARMC2 introns, and Open Targets' L2G model consistently and substantially favors ARMC2 (~0.73-0.74) over the only other genes it considers at this locus, CEP57L1 and SESN1 (~0.06 each) — a roughly 12-fold gap that held up identically across COPD main-effect, COPD x smoking-interaction, and COPD-in-non-current-smokers strata. Strong GWAS-GWAS colocalization (H4 > 0.98) between the COPD credible set and independently ascertained FEV1, FEV1/FVC, peak-expiratory-flow, and asthma credible sets at this same locus satisfies biological prior #2 (spirometric concordance strengthens prioritization) about as well as this pipeline is likely to see. The stability of the L2G signal across smoking-interaction strata, together with no evidence found linking this locus to smoking-behavior GWAS (initiation, cessation, cigarettes/day, nicotine dependence), argues against prior #1's smoking-behavior-mediation alternative for this specific locus.

However, red-team-skeptic's critique meaningfully downgrades the mechanistic case: the one citation claiming a direct in vivo airway/tracheal ciliary phenotype for ARMC2 loss (Giordani et al. 2026) came back NOT_FOUND from citation-verifier — likely a fabricated or hallucinated future-dated paper — and must not be used. Without it, the surviving verified structural evidence (Coutton 2019, Lechtreck 2022 x2) establishes ARMC2 only as a ciliary/flagellar IFT-cargo-adapter gene in the sperm flagellum (humans/mice) and in Chlamydomonas flagella — not in mammalian airway epithelium. The skeptic further notes the "associated with... reduced lung function" phrase in the Lechtreck eLife abstract is likely a circular back-citation to the same population GWAS papers (Shrine 2019, Sakornsakolpat 2019) that were themselves marked CLAIM_MISMATCH here (their abstracts don't explicitly name ARMC2 or rs2798641), rather than independent corroboration. There is also zero eQTL/pQTL/sQTL colocalization evidence for ARMC2 at this locus (explicitly checked, absent) — so no expression-based functional-genomics link confirms this is truly a lung-expression-mediated mechanism as opposed to, e.g., an LD/positional artifact of an otherwise strong statistical signal. And ARMC2's known biology is a recessive loss-of-function phenotype (MMAF, gnomAD LoF-tolerant, oe=0.75) restricted to sperm flagella, distinct in both zygosity and tissue from what a common noncoding lung-function GWAS variant of modest effect would need to be doing (most plausibly a partial regulatory/expression effect in airway epithelium, an alternative hypothesis this evidence base does not directly test).

The runner-up, FOXO3 (PoPS-prioritized), has a large and biologically coherent COPD-relevant mechanistic literature (SIRT1/SIRT3/AMPK-FOXO3 axis in smoke-induced airway/alveolar epithelial senescence and autophagy), but this literature is uniformly smoke-exposure-response biology in cell/rodent models, not genetic evidence tied to this locus. FOXO3 is entirely absent from Open Targets' L2G model at this locus (not merely low-scoring), has no colocalization evidence, sits ~270 kb from the credible-set variants, and its one candidate-gene COPD association (Markelov 2025) implicates a different SNP (rs2253310) than this credible set. Red-team-skeptic concurs this is a real absence of corroboration for locus-specific causality, while noting PoPS is a genuinely distinct method from L2G and its signal for FOXO3 should not be silently dropped from consideration, just weighted accordingly.

What would resolve this / raise confidence: an eQTL or sQTL colocalization specifically in airway epithelium or lung tissue implicating ARMC2 (or ruling in/out CEP57L1, SESN1, FOXO3); any genuine (non-fabricated) direct evidence of a respiratory/mucociliary phenotype in ARMC2 loss-of-function models or MMAF patients; or a locus-specific fine-mapping/coloc study that directly adjudicates between ARMC2 and FOXO3 at 6q21. Until then, ARMC2 is the recommended pick on statistical/positional grounds, but this should be read as "very likely the correct gene at this locus" rather than "mechanism understood" — confidence is capped at MODERATE rather than HIGH specifically because the mechanistic/functional-genomics support that would justify HIGH confidence did not survive citation verification.
