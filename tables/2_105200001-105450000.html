Prioritized gene

C2orf49 — Confidence: LOW.

This call is close, and the underlying evidence at this locus is weak and non-differentiating across all three seriously-considered genes; none has direct COPD- or FEV1-specific genetic or mechanistic evidence.

C2orf49 (= TSLIG2/ASW/ashwin) is favored primarily on statistical/positional grounds: it is both the nearest gene and the ABC-prioritized gene, it carries the two highest-PIP credible-set variants at the whole locus (lead variant rs6749911, PIP=0.109, 3'UTR; rs4851758, PIP=0.096, upstream), and it has by far the strongest Open Targets L2G score of the three genes for a respiratory-adjacent disease term ("upper respiratory tract disorder," L2G 0.702 vs. 0.113 for TGFBRAP1 and no evidence at all for FHL2 — see Open Targets summary above, a structured database lookup, not a literature claim). Its verified literature, however, provides no lung-specific mechanism at all: its only characterized molecular roles are as an essential, ubiquitously required subunit of the tRNA splicing ligase complex (PMID 24870230) and a Xenopus neural-developmental factor (PMID 16680723) — neither lung-relevant — plus one weak, correlative, non-COPD bioinformatics finding (PMID 41784837).

The runner-up, TGFBRAP1, has the most extensive lung-fibrosis-specific mechanistic literature of the three genes, but the red-team-skeptic marked it CONTESTED: its foundational mechanistic paper (CHIT1-TGFBRAP1-FOXO3-SMAD7 axis in pulmonary fibrosis, PMID 31085559) was retracted in 2024 after a 2023 correction, and the one surviving non-retracted paper claiming a TGFBRAP1-dependent antifibrotic phenotype (PMID 35679109) depends on the same disputed CHIT1-TGFBRAP1 interaction and should be treated as unreplicated, not independent support. TGFBRAP1 is also strongly LoF-constrained (essential-gene signature) with all its credible-set variants intronic/upstream, and its Open Targets disease profile is dominated by non-respiratory traits.

The second runner-up, FHL2, has real, non-retracted lung-fibrosis mechanistic evidence (bleomycin-model knockdown study, PMID 36641135; IPF biomarker study, PMID 38784225) — the clearest positive structural case of the three genes on its face — but this evidence is drawn entirely from IPF/bleomycin fibrosis models, a pathophysiologically distinct process from COPD's protease-antiprotease/airspace-destruction biology, and FHL2's entire credible-set support is a single, low-PIP (0.041, the lowest at the whole locus), synonymous variant. Open Targets shows zero COPD/FEV1 signal for FHL2, with its dominant genetic signals being cardiac and smoking-initiation.

What would resolve this tie: COPD- or FEV1-specific colocalization/eQTL evidence in lung tissue for any of the three genes (none currently exists in the checked sources); independent, non-retracted replication of a TGFBRAP1-COPD mechanism; or a COPD/emphysema-specific (rather than IPF-specific) functional model implicating FHL2. Until such evidence emerges, this recommendation should be read as "best available positional/statistical candidate," not as a mechanistically established COPD driver gene — the study team should weigh all three genes as live possibilities.
