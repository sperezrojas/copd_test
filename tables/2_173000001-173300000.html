Prioritized gene: MAP3K20 — confidence LOW

MAP3K20 is the best-supported single pick at this locus, but the case is genuinely weak and close, and confidence should not be overstated.

Why MAP3K20 over the alternatives: MAP3K20 is the only candidate gene with a dominant, locus- specific statistical-genetics signal — essentially the entire 29-variant credible set falls in its introns/upstream region, and Open Targets' own L2G model assigns it 0.671 versus 0.296 for the next gene (CDCA7, not itself a candidate here). ITGA6's candidacy is undermined by two independent findings: it has zero genetic-association evidence in Open Targets (score 0.00586, text-mining only) and the researcher's own Ensembl coordinate check found it does not physically overlap this locus at all (it sits 500-750 kb centromeric under GRCh38) — per red-team-skeptic, this pattern is "close to disqualifying" and should be flagged to the study team as a possible genome-build or PoPS-scoring data-quality issue, not treated as a live candidate on the strength of PoPS alone. RAPGEF4 has the single most mechanistically on-target piece of evidence in this entire dossier — Oldenburger et al. 2014's Epac2(-/-) mouse model showing RAPGEF4 promotes cigarette-smoke-induced neutrophilic/macrophage inflammation — but it was not flagged by any of the four internal prioritization methods, has zero statistical-genetics anchoring to this specific credible set (its inclusion rests on ~54 kb of incidental 3'-end overlap), and its one supporting mouse study is directionally complicated by a different mechanistic study in an asthma/Th2 model (Chen et al. 2019).

Why confidence is only LOW, not MODERATE or HIGH: MAP3K20's statistical dominance is undercut by an Open Targets pattern that is, per red-team-skeptic, "close to the textbook profile of a smoking- behavior locus masquerading as a lung-disease locus" — zero colocalization with FEV1/FEV1-FVC despite the strong L2G score, colocalization instead with cognitive/behavioral/educational-attainment traits, and a smoking-initiation association score (0.342) that is actually higher than the COPD association score (0.248) itself. No literature was found directly linking MAP3K20/ZAK to smoking behavior, nicotine dependence, or any COPD/lung-function-specific mechanism — every mechanistic citation for this gene is in the wrong tissue or species for COPD (hepatic/metabolic, skin, cardiac myoblast, leukemic, avian lung, or lung-cancer-cell-line systems), and one of them (Cheng et al. 2009, cardiac MMP/TIMP shift) points in a direction that, if it generalized to lung, would argue against rather than for an emphysema-promoting role. Whether this locus is among the 279 signals in the major Shrine et al. 2019 lung-function GWAS was never confirmed by researcher or skeptic and remains an open, checkable question.

What would resolve this: (1) confirmation of whether rs16861404/this credible set is among the Shrine et al. 2019 lung-function GWAS signals (a direct spirometric-concordance check); (2) a smoking-adjusted or multivariable MR analysis distinguishing a direct pulmonary effect of MAP3K20 from a smoking-behavior-mediated one; (3) resolution of the ITGA6 genome-build/coordinate discrepancy (confirm whether PoPS was run against a different build, or whether this is a genuine PoPS false positive); (4) any human COPD airway tissue or single-cell data on MAP3K20 or RAPGEF4 expression, which is currently absent for both; (5) additional independent contradiction-search for all three genes, which the red-team-skeptic was unable to complete this pass due to an exhausted web-search budget.

Runner-up: RAPGEF4, on the strength of the single Oldenburger et al. 2014 cigarette-smoke knockout-mouse finding, notwithstanding its complete lack of statistical-genetics anchoring to this locus. The study team should weigh whether a locus-specific mouse/human eQTL check for RAPGEF4 in lung tissue is warranted before deciding between MAP3K20 and RAPGEF4, and should treat ITGA6 as provisionally deprioritized pending the coordinate-discrepancy resolution above.
