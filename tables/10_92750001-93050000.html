Prioritized gene

EXOC6 — confidence: LOW.

EXOC6 is named the top pick, but this is a close call that should not be read as strong confidence. The case for EXOC6 rests almost entirely on physical/positional evidence: it is the only protein-coding gene inside the strict locus boundary, and 51 of 54 credible-set variants — including the lead variant rs10786057 (PIP=0.183) and the second-ranked variant (PIP=0.101) — sit in its introns or immediately flanking sequence (see locus-architecture fact above). Red-team-skeptic confirmed this is one of the cleaner nearest-gene cases in this pipeline precisely because there is no competing protein-coding gene physically in the window. Against that, no dedicated GWAS/TWAS/colocalization/MR study linking EXOC6 to COPD or lung function was found in the literature search, no lung-tissue-specific or COPD-specific functional study exists for EXOC6, and the Open Targets structured lookup shows only a weak L2G signal (0.330) for the composite "Chronic Obstructive Asthma" phenotype and nothing for COPD, FEV1/FVC, or lung function directly. The general exocyst-biology literature (Oztan 2007, Lipschutz 2019 review, An et al. 2022 PNAS, Lin/Wu/ Tang 2026 eLife on ciliogenesis) establishes plausible epithelial-trafficking and ciliogenesis mechanisms per priors #2/#3, but every one of these studies is in kidney, generic epithelial, or non-lung ciliogenesis models — none is airway- or lung-tissue- specific, and red-team-skeptic marked the gene-level inference WEAKENED on exactly this basis. Two researcher-identified citations directly claimed to support EXOC6 (Tanaka 2016, Mukerji 2012) were downgraded to CLAIM_MISMATCH by citation-verifier because their actual experiments targeted a different exocyst subunit (EXOC4/Sec8) or did not functionally validate EXOC6 specifically (see audit file) — removing two pieces of evidence the researcher had flagged as only indirect to begin with. No smoking-related-trait evidence exists for EXOC6 in Open Targets, so a smoking-mediated (prior #1) explanation is not supported for this gene either — the evidence is simply silent on mechanism, not adjudicating between direct-lung and smoking-mediated pathways.

Runner-up: PCGF5 — not favored, and the case against it is substantial. PCGF5's candidacy rests entirely on a PoPS score, with zero credible-set variants annotated to it. The orchestrator's independent Ensembl REST lookup confirms PCGF5 sits at 10:91,162,339-91,284,337 — approximately 1.47 Mb centromeric of this locus's window and outside even a widened Ensembl gene-overlap query performed by the researcher. Red-team- skeptic rated this gene's evidence WEAKENED, "bordering on CONTESTED," specifically because this coordinate mismatch functions as de facto contradicting evidence that PCGF5 is the gene "at" this locus at all — not because any single paper argues against it directly. If PCGF5's association is real, the Open Targets pattern (L2G 0.676 for smoking initiation, 0 for all pulmonary traits) is internally consistent with a smoking-mediated pathway per prior #1, and none of PCGF5's verified literature (limb-bud development, B-cell lymphoma chromatin biology) connects to COPD-relevant pulmonary or immune pathways. But given the physical-distance problem, PCGF5 more plausibly reflects a distinct, more centromeric genetic signal (plausibly the GSCAN smoking-initiation locus) than the gene regulated by this locus's fine-mapped COPD credible set.

What would resolve this: a lung-tissue (ideally airway epithelial or ciliated-cell) eQTL/sQTL colocalization analysis of the credible-set variants against EXOC6 expression would substantially strengthen or weaken the EXOC6 call — the current evidence never rises above positional dominance plus non-lung mechanistic plausibility. For PCGF5, confirming or ruling out whether it is genuinely among the GSCAN GWAS's 406 smoking-related loci (the Open Targets L2G signal implies it might be, but this was not independently confirmed in the literature) would clarify whether it belongs to this locus's biology at all or is an artifact of PoPS's genome-wide similarity scoring picking up a nearby-but-distinct locus. Given the LOW confidence in EXOC6 and the substantive, unresolved positional problem with PCGF5, the study team should treat this locus as needing functional follow-up (lung eQTL/ coloc at minimum) before either gene is treated as established.
