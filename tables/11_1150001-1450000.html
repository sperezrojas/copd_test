Prioritized gene

MUC5B — confidence MODERATE.

MUC5B has the strongest locus-specific (not just gene-level) evidence of the three candidates: it is the gene the fine-mapped lead variant (rs2943516, PIP=0.943) actually sits in as a missense change (the highest VEP consequence score of any gene at this position), it is the top-scoring L2G gene at this exact credible set (0.778) against a chronic-lung-disease GWAS trait, and that credible set colocalizes strongly (h4 ~0.96-0.99) with an independent UK Biobank COPD/emphysema/bronchitis diagnosis GWAS — internal consistency across two separately ascertained lung-disease phenotype definitions. General MUC5B mucus biology (Roy et al., PMID 24317696: Muc5b is required for mucociliary clearance and infection control; Van Buren et al., PMID 37352370: an independent MUC5B sputum-protein pQTL is associated with chronic bronchitis and exacerbations) supports a plausible mechanism connecting MUC5B dosage/function to COPD-relevant phenotypes (chronic bronchitis, mucus plugging, exacerbations).

The call is not higher than MODERATE because red-team-skeptic correctly identified a real gap: essentially none of the supporting human-disease literature is about this locus's actual lead variant (rs2943516). The gene's best-replicated human disease signal — the promoter variant rs35705950 — is IPF-specific and explicitly shown NOT to associate with COPD (Goos et al., PMID 35651878). rs2943516 itself has no dedicated functional characterization in the literature found by either the researcher or the skeptic's independent search. This locus also sits in an unusually gene-dense, pleiotropic interval where TOLLIP independently carries its own well-replicated IPF risk alleles (Noth et al., PMID 24429156), raising a genuine alternative hypothesis that part of this "COPD" GWAS signal could reflect IPF/ILD phenotype overlap in biobank-based COPD case ascertainment rather than pure emphysema/airflow-obstruction biology — a possibility this evidence base cannot rule out.

Runner-up: TOLLIP. TOLLIP has real, COPD-specific, smoking-conditional mechanistic evidence at the gene level (Agraval et al., PMID 40056974: cigarette smoke epigenetically suppresses TOLLIP in COPD lungs, impairing antiviral defense) and the highest Open Targets aggregate genetic_association score of the three genes. But its locus-specific case is weaker than MUC5B's: it was not the top L2G gene at the GWAS credible set, its colocalization evidence comes from a skin (not lung) eQTL — exactly the pattern expected from LD/gene-density rather than causal specificity at this interval — and no dedicated TOLLIP COPD genetic-association paper (case-control or otherwise) was found in PubMed at all; its dominant human genetics is IPF.

What would resolve the tie: (1) direct functional characterization of rs2943516 (or a lung-tissue eQTL/sQTL/pQTL specifically implicating either MUC5B or TOLLIP expression, ideally in airway epithelium rather than skin); (2) fine-mapping or colocalization repeated in a COPD cohort with ILD/IPF cases rigorously excluded, to test whether the signal survives removal of possible IPF-phenotype contamination; (3) a dedicated TOLLIP COPD case-control genetic-association study, which currently does not exist in the literature searched.

BRSK2 is not competitive at this locus on current evidence (lowest Open Targets aggregate score, not top L2G anywhere, no pulmonary/immune/smoking-behavior literature), though red-team-skeptic flagged this dismissal as reached via an incomplete search (BRSK2's AMPK-family autophagy/inflammatory-signaling biology, PMID 41258112, was not explored until after critique, and even that citation is non-pulmonary breast-cancer cell-line evidence). This does not change the ranking but is noted as a residual thoroughness caveat for the study team.

This is a recommendation for the study team to weigh, not a final determination — verify independently before acting on it, especially given the WEAKENED verdicts on both top candidates.
