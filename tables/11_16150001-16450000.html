Evidence Table - Locus 11:16150001-16450000 (nearest gene SOX6)

Lead variant: rs7932056 (11:16,360,775, GRCh38, upstream_gene_variant, PIP=0.16, top-PIP variant in a 35-variant credible set). All 35 credible-set variants are annotated gene=SOX6 (intron_variant/upstream_gene_variant, all MODIFIER impact — no missense/splice-donor/ splice-acceptor variants, so no per-variant literature search beyond the lead variant was required). SOX6 is the only candidate gene at this locus: nearest gene, and unanimously FLAMES-, MAGMA-, and PoPS-prioritized. ABC prioritization is NA. SOX6 is a very large gene (~16.0–16.6 Mb on chr11) that spans essentially the entire interval, so the locus is intragenic/immediately flanking to SOX6 itself; no second candidate gene was identified in the region.

Prioritized gene

SOX6 — Confidence: MODERATE (gene assignment), but the association is most parsimoniously explained as smoking-behavior-mediated rather than direct pulmonary biology.

SOX6 is the only candidate gene at this locus, and the gene-level assignment itself is unusually unambiguous: it is unanimously nearest-gene/FLAMES/MAGMA/PoPS-prioritized, and at the lead credible-set variant rs7932056, Open Targets' locus-to-gene model assigns SOX6 a dominant L2G score (~0.89–0.90) in every one of the 8 indexed fine-mapped credible sets that contain this variant, with the next-best candidate gene (PLEKHA7) scoring ≤0.07. There is essentially no LD/ nearest-gene ambiguity to resolve here — this is not a case of two genes in tight LD competing for the signal.

What is NOT well supported is a direct pulmonary mechanism. Three independent, convergent lines of evidence (Open Targets step-1 lookup, the researcher's own GWAS Catalog REST API check, and the red-team-skeptic's independent re-confirmation of the same API) all show: (1) the lead variant rs7932056 is genome-wide significant for smoking cessation (Xu et al. 2020, GCST011702, P=1e-9, OR=0.969) and for no other curated trait; (2) neither of the two flagship discovery GWAS for this exact phenotype pairing — Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019 (COPD, GCST007692, 41 curated hits, none at this locus) or Shrine et al. 2019 (lung function) — contains a genome-wide-significant hit at this locus; and (3) no FEV1/FEV1-FVC/lung-function trait appears anywhere in SOX6's aggregated Open Targets disease-association evidence. This is about as clean a case for prior #1 (smoking-mediated effect, see pipeline priors) as this pipeline is likely to encounter: a COPD GWAS locus whose lead variant is independently, robustly tied to smoking-cessation genetics and absent from primary COPD/lung-function GWAS catalogs.

The two pieces of evidence offered as a possible direct-pulmonary-biology counterweight — Cao et al. 2023's finding that SOX6 marks distal/alveolar epithelial patterning in human embryonic lung, and Pniewska et al. 2015's finding that SOX6 is LPS-inducible specifically in COPD-patient PBMCs — were both flagged by red-team-skeptic as overclaimed if read as establishing an independent pulmonary mechanism: the former is embryonic developmental biology, mechanistically and temporally distant from adult-onset, smoking-triggered COPD pathology (a true SOX6 lung-patterning defect would more plausibly manifest as a rare congenital anomaly than adult COPD susceptibility); the latter is a single exploratory PBMC/LPS study, not lung tissue, using a generic inflammatory stimulus, unreplicated, and vulnerable to confounding by smoking status of the COPD subjects themselves. Neither should be weighted as strongly as the convergent smoking-genetics evidence.

Recommendation for the study team: treat SOX6 as very likely the correct gene at this locus, but treat the COPD GWAS signal itself as most likely reflecting genetic liability to smoking behavior (and possibly a broader neurobehavioral/personality-trait pathway, given colocalization with mood-instability and hair-color traits at the same variant) rather than a COPD-specific or lung-specific causal pathway. This locus is a strong candidate for exclusion or down-weighting in any downstream analysis intended to capture smoking-independent COPD biology, and a strong candidate for inclusion in any analysis specifically interested in smoking-behavior-mediated COPD risk loci. What would raise confidence in a direct pulmonary mechanism: an adult lung-tissue or airway-epithelium eQTL/expression study for SOX6 in COPD cases vs. controls (none was found in this search), or a COPD/FEV1 GWAS credible set at this locus independent of smoking-behavior conditioning (none currently exists in Open Targets' indexing).
