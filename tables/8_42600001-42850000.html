Prioritized gene

Prioritized gene: CHRNB3 — Confidence: LOW

CHRNB3 is the best-supported single gene at this locus among the three candidates, but the support is for the wrong phenotype to license high confidence in a COPD-specific call. It is the ABC- prioritized nearest gene, carries the largest share of credible-set variants of any candidate (including the highest-PIP gene-assigned variants, rs13280301 and rs1530847), and has by far the strongest Open Targets aggregated genetics-backed association among the three genes — but that association is with nicotine dependence (score 0.371, genetics-backed), not COPD (score 0.026, literature-only) or lung function (no FEV1/FVC association recorded at all). This is corroborated by an extensive, independently replicated body of GWAS/candidate-gene literature (Thorgeirsson 2010, Saccone 2009, Saccone 2010, Rice 2012) establishing CHRNB3-CHRNA6 as one of the best-replicated smoking-quantity/nicotine-dependence loci in the genome — with at least one direct non-replication in an independent cohort (Keskitalo-Vuokko 2011) and phenotype-dependence within the positive studies themselves (Rice 2012). No independent spirometric (FEV1/FEV1-FVC) or COPD-genetics-backed evidence was found for CHRNB3 by the researcher, the citation-verifier, or the red-team-skeptic's independent GWAS Catalog cross-check.

Per Prior 1 (smoking-mediated effects), since the COPD GWAS underlying this locus is not adjusted for smoking behavior, the entire weight of evidence assembled here is consistent with — and does not rebut — the interpretation that this locus's COPD association is mediated through smoking initiation/nicotine dependence rather than an independent pulmonary mechanism. This is the red-team-skeptic's explicit locus-level conclusion as well.

Runner-up: CHRNA6 is nominally a runner-up by virtue of sharing the same LD block and locus-level smoking-behavior citations, but its gene-specific support is far weaker (Open Targets nicotine score 0.017 literature-only vs. CHRNB3's 0.371 genetics-backed; its only credible-set variant assignment, rs7001753, sits at PIP=0.0015 — roughly two orders of magnitude below the lead variant, at the noise floor for fine-mapping). THAP1 is not a meaningful competitor on current evidence: its candidacy rests entirely on MAGMA/PoPS statistical prioritization with zero corroborating COPD, lung-function, smoking, or lung-tissue eQTL/colocalization evidence of any kind, despite genuine gene-level constraint (LoF oe ~0.30) that would make it a plausible causal gene if any orthogonal lung-relevant evidence existed.

What would resolve this: (1) a documented independent FEV1/FEV1-FVC or COPD-genetics-backed (not literature-only) association at 8p11, ideally from a lung-function GWAS explicitly checked against its supplementary tables (the one candidate here, Shrine et al. 2019, could not be confirmed either way and should not be used); (2) lung-tissue (airway epithelium, alveolar, or lung-relevant immune cell) eQTL/colocalization data for CHRNB3, CHRNA6, or THAP1 specifically, none of which currently exists in Open Targets for this locus; (3) a COPD case-control or lung-function association from a study design that adjusts for smoking behavior, to test whether any signal survives independent of smoking mediation. Absent that, this locus should be reported to the study team as a smoking-behavior-mediated candidate with CHRNB3 as the best single-gene guess, not as a resolved pulmonary-biology finding.
