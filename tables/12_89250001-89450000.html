Evidence Table — Locus 12:89250001-89450000

Lead variant: rs704074 (12:89348954, PIP=0.20, 3' UTR variant in DUSP6) Candidate gene(s): DUSP6 (nearest gene, ABC-prioritized gene; ~28/30 credible-set variants annotate to it) and POC1B (added by a genome-build correction to the study team's MAGMA/PoPS gene-location merge — see "Targeted re-opening" note below). ~2 of 30 credible-set variants are non-coding/intergenic (gene=-) and are not separately assessed, per pipeline scope.

This locus was originally reviewed as a single-candidate-gene locus (DUSP6 only, final call DUSP6/LOW confidence). A genome-build error in the MAGMA/PoPS gene-location merge used to generate loci/copd-all-gene-prioritization.txt was subsequently discovered, and a corrected re-merge surfaced POC1B as a second candidate gene at this locus. An independent physical-overlap check (Ensembl/Open Targets coordinates, same GRCh38 build) confirmed POC1B (chr12:89,419,718-89,526,135, minus strand) does physically overlap the broad locus window (12:89,250,001-89,450,000) at its extreme 3' edge (~30kb, 89,419,718-89,450,000), but does not overlap the actual fine-mapped credible-set span (12:89,327,388- 89,382,708); the nearest credible-set variant is >35kb from POC1B's boundary, and no credible-set variant annotates to POC1B. Because POC1B does physically enter the locus window (unlike a clean "doesn't overlap at all" case), it was carried forward for full literature research per the re-opening instructions. That research (below) found no COPD/lung-relevant evidence for POC1B; DUSP6 remains the sole defensible candidate. See the audit appendix for the full reasoning trail, including the red-team-skeptic's explicit verdict that POC1B is "a statistical/positional artifact of a gene-location merge, not a biological rival."

Open Targets summary (structured database lookup — NOT a verified literature citation; did not go through citation-verifier or red-team-skeptic)

DUSP6 (ENSG00000139318)

  • Genetic constraint (gnomAD): LoF oe ≈ 0.23 (95% CI 0.12-0.45) — strongly constrained against loss-of-function variation, consistent with dosage sensitivity/haploinsufficiency intolerance. Missense oe ≈ 0.88 (mild depletion). Synonymous oe ≈ 1.13 (as expected, neutral).
  • Overall Open Targets association score for COPD (MONDO_0005002): 0.371 (rank ~10th of 540 diseases associated with DUSP6 genome-wide); genetic_association sub-score 0.603, literature sub-score 0.143. For comparison: DUSP6's single highest-scoring disease association is Kallmann syndrome/hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (0.71). "Smoking initiation" scores 0.387 (genetic_association 0.636) — essentially tied with, and ranking immediately above, COPD. Other top associations: ADHD/cannabis use, substance abuse, insomnia, atrial fibrillation, coronary artery disease, body composition/lean mass, hernia, creatinine/eGFR — a broad, pleiotropic, largely non-pulmonary profile.
  • Locus-to-gene (L2G) at lead variant rs704074: Across 47 fine-mapped credible sets from independent GWAS/QTL studies overlapping this variant, DUSP6 is the top (and essentially only) L2G-predicted gene in every study with a computed L2G score (~0.78-0.86), including a DIRECT hit in an independent COPD-diagnosis GWAS (GCST90476027, "Chronic airway obstruction" PheCode 496): L2G = 0.807. No competing gene scores comparably in any of the 47 studies (spanning lean body mass, creatinine/ eGFR, hernia, atrial fibrillation, ADHD/cannabis-use, insomnia, body composition, basal metabolic rate, and COPD). This consistency is a double-edged finding (see Prioritized gene section below): it strongly ties this variant's regulatory/functional effect to DUSP6 specifically, but the same consistency across dozens of unrelated traits means the L2G signal alone does not distinguish a COPD-specific mechanism from a generic, trait-agnostic regulatory/proximity signal.
  • Lung-function trait gap: No FEV1, FEV1/FVC, or other spirometric-trait credible set was found among the 47 studies overlapping rs704074 in Open Targets' current index. This is an absence in this specific database snapshot, not confirmed absence in all primary literature — the researcher searched PubMed directly for this and also did not find a qualifying paper (see evidence table above).
  • Molecular QTL colocalization at this variant/window (12:89349561-89351640, ~DUSP6's 3' UTR/downstream region): splicing QTL (leafcutter) for DUSP6 in neutrophils and monocytes (BLUEPRINT); splicing QTL in adipose subcutaneous (GTEx) and iPSCs (HipSci); gene-expression QTL for DUSP6 in lymphoblastoid cell lines (TwinsUK). No lung-tissue or airway-epithelium eQTL/sQTL for DUSP6 was found in this snapshot (nor in the researcher's separate PubMed search for GTEx-lung/bronchial-epithelium literature).

POC1B (ENSG00000139323) — added in this targeted re-opening

  • Physical overlap: confirmed coordinates chr12:89,419,718-89,526,135 (minus strand). Overlaps the broad locus window (12:89,250,001-89,450,000) only at its extreme 3' edge (~30kb, 89,419,718-89,450,000; excludes the promoter/TSS at the high-coordinate end). Does not overlap the fine-mapped credible-set span (12:89,327,388-89,382,708); nearest credible-set variant is >35kb from POC1B's boundary. No credible-set variant annotates to POC1B.
  • Genetic constraint (gnomAD): LoF oe ≈ 0.705 (95% CI 0.522-0.967) — mildly constrained, substantially less constrained than DUSP6 (oe 0.23). Missense oe ≈ 0.96 (essentially unconstrained). Synonymous oe ≈ 0.92 (neutral).
  • Overall Open Targets association profile: full list of all 221 diseases associated with POC1B genome-wide was retrieved. COPD, smoking initiation, lung function, and asthma do not appear anywhere in this list — not even at a score of 0. Top association: cone-rod dystrophy (0.77). Other notable associations: retinal dystrophy (0.51), Hodgkin lymphoma (0.34), Joubert syndrome (0.26), Senior-Loken syndrome (0.18), a long tail (~100 terms) of "spermatogenic failure N" (0.04-0.09), primary ciliary dyskinesia (0.034), ciliopathy (0.015) — a coherent retinal/centriole/ciliopathy-gene profile, with no lung-relevant signal at any strength.
  • Locus-to-gene (L2G) at lead variant rs704074: POC1B does not appear as an L2G-predicted gene in any of the same 47 independent GWAS/QTL credible sets where DUSP6 scores 0.78-0.86 (including the direct COPD study, L2G=0.807 for DUSP6). POC1B simply does not surface as a candidate gene in Open Targets' L2G output at this variant.
  • Molecular QTL note: a BLUEPRINT neutrophil splicing-QTL cluster exists within the POC1B gene body at 12:89,459,718-89,466,770 — but this is downstream of both the locus window boundary (89,450,000) and the credible-set span (max 89,382,708), i.e. it does not overlap this locus's actual fine-mapped variants. The red-team-skeptic flagged this as a "loose thread" (a hypothetical long-range regulatory link was not formally excluded via Hi-C/TAD-boundary analysis) but assessed it as speculative and unsupported by any COPD/lung phenotype linkage in POC1B's own disease-association data.

Prioritized gene

Gene: DUSP6 Confidence: LOW

DUSP6 is named by every locus-definition criterion available (nearest gene, ABC-prioritized gene, and the annotation of ~28/30 credible-set variants), and Open Targets' L2G analysis gives it the strongest, most consistent gene-level score of any gene at this locus (0.78- 0.86 across 47 independent studies, including L2G=0.807 in a direct COPD-diagnosis GWAS).

This targeted re-opening evaluated POC1B, a second candidate gene surfaced by a genome-build correction to the MAGMA/PoPS gene-location merge, and found it does not change this call. POC1B physically enters the broad locus-prioritization window only at its extreme 3' edge (~30kb) and does not overlap the fine-mapped credible-set span at all (nearest credible-set variant >35kb away); it is not L2G-predicted at the lead variant in any of the same 47 studies where DUSP6 dominates; and a full literature search (22 citations, 21 VERIFIED) found zero COPD, lung-function, or smoking-behavior evidence for POC1B anywhere, plus an explicitly-tested and rejected PCD/airway-ciliopathy alternative hypothesis. The red-team-skeptic's verdict for POC1B was NOT SUPPORTED, characterizing it as "a statistical/positional artifact of a gene-location merge, not a biological rival" — this is not a close call or a tie requiring a runner-up designation; POC1B fails to clear even a minimal bar for consideration. DUSP6 remains the sole defensible candidate gene at this locus, and — per the skeptic's fresh critique — this re-opening exercise modestly increases confidence in DUSP6 as the correct gene at this locus specifically (since the one plausible rival gene tested does not compete for L2G at this variant at all, arguing against pure nearest-gene-default behavior), even though it does not resolve the separate, still-open question of mechanism.

The confidence in DUSP6 as reflecting a genuine, COPD-specific pulmonary or immune mechanism (rather than a generic locus effect) remains LOW, for reasons the red-team critique lays out concretely (unchanged from the original review):

  1. Absence from spirometry GWAS. DUSP6 is absent from the genome-wide-significant loci of the 2014 COPD GWAS (PMID 24621683) and is not named among the discussed/exemplar genes of the 2023 definitive multi-ancestry lung-function GWAS (PMID 36914875), which named 559 causal genes — a low bar DUSP6 apparently did not clear among the highlighted set. No FEV1/FEV1-FVC signal was found at this variant in Open Targets' L2G index or in direct literature search. This directly engages Prior 2 (spirometric concordance): its absence here is a real gap, not just a search-thoroughness artifact, given how comprehensive the Shrine et al. resource is.
  2. Undifferentiated L2G — plausible "nearest constrained gene" artifact, only partially addressed by ruling out POC1B. DUSP6 tops L2G for essentially every trait fine-mapped near this variant, from hernia to atrial fibrillation to insomnia to COPD, with no trait-specific differentiation. The fresh critique notes that POC1B's total absence from L2G at this variant is modest evidence against a purely indiscriminate nearest-gene default (if L2G just picked "whatever's closest," POC1B might occasionally surface), but this does not fully resolve whether DUSP6 wins on true causal biology versus winning by default against a regulatory-poor neighborhood on the other side.
  3. Smoking-initiation/neurobehavioral pleiotropy is essentially tied with COPD. Open Targets' own aggregate score for smoking initiation (0.387) narrowly exceeds and ranks immediately above COPD (0.371) for this gene, and DUSP6's single strongest disease association by far is Kallmann syndrome/GnRH-neuron biology (0.71), independently corroborated by two hypogonadotropic-hypogonadism candidate-gene screens (PMID 40508017, PMID 38593951) and by an independent bipolar-disorder genetic association at the same haplotype block (PMID 22155192). This directly engages Prior 1: a smoking-initiation/neurobehavioral mechanism is at least as plausible, on current evidence, as a direct pulmonary mechanism, and the single fact that would help resolve this — whether rs704074/DUSP6 reaches genome-wide significance for smoking initiation in GSCAN (PMID 30643251) — could not be confirmed by either the researcher or the citation-verifier within this session's tool budget. This is the single highest-value follow-up before this call can be strengthened or weakened further.
  4. Immune evidence is real but not lung/COPD-specific. DUSP6 clearly modulates inflammation in multiple systems (endothelial ICAM-1/neutrophil recruitment in a sepsis-model lung injury; myeloid-cell arthritogenicity; vitamin-D/PBMC innate-immune hub gene), engaging Prior 3, but none of this evidence is airway tissue or COPD-specific, and one paper (PMID 30416500) explicitly found DUSP6's role non-essential in its system. The one closest-to-relevant structured signal — neutrophil/monocyte splicing QTL at this exact variant — is Open Targets database evidence, not verified literature.
  5. The one clearly on-topic mechanistic paper (PMID 31557398, TUG1/miR-145-5p/DUSP6 in cigarette-smoke-induced airway remodeling) is genuinely the best direct pulmonary evidence found, but it is single-group, unreplicated, uses an lncRNA-sponge mechanism class with a documented reproducibility history, and its direction of effect (more DUSP6 = harmful) sits in tension with the ERK-activity-reducing missense variants characterized in PMID 22155192 — a coherence problem, not just a footnote.

Runner-up: none. POC1B was formally evaluated in this re-opening as a build-correction candidate and rejected (NOT SUPPORTED) — it is not carried forward as a runner-up because it has no non-trivial independent support (0/30 credible-set variants, 0/47 L2G studies, 0/22 literature citations connecting it to COPD/lung/smoking). This remains a single-gene locus where the open question is not "which gene" (now doubly confirmed) but "which biological pathway" (direct pulmonary/immune vs. smoking-behavioral/neurodevelopmental) explains why this variant associates with COPD risk at all.

What would resolve this: (a) direct confirmation of whether rs704074/the DUSP6 region reaches genome-wide significance for smoking initiation in GSCAN/GWAS Catalog (the single highest-value follow-up per the red-team critique); (b) a direct check of the Shrine et al. 2023 full supplementary gene table (559 genes) for DUSP6's presence/absence and its variant-to-gene mapping criteria score, which was not accessible in this session; (c) an independent replication of the TUG1/DUSP6 airway-remodeling mechanism, or any lung-tissue/ airway-epithelium eQTL/sQTL data for DUSP6, to corroborate a lung-autonomous mechanism distinguishable from the smoking-behavior/neurodevelopmental explanation. POC1B does not require further follow-up absent new fine-mapping or functional data specifically implicating it.

The study team should weigh this recommendation accordingly: DUSP6 is the correct gene to carry forward for this locus (POC1B, the only rival candidate ever surfaced, has been formally evaluated and ruled out), but DUSP6 should not yet be presented as a confirmed COPD-pulmonary-mechanism gene without the above follow-up.
