Prioritized-gene synthesis

Prioritized gene: ARHGEF18. Confidence: LOW.

The positional case for ARHGEF18 is essentially unambiguous: all 55 credible-set variants (ABF fine-mapping) sit in its introns, and it is the only gene at this locus with any Open Targets L2G prediction at all — PEX11G, the PoPS-prioritized alternative, has no fine-mapping overlap and no L2G signal whatsoever (see Open Targets summary above), and the researcher found essentially no COPD/lung/smoking/immune-relevant literature for PEX11G (evidence table rows above, all gene-level HOLDS). On pure "which gene does the credible set point to" grounds, ARHGEF18 is the clear pick over PEX11G and over the other regional genes (MCOLN1, PNPLA6, SAXO5, ZNF358), none of which have any fine-mapping or L2G support and are included in the table only as background.

However, the red-team-skeptic's critique (preserved in full in audits/19_7350001-7550000_audit.md) identifies a real and unresolved tension that keeps confidence at LOW rather than HIGH or MODERATE: every genome-wide-significant trait Open Targets has indexed at this locus is lipid/hematological (HDL cholesterol, white blood cell count, neutrophil count, blood bicarbonate, waist circumference) — not COPD, and not any lung-function/spirometry trait, which per this pipeline's prior #2 would be the strongest corroborating evidence for a pulmonary mechanism. The ARHGEF18 fibroblast eQTL colocalizes only with those same non-pulmonary traits (h4 0.64–0.77), not with COPD. The structural/epithelial-biology literature for ARHGEF18 (rows above), while substantial and well-replicated, is drawn overwhelmingly from non-airway tissue contexts (retina, placenta, breast-cancer cell line, generic non-airway ciliated cells, and a null cardiac knockout) — only two of the twelve structural citations are actually in bronchial epithelium (PMID 28512143, 23648482), and the immune evidence is scattered across eosinophil/type-2, monocyte-associative, and disease-nonspecific findings that do not cleanly match COPD's more classically neutrophilic/Th1/Tc17 inflammatory programs. No COPD-specific GWAS, TWAS, MR, or lung-function association was found in the literature for ARHGEF18 at all (absence of evidence, explicitly noted by the researcher), and no smoking-behavior trait is associated with this locus either — so the signal cannot currently be cleanly assigned to either the "smoking-mediated" or "lung-function-mediated" pathway this pipeline's priors describe.

The runner-up, PEX11G, is not a close competitor on the evidence assembled — it lacks fine-mapping support, L2G support, and any disease-relevant literature — so this is not a tied call between two genes in LD. It is instead a confident gene assignment (ARHGEF18) paired with genuine uncertainty about the assigned gene's mechanism: this locus may reflect airway-epithelial/RhoA-junction biology as the researcher's structural evidence suggests, or it may equally plausibly be a hematological/neutrophil-count pleiotropy signal (COPD patients have altered circulating neutrophil counts) that reaches significance in the COPD GWAS through that route rather than through lung-structural pathobiology — the assembled evidence does not distinguish between these two mechanistic hypotheses. What would resolve this: (1) a direct COPD or FEV1/FEV1-FVC colocalization or L2G analysis at this locus once available (absent from Open Targets currently), (2) an airway-tissue (not fibroblast) eQTL/sQTL colocalization for ARHGEF18 specifically with COPD or lung function, and (3) Mendelian-randomization or multi-trait analysis testing whether the COPD association here is fully explained by neutrophil-count/hematological mediation. Given these gaps, ARHGEF18 is recommended as the best-supported single gene at this locus by positional/fine-mapping and structural-biology criteria, but the study team should treat the mechanism (and therefore the biological interpretation) as unresolved, not confirmed.
