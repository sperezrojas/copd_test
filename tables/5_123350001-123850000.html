Prioritized-gene synthesis

Prioritized gene: CEP120. Confidence: LOW.

This call is close, and the pipeline's own red-team-skeptic review pushed back hard on it, so the reasoning below tries to be transparent about exactly how much weight each piece of evidence can bear.

In favor of CEP120: both independent gene-prioritization methods used by this pipeline (FLAMES and ABC) point to CEP120 rather than the nearest gene, and Open Targets' own L2G-like aggregation is markedly stronger for CEP120 than CSNK1G3 for both COPD (0.761 vs 0.096) and FEV1 (up to 0.840 across 14 rows vs. 0.079-0.081 across 2 rows) (Open Targets summary table above). CEP120 also has a well-replicated, VERIFIED Mendelian-genetics literature base (PMID 25361962, 29847808, 27208211, 30988386) establishing that loss of CEP120 causes centriole/ciliogenesis defects and a skeletal ciliopathy (JATD) with a mechanistically plausible, non-smoking-mediated route to lung pathology (per this pipeline's prior #2, structural/pulmonary mechanisms are to be weighed alongside COPD case-control signal).

Against a confident call: the red-team-skeptic identified a genuine, unresolved architecture and phenotype gap that this evidence base does not bridge. Every verified CEP120 functional citation concerns rare, biallelic loss-of-function/hypomorphic mutations causing a distinct monogenic disease (JATD/Joubert syndrome) with restrictive (not obstructive) thoracic/lung physiology -- not the common noncoding regulatory variant (rs35390568, PIP 0.092, downstream_gene_variant) actually driving this GWAS credible set. No verified citation demonstrates that common CEP120 regulatory variation, partial/heterozygous dosage effects, or airway epithelial ciliary dysfunction specifically produces an obstructive, COPD-relevant phenotype. The one citation that came closest to bridging rare-disease genetics with the qualitative phenotype in question (PMID 37547106) was flagged CLAIM_MISMATCH by citation-verifier (the researcher's claim of "no CEP120-specific finding" was itself wrong -- the paper does report a CEP120 variant in a JBTS patient with breathing abnormalities -- but this remains rare-variant JBTS evidence, not a common-variant/GWAS bridge, and per pipeline rules cannot be used in the main table above as originally framed). The red-team-skeptic also raised a plausible alternative explanation for the L2G scores themselves (gene-distance/chromatin-annotation features rather than curated mechanism) and flagged that the credible-set variants near CEP120 sit in intergenic/non-coding-transcript regions near a pseudogene (KRT18P16), leaving open the unexplored possibility that the functional element regulates something other than CEP120 (KRT18P16 itself, or a currently unannotated element, or even acts at a distance on CSNK1G3).

Runner-up: CSNK1G3. It is the physically nearest gene and contains the lead variant and most high-PIP credible-set variants intronically, and is moderately constrained for LoF (LOEUF ~0.62, more constrained than CEP120's ~0.93). However, despite dedicated literature searches across statistical genetics, structural/developmental, immune, and smoking-behavior domains, no VERIFIED citation links CSNK1G3 to any lung-relevant biology; all four verified citations are off-target (renal MR, population-genetics selection scan, dairy-cattle GWAS, cancer-cell-line kinome screen), and its own Open Targets L2G scores are markedly weaker than CEP120's at this same locus.

What would resolve the tie: (1) a lung-tissue or airway-epithelium eQTL/sQTL colocalization analysis for CEP120 and CSNK1G3 at rs35390568 specifically (neither the researcher nor the skeptic could complete this given tool/search-budget limitations this session); (2) direct lookup of this locus (chr5:123.6 Mb, rs35390568, CEP120, CSNK1G3) in the Shrine et al. 2019 and Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019 primary supplementary tables, which the researcher attempted but could not complete via available full-text tools; (3) any study of heterozygous CEP120 carriers or common CEP120 regulatory variation and airway/spirometric phenotype, as opposed to biallelic monogenic ciliopathy. Until at least one of these is available, CEP120 should be treated as "statistically prioritized, mechanistically unproven" rather than a confidently established causal gene, and this LOW confidence rating should not be read by the study team as a recommendation against further investigation -- rather, it flags exactly where the next unit of evidence would be most decisive.
