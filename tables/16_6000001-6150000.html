Prioritized-gene synthesis

Prioritized gene: RBFOX1 Confidence: LOW

RBFOX1 is named here not because the evidence strongly supports a specific COPD mechanism, but because it is the only candidate gene physically present at this locus — every credible-set variant (11/11, all intronic, no missense/splice variants) falls within RBFOX1 itself, and all four annotation methods that returned a call (nearest gene, FLAMES, PoPS) point to RBFOX1, with MAGMA and ABC returning no gene at all rather than disagreeing with RBFOX1. There is no tied runner-up gene to weigh against it; the uncertainty here is not about which gene but about what this signal means.

The evidentiary picture is weaker than it first appeared. Two of the three GWAS citations initially assembled to support either a direct pulmonary mechanism (Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019, the largest published COPD case-control GWAS) or a smoking-behavior mechanism (Liu et al. 2019, the GSCAN smoking/alcohol GWAS) were found by citation-verifier to be CLAIM_MISMATCH: RBFOX1 does not appear in either paper's reported loci, despite being the two most directly relevant, best-powered studies available. The companion lung-function GWAS (Shrine et al. 2019) was also CLAIM_MISMATCH for the same reason. Combined with Open Targets showing zero COPD-disease-association evidence and no colocalization for RBFOX1, the case for a lung-function/COPD-specific genetic signal at this gene is not merely unsupported — it is actively unconfirmed by the two studies best positioned to confirm it.

What remains, per red-team-skeptic's critique, is a narrower and weaker smoking-mediation hypothesis than originally framed: two verified citations (Chen et al. 2020, PMID 31294817; Chen et al. 2016, PMID 27164557) tie RBFOX1 to nicotine-dependence severity phenotypes (FTND, TTFC, cotinine) rather than smoking initiation or heaviness — the phenotypes most directly relevant to COPD risk. This is a real but modest signal, not a demonstrated mediating pathway, and neither paper localizes to this exact credible set. No verified lung/airway-specific structural, developmental, or immune biology exists for RBFOX1 at all; the remaining immune-adjacent citations (Gao et al. 2026, Ding et al. 2013, Dery et al. 2023, Chen et al. 2024) are non-lung, enrichment-level, review-derived, or peripheral to their source papers' main findings.

The red-team-skeptic additionally raised two alternative explanations that this evidence base cannot rule out: (1) RBFOX1 is one of the largest genes in the genome (~1.7 Mb) in a low-recombination region, and its broad, scattershot pleiotropy across unrelated traits (blood pressure, periodontal disease, bladder remodeling, asthma-rank, schizophrenia, addiction) is at least as consistent with a statistical/LD-magnet false-positive pattern as with genuine multi-organ biology; (2) RBFOX1 could have an entirely uncharacterized lung-relevant splicing function (its paralog RBFOX2 has described roles in epithelial splicing programs) that simply has not been studied — absence of lung literature is not evidence of absence of lung function.

Given: no confirmed COPD/lung-function GWAS support (and two explicit disconfirmations), no lung-specific structural or immune biology, and a smoking-mediation hypothesis resting on narrower evidence than initially claimed, this locus should be reported to the study team as a single-gene locus (RBFOX1) with an unresolved mechanism — plausibly smoking-behavior-related, but not established, and a real possibility of a statistical/LD artifact given the gene's size and pleiotropic promiscuity. Confidence is LOW rather than INSUFFICIENT only because gene assignment itself (RBFOX1 as the physically implicated gene) is essentially unambiguous; it is the causal mechanism, not the gene identity, that remains unresolved. What would resolve this: (a) full-text confirmation of whether RBFOX1/this exact region reaches significance in a smoking-adjusted COPD or lung-function GWAS; (b) a smoking-initiation-specific (not just nicotine-dependence-severity) GWAS hit at this locus; (c) any lung/airway single-cell or functional dataset directly testing RBFOX1.
