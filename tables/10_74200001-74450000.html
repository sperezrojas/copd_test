Prioritized gene

ADK — Confidence: LOW.

ADK is the only protein-coding gene physically within the credible-set interval (confirmed by Ensembl REST overlap lookup — all 34 fine-mapped credible-set variants, including the PIP=0.151 lead SNP rs7919010, are intronic to ADK), and it is the consistent, dominant top locus-to-gene (L2G) prediction at the lead variant across all 55 independent GWAS/QTL credible sets Open Targets has indexed there. On pure positional and statistical-genetic fine-mapping grounds, ADK is the clear candidate gene at this locus, and MICU1 — the only competing candidate nominated by this pipeline's MAGMA/PoPS methods — is convincingly de-prioritized: it sits ~1.5 Mb outside the credible-set interval, was never a top L2G gene at the lead variant, and has zero Open Targets COPD disease-association evidence of any kind (see Open Targets summary above). That exclusion holds even after red-team critique surfaced a genuine, previously-missed piece of MICU1 lung biology (Ali et al. 2022, PMID 35050901 — MICU1 required for AT2-to-AT1 alveolar regeneration) — real cell-type-specific biology, but not evidence that this specific fine-mapped signal acts through MICU1 rather than through ADK.

The LOW confidence qualifier reflects that the case for ADK is strong on "which gene is physically/statistically implicated" but weak on "via what pathway, and is it lung biology at all." Both the researcher and red-team-skeptic agree: (1) the two citations that would most directly establish or rule out ADK as a genome-wide-significant COPD/lung-function locus (Sakornsakolpat 2019 COPD GWAS, Shrine 2019 lung-function GWAS) came back CLAIM_MISMATCH — the researcher's negative claim about ADK's absence from those loci tables was not actually confirmed from accessible text, so this pipeline has no verified statement either way from the two largest published COPD/lung-function GWAS; (2) Open Targets' own indexed evidence at the exact lead SNP is overwhelmingly BMI/adiposity/cardiometabolic, not pulmonary, and carries zero genetic-association-datatype evidence for COPD; (3) ADK's own best-established, VERIFIED literature is metabolic/adiposity-regulating (hepatocyte, endothelial, beta-cell knockout studies), not pulmonary; (4) the only pulmonary-mechanistic literature identified studies a related pathway gene (ADA) or receptor (ADORA2B), not ADK itself, and is explicitly pathway-level, directionally-plausible-but-untested support, per the skeptic's WEAKENED verdict; (5) three independent VERIFIED Mendelian-randomization studies establish that BMI/body-fat causally reduces lung function and increases COPD risk — per the skeptic, this makes an adiposity-mediated pathway (real, causal, but indirect pulmonary physiology) currently the better-supported explanation for any COPD association at this locus than a direct lung-tissue mechanism specific to ADK.

A secondary, unresolved gap: PLAU (urokinase, ~290 kb outside the interval, upstream of ADK) was flagged by the red-team-skeptic as likely under-searched by the researcher (probable search-term artifact missing the well-established uPA/uPAR protein literature in COPD/emphysema — see evidence table above). PLAU was not assessed for L2G/colocalization support at this locus and should not be treated as ruled out; it is not, however, competitive with ADK on current positional/L2G evidence, so it does not change the top pick here.

What would resolve this: (a) direct confirmation (full locus-table check, not abstract-only) of whether rs7919010/ADK reaches genome-wide significance in the Sakornsakolpat 2019 COPD meta-analysis or Shrine 2019 lung-function GWAS; (b) a colocalization or multivariable-MR analysis formally testing whether this locus's COPD association (if any exists in the internal GWAS driving this pipeline) is mediated by BMI/adiposity rather than acting directly on lung tissue; (c) an ADK-specific (not ADA/ADORA2B pathway-level) functional study in airway or alveolar tissue; (d) a proper re-search and L2G/positional evaluation of PLAU. Until then, ADK should be reported to the study team as "the locus's implicated gene by fine-mapping and L2G, but with a live, currently better-evidenced alternative hypothesis that this is a metabolic/adiposity locus with only indirect, BMI-mediated relevance to COPD" rather than as a confirmed pulmonary-biology COPD gene.
