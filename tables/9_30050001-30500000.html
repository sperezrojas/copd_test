Open Targets summary (structured database lookup — NOT verified literature; see methods note)

This section reports a structured GraphQL lookup against the Open Targets Platform. It did not go through citation-verifier or red-team-skeptic and should not be read as a literature claim.

  • Locus-level (lead variant rs2031830 / 9_30314034_G_A): Open Targets indexes 5 credible sets for this exact variant, none of which is the (unpublished/internal) COPD study under review. The indexed studies are 4 separate osteoarthritis GWAS-Catalog studies (GCST90134288, GCST90134286, GCST90134284, GCST90134290) and 1 maximum habitual alcohol consumption study (GCST90244185). No L2G (locus-to-gene) prediction is resolved for any of these five credible sets — Open Targets has not computationally assigned a prioritized gene to this variant for any indexed trait. No FEV1/FVC or COPD credible set is indexed for this variant at all.
  • LINGO2 (ENSG00000174482, protein-coding, ~836 kb from locus): Genetic constraint: lof oe=0.457 (90% CI 0.301-0.714, score 0.071); mis oe=0.935; syn oe=1.053 — modest LoF intolerance signal with a wide interval. Of 273 associated diseases, top scores are obesity disorder (0.44), type 2 diabetes (0.44), diabetes mellitus (0.44), restless legs syndrome (0.35), smoking initiation (0.33, rank 7), preeclampsia, migraine, ALS, smoking behavior (0.27), sleep apnea, smoking cessation (0.21). COPD scores only 0.0078 (near the bottom of the list); FEV1/FVC has no recorded association for LINGO2 at all.
  • ACO1 (ENSG00000122729, protein-coding, ~1.88 Mb from locus): Genetic constraint: lof oe=0.681 (CI 0.555-0.839, effectively unconstrained by this metric); mis oe=0.823; syn oe=0.882. Of 435 associated diseases, top score is smoking initiation (0.27), then male infertility, pleural empyema, pneumothorax (0.255), restless legs syndrome, smoking behavior (0.22). Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis scores 0.079, pulmonary fibrosis 0.02, pulmonary hypertension 0.03, chronic lung disease 0.016, COPD only 0.0115 (mid-to-low of 435); FEV1/FVC has no recorded association for ACO1 either.
  • LINC01242 (ENSG00000280683, lncRNA, within the locus): No genetic-constraint data (not computed for non-coding genes). Only 2 associated diseases with any nonzero score in the entire Open Targets database (breast carcinoma 0.042, hepatocellular carcinoma 0.0015), both irrelevant to COPD/lung biology. No COPD, FEV1/FVC, or smoking-trait association recorded at all.

Prioritized gene

Prioritized gene: none — INSUFFICIENT confidence to name a single candidate.

This locus does not support a defensible single-gene call. The reasons, referencing the rows above:

  • The credible set itself is unresolved (max PIP 0.094 at the lead variant; no missense/splice variant anywhere in ~110 variants), and the internal prioritization pipeline (FLAMES, MAGMA, PoPS, ABC-model) independently found nothing to nominate at this locus — all four returned NA.
  • LINGO2, the nearest protein-coding gene (~836 kb away), was marked CONTESTED by the skeptic: its one potentially lung-relevant citation (Hansel et al. 2015) both failed citation-verifier detail checks (CLAIM_MISMATCH) and — even taken at face value — describes a genome-wide-significant signal that does not spatially overlap this locus's credible-set interval. The remainder of LINGO2's verified literature is neurobehavioral (essential tremor, Parkinson disease, neuroticism), and the Open Targets summary above shows LINGO2's own strongest disease associations are smoking initiation/behavior/cessation, with COPD near the bottom of 273 associated diseases and no FEV1/FVC association at all.
  • ACO1, the other flanking gene (~1.88 Mb away, i.e., roughly twice as far and on the far side of a large gene desert), was marked WEAKENED by the skeptic: its only lung-adjacent citations concern idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (a mechanistically and clinically distinct, restrictive rather than obstructive, disease) and a different paralog (ACO2) in lung cancer. No ACO1-specific COPD/FEV1-FVC GWAS, TWAS, or colocalization evidence exists in the literature, and Open Targets shows the same smoking-weighted, COPD/FEV1-FVC-poor profile as LINGO2.
  • LINC01242, the only gene the credible set itself annotates, has zero PubMed-indexed literature and almost no Open Targets disease-association data; its credible-set annotation covers under 2% of total PIP mass and is judged by the skeptic to be essentially an artifact of variant-transcript overlap rather than a biologically meaningful signal.
  • No Hi-C/ABC/eQTL literature connects this interval to any other, more distant gene.

Runner-up ranking, if forced to choose one for further investigation: LINGO2 over ACO1 over LINC01242, purely on physical proximity (836 kb vs. 1.88 Mb vs. within-locus-but-uncharacterized) — but this ordering is not supported by any gene-specific, locus-overlapping literature evidence for any of the three, and the researcher and skeptic both independently raise the alternative possibility that this signal is either (a) a statistical false positive from a poorly-resolved, non-smoking-adjusted COPD GWAS, or (b) a real signal mediated by smoking-related behavior/neurobiology rather than pulmonary structural or immune biology, given the concordant smoking-trait enrichment seen for both flanking genes in Open Targets and LINGO2's own verified neurobehavioral literature.

What would resolve this: (1) a COPD/FEV1-FVC-specific fine-mapping or colocalization study with genome-wide-significant, locus-overlapping signal assigned to either LINGO2 or ACO1 (not merely nearby, non-overlapping signals); (2) direct GWAS Catalog / primary-literature confirmation (not just Open Targets aggregation) of what rs2031830 or close proxies are associated with, including whether smoking-behavior GWAS (e.g., GSCAN) reach genome-wide significance at this exact interval; (3) any eQTL, Hi-C, or ABC-model enhancer-promoter evidence directly linking this intergenic interval to LINGO2, ACO1, or a more distant gene; (4) tissue- resolved expression data (lung epithelium, airway smooth muscle, immune cells) for LINGO2/ACO1 that current literature does not provide. Given the current state of evidence, this locus should be treated as unresolved by the study team pending such data, not assigned to any single gene.
