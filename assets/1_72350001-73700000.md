# COPD locus evidence table: 1:72,350,001-73,700,000 (GRCh38)

**Nearest gene:** LRRIQ3 | **PoPS prioritized:** NEGR1 | **FLAMES/MAGMA/ABC prioritized:** NA
**Lead variant:** rs12142152 (1:73,189,630, PIP=0.322) | **Credible set size:** 237 variants (ABF finemapping)

**Locus/region note:** This 1.35 Mb window is a genuine gene desert (confirmed via Ensembl
overlap query by the researcher) — no protein-coding gene lies inside the region itself.
LRRIQ3 sits ~840 kb from the lead variant, past the region's centromeric boundary; NEGR1
sits ~70 kb telomeric of the region's start, also outside the window. FPGT-TNNI3K is
immediately adjacent to (3' of) LRRIQ3. The 237-variant credible set contains no
missense, splice-donor, or splice-acceptor variant; nearly all variants are
intergenic/upstream/downstream of a 7SK snRNA pseudogene (RN7SKP19) or non-coding, and one
very-low-PIP variant is nearest a keratin pseudogene (KRT8P21). Per pipeline scope, no
variant-by-variant literature search was performed beyond the lead variant; a targeted
PubMed search on the lead variant rsID (rs12142152) returned zero results.

## Notes on evidence strength (read together with skeptic verdicts above)
- No verified citation in this table establishes lung-structural, airway-epithelial, or
  COPD-relevant immune (neutrophil/T-cell/macrophage) biology for either LRRIQ3 or NEGR1.
  The one citation that would have provided this (a suggestive DLCO GWAS hit for NEGR1)
  was **excluded** as CLAIM_MISMATCH — see audit appendix.
- The LRRIQ3-region GWAS evidence (opioid use, chronic pain/MDD) is built on a different
  variant (rs640561) than this locus's lead variant (rs12142152), and the strongest
  supporting source (Johnston et al.) itself names multiple co-located candidate genes
  (LINC01360, LRRIQ3, FPGT, FPGT-TNNI3K) rather than pinpointing LRRIQ3 — see audit
  appendix for the skeptic's full reasoning on why gene-specificity is NOT established.
- All verified evidence for both genes is neurobehavioral/psychiatric/addiction in nature.
  This pattern, taken together with the (unverified, database-only) Open Targets
  colocalization pattern in the summary below, is consistent with biological prior #1
  (smoking-mediated/neurobehavioral pathways) — but the skeptic emphasizes this rests on
  an *absence* of pulmonary evidence rather than a *positive* smoking-mechanism finding at
  the gene level (Wang et al. 2025 for NEGR1 is the closest thing to a positive
  smoking-specific gene-level finding).

## Prioritized gene

**Prioritized gene: NONE with confidence above LOW — best-supported single pick is LRRIQ3, confidence LOW.**

This locus does not permit a confident single-gene call, and that should not be
obscured. The credible set (237 variants) contains no coding or splice variant, the
associated interval is a genuine gene desert, and both candidate genes (LRRIQ3, PoPS's
NEGR1) sit well outside the window (840 kb and 70 kb away respectively), with no
gene-specific eQTL colocalization evidence for either one anywhere in this dossier. The
Open Targets L2G model nominally favors LRRIQ3, but red-team-skeptic's critique — which I
find persuasive — shows this is very likely a distance-based artifact (the identical 0.08
score recurs across 43 unrelated traits) rather than trait-specific resolution, so it
cannot be treated as a real tie-breaker. The strongest gene-level literature for LRRIQ3
(opioid use / chronic pain GWAS via rs640561) comes from a source (Johnston et al. 2019)
that itself names multiple co-located candidate genes (LINC01360, FPGT, FPGT-TNNI3K) in
the same gene-dense region, so it does not establish LRRIQ3-specificity either.

I am naming **LRRIQ3** as the nominal top pick only because (i) it is the Open Targets
L2G-preferred gene even if that preference is weak/likely-artifactual, and (ii) it has a
directly-relevant (if not gene-uniquely-resolved) verified GWAS association with an
opioid/pain/psychiatric-behavior phenotype cluster at essentially the same locus. The
runner-up, **NEGR1**, has equally strong (arguably stronger, via Wang et al. 2025)
verified smoking-behavior-adjacent genetic evidence and is a genuinely LoF-constrained
gene (unlike LRRIQ3), but was never L2G-preferred and its only would-be pulmonary-specific
evidence (a suggestive DLCO GWAS hit) was excluded on citation-verification grounds (see
audit appendix) — leaving NEGR1 with zero verified pulmonary evidence.

What would resolve this tie: gene-specific eQTL/pQTL colocalization for LRRIQ3, NEGR1,
and FPGT-TNNI3K in lung and/or brain tissue at this exact credible set; a successfully
verified full-text confirmation of the Sakornsakolpat DLCO finding (or a fresh
DLCO/FEV1/COPD GWAS lookup) for NEGR1; and, ideally, an independent literature or
functional check on FPGT-TNNI3K, which was not evaluated as a candidate in this review
despite being named alongside LRRIQ3 in the strongest cited source.

Independent of which gene is causal, the convergent pattern across the Open Targets
colocalization (near-null FEV1/chronic-bronchitis h4, strong smoking-initiation/status h4)
and the gene-level literature (both candidates dominated by psychiatric/addiction/behavioral
findings, one with a direct smoking-behavior genetic link) is consistent with biological
prior #1: this COPD GWAS signal more likely reflects a neurobehavioral/smoking-mediated
pathway than direct pulmonary structural or COPD-intrinsic immune biology. Per
red-team-skeptic's critique, this conclusion currently rests more on an *absence* of
pulmonary evidence than on a *positive*, gene-level smoking mechanism (with the partial
exception of the verified NEGR1-smoking-behavior cross-trait GWAS finding), and the study
team should weigh it accordingly.
