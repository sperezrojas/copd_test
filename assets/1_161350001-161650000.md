## Additional narrative findings (not independent citations)

- **Smoking-mediation check (prior #1):** No evidence was found that this locus overlaps known smoking-initiation, cigarettes-per-day, nicotine-dependence, or cessation GWAS signals. This argues against a smoking-behavior-neurobiology mechanism for this COPD signal, though it is an absence-of-evidence finding from PubMed-indexed literature rather than a confirmed negative from GWAS Catalog/GSCAN summary statistics directly.
- **Lung-function concordance check (prior #2):** No FEV1 or FEV1/FVC spirometry GWAS association was found in the literature for either HSPA6 or FCGR3A specifically. This is a notable gap under standing prior #2 — a COPD association without lung-function concordance warrants scrutiny of alternative (here, immune/autoimmune-pleiotropic) pathways.
- **Immune complexity/cell-type specificity (prior #3):** FCGR3A's biology (NK cells, non-classical CD16+ monocytes, ADCC/immune-complex handling) is immune-cell-intrinsic and mechanistically plausible for immune-mediated COPD subtypes, but no COPD-specific, sputum, or airway-inflammation study was found testing this directly. HSPA6 has no characterized cell-type-specific immune role distinct from general HSP70-family stress biology.

## Prioritized gene

**FCGR3A — confidence: LOW.**

Neither candidate gene has direct COPD-specific genetic or functional evidence: PubMed searches
for "HSPA6 COPD" and "FCGR3A COPD" each returned essentially no on-topic hits, and neither gene
has a published lung-function (FEV1/FEV1-FVC) association. The choice between them therefore
rests on indirect evidence, and that evidence is genuinely mixed rather than decisive.

FCGR3A is favored over HSPA6 for three reasons, each individually modest: (1) the locus's own
lead variant, rs2099684 (PIP=0.85), has a specific, verified functional annotation — a
cell-type-resolved eQTL for FCGR3A in NK cells (Yazar et al. 2026, PMID 42102734) — whereas the
same variant has no analogous functional link to HSPA6; if anything this finding is a competing
mechanism that argues against HSPA6, which is why red-team-skeptic marked HSPA6 CONTESTED rather
than merely under-supported. (2) FCGR3A's established immune-cell-intrinsic biology (NK cells,
CD16+ monocytes, ADCC/immune-complex handling) is at least mechanistically compatible with the
heterogeneous immune programs implicated in COPD (standing prior #3), even though no COPD-
specific study tests this directly. (3) Open Targets' COPD target-disease association score is
higher for FCGR3A (0.104) than HSPA6 (0.041) on both the genetic_association and literature
datatype components.

However, this preference for FCGR3A should not be read as strong. Red-team-skeptic identified
substantial reasons for caution that are preserved here rather than smoothed over: the
NK-cell eQTL is a blood/PBMC finding, not lung-tissue evidence; the Takayasu-arteritis and
malaria-antibody associations cited in support of FCGR3A are both non-COPD, non-lung phenotypes
used as mechanistic analogy rather than direct corroboration, and the Takayasu signal itself is
explicitly reported as a joint "FCGR2A/FCGR3A" association, not FCGR3A-specific; and in one of
the two malaria studies cited, rs2099684 itself was not even the variant that reached
significance. Most importantly, FCGR3A cannot be confidently distinguished from its immediate,
highly homologous paralogs FCGR3B and FCGR2A at this locus — no cited study isolates FCGR3A's
effect from FCGR3B/FCGR2A using allele-specific or CNV-aware data, and MAGMA (the only internal
method prioritizing FCGR3A) is known to have limited resolution among tightly linked paralogous
genes. This is compounded by Open Targets' own structured data: FCGR3B, a gene not even on this
locus's internal candidate list, has both the highest COPD genetic_association score (0.433) and
the most frequent top-L2G ranking of any gene in this cluster across the non-COPD traits
surveyed. Red-team-skeptic explicitly flagged that the true causal gene at this locus may not be
HSPA6 or FCGR3A at all, but rather FCGR3B — a possibility this review cannot resolve because
FCGR3B was outside the candidate-gene scope defined by the internal pipeline for this locus.

**Runner-up:** HSPA6 (nearest gene, PoPS- and ABC-prioritized) — marked CONTESTED by red-team-
skeptic on the strength of the specific competing eQTL finding above, not merely absent evidence.
**Out-of-scope but flagged:** FCGR3B — not a candidate gene per the internal pipeline definition
for this locus, but surfaced by both Open Targets structured data and red-team-skeptic critique
as plausibly the strongest gene-level candidate in this cluster; it involves neutrophil biology
directly relevant to COPD's neutrophilic-inflammation axis (standing prior #3).

**What would resolve this:** (1) A COPD- or lung-function-specific eQTL/colocalization study in
lung tissue or relevant lung-infiltrating immune-cell types (not blood/PBMC) for FCGR3A vs.
FCGR3B vs. FCGR2A vs. HSPA6 at this exact locus. (2) CNV-aware or allele-specific expression
analysis capable of separating FCGR3A from FCGR3B, given FCGR3B's common copy-number variation.
(3) Direct FEV1/FEV1-FVC association testing for this credible set. (4) Extension of the
candidate-gene list at this locus to formally include FCGR3B, given the structured database
signal pointing toward it. Given the number of open questions, this locus's gene assignment
should be treated as unresolved/low-confidence by the study team pending further evidence, not
as a settled call between HSPA6 and FCGR3A alone.
